# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Time, Speed & Distance?

**Time, Speed & Distance (TSD)** is the study of motion — how objects move from one place to another, how fast they move, and how long they take.

> A train leaves Delhi at 60 km/h. Another train leaves Mumbai at 80 km/h.
> When and where will they meet?
> This is the essence of TSD.

### The Golden Formula

> **Distance = Speed × Time**
>
> **D = S × T**
>
> This single equation governs ALL of TSD.

### The Three Rearrangements

| Find | Formula | Think |
|---|---|---|
| **Distance** | D = S × T | \"How far?\" |
| **Speed** | S = D / T | \"How fast?\" |
| **Time** | T = D / S | \"How long?\" |

### Units & Conversions (CRITICAL!)

| Conversion | Value | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| **km/h → m/s** | × 5/18 | \"Multiply by 5, divide by 18\" |
| **m/s → km/h** | × 18/5 | \"Multiply by 18, divide by 5\" |
| **1 km** | 1000 m | — |
| **1 hour** | 60 min = 3600 sec | — |
| **1 mile** | ≈ 1.6 km | Rare in Indian exams |

### Quick Conversion Table

## CONCEPT 1: Basic Speed, Distance & Time

### The Fundamental Triangle

```
         D
        / \
       /   \
      S  ×  T
```

> **D = S × T** | **S = D/T** | **T = D/S**

### Example 1

A car travels 240 km in 4 hours. Find speed.

S = D/T = 240/4 = **60 km/h** ✅

### Example 2

A train runs at 90 km/h. How far does it go in 40 minutes?

T = 40 min = 40/60 hours = 2/3 hours
D = 90 × 2/3 = **60 km** ✅

### Example 3

How long to cover 450 km at 75 km/h?

T = D/S = 450/75 = **6 hours** ✅

### Unit Matching Rule

> **Always ensure units match!**
> If speed in km/h and time in minutes → convert time to hours first.
> If speed in m/s and distance in km → convert distance to meters first.

## CONCEPT 2: Average Speed

### THE MOST COMMON TRAP

> **Average Speed ≠ Average of Speeds!**
>
> **Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time**

### Case 1: Same Distance, Different Speeds

> If a person travels distance **d** at speed **a** and returns at speed **b**:
>
> **Average Speed = 2ab / (a + b)** (Harmonic Mean)

### Proof

Going: Time₁ = d/a. Returning: Time₂ = d/b.
Total distance = 2d. Total time = d/a + d/b = d(a+b)/ab.
Average speed = 2d / [d(a+b)/ab] = **2ab/(a+b)** ✅

### Example

A person goes from A to B at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Average speed?

Avg = 2 × 40 × 60 / (40 + 60) = 4800/100 = **48 km/h** ✅

**NOT** (40+60)/2 = 50 km/h ❌

### Case 2: Same Time, Different Speeds

> If a person travels for time **t** at speed **a**, then for time **t** at speed **b**:
>
> **Average Speed = (a + b) / 2** (Arithmetic Mean)

### Case 3: General Case

> Different distances d₁, d₂ at speeds s₁, s₂:
>
> **Average Speed = (d₁ + d₂) / (d₁/s₁ + d₂/s₂)**

### Example

300 km at 60 km/h, then 200 km at 50 km/h.

Time₁ = 300/60 = 5 hrs. Time₂ = 200/50 = 4 hrs.
Avg = 500/9 = **55.56 km/h** ✅

## CONCEPT 3: Proportionality in TSD

### D = S × T — Three Variables, Three Relationships

| If Constant | Relationship | Proportionality |
|---|---|---|
| **Distance constant** | Speed ↑ → Time ↓ | S ∝ 1/T (Inversely proportional) |
| **Speed constant** | Distance ↑ → Time ↑ | D ∝ T (Directly proportional) |
| **Time constant** | Speed ↑ → Distance ↑ | D ∝ S (Directly proportional) |

### Example 1 (Constant Distance)

If speed increases by 25%, time decreases by what %?

New speed = 1.25S. New time = D/1.25S = T/1.25 = 0.8T.
Time decreases by **20%** ✅

### Example 2 (Speed Ratio → Time Ratio)

Speeds of A and B are in ratio 3:4. Time ratio for same distance?

Time ratio = 4:3 (inverse of speed ratio) ✅

### Example 3

A person walks at 4 km/h and is 10 minutes late. At 5 km/h, he is 10 minutes early. Find the distance.

Let distance = d km.
Time at 4 km/h − Time at 5 km/h = 20 minutes = 1/3 hour
d/4 − d/5 = 1/3
d(5−4)/20 = 1/3
d/20 = 1/3
d = **20/3 km ≈ 6.67 km** ✅

### The Late/Early Formula

> If at speed **a**, person is **t₁** minutes late, and at speed **b**, person is **t₂** minutes early:
>
> **Distance = ab(t₁ + t₂) / [60(b − a)]** (when t₁, t₂ in minutes)

## CONCEPT 4: Relative Speed

### The Core Idea

> **Relative speed** is how fast the distance between two objects is changing.

### Same Direction

> **Relative Speed = |S₁ − S₂|** (Difference of speeds)
>
> Objects moving in the same direction come closer slowly.

### Opposite Direction

> **Relative Speed = S₁ + S₂** (Sum of speeds)
>
> Objects moving toward each other come closer quickly.

### Example 1

A walks at 5 km/h, B walks at 3 km/h. Both walk east.
Relative speed = 5 − 3 = **2 km/h** (A gains on B at 2 km/h)

### Example 2

A walks east at 5 km/h, B walks west at 3 km/h.
Relative speed = 5 + 3 = **8 km/h** (they separate at 8 km/h)

### Meeting Problems

> Two objects start from A and B (distance D apart) and move toward each other:
>
> **Time to meet = D / (S₁ + S₂)**
>
> **Meeting point from A = S₁ × Time to meet**

### Example

A and B are 300 km apart. A starts at 50 km/h, B at 70 km/h. They move toward each other.

Time to meet = 300/(50+70) = 300/120 = **2.5 hours**
Meeting from A: 50 × 2.5 = **125 km from A** ✅

### Catching Up / Overtaking

> A is ahead of B by distance **d**. B is faster.
>
> **Time for B to catch A = d / (S_B − S_A)**

## CONCEPT 5: Trains — Crossing a Pole / Man (Stationary Object)

### The Key Insight

> When a train crosses a **pole** or a **standing person**, the train must cover **its own length**.
>
> **Time = Length of Train / Speed of Train**

### Why?

The train starts crossing when its front reaches the pole. It finishes when its tail passes the pole. Distance covered = length of train.

### Example 1

A train 150 m long crosses a pole in 10 seconds. Speed?

S = 150/10 = 15 m/s = 15 × 18/5 = **54 km/h** ✅

### Example 2

A train at 72 km/h crosses a man in 12 seconds. Length of train?

Speed = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s
Length = S × T = 20 × 12 = **240 m** ✅

---

## CONCEPT 6: Trains — Crossing a Platform / Bridge (Stationary Object with Length)

### The Key Insight

> When a train crosses a **platform** or **bridge**, it must cover **its own length + platform's length**.
>
> **Time = (Length of Train + Length of Platform) / Speed of Train**
>
> **Distance = L_train + L_platform**

### Example 1

A train 200 m long crosses a 300 m platform in 25 seconds. Speed?

D = 200 + 300 = 500 m
S = 500/25 = 20 m/s = **72 km/h** ✅

### Example 2

A train at 90 km/h crosses a bridge in 36 seconds. Train length = 200 m. Bridge length?

Speed = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s
D = S × T = 25 × 36 = 900 m
Bridge = 900 − 200 = **700 m** ✅

### Example 3

A train crosses a pole in 8 seconds and a 264 m platform in 20 seconds. Find train's length and speed.

Let train length = L, speed = S.
Pole: L/S = 8 → L = 8S … (i)
Platform: (L + 264)/S = 20 → L + 264 = 20S … (ii)

From (i): L = 8S. Put in (ii): 8S + 264 = 20S → 12S = 264 → S = 22 m/s
L = 8 × 22 = **176 m**
Speed = 22 m/s = 22 × 18/5 = **79.2 km/h** ✅

---

## CONCEPT 7: Trains — Crossing Another Train

### Two Trains, Two Cases

#### Case 1: Opposite Direction (Moving Toward Each Other)

> **Relative Speed = S₁ + S₂**
> **Distance = L₁ + L₂** (sum of both train lengths)
> **Time = (L₁ + L₂) / (S₁ + S₂)**

### Example

Train A: 120 m, 40 km/h. Train B: 180 m, 50 km/h. Moving toward each other.

Relative speed = 40 + 50 = 90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s
Distance = 120 + 180 = 300 m
Time = 300/25 = **12 seconds** ✅

#### Case 2: Same Direction (One Overtaking the Other)

> **Relative Speed = |S₁ − S₂|** (faster − slower)
> **Distance = L₁ + L₂** (sum of both train lengths)
> **Time = (L₁ + L₂) / |S₁ − S₂|**

### Example

Train A: 150 m, 72 km/h. Train B: 250 m, 36 km/h. A overtakes B.

Relative speed = 72 − 36 = 36 km/h = 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s
Distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m
Time = 400/10 = **40 seconds** ✅

### Key Summary Table

| Scenario | Relative Speed | Distance to Cover |
|---|---|---|
| Train crosses pole/man | Train's speed | Train's length |
| Train crosses platform/bridge | Train's speed | Train length + Platform length |
| Two trains, opposite direction | S₁ + S₂ | L₁ + L₂ |
| Two trains, same direction | S₁ − S₂ | L₁ + L₂ |
| Train crosses a person walking same direction | S_train − S_person | Train's length |
| Train crosses a person walking opposite direction | S_train + S_person | Train's length |

## CONCEPT 8: Boats & Streams

### The Core Idea

> A boat has its own speed in **still water** (no current).
> A **stream/current** adds or subtracts from this speed depending on direction.

### Key Terms

| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed of boat in still water** | B | Boat's own speed |
| **Speed of stream/current** | S | Water's speed |
| **Downstream speed** | D | Boat goes WITH the current = **B + S** |
| **Upstream speed** | U | Boat goes AGAINST the current = **B − S** |

### The Formulas

> **Downstream Speed = B + S**
> **Upstream Speed = B − S**
>
> From these:
> **B = (D + U) / 2** (Still water speed = average of downstream & upstream)
> **S = (D − U) / 2** (Stream speed = half the difference)

### Example 1

A boat's speed in still water = 10 km/h. Stream speed = 2 km/h.

Downstream = 10 + 2 = **12 km/h**
Upstream = 10 − 2 = **8 km/h** ✅

### Example 2

Downstream speed = 18 km/h, Upstream speed = 12 km/h. Find B and S.

B = (18 + 12)/2 = **15 km/h**
S = (18 − 12)/2 = **3 km/h** ✅

### Example 3

A boat covers 36 km downstream in 4 hours and returns in 6 hours. Find B and S.

Downstream speed = 36/4 = 9 km/h
Upstream speed = 36/6 = 6 km/h

B = (9 + 6)/2 = **7.5 km/h**
S = (9 − 6)/2 = **1.5 km/h** ✅

### Example 4

A man rows 12 km downstream and 8 km upstream in 4 hours. He rows 16 km downstream and 12 km upstream in 6 hours. Find B and S.

12/(B+S) + 8/(B−S) = 4 … (i)
16/(B+S) + 12/(B−S) = 6 … (ii)

Let 1/(B+S) = x, 1/(B−S) = y.
12x + 8y = 4 … (i)
16x + 12y = 6 … (ii)

(i) × 3: 36x + 24y = 12
(ii) × 2: 32x + 24y = 12
Subtracting: 4x = 0 → x = 0? 

Let me re-check: these give 4x = 0, which means the numbers don't work out. Let me use better numbers:

A man rows 30 km downstream and 18 km upstream in 6 hours. He rows 45 km downstream and 24 km upstream in 8.5 hours.

30/(B+S) + 18/(B−S) = 6 … (i)
45/(B+S) + 24/(B−S) = 8.5 … (ii)

Let x = 1/(B+S), y = 1/(B−S).
30x + 18y = 6 → 5x + 3y = 1 … (i)
45x + 24y = 8.5 → 90x + 48y = 17 … (ii)

From (i): x = (1 − 3y)/5. Sub in (ii):
90(1−3y)/5 + 48y = 17 → 18(1−3y) + 48y = 17 → 18 − 54y + 48y = 17 → −6y = −1 → y = 1/6

B − S = 6. x = (1 − 3/6)/5 = (1 − 1/2)/5 = 1/10. B + S = 10.

B = (10 + 6)/2 = **8 km/h**. S = (10 − 6)/2 = **2 km/h** ✅

### Key Relationship

> **Time downstream : Time upstream = (B−S) : (B+S)**
> (for same distance — time is inversely proportional to speed)

## CONCEPT 9: Races & Circular Tracks

### Races — Linear Track

#### Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A beats B by x meters** | When A finishes, B is x meters behind the finish line |
| **A beats B by t seconds** | A finishes t seconds before B |
| **Dead heat** | Both finish at the same time |
| **Head start / Start** | One person starts ahead of the other |

#### Example 1: A beats B by 20 m in a 100 m race

When A runs 100 m, B runs only 80 m (in the same time).
Speed ratio = 100 : 80 = **5 : 4**

#### Example 2: A gives B a 20 m head start

B starts 20 m ahead. A must cover 100 m, B covers only 80 m.
For a dead heat: Time_A = Time_B → 100/S_A = 80/S_B → S_A/S_B = 100/80 = **5/4**

#### Example 3: A beats B by 20 m and B beats C by 25 m in 200 m race.

A runs 200 m, B runs 180 m (in that time).
B runs 200 m, C runs 175 m.
When B runs 180 m, C runs = 175 × 180/200 = **157.5 m**
A beats C by 200 − 157.5 = **42.5 m** ✅

### Formula for Combined Beat

> In a race of **D** meters:
> A beats B by **a** meters, B beats C by **b** meters.
>
> **A beats C by: a + b − ab/D** meters

Verify: a=20, b=25, D=200 → 20 + 25 − 20×25/200 = 45 − 2.5 = **42.5** ✅

### Circular Track

#### Two People Running Around a Circular Track

**Same Direction:**
> They meet when the faster person gains a full lap.
> **Time to first meet = Circumference / |S₁ − S₂|**

**Opposite Direction:**
> They meet when they collectively cover a full lap.
> **Time to first meet = Circumference / (S₁ + S₂)**

#### Example

Track circumference = 400 m. A: 5 m/s, B: 3 m/s.

Same direction: Time = 400/(5−3) = 400/2 = **200 seconds**
Opposite direction: Time = 400/(5+3) = 400/8 = **50 seconds** ✅

#### Meeting Point

> When running in same direction, they meet at the starting point after:
> **LCM(T_A, T_B)** where T_A = Circumference/S_A, T_B = Circumference/S_B

#### Three or More on Circular Track

For n people with speeds S₁, S₂, …, Sₙ running same direction:

Time for all to meet for the first time at any point:
> **LCM of pairwise meeting times**

Time for all to meet at the starting point:
> **LCM(C/S₁, C/S₂, …, C/Sₙ)** where C = circumference

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Constant Distance → Speed and Time are Inversely Proportional

> **S₁/S₂ = T₂/T₁**
>
> If speed ratio is 3:4, time ratio is 4:3.

This avoids calculating distance entirely.

---

## Shortcut 2: Late/Early Shortcut

> Speed **a** → late by **t₁** min. Speed **b** → early by **t₂** min.
>
> **Distance = a × b × (t₁ + t₂) / [60 × (b − a)]**

Example: 4 km/h → 20 min late. 6 km/h → 20 min early.
D = 4 × 6 × 40 / (60 × 2) = 960/120 = **8 km** ✅

---

## Shortcut 3: Two Trains Crossing — Convert to m/s First

Always convert to **m/s** before doing train problems. Lengths are in meters, times in seconds.

72 km/h = 20 m/s. Do this FIRST — saves confusion.

---

## Shortcut 4: Boats — Direct B and S

> Given downstream time **t₁** and upstream time **t₂** for same distance **d**:
>
> B = d/2 × (1/t₁ + 1/t₂)
> S = d/2 × (1/t₁ − 1/t₂)

---

## Shortcut 5: Speed Increase/Decrease → Time Change

> Speed becomes **n/d** of original → Time becomes **d/n** of original.
>
> Speed ↑ by 25% (becomes 5/4) → Time ↓ by 1/5 (becomes 4/5) → Time ↓ by 20%.

Common conversions:

| Speed Change | Speed Multiplier | Time Multiplier | Time Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ↑ 20% | 6/5 | 5/6 | ↓ 16.67% |
| ↑ 25% | 5/4 | 4/5 | ↓ 20% |
| ↑ 33.33% | 4/3 | 3/4 | ↓ 25% |
| ↑ 50% | 3/2 | 2/3 | ↓ 33.33% |
| ↑ 100% (doubled) | 2 | 1/2 | ↓ 50% |
| ↓ 20% | 4/5 | 5/4 | ↑ 25% |
| ↓ 25% | 3/4 | 4/3 | ↑ 33.33% |
| ↓ 50% | 1/2 | 2 | ↑ 100% |

---

## Shortcut 6: Meeting Point — Time Based

> A and B start from two ends (distance D) at speeds S₁ and S₂ toward each other.
> They meet at a point that divides D in ratio **S₁ : S₂**.

---

## Shortcut 7: Train Passing a Moving Person

> Same direction: Relative speed = S_train − S_person. Distance = L_train.
> Opposite direction: Relative speed = S_train + S_person. Distance = L_train.

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Average Speed ≠ Average of Speeds

| Scenario | Correct Formula |
|---|---|
| Same distance at speeds a, b | 2ab/(a+b) — **Harmonic Mean** |
| Same time at speeds a, b | (a+b)/2 — **Arithmetic Mean** |
| Different distances & speeds | Total Distance / Total Time |

Students almost always use (a+b)/2. This is wrong for same-distance problems.

---

## Confusion 2: km/h vs m/s — Forgetting to Convert

| Problem gives | Length in meters, time in seconds | Speed should be in |
|---|---|---|
| Speed in km/h | Yes | **m/s** → Multiply by 5/18 |
| Speed in m/s | Distance in km, time in hours | **km/h** → Multiply by 18/5 |

> **Always check units match before plugging into D = S × T.**

---

## Confusion 3: Distance in Train Problems

| Crossing | Distance = |
|---|---|
| Pole / Man (stationary, no length) | **Train's length only** |
| Platform / Bridge (has length) | **Train length + Platform length** |
| Another train | **Sum of both train lengths** |

Students often forget to add platform length, or add man's \"height\" (irrelevant!).

---

## Confusion 4: Same Direction vs Opposite Direction

| Same Direction | Opposite Direction |
|---|---|
| Relative speed = **Difference** | Relative speed = **Sum** |
| Takes **longer** to cross/meet | Takes **shorter** to cross/meet |
| Think: two cars on highway | Think: head-on approach |

---

## Confusion 5: Upstream = Slower, Downstream = Faster

Students sometimes add stream speed to upstream. Remember:

> **Upstream = AGAINST current = SLOWER = B − S**
> **Downstream = WITH current = FASTER = B + S**
>
> Think: rowing against the river is HARD → slower.

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


| # | Trap | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Average speed trap: (a+b)/2 for same-distance** | Use 2ab/(a+b) for same distance |
| 2 | **Units mismatch: km/h with seconds** | Always convert first |
| 3 | **Train crossing pole vs platform** | Pole: L only. Platform: L+P |
| 4 | **Same direction vs opposite in trains** | Same: subtract speeds. Opposite: add speeds |
| 5 | **Upstream speed: adding stream speed** | Upstream = B−S (subtract!) |
| 6 | **\"Beats by x meters\": confusing who is behind** | A beats B → B is behind by x m when A finishes |
| 7 | **Circular track: using wrong formula** | Same dir: divide by diff. Opposite: divide by sum |
| 8 | **Train length not given: assumed zero** | If crossing a pole, distance = train length (find it) |
| 9 | **Forgetting to convert minutes to hours** | 20 min = 20/60 = 1/3 hour |
| 10 | **Speed of man on platform ignored** | Man on platform = stationary (speed = 0) |

| \"Upstream/downstream\" | Boats & Streams | Down=B+S, Up=B−S |
| \"Still water speed\" | Boats | B = (D+U)/2 |
| \"A beats B by ___ m\" | Races | Speed ratio from distance ratio |
| \"Circular track, same/opposite direction\" | Circular Track | C/(diff or sum of speeds) |
| \"Speed increased by ___%, time changes by?\" | Proportionality | Inverse relationship |
| \"Two people start from A and B toward each other\" | Meeting Point | Time = D/(S₁+S₂) |

# STEP 9 — SOLVING STRATEGY


| Question Type | Fastest Method |
|---|---|
| Basic D, S, T | Direct formula D = S × T |
| Average speed (same dist) | 2ab/(a+b) directly |
| Late/Early | Shortcut formula |
| Train problems | Convert to m/s first, then apply |

| Train crossing platform | (L+P)/S |

| Races | Speed ratios from distance ratios |
| Circular track | C/Relative speed |
| Proportionality | Ratio method — S₁/S₂ = T₂/T₁ |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


## Speed Rule 1: Convert km/h to m/s IMMEDIATELY for Train Problems

Don't do any calculation until speeds are in m/s. This prevents unit errors and simplifies everything.

## Speed Rule 2: Memorize the Conversion Table

18→5, 36→10, 54→15, 72→20, 90→25, 108→30. Know these by heart.

## Speed Rule 3: Use Ratios Instead of Absolute Values

If speeds are 3:4, don't compute actual speeds. Work with ratios — much faster.

## Speed Rule 4: Average Speed — Use 2ab/(a+b) Only for Same Distance

If the question says \"goes and comes back\" or \"covers equal distances at different speeds\" → Harmonic mean.

## Speed Rule 5: Boats — Just Find D and U, Then B and S

Downstream speed = Distance/Downstream time.
Upstream speed = Distance/Upstream time.
Then: B = (D+U)/2, S = (D−U)/2.

## Speed Rule 6: Train Crossing Another Train — One Formula

Just apply: **Time = (L₁+L₂) / Relative Speed**. Pick sum or difference based on direction.

## Speed Rule 7: Race Problems — Use Ratios

\"A beats B by 20 m in 100 m race\" → When A runs 100, B runs 80.
Ratio = 100:80 = 5:4. Done. No need to find actual speeds.

# STEP 11 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES


## Mnemonic 1: D = S × T

> **\"DST\"** — **D**istance = **S**peed × **T**ime
>
> Or visualize the \"DST Triangle\": D on top, S and T at the bottom.

## Mnemonic 2: km/h to m/s

> **\"Kill the 18, Save the 5\"**
> km/h → m/s: Divide by 18, multiply by 5 (or × 5/18)
>
> **\"18 Marches, 5 Kills\"**
> m/s → km/h: Multiply by 18, divide by 5 (or × 18/5)

## Mnemonic 3: Average Speed (Same Distance)

> **\"Not the average! Use 2ab/(a+b)\"**
>
> Remember: **\"Harmonic, not Arithmetic\"**

## Mnemonic 4: Boats Direction

> **\"Downstream = Easy = Add\"** (B + S)
> **\"Upstream = Hard = Subtract\"** (B − S)
>
> Think: Going downstream is like surfing — the water helps. Going upstream is like swimming against the current — it slows you down.

## Mnemonic 5: Train Crossing

> **\"Pole = Length only\"** (train covers its own body)
> **\"Platform = Length + Length\"** (train covers its body + the bridge)
> **\"Another train = Both bodies\"** (L₁ + L₂)

## Mnemonic 6: Relative Speed Direction

> **\"Same = Slow (Subtract)\"** — Same direction, subtract speeds.
> **\"Opposite = Quick (Add)\"** — Opposite direction, add speeds.
>
> Think: Walking the same direction as traffic → cars pass you slowly. Walking against traffic → cars zoom past.

## Mnemonic 7: Races

> **\"A beats B by x\"** = B is **x meters short** when A reaches the finish.
> B's distance = Race distance − x.

# STEP 12 — PRACTICE SESSION


---

**Q1.** A car covers 300 km in 5 hours. Speed?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
S = 300/5 = **60 km/h**
</details>

**Q2.** Convert 90 km/h to m/s.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
90 × 5/18 = **25 m/s**
</details>

**Q3.** A goes from X to Y at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Average speed?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
2 × 40 × 60 / (40+60) = 4800/100 = **48 km/h**
</details>

**Q4.** A train 250 m long crosses a pole in 10 sec. Speed in km/h?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
S = 250/10 = 25 m/s = 25 × 18/5 = **90 km/h**
</details>

**Q5.** A train 200 m long at 72 km/h crosses a 400 m bridge. Time?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Speed = 20 m/s. D = 200+400 = 600 m. T = 600/20 = **30 seconds**
</details>

**Q6.** Two trains (150 m, 250 m) approach each other at 60 km/h and 40 km/h. Crossing time?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Relative = 100 km/h = 250/9 m/s. D = 400 m. T = 400/(250/9) = 3600/250 = **14.4 seconds**
</details>

**Q7.** Downstream: 24 km in 3 hrs. Upstream: 24 km in 4 hrs. Find B and S.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
D = 24/3 = 8 km/h. U = 24/4 = 6 km/h. B = (8+6)/2 = **7 km/h**. S = (8−6)/2 = **1 km/h**
</details>

**Q8.** In a 200 m race, A beats B by 40 m. Speed ratio?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
A:200, B:160. Ratio = 200:160 = **5:4**
</details>

**Q9.** Circular track 600 m. A: 10 m/s, B: 4 m/s. Same direction, first meeting time?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
T = 600/(10−4) = 600/6 = **100 seconds**
</details>

**Q10.** A person walks at 5 km/h and is 20 min late. At 7 km/h, he is 20 min early. Distance?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
D = 5 × 7 × (20+20) / [60 × (7−5)] = 35 × 40 / (60 × 2) = 1400/120 = **35/3 km ≈ 11.67 km**
</details>

# STEP 14 — REVISION PACK


```
TIME, SPEED & DISTANCE — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

D = S × T.  S = D/T.  T = D/S.

CONVERSION:
  km/h → m/s: × 5/18.  m/s → km/h: × 18/5.
  Quick: 18↔5, 36↔10, 54↔15, 72↔20, 90↔25.

AVERAGE SPEED:
  Same distance: 2ab/(a+b).
  Same time: (a+b)/2.
  General: Total D / Total T.

RELATIVE SPEED:
  Same direction: S₁ − S₂.
  Opposite direction: S₁ + S₂.

TRAINS:
  Crosses pole/man: T = L / S.
  Crosses platform/bridge: T = (L+P) / S.
  Crosses another train: T = (L₁+L₂) / Relative Speed.
  Opposite: add speeds. Same: subtract speeds.

BOATS & STREAMS:
  Downstream = B + S. Upstream = B − S.
  B = (D+U)/2. S = (D−U)/2.

RACES:
  A beats B by x m in D m race → speed ratio = D : (D−x).
  Combined: A beats C by a + b − ab/D meters.

CIRCULAR TRACK:
  Same dir: T = C/(S₁−S₂). Opp dir: T = C/(S₁+S₂).
  Meet at start: LCM(C/S₁, C/S₂).

PROPORTIONALITY:
  Constant D: S ∝ 1/T.
  Speed ↑ 25% → Time ↓ 20%.
```

## 🚨 Trap Sheet

| Trap | Defense |
|---|---|
| Average speed = (a+b)/2 | NO! Use 2ab/(a+b) for same distance |
| Units mismatch | Convert km/h to m/s for train problems |
| Pole vs Platform distance | Pole: L only. Platform: L+P |
| Same dir: adding speeds | NO! Same = Subtract |
| Upstream: B + S | NO! Upstream = B − S |
| Minutes not converted to hours | Convert! 20 min = 1/3 hr |
| Man's height in train problem | Irrelevant — man has no \"length\" |
