# 02. Classification (Odd One Out)

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Classification?**
Classification literally means "grouping." In competitive exams, this topic is popularly known as "Odd One Out." You will be given 4 (or sometimes 5) options. 
**Your job is NOT simply to find the one that looks different.** 
Your primary job is to find the **strict logical rule that binds the OTHER THREE options together into a perfect family**, leaving the remaining one completely out of the group.

**Why do examiners test this?**
Examiners test your analytical grouping skills. As a government officer, you must quickly categorize vast amounts of information and spot anomalies. This topic tests your vocabulary, mathematical agility, and observational speed under pressure.

**How Examiners Create Traps (The "Odd One" Fallacy):**
The biggest trap in Classification exploits human psychology. Students naturally look for the weirdest option.
*Example:* A) Apple, B) Banana, C) Carrot, D) Orange.
An amateur says, "Carrot is a vegetable, the rest are fruits, so Carrot is odd." This is correct.
*Trap Example:* A) 16, B) 25, C) 36, D) 73.
An amateur says, "73 is the only prime number, so it's the odd one out." 
*Wait!* Does a strict rule bind A, B, and C? Yes, they are all perfect squares. 
The trap is when a student finds a random reason for an option to be odd, *without ensuring the other three form a strict group based on the Priority Rule*. ALWAYS form the group first!

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations
Like Analogy, Classification is built on three major pillars.

### 1. Word Classification (General Knowledge / Vocabulary)
*   **Categories:** Finding the one item that doesn't belong to the natural category (e.g., three metals and one alloy, three capitals and one city, three rivers and one lake).
*   **Synonyms/Antonyms:** Three options are synonyms of each other, one is an antonym or unrelated.
*   **Function/Usage:** Three tools used by a carpenter, one used by a blacksmith.
*   **Biology/Geography:** Three Kharif crops vs one Rabi crop; three mammals vs one reptile.

### 2. Number Classification (Math & Logic - Very High Frequency)
*   **Prime / Composite:** Three primes, one composite (or vice versa).
*   **Squares / Cubes:** Three perfect squares/cubes (or square/cube $\pm$ constant), one random number.
*   **Divisibility / Multiples:** Three numbers are exact multiples of 7, 13, 17, etc., one is not.
*   **Digital Sum/Product:** The sum of the digits of three numbers is identical (e.g., all sum to 12), the fourth does not.
*   **Even / Odd:** (Rarely the main logic in modern exams, usually an amateur trap).

### 3. Letter Classification (Alphabet Logic)
*   **Positional Gaps:** A sequence of letters with consistent gaps (e.g., +2, +3, +4). Three options follow the exact gap rule, one breaks it.
*   **Vowels vs. Consonants:** Three options contain exactly 2 vowels, one contains only 1. Or three options start with vowels.
*   **Meaningful Words:** Jumbled letters that form three animals and one piece of furniture when unscrambled.
*   **Opposite Pairs:** Three options contain opposite letter pairs (A-Z, B-Y), one does not.

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **The Golden Rule:** Do not search for the odd one. Search for the **GROUP**. 
2. Ask yourself: *"Can I write a single, strict mathematical or logical rule that perfectly applies to options A, B, and C?"* If yes, D is the answer.
3. **The Number Priority Rule (BODMAS of Classification):**
    If multiple logics seem to work, you MUST follow this priority:
    1. Prime Numbers logic
    2. Squares & Cubes (and their derivatives like $N^2+1$)
    3. Mathematical Operations (Multiplication/Divisibility by a specific prime like 13 or 17)
    4. Digital Sum / Digital Logic
    5. Odd / Even (Absolute last resort)

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional (Check All)
*   Check option A. Check option B. Compare them. Form a hypothesis rule. Test hypothesis on C. If it works, D is the odd one.
*   *When to use:* Word and Letter classification.

### Method 2: Fast Method (Pairing)
*   Look at the options and immediately try to pair two options that share an obvious strong trait (e.g., you spot two perfect cubes). If you find two, the rule *must* involve that trait. Check the third. If it fits, you're done.
*   *When to use:* Number classification when you spot recognizable numbers.

### Method 3: Expert Method (Visual Elimination)
*   For letters, experts read the positional values as a continuous number string mentally and spot the irregular rhythm instantly. (e.g., reading CEG as "3-5-7", spotting the +2 rhythm without writing anything down).

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Are numbers 3 or 4 digits long (e.g., 4268)?** -> Do NOT waste time checking squares/cubes. Immediately check Digital Sum or Divisibility by 11.
*   **Are numbers near known squares/cubes (e.g., 26, 65, 126)?** -> It's a Square/Cube $\pm 1$ logic.
*   **Letters look like random jumbles without a clear alphabetic gap?** -> Count the number of Vowels immediately.
*   **Words are all geographical/biological?** -> Look for the specific scientific subclass (e.g., Marsupials vs Mammals).

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **Divisibility Rules:** You MUST know divisibility rules for 3, 4, 7, 9, 11, and 13. TCS (exam conductor) loves using multiples of 13 and 17 as the grouping logic.
2. **Positional Values (EJOTY):** A=1, E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25.
3. **Opposite Letters:** The sum of opposite letters is ALWAYS 27. (If an option is D-W, $4 + 23 = 27$).

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **The "Odd One" Fallacy:** Finding a unique property for Option D, but failing to realize Options A, B, and C actually share nothing in common. **(A must = B = C).**
*   **Falling for Odd/Even too early:** Choosing an even number because the other three are odd. *Trap!* The three odd numbers might be primes, making it a "Prime Logic" question, not odd/even. Priority dictates Primes > Odd/Even.
*   **Ignoring Vowels:** Spending 2 minutes calculating positional gaps (+4, -2, +7) for letter clusters when the simple logic was "Three options have 1 vowel, one option has 2." Always check vowels first if gaps look completely random.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression
**Level 1 (Very Easy)**
*   A) Car  B) Bicycle  C) Motorcycle  D) Ship 
    *   *(A, B, C travel on land. Ship travels on water).*

**Level 3 (Moderate)**
*   A) 121  B) 169  C) 225  D) 289
    *   *(A, B, and D are squares of PRIME numbers 11, 13, 17. Option C is the square of 15, which is composite. C is odd).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO / SSC CGL Tier-1)**
*   A) BDGJ  B) QSVY  C) JLOS  D) HKNQ
    *   *(Gaps: B(+2)D(+3)G(+3)J. Q(+2)S(+3)V(+3)Y. J(+2)L(+3)O(+4)S. H(+2)K(+3)N(+3)Q. Option C breaks the +2, +3, +3 rhythm).*

**Level 8 (Mixed High-Level - TCS Pattern)**
*   A) 13 - 171  B) 11 - 123  C) 5 - 27  D) 9 - 82
    *   *(Logic is $N^2 + 2$. $13^2+2 = 171$. $11^2+2 = 123$. $5^2+2 = 27$. $9^2+2 = 83$. Option D is 82 instead of 83, so it is the odd one).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation)**
*   A) 426  B) 369  C) 279  D) 159
    *   *(Logic: First digit + Second digit = Third digit. 4+2=6. 3+6=9. 2+7=9. But 1+5=6, not 9. So D is the odd one. If you tried odd/even, A is even, B,C,D are odd, so A would be the odd one. BUT Digital Logic is a stronger group bind than odd/even!)*

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
When practicing Classification independently:
1. Scan A, B, and C. Find what binds them. Do not immediately attack option D.
2. If you are stuck on Number Classification for more than 40 seconds, leave it. You might be missing a weird multiple (like $\times 13 + 5$) which will drain 3 minutes of your time. Skip and return later.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Word Classification:** 
    *   Ideal Time: 10 seconds.
    *   Expert Time: 5 seconds. (Pure GK - you either know the category or you don't).
*   **Letter Classification:** 
    *   Ideal Time: 20-25 seconds (Writing positional values takes time).
    *   Expert Time: 10-15 seconds (Mental gap calculation).
*   **Number Classification:**
    *   Ideal Time: 30-40 seconds.
    *   Expert Time: 20 seconds.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Train your brain to identify these instantly:
1. `(13, 17, 23, 27)` -> 27 is odd (Composite), others are Prime.
2. `(120, 24, 6, 60)` -> All are $N^3 - N$. ($5^3-5, 3^3-3, 2^3-2, 4^3-4$).
3. `(AE, CG, EI, GK)` -> Gaps are +4 for all. If one is +5, it's odd.
4. `(Wheat, Rice, Maize, Millet)` -> Wheat is a Rabi crop, others are Kharif. *Always brush up on crop types and biological classifications!*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Classification = GROUPING.** Do not look for the odd one; look for the rule that binds the other three.
*   **Priority Rules for Numbers:** Prime > Cubes > Squares > Multiplication/Divisibility > Digital Sum > Addition/Subtraction > Odd/Even.
*   **Large Numbers:** Check Digital Sum first.
*   **Random Letters:** Check Vowels first before calculating complex positional gaps.
*   **Mental Check:** "Are A, B, and C exactly the same logically?" If yes, D is the answer.
