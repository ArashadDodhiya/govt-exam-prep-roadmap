# 18. Missing Number

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Missing Number?**
In this topic, you are presented with a matrix (grid of numbers) or a geometric figure (circle, star, triangle) filled with numbers. One number is missing and replaced with a `?`. Your job is to figure out the hidden mathematical pattern connecting the numbers and calculate the missing value.

**Why do examiners test this?**
This is a pure test of pattern recognition, numerical fluency, and lateral thinking. It tests how fast your brain can jump between addition, multiplication, squares, cubes, and digit-sums. 

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Time Sink Trap:** This is the most dangerous topic in reasoning. Sometimes a logic is so obscure (e.g., $(Row 1 \times 3) - (Row 2^2) = Row 3$) that you can waste 4 minutes on it. The examiner *wants* you to refuse to give up.
2. **The "Almost Works" Trap:** You find a logic (like $A + B = C$) that works perfectly for the first row, but fails on the second row. You try to force it anyway.
3. **The Column vs Row Confusion:** In a matrix, you don't know if the logic flows horizontally (rows) or vertically (columns). 

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Matrix Based (Grid)
*   Usually a $3 \times 3$ or $4 \times 3$ grid. The logic can run horizontally or vertically.

### 2. Circular / Wheel Based
*   A circle divided into sectors. The logic usually flows clockwise, anti-clockwise, or across opposite sectors.

### 3. Figure Based (Triangles, Stars, Stick figures)
*   Numbers around the outside of a triangle mathematically combine to produce the number in the center.

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **The 30-Second Bailout Rule:** This is the most important rule. Look at the puzzle for 30 seconds. Run through your standard mental checklist. If the logic doesn't click in 30 seconds, SKIP IT and come back at the end of the exam. 
2. **Hunt for the Big Number:** In a matrix, look for where the largest numbers are. If the largest numbers are at the bottom of the columns, the logic flows *vertically* (Top + Middle = Bottom). If they are on the far right, it flows *horizontally*.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: The Standard Mathematical Checklist
*   Mentally check these in order:
    1. Addition/Subtraction
    2. Multiplication/Division
    3. Squares and Cubes
    4. Sum of Digits (e.g., 23 $\rightarrow 2+3=5$)
    5. Prime Numbers

### Method 2: The "Opposite" Method (For Circles)
*   If the numbers don't follow a logical sequence clockwise (e.g., 4, 7, 15, 29...), check the numbers directly opposite each other across the circle. (e.g., Opposite of 4 is 16 $\rightarrow 4^2$).

### Method 3: The Outside-In Method (For Figures)
*   If you have a triangle with numbers on the 3 corners and 1 in the middle, the 3 corner numbers MUST interact (usually multiply or add, then divide by a constant) to equal the middle number.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Are the numbers increasing very rapidly?** -> It is a Multiplication or Square/Cube logic. Addition won't get you from 5 to 125 quickly.
*   **Are all the numbers very large but close to each other?** -> Try subtracting them or adding the individual digits.
*   **Is one column/row significantly larger than the others?** -> That column/row is the *Result*. Combine the other rows/columns to achieve that result.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **Memorize Squares to 30, Cubes to 15:** You cannot solve Missing Number puzzles quickly if you don't instantly recognize that 343 is $7^3$ or 289 is $17^2$.
2. **The "Minus 1 / Plus 1" Trick:** Often, the logic is $x^2 - 1$ or $x^3 + 1$. Recognize numbers like 24 (which is $5^2 - 1$), 80 ($9^2 - 1$), or 126 ($5^3 + 1$).
3. **The Constant Divider:** If Corner A $\times$ Corner B is 100, and the center is 10. The logic might be $(A \times B) / 10$. Always look for a constant number being added or divided at the end.

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Ego Solving:** Thinking "I am good at math, I *will* solve this" and wasting 5 minutes. This destroys your overall score.
*   **Ignoring the Second Row:** Finding a logic in Row 1 and immediately applying it to Row 3 (the missing one) without verifying if it holds true for Row 2.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC MTS / GD)**
*   Matrix: 
    Row 1: 5, 4, 20
    Row 2: 3, 8, 24
    Row 3: 9, 4, ?
    *   *(Logic: Col 1 $\times$ Col 2 = Col 3. Answer: $9 \times 4 = 36$).*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   Matrix:
    Row 1: 8, 17, 33
    Row 2: 12, 5, 29
    Row 3: 10, 13, ?
    *   *(Logic: $(Col 1 \times 2) + Col 2 = Col 3$. For Row 1: $(8 \times 2) + 17 = 33$. For Row 3: $(10 \times 2) + 13 = 33$).*

**Level 5 (State PSC / IBPS PO)**
*   Circle with sectors: 5, 11, 23, 47, 95, ?
    *   *(Logic: $\times 2 + 1$. $5 \times 2+1 = 11$, $11 \times 2+1 = 23$... $95 \times 2+1 = 191$).*

**Level 8 (Exam Simulation - CGL Tier 2)**
*   Matrix:
    Row 1: 42, 37, 17
    Row 2: 18, 24, 15
    Row 3: 56, 29, ?
    *   *(Logic: Sum of digits of Col 1 + Sum of digits of Col 2 = Col 3. Row 1: $(4+2) + (3+7) = 6 + 10 = 16$. Wait, it says 17. Ok, maybe $(Col 1 - Col 2) \times something$? The logic will be highly non-linear. Answer might be $(42-37)^2 - 8$... Requires extreme lateral thinking).*

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **The 30-Second Timer:** Always practice these with a stopwatch. If you don't get the logic in 30 seconds, check the solution. You need to expose your brain to *as many different patterns as possible*, not prove you can solve one pattern in 10 minutes.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Basic Matrix/Circle:** Ideal: 20s | Expert: 10s.
*   **Complex Matrix (Squares/Cubes):** Ideal: 45s | Expert: 30s.
*   **Bailout Time:** MAXIMUM 60 seconds. Then move on.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `Matrix has large numbers on the right side.` -> Where should you look for the logic? *(Answer: Horizontally. The left numbers combine to form the right number).*
2. `Numbers in a circle: 2, 9, 28, 65, ?` -> What is the next number? *(Answer: $1^3+1, 2^3+1, 3^3+1, 4^3+1$. Next is $5^3+1 = 126$).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Checklist:** 1. Add/Sub, 2. Mult/Div, 3. Squares/Cubes $\pm 1$, 4. Digit Sum.
*   **Big Number Rule:** The location of the largest number points to the direction of the logic (Rows vs Columns).
*   **The Golden Rule:** 30 seconds or SKIP. Save your exam.
