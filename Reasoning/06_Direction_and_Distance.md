# 06. Direction and Distance

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Direction and Distance?**
This topic tests your spatial orientation, mental mapping, and basic geometry. You are given a sequence of movements (turns, angles, and distances) taken by a person or object, and you must determine their final facing direction, total distance covered, or shortest straight-line distance from the starting point.

**Why do examiners test this?**
It evaluates your ability to process left/right orientations from multiple perspectives (e.g., what is "left" when facing South?), your spatial memory, and your speed in applying the Pythagorean theorem under pressure.

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Proportion Trap:** You draw a 10m line heading North, and then later draw a 5m line heading South that is *physically longer* than the 10m line on your paper. This completely destroys your visual map, causing you to answer "North-East" instead of "South-East".
2. **The "Relative Direction" Trap:** The question asks, *"In which direction is the starting point with respect to the end point?"* Students calculate the map perfectly but instinctively answer where the end point is from the start. You must reverse the perspective.
3. **The "South-Facing Right" Trap:** When a person is facing North, their Right is your Right. When facing South, their Right is your *Left*. Under extreme time pressure, students default to their own Left/Right and ruin the entire map.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Basic Movement (SSC, Railways, State PSC)
*   Standard left/right turns with specific distances. Finding the shortest distance using Pythagoras.

### 2. Degree / Angle Based Turns (SSC, Banking)
*   "He is facing North-West. He turns 45° Clockwise, then 135° Anti-Clockwise." No distances are given, just facing directions.

### 3. Shadow Based Questions (State PSC, SSC, CSAT)
*   "One morning after sunrise, A and B were talking face to face. A's shadow fell exactly to the right of B. Which direction is A facing?"

### 4. Coded Direction (Banking Mains, RBI Grade B)
*   "A @ B means A is 5m North of B. A # B means A is 12m East of B." This combines syllogism-like coding with complex spatial mapping.

### 5. Point-Based Puzzles (Banking Prelims/Mains)
*   A scattered map of interconnected points: "Point A is 5m West of Point B. Point C is 10m North of Point B..."

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **The Standard Compass:** Always fix your paper mentally: North is UP, South is DOWN, East is RIGHT, West is LEFT. 
2. **Start with a bold Dot:** Always mark your starting point heavily so you never lose it in a complex web of lines.
3. **Draw Proportionally:** A 10km line MUST be visibly twice as long as a 5km line on your rough sheet.
4. **The End-Point Crosshair:** If the question asks for the direction with respect to the starting point, draw a faint `+` sign at the starting point. This creates 4 quadrants, making it instantly obvious which quadrant the end point lies in.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional (Map Drawing)
*   Read sentence by sentence, draw the lines proportionally, apply the Pythagoras theorem at the end.
*   *When to use:* Point-based puzzles and standard distance questions.

### Method 2: Fast Method (The NESW Cancellation Trick)
*   If the question ONLY asks for the final facing direction (and distance is irrelevant), **DO NOT DRAW**. 
*   *Trick:* Write down the starting direction. Every "Right" turn is +90°. Every "Left" turn is -90°. Cancel them out.
*   *Example:* Facing North. Turns Right, Left, Right, Right. 
    *   One Right cancels one Left. Two Rights remain.
    *   North + 2 Rights (180°) = South. Solved in 3 seconds.

### Method 3: Expert Method (Mental Pythagorean Triplets)
*   Experts do not manually calculate $x = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2}$. They have memorized Pythagorean triplets (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17). The moment they see a base of 8 and a vertical of 15 on their map, they instantly write 17.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Does the question ask ONLY for the final facing direction?** -> Drop your pen. Use the Cancellation trick (Right cancels Left).
*   **Does the question involve degrees (45°, 135°)?** -> Add all Clockwise (CW) degrees together. Add all Anti-Clockwise (ACW) degrees together. Subtract the smaller from the larger. Turn the remainder from the starting direction.
*   **Is it a Morning/Evening shadow question?** -> The sun is the anchor. Morning = Sun in East = ALL shadows fall West. Evening = Sun in West = ALL shadows fall East.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **Right/Left Rotation Shortcuts:**
    *   Right Turn = 90° Clockwise (CW)
    *   Left Turn = 90° Anti-Clockwise (ACW)
    *   3 Left turns = 1 Right turn. (Saves time mentally rotating).
    *   2 Right or 2 Left turns = Exact Opposite direction (180°).
2. **The Shadow Master Rule:**
    *   *Morning:* Shadow is always WEST. If a shadow is to a person's Right, they must be facing South (because South's right is West). If the shadow is to their Left, they are facing North.
    *   *Evening:* Shadow is always EAST. If a shadow is to their Right, they are facing North. If the shadow is to their Left, they are facing South.
    *   *12:00 Noon:* NO SHADOW is formed (sun is exactly overhead).
3. **Pythagorean Triplets (Memorize these base pairs):**
    *   (3, 4) $\rightarrow$ 5
    *   (5, 12) $\rightarrow$ 13
    *   (8, 15) $\rightarrow$ 17
    *   (7, 24) $\rightarrow$ 25
    *   (9, 40) $\rightarrow$ 41
    *   *Note:* Multiples also work. If (3,4)$\rightarrow$5, then (6,8)$\rightarrow$10, and (30,40)$\rightarrow$50.

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Disproportionate Drawing:** Drawing a 5m line longer than a 15m line, which causes a point that should be physically East to look like it's West on your paper.
*   **Misreading the Final Question Direction:** 
    *   "In which direction is he facing?" $\rightarrow$ Look at the direction the arrow is currently pointing.
    *   "In which direction is he from the starting point?" $\rightarrow$ Draw a straight line from Start to Finish.
    *   "In which direction is the starting point from him?" $\rightarrow$ Draw a straight line from Finish to Start. (The exact opposite!)
*   **Left/Right confusion when facing South:** Train your brain: South is inverted. Right is West, Left is East.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC GD)**
*   A man walks 5km North, turns Right and walks 3km, turns Right and walks 5km. Which direction is he facing?
    *   *(Start North. Right, Right = 2 Rights = 180° = South).*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   A man walks 10m East, turns left and walks 10m. He turns right and walks 5m. Finally, he turns right and walks 18m. How far and in which direction is he from the starting point?
    *   *(Map: Base is $10 + 5 = 15$ East. Vertical is $10$ North, then $18$ South, so net vertical is $8$ South. The start and end points form a right triangle with base 15 and height 8. Triplet (8,15) $\rightarrow$ distance is 17m. Direction is South-East).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO Prelims - Point Puzzle)**
*   Point A is 8m North of Point B. Point C is 12m East of B. Point D is 5m South of C. Point E is 12m West of D. How far is E from A?
    *   *(Drawing carefully reveals E is exactly below A on the vertical axis because East 12m and West 12m cancel out. Distance A to B is 8m. Distance B to E is the same as C to D which is 5m. So A to E = $8 + 5 = 13m$.)*

**Level 8 (SBI PO Prelims / CGL Mains - Shadow/Degrees)**
*   One morning, Ram and Shyam were talking facing each other. Shyam's shadow fell exactly to the right of Ram. Which direction was Shyam facing?
    *   *(Morning = Shadow is West. "Shadow fell to the right of Ram" means Ram's Right is West. If Ram's Right is West, Ram is facing North. Since they face each other, Shyam is facing South).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation - SBI PO Mains / RBI Grade B)**
*   Coded Direction with Variable Distances: 
    *   $P \% Q (12)$ means P is 14m North of Q. 
    *   $P \& Q (15)$ means P is 17m East of Q. 
    *   *(Notice the trap: the actual map distance is the bracket number + 2). You must decode a complex string of 8 points and find the area of the polygon formed. Pure speed and accuracy test.*

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **Never Rotate Your Paper:** Some students physically turn their rough sheet upside down when the person walks South to figure out left/right. **Do not do this.** It wastes time and causes confusion when you turn it back. Train your brain to invert Left/Right mentally.
2. **Use Arrows:** Always put a tiny arrowhead at the end of every line segment so you know which way the person is currently looking. A line without an arrowhead is a dead map.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Direction Only (No distance):** Ideal: 10s | Expert: 5s (Cancellation trick).
*   **Standard Distance & Pythagoras:** Ideal: 40s | Expert: 20s (Using memorized Triplets).
*   **Shadow Questions:** Ideal: 15s | Expert: 5s (Pure logic, zero drawing).
*   **Point Puzzles (3-5 questions):** Ideal: 2 mins | Expert: 1 min.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `Facing North-West, turning 225° Clockwise.` -> What is the final direction? *(Answer: NW (315°) + 225° = 540°. $540 - 360 = 180°$, which is South).*
2. `Base is 7, height is 24.` -> What is the hypotenuse? *(Answer: 25. Instantly).*
3. `Facing South, you take 3 Left turns.` -> Which way are you facing? *(Answer: 3 Lefts = 1 Right. South + Right = West).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **NESW Cancellation:** R cancels L. 2R or 2L = Opposite direction. 3R = 1L. 3L = 1R. 4R = Same direction.
*   **Morning Shadow (Sun in East):** Falls West.
*   **Evening Shadow (Sun in West):** Falls East.
*   **Right of North:** East. **Left of North:** West.
*   **Right of South:** West. **Left of South:** East.
*   **Triplets:** 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, 9-40-41.
