# 10. Statement and Conclusion

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Statement and Conclusion?**
In this topic, you are given a statement (a fact, a rule, or an observation) and a few conclusions. Your job is to decide which conclusion logically and undeniably follows *only* from the given statement. 

**Why do examiners test this?**
It tests your analytical reasoning and your ability to separate **Facts from Opinions**. Examiners want to see if you can make objective deductions without letting your personal biases or outside knowledge interfere. It is heavily tested in UPSC CSAT, State PSCs, SSC, and Banking exams.

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The "Outside Knowledge" Trap:** The statement says: "Apples are good for health." Conclusion: "Oranges are bad for health." *Trap!* You might know oranges are good, but based strictly on the statement, you know *nothing* about oranges.
2. **The Extreme Word Trap:** Statement: "Many politicians are corrupt." Conclusion: "ALL politicians are corrupt." *Trap!* Words like *All, Every, Never, Always, None* usually make a conclusion false unless explicitly supported by the statement.
3. **The Assumption disguised as Conclusion:** A conclusion must be a direct deduction. If you have to say "Well, maybe if X happens, then Y..." — that is an Assumption, not a Conclusion.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Logical/Analytical Conclusion (SSC, Railways, CSAT)
*   Standard paragraph or sentence followed by logical deductions.

### 2. Mathematical Inequalities (Bank PO, RBI Grade B)
*   Often grouped under this heading in banking. Statements: $A > B \ge C = D$. Conclusions: I. $A > D$, II. $B > D$.

### 3. Syllogistic Conclusion
*   (Covered previously in Topic 09, but conceptually related).

### 4. Cause and Effect styled Conclusions
*   Statement describes an event, and the conclusion tries to deduce the absolute outcome.

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **The "Island" Rule:** Treat the statement as an isolated island of truth. Nothing exists outside this island. If a conclusion mentions something off the island, it is instantly FALSE.
2. **The "100% Certainty" Rule:** A conclusion must be 100% true. If it is only 99% true or "probably true," it is FALSE as a conclusion (it might be a valid assumption, but not a conclusion).

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: The Elimination Method (Logical)
*   Read the statement. Read the conclusion. 
*   Look for Extreme Words (All, Never, Only). If present, and not in the statement -> Eliminate.
*   Look for New Subjects. If present -> Eliminate.
*   *When to use:* SSC, CSAT, State PSC.

### Method 2: The Open/Closed Door Method (Mathematical Inequalities)
*   For statements like $A > B \ge C \le D$.
*   Treat `>` as an open door from A to B. You can walk from A to B, but not B to A (closed door).
*   Treat `=` as a door open on both sides.
*   *When to use:* Banking Prelims and Mains.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Is it an English statement?** -> Grab your mental highlighter. Highlight extreme words in the conclusions immediately. 
*   **Does the conclusion say "Only"?** -> *Red Alert!* "Only" is the most dangerous word. "Only X can do Y" is almost always a false conclusion unless the statement specifically restricts it.
*   **Is it a Math Inequality ($>$ / $\le$)?** -> Don't read the statement first. Look at the conclusion ($A > D$), and immediately trace the path from A to D in the statement.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **The Extreme Word Hitlist (Reject these in Logical Conclusions):**
    *   *All, Always, Every, Never, None, Only, Definitely, Exactly.*
    *   (Exception: If the original statement uses an extreme word, the conclusion can too).
2. **The Safe Word List (Likely to be True):**
    *   *Some, Many, Sometimes, May, Can, A few, Often.*
3. **Inequality Priority Rule (Banking Shortcut):**
    *   King: `>` or `<`
    *   Minister: $\ge$ or $\le$
    *   Servant: `=`
    *   *Rule:* If you travel from A to C, and pass a King and a Minister ($A > B \ge C$), the King's sign wins. Therefore, $A > C$ is the only true conclusion. If you pass opposing signs ($A > B < C$), relation is blocked (False).

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Applying General Knowledge:** Statement: "The government has increased the price of petrol." Conclusion: "People will stop using cars." *Mistake!* You are assuming human behavior. The conclusion does not 100% follow.
*   **Confusing Assumption with Conclusion:** 
    *   *Statement:* "A new medicine cures cancer in 3 days."
    *   *Assumption:* People will buy this medicine. (Valid).
    *   *Conclusion:* Cancer is completely eradicated from the world. (Invalid/Extreme).

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC GD)**
*   Statement: All birds in the zoo are green.
*   Conclusion: I. There are no black birds in the zoo.
    *   *(True. Direct deduction from "All").*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   Statement: Reading books improves vocabulary.
*   Conclusions: I. Those who do not read have poor vocabulary. II. Vocabulary can only be improved by reading.
    *   *(I is False: Non-readers might improve via speaking. II is False: The word "only" makes it extreme).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO Prelims - Inequalities)**
*   Statement: $P \ge Q = R > S \le T$
*   Conclusions: I. $P > S$. II. $Q \le T$.
    *   *(I is True: Path P to S has $\ge, =, >$. The `>` is King. So $P > S$. II is False: Path Q to T hits opposing signs `>` and `\le`. Blocked).*

**Level 8 (UPSC CSAT / State PSC)**
*   Statement: A nation's economic growth relies heavily on its manufacturing sector, though agriculture provides the baseline sustenance.
*   Conclusions: I. Without manufacturing, a nation will definitely collapse. II. Agriculture does not contribute to economic growth.
    *   *(I is False: "Definitely collapse" is an extreme assumption. II is False: Directly contradicts the spirit of the statement).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation - SBI PO Mains / RBI Grade B)**
*   Coded Inequalities: $A @ B$ means $A$ is not smaller than $B$. $A \# B$ means $A$ is neither greater nor equal to $B$. You must decode 5 statements, combine them, and evaluate 4 complex conclusions under 60 seconds.

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **Be a Robot:** When practicing Logical Conclusions, turn off your human empathy, general knowledge, and common sense. Be a strict parsing engine. Does X definitively lead to Y?
2. **Pathfinding:** For inequalities, practice visual pathfinding without rewriting the equation.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Logical Conclusion:** Ideal: 20s | Expert: 10s (Scan for extreme words).
*   **Direct Inequalities:** Ideal: 15s | Expert: 5s (Visual trace).
*   **Coded Inequalities:** Ideal: 45s | Expert: 30s.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `Statement: Sachin is a good batsman. Conclusion: Sachin is the best batsman in the world.` -> *(Answer: False. "Best" is an extreme comparative not supported by the statement).*
2. `Path: X > Y >= Z.` -> What is the relation between X and Z? *(Answer: X > Z. The `>` overrides the `>=`).*
3. `Path: M >= N <= O.` -> What is the relation between M and O? *(Answer: No relation. Opposing signs block the path).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **The Island Rule:** Do not use outside knowledge.
*   **Extreme Words = DANGER:** All, Only, Never, Always, Definitely. (Usually false).
*   **Safe Words = GOOD:** Some, May, Can, Often. (Usually true).
*   **Inequality Priority:** `>` beats `>=` beats `=`.
*   **Inequality Block:** Opposing signs (`<` and `>`) facing each other mean NO RELATION.
