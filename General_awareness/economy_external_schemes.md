# Indian Economy — External Sector, Indicators & Government Schemes

## 1. Overview
This topic covers India's economic relationship with the world (Balance of Payments, FDI/FII, WTO), key socio-economic indicators (poverty, unemployment, inflation, HDI), major welfare schemes, and the public sector landscape (disinvestment, Navratna/Maharatna companies). This is one of the most current-affairs-adjacent GK topics — scheme names and PSU categorizations get updated periodically.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Balance of Payments (BoP) & External Sector**
- Balance of Payments — systematic record of ALL economic transactions between a country's residents and the rest of the world in a given period **[High]**
- Two main accounts: Current Account (trade in goods/services, income, transfers) and Capital Account (investments, loans, capital transfers) **[High]**
- Current Account Deficit (CAD) — when imports of goods/services/transfers exceed exports; India typically runs a CAD **[High]**
- Trade Deficit — specifically when merchandise (goods) imports exceed exports (narrower than CAD) **[Medium]**
- FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) — investment involving lasting management interest/control (e.g., setting up a company, buying significant stake) **[High]**
- FII/FPI (Foreign Institutional/Portfolio Investment) — investment in financial assets (stocks, bonds) WITHOUT management control; more volatile/short-term than FDI **[High]**
- Forex Reserves — foreign currency + gold + SDRs held by RBI; used to manage currency stability and cover import needs **[Medium]**
- EXIM Policy (Foreign Trade Policy) — governs India's import-export regulations, incentives, and trade facilitation, reviewed periodically by the Commerce Ministry **[Medium]**
- WTO (World Trade Organization) — established 1995 (successor to GATT), headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland; governs international trade rules **[High]**
- Convertibility — Indian Rupee is fully convertible on Current Account, but only PARTIALLY convertible on Capital Account **[Medium]**

**Poverty, Unemployment, Inflation**
- Poverty Line — income/consumption threshold below which a person is considered poor; in India, historically estimated via Tendulkar Committee and Rangarajan Committee methodologies **[Medium]**
- Types of unemployment: Disguised (more people employed than needed, common in agriculture), Seasonal (only certain seasons, e.g., agriculture), Structural (skill mismatch), Cyclical (due to economic downturns), Frictional (temporary, between jobs) **[High]**
- CPI (Consumer Price Index) — measures inflation from the CONSUMER's perspective (retail prices); used by RBI for inflation targeting **[High]**
- WPI (Wholesale Price Index) — measures inflation at the WHOLESALE/producer level (before reaching retail); does NOT include services **[High]**
- CPI is the OFFICIAL inflation measure used for RBI's monetary policy (since 2014); WPI was used earlier as the primary measure **[High]**
- HDI (Human Development Index) — composite index by UNDP, published annually in the Human Development Report; based on 3 dimensions: Health (life expectancy), Education (mean & expected years of schooling), Standard of Living (GNI per capita) **[High]**
- India's HDI category — consistently in the "Medium Human Development" category in recent years (exact rank/value changes yearly) **[Medium]**

**Major Government Welfare Schemes**
- MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act), 2005 — guarantees 100 days of wage employment per year to rural households **[High]**
- PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana), 2014 — financial inclusion scheme, zero-balance bank accounts for all **[High]**
- PMSBY (Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana) — accident insurance scheme, very low annual premium **[Medium]**
- PMJJBY (Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana) — life insurance scheme, low annual premium **[Medium]**
- APY (Atal Pension Yojana) — pension scheme targeting the unorganized sector **[Medium]**
- PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana), 2016 — crop insurance scheme for farmers against crop loss/damage **[High]**
- Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY), 2018 — health insurance scheme, covers up to ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary/tertiary hospitalization; termed world's largest government-funded health insurance scheme **[High]**
- Ujjwala Yojana, 2016 — provides free LPG connections to women from BPL households **[High]**
- Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, 2014 — nationwide cleanliness/sanitation campaign, aimed at eliminating open defecation **[High]**
- Digital India, 2015 — initiative to improve digital infrastructure and government service delivery online **[Medium]**
- PM Awas Yojana (PMAY) — "Housing for All" scheme, rural (PMAY-G) and urban (PMAY-U) components **[Medium]**

**Disinvestment & Public Sector**
- Disinvestment — sale of government's equity/stake in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) to private entities **[High]**
- Purpose: raise revenue, improve efficiency, reduce fiscal burden **[Medium]**
- Navratna, Maharatna, Miniratna — categories of CPSEs (Central Public Sector Enterprises) based on financial performance & operational autonomy given **[High]**
- Maharatna — highest category, greatest financial autonomy (e.g., ONGC, Indian Oil, NTPC, Coal India, SAIL, BHEL among typical members — exact list updated periodically) **[High]**
- Navratna — second tier, significant autonomy but less than Maharatna **[Medium]**
- Miniratna — smaller PSUs with limited autonomy, divided into Category I and Category II **[Low]**
- DIPAM (Department of Investment and Public Asset Management) — nodal department managing central government's disinvestment process **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| FDI vs FII/FPI | FDI = long-term, WITH management control/ownership stake; FII/FPI = short-term, financial assets only, NO control — FDI is more stable, FII is more volatile |
| CPI vs WPI | CPI = RETAIL/consumer level, used for RBI's official inflation targeting; WPI = WHOLESALE level, excludes services entirely |
| Current Account Deficit vs Trade Deficit | CAD = broader (goods + services + income + transfers); Trade Deficit = narrower (ONLY merchandise/goods) |
| Navratna vs Maharatna vs Miniratna | Maharatna = HIGHEST autonomy/financial strength; Navratna = second tier; Miniratna = smallest, most limited autonomy — ranked in descending order of autonomy |
| MGNREGA vs PMFBY vs Ayushman Bharat | MGNREGA = employment guarantee (rural); PMFBY = crop INSURANCE (farmers); Ayushman Bharat = health INSURANCE (families) — three different sectors of welfare |
| PMSBY vs PMJJBY | PMSBY = accident INSURANCE; PMJJBY = life INSURANCE — both low-premium schemes but cover different risks |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **FDI vs FII control level** — "FDI = Full Direct Involvement (control); FII = Fleeting Indirect Investment (no control, can exit quickly)" — the words "Direct" and "Indirect" in the mnemonic mirror the actual acronym letters.
- **CPI vs WPI** — "**C**PI = **C**onsumer/Common man's basket (retail); **W**PI = **W**holesale warehouse level (before retail)" — C for Consumer, W for Wholesale, matching the first letters directly.
- **PSU Ratna hierarchy** — "Maha > Nav > Mini" — like Hindi/Sanskrit size words: Maha (great) is bigger than Nav (nine, a set) which is bigger than Mini (small) — the words' literal meanings mirror their ranking.
- **Scheme-purpose quick pairing** — build a mental "welfare basket": Jan Dhan=Bank Account, Ujjwala=Gas Cylinder, Swachh Bharat=Toilet/Cleanliness, Ayushman Bharat=Hospital Card, PMFBY=Crop Insurance — one iconic object per scheme.
- **MGNREGA "100 days"** — remember it as "100 days = 1 guarantee" — a single clean number tied to a single scheme, easy to isolate from other schemes with different benefit structures.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **FDI vs FII** — the most tested distinction; questions often describe a scenario (buying company shares for control vs buying stocks for quick profit) and ask which type of investment it represents.
- **CPI vs WPI** — assuming WPI is used for RBI's inflation targeting; it is actually CPI (since 2014) that RBI officially targets.
- Confusing **which scheme covers which risk** — PMSBY (accident) and PMJJBY (life) get swapped frequently in "match the scheme" questions.
- **Navratna vs Maharatna** — assuming Navratna is the HIGHEST category; Maharatna actually ranks above Navratna in autonomy and status.
- Scheme launch YEAR swaps — e.g., mixing up 2014 schemes (Swachh Bharat, PMJDY) with 2016 schemes (PMFBY, Ujjwala, Startup India) since several major schemes cluster around these two years.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- BoP: Current Account (trade/income/transfers) + Capital Account (investments); India typically runs CAD.
- FDI (control, long-term) vs FII/FPI (no control, volatile); WTO est. 1995, Geneva.
- CPI=retail/RBI's official measure; WPI=wholesale, excludes services.
- HDI (UNDP): Health + Education + Standard of Living; India = "Medium Human Development."
- Key schemes: MGNREGA (2005, 100 days), PMJDY (2014, bank accounts), PMFBY (2016, crop insurance), Ayushman Bharat (2018, ₹5 lakh health cover), Ujjwala (2016, LPG), Swachh Bharat (2014, sanitation).
- PSU categories (autonomy, high to low): Maharatna → Navratna → Miniratna (Cat I & II); DIPAM manages disinvestment.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which type of foreign investment involves management control and is considered more stable/long-term?
A) FII B) FDI C) FPI D) ODI

**Q2.** Which inflation index is officially used by the RBI for its inflation-targeting monetary policy?
A) WPI B) CPI C) GDP Deflator D) PPI

**Q3.** The MGNREGA scheme guarantees how many days of wage employment per rural household per year?
A) 50 days B) 75 days C) 100 days D) 150 days

**Q4.** Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY) provides health insurance coverage of up to how much per family per year?
A) ₹1 lakh B) ₹2 lakh C) ₹5 lakh D) ₹10 lakh

**Q5.** Among Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) categories, which one has the HIGHEST level of financial and operational autonomy?
A) Miniratna Category I B) Navratna C) Maharatna D) Miniratna Category II

## Answer Key
1. **B) FDI** — Foreign Direct Investment involves a lasting management interest, unlike FII which is portfolio-based and more volatile.
2. **B) CPI** — Since 2014, the RBI has officially used CPI (Consumer Price Index) as its target measure for inflation, per the flexible inflation targeting framework.
3. **C) 100 days** — MGNREGA guarantees up to 100 days of wage employment per rural household annually.
4. **C) ₹5 lakh** — Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY) provides health cover of up to ₹5 lakh per family per year for eligible beneficiaries.
5. **C) Maharatna** — Maharatna is the highest CPSE category, offering the greatest financial and operational autonomy, above Navratna and Miniratna.
