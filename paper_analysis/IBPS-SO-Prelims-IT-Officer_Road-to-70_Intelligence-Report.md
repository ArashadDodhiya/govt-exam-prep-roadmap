## 1. EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE (read this first)

- **The paper:** 150 questions, **125 marks**, 120 minutes, **sectional timing (40 min each, no going back)**. Three sections: English, Reasoning, Quantitative Aptitude.
- **The single most important fact nobody tells you:** English carries **50 questions but only 25 marks (0.5 each)**. Reasoning and Quant carry **50 questions = 50 marks each (1 mark each)**. **A Reasoning/Quant question is worth DOUBLE an English question.** Your 70 marks must come mainly from Reasoning + Quant.
- **Is 70 even necessary?** No — recent general-category **overall cutoffs sit around ~40–48/125**. So **70 is not a "pass" target, it is a "guaranteed-safe, sleep-easy" target.** Hitting it means you clear Prelims comfortably, even if a section is unexpectedly hard.
- **The 70 formula:** Reasoning ~32 + Quant ~28 + English ~11 ≈ **71.5 net**, achieved by attempting ~40 Reasoning, ~35 Quant, ~26 English at **~85% accuracy**.
- **The real lever is accuracy, not speed.** At 85% accuracy the plan yields ~72. At 80% it collapses to ~66. Every reckless guess costs you twice (lost mark + penalty).
- **4-day mandate:** Do NOT learn new topics. Lock in high-yield topics you already know, drill speed+accuracy, take 3 full timed mocks, and rehearse the question-selection order in Section 6.

> ⚠️ **Reality check:** Prelims is **only a qualifying gate**. These marks are **NOT added to your final merit** — final selection = **Mains (Professional Knowledge) + Interview**. So clear Prelims safely, but your 2015-style IT knowledge paper is where selection is actually decided. (More in Section 14.)

## 2. THE EXAM, DECODED

### Verified pattern (IT Officer Scale I — 2024/2025 cycle)

| Section | Questions | Marks | Marks/Q | Penalty/wrong | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| English Language | 50 | **25** | **0.5** | **−0.125** | 40 min |
| Reasoning Ability | 50 | **50** | **1.0** | −0.25 | 40 min |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 50 | **50** | **1.0** | −0.25 | 40 min |
| **TOTAL** | **150** | **125** | — | — | **120 min** |

- **Negative marking:** ¼ of the marks assigned to that question. So a wrong English answer costs 0.125; a wrong Reasoning/Quant answer costs 0.25. Blank = 0.
- **Sectional timing:** You get exactly 40 minutes per section and **cannot return** to a section once time is up. Plan each section as its own 40-minute battle.
- **Bilingual:** All sections except English are available in English + Hindi.
- **Qualifying only:** You must clear **each sectional cutoff AND the overall cutoff**. Prelims marks do not carry forward.

### What this structure forces you to do

> **A → B → C:** English is half-priced → so equal effort in English earns half the marks → therefore **guard Reasoning & Quant time jealously and treat English as a controlled, fast, accuracy-first section.**

## 4. PATTERN ANALYSIS (cross-year, 2017 → 2025)

1. **Structure is rock-solid.** Every Prelims paper (2021, 2023, 2024, 2025) runs the identical 50-English / 50-Reasoning / 50-Quant frame. No surprises in layout.
2. **Puzzles + DI = ~50% of all marks.** Reasoning is puzzle-dominated (22–30 of 50) and Quant is DI-dominated (20–25 of 50). **Whoever controls puzzles and DI controls this exam.** This is also where time is won or lost.
3. **Vocabulary-in-context is rising in English.** Word swap / word usage / word replacement now appear heavily (8–12 Q) and are far faster and more reliable than RC. **This is the cheapest English marks available.**
4. **"Selection" question types are increasing** — sentence connectors, "all are incorrect," "sentence D fixed," statement-inference. These are designed to eat time. Recognize and skip the slow ones.
5. **Rotating quick-win blocks in Quant.** Quadratic equations (2024) and Approximation/Simplification (2025) alternate in and out. When present, they are ~5–6 nearly-free marks — always hunt for them first.
6. **Difficulty is Moderate overall**, front-loaded with traps: the *first* puzzle or DI set is often the hardest. Difficulty ≠ question order. Don't attempt in serial order.

> **Detected pattern:** The exam rewards **selection over completion**. Nobody finishes all 150. The winners are those who reliably harvest the ~90–100 easy/moderate questions and refuse to bleed time on the ~50 hard ones.

## 5. THE 70-MARK BATTLE PLAN

### Principle 1 — Marks efficiency (memorize this)
Net marks = (correct × mark-value) − (wrong × penalty).
- Reasoning/Quant: each correct = **+1.0**, each wrong = **−0.25**
- English: each correct = **+0.5**, each wrong = **−0.125**

**One English question = half a Reasoning/Quant question.** Spend your best time and energy on R & Q.

| Section | Attempt (of 50) | Accuracy | ≈ Correct | ≈ Wrong | **Net** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 40 | 85% | 34 | 6 | **≈ 32.5** |
| Quantitative | 35 | 85% | 30 | 5 | **≈ 28.4** |
| English | 26 | 85% | 22 | 4 | **≈ 10.6** |
| **TOTAL** | **101** | — | **86** | **15** | **≈ 71.5** |

This clears every sectional cutoff (approx English ~7–9, Reasoning ~9–12, Quant ~8–11) with a huge margin.

### Principle 3 — Accuracy is the whole game

| If your accuracy is… | …the same plan nets |
|---|---|
| 90% | ~76 (very safe) |

**Takeaway:** Do not chase attempts beyond the plan by guessing blindly. One extra reckless attempt at 50/50 odds is worth roughly **+0.375 expected marks in R/Q** — barely positive, and negative if your real odds are worse. **Attempt only when you can eliminate to a genuine 2-option choice or better.**

## 6. IN-EXAM EXECUTION — the 40-minute-per-section drill

Because timing is **sectional and locked**, rehearse this exact order in your mocks.

### English (40 min) — accuracy-first, get in and out
1. **Vocabulary cluster first** (word usage/swap/synonym/antonym) — fastest, most reliable. (~6–8 min)
2. **Fillers / cloze** — pattern-based, quick. (~6 min)
3. **Phrase replacement + error spotting** — do the obvious ones, skip the ambiguous "all are incorrect" traps. (~8 min)
4. **ONE Reading Comprehension** — pick the passage on the more familiar topic; do vocab/direct questions, skip inference if slow. (~12 min)
5. Leave para-jumble/new-pattern for last; attempt only if confident. **Target: ~26 attempts, ~11 net. Then stop and protect accuracy.**

### Reasoning (40 min) — harvest quick wins, then puzzles
1. **Sweep the easy blocks first:** inequality → syllogism → direction → blood relation → alphanumeric series → coding → letter/word misc. This is ~15–20 near-certain marks in ~12–14 min.
2. **Then puzzles:** scan all sets in 60 seconds, rank them easy→hard. Attempt the **2–3 easiest fully** (a solved 5-mark puzzle is gold). (~20 min)
3. **Skip the hardest puzzle and any set you can't crack the base of in 90 seconds.** **Target: ~40 attempts, ~32 net.**

### Quant (40 min) — quick wins, then the easy DI
1. **Hunt the rotating freebies first:** number series → quadratic equations → approximation/simplification (whichever are present). ~10–12 fast marks. (~10 min)
2. **Pick the 1–2 easiest DI sets** (usually table or line; caselet is often slowest). Do those fully. (~15 min)
3. **Cherry-pick arithmetic word problems** you recognize instantly (ratio, %, ages, P&L, average). (~13 min)
4. **Skip:** the hardest DI set, probability if fiddly, anything needing >90 sec of setup. **Target: ~35 attempts, ~28 net.**

> **Universal in-exam rule:** If a question isn't cracking in ~60–90 seconds, flag and move. Coming back is free; getting stuck is fatal under sectional timing.

## 7. ⚠️ TRAP FILE — how candidates lose the marks they should get

**Marking / strategy traps**
- **The guessing tax.** Blind guesses at 20–25% accuracy have negative expected value. *Looks* like "extra attempts," *is* net marks lost. Only guess after eliminating to 2 options.
- **The English time-sink.** Spending 15 min chasing a hard RC for 0.5-mark questions while Quant quick-wins expire in the next section. English is half-price — treat it that way.
- **Serial-order trap.** Attempting Q1→Q50 in order. The first DI set / first puzzle is frequently the hardest. Always scan and pick, never march.
- **Sectional-clock trap.** Over-investing in Section 1 doesn't borrow time for Section 3 — the clock resets and locks. Budget within each 40-min box.

**English content traps**
- "**All are incorrect**" and "**pick the only correct sentence**" formats invert your instinct — you must verify *every* option, not find one error. Slow; do late.
- Word-swap questions: the trap is swapping only 2 of the highlighted words when the correct answer swaps a different pair. Re-read the sentence after swapping.

**Reasoning content traps**
- **Syllogism "possibility" cases** and **"either–or"** conclusions — the classic trap where a conclusion looks false but is valid via complementary pairs.
- **Coded inequality** where '≥' vs '>' changes the answer for "either" cases.
- **Puzzles with a hidden second variable** (e.g., floor + subject, or seating + facing direction) — misreading direction flips left/right.

**Quant content traps**
- **DI "% more/less" vs "ratio" vs "average"** — misreading what's asked. Underline the ask.
- **Approximation:** rounding too early or reading "% of" wrong.
- **Mixture/alligation & partnership**: time-weighting (months invested) is the usual trap.
- **Number series:** the "wrong term" variant vs "missing term" variant — answer the one actually asked.

## 9. THE 4-DAY CRASH PLAN

**Guiding rule: no new topics. Convert what you know into speed + accuracy + a rehearsed selection reflex.**

**Day 1 — Diagnose & anchor**
- Morning: Take the **2025 (or 2024) paper as a full, sectional-timed mock** (40+40+40). Simulate real conditions.
- Afternoon: Deep error-analysis — tag every miss as *concept gap*, *silly error*, or *time-waste*. This tells you your real accuracy per section.
- Evening: Drill the **Reasoning quick-win blocks** (inequality, syllogism, direction, blood relation, coding, series) to near-100% accuracy.

**Day 2 — Quant + English machinery**
- Morning: **Quant sectional practice** — 5 DI sets + 2 number-series sets + quadratic/approximation. Time each set; log which DI types are fastest for you.
- Afternoon: **Arithmetic sprints** — 8–10 questions each of ratio, %, ages, P&L, time-work, TSD, mixture.
- Evening: **English efficiency** — vocabulary cluster + fillers/cloze drills; one RC for stamina. Practice the "get 11 net in 25 min" routine.

**Day 3 — Reasoning depth + full mock #2**
- Morning: **Puzzle marathon** — 6–8 puzzles of varied types; practice the 60-second "attempt or skip" decision.
- Afternoon: **Full timed mock #2** (use the 2023 paper). Apply the Section-6 order exactly.
- Evening: Error analysis; re-drill only the 2–3 weakest quick-win topics.

**Day 4 — Rehearse & taper**
- Morning (at your real exam time): **Full timed mock #3** (2021 paper). Pure execution rehearsal — order, timing, skip-discipline.
- Afternoon: Light review — formula sheet, DI shortcuts, syllogism/inequality rules. **No hard new problems.**
- Evening: Stop studying early. Sleep. Fatigue destroys accuracy, and accuracy is your whole plan.
