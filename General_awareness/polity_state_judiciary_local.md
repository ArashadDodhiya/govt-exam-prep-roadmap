# Indian Polity — State Government, Judiciary, Local Government

## 1. Overview
This topic covers state-level governance (Governor, Chief Minister, State Legislature, High Court), the full judicial hierarchy (Supreme Court down to subordinate courts, judicial review, PIL, writs), and local self-government (Panchayati Raj and Municipalities). Exams test this via structural parallels to the Union Government topic and via specific landmark cases/amendments.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Governor (State Executive Head)**
- Nominal/constitutional head of a state, appointed by the PRESIDENT (not elected), holds office "during pleasure of the President" **[High]**
- Can be a Governor for more than one state simultaneously (since 7th Amendment, 1956) **[Medium]**
- Term: normally 5 years, but serves at President's pleasure (can be removed anytime) **[Medium]**
- Minimum age: 35 years; need not be a resident of the state they govern **[Medium]**
- Key powers: appoints CM (leader of majority party), can reserve a state bill for President's consideration, Ordinance-making power (Art 213) when state legislature not in session **[High]**
- Acts on the "aid and advice" of the state Council of Ministers headed by CM, similar to President-PM relationship **[Medium]**

**Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers**
- Real executive head of the state, appointed by Governor (usually majority party/coalition leader in state Assembly) **[High]**
- Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) **[High]**
- CM need not be a member of the state legislature at appointment but must become one within 6 months **[Medium]**

**State Legislature**
- Can be Unicameral (only Vidhan Sabha) or Bicameral (Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad); most states are unicameral **[High]**
- States WITH Vidhan Parishad (bicameral, as of recent years): Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh (6 states) **[Medium]**
- Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly) — directly elected, max strength 500, min 60; term 5 years **[High]**
- Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council) — indirectly elected/nominated, permanent body (like Rajya Sabha), 1/3 members retire every 2 years **[Medium]**
- Money Bills in states can only originate in Vidhan Sabha, similar to Lok Sabha's role at the Centre **[Medium]**

**High Court**
- Highest judicial body at the STATE level; India has 25 High Courts (some cover multiple states/UTs jointly) **[High]**
- Judges appointed by the PRESIDENT (in consultation with CJI, Governor, and Chief Justice of that High Court, per Collegium system) **[High]**
- Calcutta High Court — oldest High Court in India (established 1862) **[High]**
- Allahabad High Court — largest High Court by number of judges **[Medium]**
- Retirement age of High Court judges: 62 years (vs 65 for Supreme Court judges) **[High]**
- Has power of Judicial Review and can issue writs (Article 226) — WIDER than Supreme Court's Article 32 power since it covers Fundamental Rights AND other legal matters **[High]**

**Judiciary — Full Hierarchy**
- Supreme Court (apex, national level) → High Courts (state level) → District Courts → Subordinate/Lower Courts **[High]**
- Supreme Court — established 1950; Chief Justice of India (CJI) + other judges (currently sanctioned strength 34, including CJI) **[High]**
- Retirement age of Supreme Court judges: 65 years **[High]**
- Judges appointed by President via the Collegium system (CJI + 4 senior-most judges recommend appointments) **[High]**
- Original Jurisdiction — disputes between Centre-State or State-State; Appellate Jurisdiction — appeals from High Courts; Advisory Jurisdiction (Art 143) — President can seek SC's opinion **[Medium]**
- Judicial Review — power of courts to examine constitutionality of laws/executive actions; rooted in Art 13, 32, 226 **[High]**
- Basic Structure Doctrine — established in Kesavananda Bharati case (1973); Parliament can amend the Constitution but not alter its "basic structure" **[High]**
- PIL (Public Interest Litigation) — allows any public-spirited citizen/organization to approach courts on behalf of those who can't (introduced/expanded in India via judges like P.N. Bhagwati & V.R. Krishna Iyer in late 1970s-80s) **[High]**
- District Courts — headed by District Judge, handle civil (District Judge) and criminal (Sessions Judge, same person usually) matters at district level **[Medium]**

**Local Government — Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment, 1992)**
- 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs), added Part IX and Schedule 11 **[High]**
- Three-tier system: Gram Panchayat (village level) → Panchayat Samiti (block/intermediate level) → Zila Parishad (district level) **[High]**
- Gram Sabha — body of all registered voters in a village, foundation of the Panchayati Raj system **[High]**
- Mandatory elections every 5 years via State Election Commission; reservation for SC/ST and WOMEN (minimum 1/3rd, many states have 50%) **[High]**
- Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) — first recommended the three-tier Panchayati Raj system **[Medium]**
- Rajasthan (Nagaur district) — first state to implement Panchayati Raj (1959) **[Medium]**

**Local Government — Municipalities (74th Amendment, 1992)**
- 74th Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) gave constitutional status to urban local bodies, added Part IXA and Schedule 12 **[High]**
- Three types of Urban Local Bodies: Nagar Panchayat (transitional area, rural→urban), Municipal Council (smaller urban area), Municipal Corporation (larger urban area/city) **[High]**
- Mayor — ceremonial/nominal head of Municipal Corporation; Municipal Commissioner is the executive/administrative head **[Medium]**
- Both 73rd & 74th Amendments came into force on the same date: 24 April 1993 **[Medium]**
- Ward Committees — mandatory for municipalities with population above 3 lakh **[Low]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Governor's appointment vs President's election | Governor = APPOINTED by President (no election); President = ELECTED by Electoral College — Governor is more like a Presidential representative at state level |
| Article 32 vs Article 226 (writ jurisdiction) | Article 32 = Supreme Court ONLY, for Fundamental Rights only; Article 226 = High Court, WIDER scope (Fundamental Rights + other legal rights) |
| Supreme Court vs High Court judge retirement age | Supreme Court judges retire at 65; High Court judges retire at 62 — a very frequently tested numeric trap |
| 73rd Amendment vs 74th Amendment | 73rd = Panchayati Raj (RURAL local government, Part IX); 74th = Municipalities (URBAN local government, Part IXA) — both passed in 1992, enforced same date (1993) |
| Vidhan Sabha vs Vidhan Parishad | Vidhan Sabha = directly elected, CAN be dissolved (like Lok Sabha); Vidhan Parishad = indirectly elected/nominated, PERMANENT body (like Rajya Sabha) — only 6 states have a Vidhan Parishad |
| Mayor vs Municipal Commissioner | Mayor = ceremonial/nominal head (elected representative); Municipal Commissioner = actual executive/administrative head (appointed official) — parallel to Governor (nominal) vs CM (real power) dynamic |
| Oldest vs Largest High Court | Calcutta HC = OLDEST (1862); Allahabad HC = LARGEST by number of judges |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Judicial hierarchy (top to bottom)** — "**S**uper **H**eroes **D**efend **S**ociety" → **S**upreme Court → **H**igh Court → **D**istrict Court → **S**ubordinate Court.
- **73 vs 74 Amendment split** — "7**3** = **3**-tier rural (Panchayat)" and "7**4** = urban (think '4' looks like a building/city skyline)" — linking the number itself to rural vs urban.
- **Panchayati Raj 3 tiers** — "**G**o **P**lay **Z**ones" → **G**ram Panchayat (village) → **P**anchayat Samiti (block) → **Z**ila Parishad (district), smallest to largest.
- **Judge retirement ages** — "**S**upreme = **65** (retirement age of a 'senior' person), **H**igh Court = **62** (3 years earlier, junior court)" — SC judges retire 3 years later than HC judges, a fixed gap worth memorizing directly.
- **Governor vs President parallel structure** — Governor is a "mini-President" at the state level: both appoint the real executive (CM/PM), both act on "aid and advice," both have ordinance powers (Art 213 vs Art 123) — map every Governor power to its Presidential equivalent.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Retirement age swap** between Supreme Court (65) and High Court (62) judges is one of the most repeated numeric traps in this topic.
- Confusing **Article 32 and Article 226** — questions often ask "which article gives WIDER writ jurisdiction" (answer: 226, because it covers more than just Fundamental Rights).
- **73rd vs 74th Amendment** — swapping which one covers rural (Panchayati Raj) vs urban (Municipalities) local bodies, or their Parts (IX vs IXA) and Schedules (11 vs 12).
- Assuming **all states have a Vidhan Parishad** — only 6 states currently do; this "exception list" is a favorite trap.
- Mixing up **Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat** — Gram Sabha = ALL village voters (a general body); Gram Panchayat = the ELECTED executive body of the village.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Governor: appointed by President, "President's representative" at state level, ordinance power (Art 213), appoints CM.
- CM & Council of Ministers: real executive, responsible to Vidhan Sabha.
- State Legislature: mostly unicameral; 6 states bicameral (with Vidhan Parishad).
- High Court: 25 HCs in India, Calcutta=oldest, Allahabad=largest, judges retire at 62, wider writ power (Art 226).
- Judiciary hierarchy: Supreme Court (retire 65, Collegium-appointed) → High Court → District Court → Subordinate Court; Judicial Review + Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973); PIL tool for public interest access to justice.
- Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment, 1992): 3-tier (Gram Panchayat→Panchayat Samiti→Zila Parishad), Balwant Rai Mehta Committee recommended it, Rajasthan first implemented (1959).
- Municipalities (74th Amendment, 1992): Nagar Panchayat→Municipal Council→Municipal Corporation; both amendments enforced 24 April 1993.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** The Governor of a state is appointed by:
A) Chief Minister B) President of India C) Prime Minister D) State Legislature

**Q2.** At what age do Supreme Court judges retire, compared to High Court judges?
A) SC-62, HC-65 B) SC-65, HC-62 C) SC-60, HC-58 D) SC-65, HC-65

**Q3.** The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) relates to:
A) Municipalities B) Panchayati Raj Institutions C) Judicial reforms D) Election Commission powers

**Q4.** Which is the oldest High Court in India?
A) Allahabad High Court B) Bombay High Court C) Calcutta High Court D) Madras High Court

**Q5.** The three-tier Panchayati Raj system, from village to district level, is:
A) Gram Sabha → Zila Parishad → Panchayat Samiti
B) Gram Panchayat → Panchayat Samiti → Zila Parishad
C) Panchayat Samiti → Gram Panchayat → Zila Parishad
D) Zila Parishad → Panchayat Samiti → Gram Panchayat

## Answer Key
1. **B) President of India** — The Governor is appointed by the President and holds office at the President's pleasure, unlike the elected Chief Minister.
2. **B) SC-65, HC-62** — Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years, while High Court judges retire at 62 years.
3. **B) Panchayati Raj Institutions** — The 73rd Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to rural local self-government; the 74th Amendment covers municipalities.
4. **C) Calcutta High Court** — Established in 1862, it is the oldest High Court in India.
5. **B) Gram Panchayat → Panchayat Samiti → Zila Parishad** — This is the correct ascending order from village to district level in the Panchayati Raj system.

⚠️ The exact list of states with a Vidhan Parishad (bicameral legislature) and current sanctioned strength of the Supreme Court can change over time — verify current status if relevant to a recent exam.
