## STEP 1 — FOUNDATION

**What it is:** Every sentence can be expressed two ways — **active voice**, where the subject performs the action ("The chef cooks the meal"), or **passive voice**, where the subject receives the action ("The meal is cooked by the chef"). You're tested on converting correctly between the two, or spotting a broken conversion.

**Why it matters:** A formula-driven, high-accuracy-achievable topic once the tense-conversion chart (Step 4) is memorized — moderate but consistent weightage.

**Where it appears:**
- SSC CGL/CHSL — direct "convert to passive/active" questions
- IBPS/SBI, RBI — sometimes standalone, sometimes folded into Sentence Improvement/Error Spotting
- Insurance, EPFO, GPSC, Railways — pattern similar to SSC

**How examiners test it:** By checking whether you correctly matched the auxiliary "be" verb to the original tense, used the correct past participle (especially for irregular verbs), and correctly repositioned subject/object — a single wrong auxiliary or verb form invalidates the whole conversion.

## STEP 3 — CORE CONCEPTS

- **Definition:** Passive voice restructures a sentence so the original **object** becomes the new **subject**, the verb becomes **"be" (matched to the original tense) + past participle (V3)**, and the original subject becomes an optional "by + agent" phrase.
- **Rule:** Only **transitive verbs** (verbs that take a direct object) can be converted to passive voice — a verb with no object (e.g., "he laughed," "she arrived") cannot be passivized at all.
- **Why used:** Passive voice is preferred when the agent (who did it) is unknown, unimportant, or when the focus should be on the action/object rather than the doer ("The bridge was built in 1990" — the builders aren't the point).
- **Common mistake:** Getting the auxiliary "be" verb's tense wrong, or using the simple past form (V2) instead of the past participle (V3) for irregular verbs.
- **Memory trick:** Identify the **tense of the active sentence first** — everything else in the conversion formula follows automatically from that one identification.

## STEP 4 — TENSE-BY-TENSE CONVERSION FORMULA CHART

| Tense | Active Formula | Passive Formula | Example (Active → Passive) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | S + V1(s/es) + O | O + is/am/are + V3 + (by S) | *"The chef cooks the meal" → "The meal is cooked by the chef"* |
| Simple Past | S + V2 + O | O + was/were + V3 + (by S) | *"She wrote a letter" → "A letter was written by her"* |
| Simple Future | S + will/shall + V1 + O | O + will/shall + be + V3 + (by S) | *"They will complete the project" → "The project will be completed by them"* |
| Present Continuous | S + is/am/are + V-ing + O | O + is/am/are + being + V3 + (by S) | *"She is painting the wall" → "The wall is being painted by her"* |
| Past Continuous | S + was/were + V-ing + O | O + was/were + being + V3 + (by S) | *"They were building the house" → "The house was being built by them"* |
| Present Perfect | S + has/have + V3 + O | O + has/have + been + V3 + (by S) | *"He has finished the work" → "The work has been finished by him"* |
| Past Perfect | S + had + V3 + O | O + had + been + V3 + (by S) | *"They had completed the survey" → "The survey had been completed by them"* |
| Future Perfect | S + will have + V3 + O | O + will have been + V3 + (by S) | *"She will have finished the report" → "The report will have been finished by her"* |
| Modals (can/could/may/might/must/should/would) | S + modal + V1 + O | O + modal + be + V3 + (by S) | *"You must complete the assignment" → "The assignment must be completed by you"* |

**Note:** Future continuous, present perfect continuous, and past perfect continuous are rarely converted to passive in standard usage — these are almost never tested; don't spend memorization effort on them.

## STEP 4B — SPECIAL SENTENCE TYPES

**Imperative sentences (commands/requests):**
- Command: *"Close the door."* → *"Let the door be closed."* (formula: **Let + O + be + V3**)
- Negative command: *"Don't touch the wire."* → *"Let the wire not be touched."*
- Request: *"Please help me."* → *"You are requested to help me."*

**Interrogative sentences (questions):**
- Yes/No question: *"Did she write the report?"* → *"Was the report written by her?"*
- Wh-question: *"Who wrote this letter?"* → *"By whom was this letter written?"*
- Wh-question (object as wh-word): *"What did he buy?"* → *"What was bought by him?"*

**Sentences with two objects (indirect + direct object):**
- *"He gave me a gift"* can become **either**: *"I was given a gift by him"* **or** *"A gift was given to me by him"* — both are grammatically valid; exams usually accept the version keeping the *person* as the new subject as the more natural default.

## STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES

| Confusion | Explanation | How Examiners Exploit It |
|---|---|---|
| Intransitive verbs cannot be passivized | Verbs with no direct object ("he laughed," "she arrived," "they slept") have no passive form at all | An option "converts" an intransitive sentence to passive anyway — this is simply wrong, not just a different valid form |
| "By" vs "with" for the agent | "By" introduces the *doer* (person/agent); "with" introduces the *tool/instrument* used | *"The letter was written by him"* vs *"The letter was written with a pen"* |
| Two-object sentence ambiguity | Two different, both-valid passive versions exist depending on which object becomes the subject | Only one is offered as "correct" in MCQs — usually the version with the person as subject |
| V2 vs V3 confusion in irregular verbs | Simple past form (V2) is mistakenly used in the passive instead of past participle (V3) | For regular verbs V2 and V3 are identical, hiding this error — irregular verbs expose it |

---

## STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE

1. **Wrong auxiliary tense trap:** The "be" verb doesn't match the original active sentence's tense (e.g., using "is" when the original was past tense).
2. **V2-instead-of-V3 trap:** Using the simple past form of an irregular verb instead of its past participle ("The letter was wrote" instead of "was written").
3. **Subject/object swap error:** The original subject and object aren't correctly repositioned.
4. **Missing "be" after modal trap:** Forgetting "be" when converting a modal sentence ("must completed" instead of "must be completed").
5. **Intransitive-verb trap:** Attempting to passivize a sentence whose verb has no object at all.
6. **Agent-preposition trap:** Using "with" instead of "by" (or vice versa) for the agent/instrument.

## STEP 8 — PATTERN RECOGNITION

| If the active sentence has... | Apply this passive pattern... |
|---|---|
| A simple tense main verb (V1/V2/will+V1) | Match Step 4's simple-tense row |
| "is/am/are/was/were" + V-ing | Match the continuous-tense row, adding "being" |
| "has/have/had" + V3 | Match the perfect-tense row, adding "been" |
| A modal (can, must, should, etc.) | Match the modal row — modal stays, add "be" + V3 |
| An imperative structure (no explicit subject, command form) | Use "Let + O + be + V3" |
| A question structure (auxiliary before subject) | Invert to passive question form, keeping the question's auxiliary position logic |
| No object after the verb at all | Do **not** attempt passive conversion — the verb is intransitive |

## STEP 11 — SPEED TECHNIQUES

- **Identify tense first, always:** Before touching the sentence, name its tense — this single step determines the entire rest of the conversion formula.
- **Memorize irregular V3 forms as a batch**, not one at a time — grouping them (Step 5) builds faster recall than learning them scattered across different practice sessions.
- **Check for an object before attempting conversion** — if there's no object, stop; the sentence can't be passivized.
- **Don't forget "being"/"been"** — these are the two words most commonly dropped under time pressure in continuous/perfect tense conversions.

---

## STEP 12 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES

- **"OBA" checklist:** **O**bject becomes subject → **B**e-verb matches original tense → **A**gent (by + subject) goes last, and is optional.
- **Visualize the formula chart as a ladder:** Simple → Continuous → Perfect, each rung adding one more piece ("being" for continuous, "been" for perfect) to the same core "be + V3" structure.
- **Irregular verb flashcards:** Drill Step 5's table specifically — these are the words most likely to trip up an otherwise-correct conversion.

## STEP 14 — ERROR DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

When you get a question wrong, don't just note "wrong answer" — identify *why*:

| Diagnosis | Signs | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Tense misidentification** | You converted using the wrong row of the Step 4 chart | Practice naming the active sentence's exact tense before converting anything |
| **Irregular verb error** | You used V2 instead of V3, or an incorrect past participle | Drill Step 5's irregular verb table specifically |
| **Dropped "being"/"been"** | Your continuous/perfect passive conversion is missing a key auxiliary word | Slow down and mentally recite the full formula before writing the answer |
| **Modal formula error** | You forgot "be" after the modal verb | Re-check the modal row in Step 4 — modal never changes, but "be" is mandatory after it |
| **Attempted to passivize an intransitive verb** | The sentence had no object, yet you tried a conversion anyway | Check for an object *before* starting any conversion |
| **Two-object sentence confusion** | You picked a version that changes the meaning or is grammatically unnatural | Default to making the *person* (not the thing) the new subject when both are valid |

## STEP 17 — ONE-PAGE REVISION SHEET

**Before every Active-Passive question, run this mental checklist:**
1. Identify the exact tense of the given sentence.
2. Confirm the verb has an object — if not, stop, it can't be passivized.
3. Apply the matching formula row from Step 4 (or Step 4B for imperative/interrogative).
4. Use the correct past participle (V3) — double-check irregular verbs against Step 5.
5. Place "by + agent" last, and consider omitting it if the agent is obvious/unimportant.
6. Re-read the full converted sentence once more before finalizing.

**Trap reminders:** wrong auxiliary tense • V2-instead-of-V3 • subject/object swap • missing "be" after modal • intransitive-verb attempt • agent-preposition (by vs with).
