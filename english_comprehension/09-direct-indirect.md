## STEP 1 — FOUNDATION

**What it is:** **Direct speech** reports someone's exact words in quotation marks ("He said, 'I am tired.'"). **Indirect (reported) speech** paraphrases those words without quotes, introduced by a reporting verb ("He said that he was tired."). You're tested on converting correctly between the two.

**Why it matters:** A formula-driven topic like Active-Passive Voice — moderate, consistent weightage, and high accuracy is achievable once the backshift and pronoun-change rules (Step 4) are internalized.

**Where it appears:**
- SSC CGL/CHSL — direct "convert to indirect/direct speech" questions
- IBPS/SBI, RBI — sometimes standalone, sometimes folded into Sentence Improvement
- Insurance, EPFO, GPSC, Railways — pattern similar to SSC

**How examiners test it:** By checking three things simultaneously — did you correctly **backshift the tense**, correctly **change the pronouns**, and correctly **change the time/place words**? Missing any one of the three invalidates the conversion, even if the other two are perfect.

## STEP 3 — CORE CONCEPTS

- **Definition:** Converting speech from direct (exact quoted words) to indirect (paraphrased, no quotes) requires changing **three things together**: the tense (backshifted, usually one step into the past), the pronouns (adjusted to the new speaker's perspective), and time/place references (adjusted since the reporting happens at a different time/place than the original speech).
- **Rule:** The reporting verb and the type of sentence (statement/question/command/exclamation) determine the exact conjunction and structure used to introduce the reported content.
- **Why it works (as a test):** It's a compound skill — three separate transformations must all be applied correctly and simultaneously, which is exactly what makes partial-credit-style traps effective (an option that's "mostly right" but misses just the pronoun, for instance).
- **Common mistake:** Correctly backshifting the tense but forgetting to change a pronoun or time-word, or vice versa — examiners deliberately design distractor options to isolate exactly one of these three failures.
- **Memory trick:** Run through **"TTP"** — **T**ense, **T**ime/place, **P**ronoun — as a fixed three-item checklist on every single conversion, never skipping straight to just one.

## STEP 4 — CORE CONVERSION RULES

### 4.1 Reporting Verb Changes
- **"Say" stays "say"** if no listener is mentioned: *"He said, 'I am tired'"* → *"He said that he was tired."*
- **"Say to" becomes "tell"** if a listener is mentioned: *"He said to me, 'You are late'"* → *"He told me that I was late."*
- **"Tell" always needs a direct object** (the listener) — you can't say "he told that..." without naming who was told.

### 4.2 Tense Backshift Table

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | Simple Past | *"I am happy"* → *"he was happy"* |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous | *"I am working"* → *"he was working"* |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect | *"I have finished"* → *"he had finished"* |
| Simple Past | Past Perfect | *"I finished"* → *"he had finished"* |
| Past Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous | *"I was working"* → *"he had been working"* |
| Simple Future (will) | Would | *"I will call"* → *"he would call"* |
| Modal: can | could | *"I can swim"* → *"he could swim"* |
| Modal: may | might | *"I may come"* → *"he might come"* |
| Modal: must | had to (usually) | *"I must go"* → *"he had to go"* |
| Modal: shall | should | *"I shall help"* → *"he should help"* |

**Exception — no backshift needed:** Universal truths, habitual facts, or statements still true at the time of reporting keep their original tense. *"The teacher said, 'The earth revolves around the sun'"* → *"The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun"* (not "revolved").

### 4.3 Pronoun Changes
- **First-person pronouns** (I, me, my, we, us, our) change according to the **subject** of the reporting verb.
- **Second-person pronouns** (you, your) change according to the **object** of the reporting verb.
- **Third-person pronouns** (he, she, it, they) generally stay unchanged.
- Example: *"He said to me, 'You are late'"* → *"He told me that **I** was late"* ("you" → "I," since the reporting verb's object is "me").

### 4.4 Time/Place Word Changes

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| ago | before |
| last night | the previous night |
| next week | the following week |

**Note:** If the reporting happens on the *same day* as the original speech, some time words don't need to change (e.g., "today" can stay "today" if you're reporting it later the same day) — use judgment based on context, not blind substitution.

### 4.5 Questions
- Reporting verb becomes **"asked"** (not "said").
- Remove the question mark; sentence structure inverts back to statement order (auxiliary moves after the subject).
- **Yes/No questions:** add **"if" or "whether"**. *"He said, 'Are you coming?'"* → *"He asked if I was coming."*
- **Wh-questions:** keep the wh-word, no "if/whether" needed. *"She said, 'Where do you live?'"* → *"She asked where I lived."*

### 4.6 Commands and Requests
- Reporting verb becomes **ordered / told / requested / advised / warned** + **infinitive (to + V1)**.
- Negative commands: **"not to" + V1**.
- *"He said, 'Close the door'"* → *"He ordered/told him to close the door."*
- *"She said, 'Please help me'"* → *"She requested him to help her."*

### 4.7 Exclamatory Sentences
- Reporting verb becomes **"exclaimed with joy/sorrow/wonder/regret"** (matching the emotion) + **"that"** + statement form.
- Interjections ("Wow!", "Alas!", "Hurrah!") are removed; degree words like "what a" are converted to "very."
- *"She said, 'Wow! What a beautiful sunset!'"* → *"She exclaimed with joy that it was a very beautiful sunset."*

## STEP 5 — REPORTING VERB VARIATIONS (Beyond Said/Told/Asked)

Higher-difficulty papers (RBI, UPSC) often expect a *nuanced* reporting verb matching the tone of the original statement, not just generic "said."

| Original Tone | Reporting Verb | Example Context |
|---|---|---|
| Suggesting an idea | Suggested | *"Let's go for a walk"* → suggested going for a walk |
| Giving advice | Advised | *"You should rest"* → advised resting/to rest |
| Firm instruction | Ordered / Instructed | *"Leave now"* → ordered them to leave |
| Polite ask | Requested | *"Please help me"* → requested help |
| Denying something | Denied | *"I didn't do it"* → denied doing it |
| Admitting something | Admitted | *"I made a mistake"* → admitted making a mistake |
| Making a promise | Promised | *"I will help you"* → promised to help |
| Warning someone | Warned | *"Don't go there"* → warned against going there |
| Expressing doubt | Wondered | *"Will it rain?"* (to oneself) → wondered if it would rain |

## STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES

| Confused Pair | Difference | How Examiners Exploit It |
|---|---|---|
| Say vs Tell | "Say" doesn't need a direct object (listener); "Tell" always does | *"He said to me"* is grammatically fine but sounds unnatural in indirect speech — should typically become "told me" |
| Ask vs Tell (for reporting) | "Asked" is for questions; "told" is for statements/commands directed at someone | Sentence type must match the correct reporting verb |
| Will vs Would in backshift | "Will" always becomes "would" under standard backshift, but is often mistakenly left unchanged | Distractor options frequently keep "will" to test whether backshift was actually applied |
| Universal truth exception | No backshift is needed for statements still true/timeless | Test-takers over-apply backshift out of habit, converting a fact into an unnecessarily past-tense (and now factually odd) claim |

## STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE

1. **Forgot tense backshift trap:** The pronoun and time-word changes are correct, but the verb tense is left exactly as it was in direct speech.
2. **Forgot pronoun change trap:** Tense and time-words are handled correctly, but a pronoun still refers to the wrong person.
3. **Forgot time/place word change trap:** "Today," "here," "tomorrow" etc. are left unchanged despite the reporting context clearly being different.
4. **Wrong reporting verb for sentence type trap:** Using "said" for a question (should be "asked"), or "told" for a command (should be "ordered/advised/requested").
5. **Missing conjunction/connector trap:** Forgetting "that" for statements, or using "that" instead of "if/whether" for yes/no questions.
6. **Retained question word order trap:** Keeping the direct-speech question structure (auxiliary before subject) instead of inverting it to statement order in the indirect version.
7. **Over-applying backshift trap:** Backshifting a universal truth or still-true fact unnecessarily.

## STEP 8 — PATTERN RECOGNITION

| If the direct speech is... | It's testing... | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| A plain statement ("I am...", "He has...") | Statement conversion | Apply TTP: tense backshift + pronoun change + time/place change, add "that" |
| A yes/no question ("Are you...?", "Did she...?") | Question conversion | Reporting verb → "asked"; add "if/whether"; invert to statement word order |
| A wh-question ("Where do you...?", "What is...?") | Question conversion | Reporting verb → "asked"; keep the wh-word; invert to statement word order |
| An imperative ("Close the door", "Please help me") | Command/request conversion | Reporting verb → ordered/told/requested; use infinitive "to + V1" |
| An exclamation ("Wow!", "What a...!", "Alas!") | Exclamatory conversion | Reporting verb → "exclaimed with [emotion]"; remove interjections; add "that" |
| A statement of general/timeless fact | Exception case | Do **not** backshift the tense |

## STEP 11 — SPEED TECHNIQUES

- **Classify sentence type first:** Statement, question, command, or exclamation — this single classification determines the entire conversion structure to apply.
- **Run the TTP checklist every time, in order:** Tense → Time/place → Pronoun — treating it as a fixed sequence prevents skipping any one of the three.
- **Memorize the backshift table as pairs, not individually:** "will→would," "can→could," "may→might" — learning them as a rhyming/paired set is faster than isolated memorization.
- **Watch for the universal-truth exception explicitly** — actively ask "is this still true/timeless?" before backshifting, rather than backshifting reflexively.

---

## STEP 12 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES

- **"TTP" as a fixed mental checklist:** Tense, Time/place, Pronoun — say it to yourself on every single conversion until it's automatic.
- **Backshift ladder visualization:** Picture each tense stepping "one rung back" in time — present steps back to past, past steps back to past perfect, will steps back to would.
- **Reporting-verb-by-sentence-type table:** Memorize the mapping (statement→said/told, question→asked, command→ordered/requested, exclamation→exclaimed) as a single lookup table rather than recalling each in isolation.

## STEP 14 — ERROR DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

When you get a question wrong, don't just note "wrong answer" — identify *why*:

| Diagnosis | Signs | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgot tense backshift** | Pronouns/time-words are correct, tense is not | Run the TTP checklist explicitly, starting with Tense every time |
| **Forgot pronoun change** | Tense/time-words are correct, a pronoun is wrong | Double-check every pronoun against Step 4.3's subject/object rule |
| **Forgot time/place word change** | Tense/pronouns are correct, "today/here/tomorrow" left unchanged | Cross-check every time/place word against Step 4.4's table |
| **Wrong reporting verb for sentence type** | Used "said" for a question, or "told" for a command | Re-classify the sentence type first (Step 8) before choosing the reporting verb |
| **Over-applied backshift to a universal truth** | A timeless/still-true fact was incorrectly shifted to past tense | Explicitly ask "is this still true?" before backshifting |
| **Kept question word order** | The indirect version still has the auxiliary before the subject | Remember: indirect questions use statement word order, not question word order |

## STEP 17 — ONE-PAGE REVISION SHEET

**Before every Direct-Indirect Speech question, run this mental checklist:**
1. Classify the sentence type: statement, question, command, or exclamation.
2. Choose the matching reporting verb structure (Step 4/Step 8).
3. Run TTP — Tense (backshift), Time/place (word change), Pronoun (change) — in that order, every time.
4. Check: is this a universal truth/still-true fact? If so, skip the tense backshift.
5. For questions — remember to invert back to statement word order.
6. Re-read the full converted sentence once more before finalizing.

**Trap reminders:** forgot-backshift • forgot-pronoun-change • forgot-time/place-change • wrong-reporting-verb • missing-conjunction • retained-question-word-order • over-applied-backshift.
