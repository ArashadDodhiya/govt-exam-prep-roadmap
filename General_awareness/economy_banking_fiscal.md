# Indian Economy — Banking & Fiscal Policy

## 1. Overview
This topic covers India's banking and monetary system centered on the RBI, key financial institutions (NABARD, SIDBI), banking health indicators (NPA, Basel Norms), and fiscal policy tools (Budget, taxation, FRBM Act, fiscal deficit). This is one of the most current-affairs-linked GK topics since rates and figures change periodically — but the mechanisms and definitions remain stable and highly testable.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**
- Central bank of India, established 1 April 1935 (under RBI Act 1934), based on the Hilton Young Commission's recommendation **[High]**
- Originally privately owned, NATIONALIZED in 1949 **[High]**
- Headquartered in Mumbai; Governor is the chief executive, appointed by the Central Government **[Medium]**
- Key functions: issues currency (sole note-issuing authority, except Re. 1 note/coins issued by Finance Ministry), banker to the government, banker's bank, manages foreign exchange (FEMA), regulates monetary policy, banking supervision **[High]**
- Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) — 6-member body (3 from RBI + 3 external), decides key policy rates; set up in 2016 for inflation targeting **[High]**
- Inflation targeting — RBI mandated to keep CPI inflation at 4% (+/- 2% band, i.e., 2-6%) **[High]**

**RBI Monetary Policy Tools**
- Repo Rate — rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks; MAIN policy rate tool, controls inflation/liquidity **[High]**
- Reverse Repo Rate — rate at which RBI borrows money FROM commercial banks (opposite of repo) **[High]**
- CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) — % of a bank's total deposits that must be kept as reserves with RBI (in cash form, cannot be used for lending) **[High]**
- SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) — % of deposits banks must maintain in liquid assets (cash, gold, government securities), held by the BANK ITSELF (not RBI) **[High]**
- MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate) — internal benchmark rate used by banks to set actual lending rates to customers; replaced the older "Base Rate" system **[Medium]**
- Bank Rate — rate at which RBI lends long-term funds to banks (without collateral); different from Repo Rate (short-term, collateralized via repurchase agreements) **[Medium]**
- Open Market Operations (OMO) — RBI buys/sells government securities in the open market to control money supply/liquidity **[Medium]**
- Increasing Repo/CRR/SLR = contractionary (reduces liquidity, controls inflation); Decreasing them = expansionary (increases liquidity, boosts growth) **[High]**

**Commercial Banks & Institutions**
- Commercial Banks classified as: Public Sector Banks (PSBs, govt majority ownership), Private Sector Banks, Foreign Banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Small Finance Banks, Payment Banks **[Medium]**
- State Bank of India (SBI) — largest public sector bank in India **[Medium]**
- NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) — apex institution for rural/agricultural credit and development, established 1982 **[High]**
- SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India) — apex institution for MSME sector financing, established 1990 **[High]**
- EXIM Bank (Export-Import Bank of India) — supports foreign trade financing, established 1982 **[Medium]**
- NHB (National Housing Bank) — apex institution for housing finance sector, established 1988 (regulatory powers later shifted to RBI, 2019) **[Low]**
- Bank Nationalization — first phase in 1969 (14 banks), second phase in 1980 (6 more banks) under Indira Gandhi's government **[High]**

**NPA (Non-Performing Asset) & Basel Norms**
- NPA — a loan/advance where interest or principal payment remains overdue for more than 90 days **[High]**
- Gross NPA vs Net NPA — Gross NPA = total NPAs before provisioning; Net NPA = Gross NPA minus provisions made by the bank **[Medium]**
- Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016 — legal framework for resolving corporate insolvency/bad loans in a time-bound manner **[Medium]**
- Basel Norms — international banking regulatory framework by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS), set under Bank for International Settlements (BIS) **[High]**
- Basel III — current global standard, focuses on capital adequacy, stress testing, liquidity risk; introduced post-2008 global financial crisis **[High]**
- CRAR / CAR (Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio) — minimum capital banks must hold relative to their risk-weighted assets, mandated under Basel Norms **[Medium]**

**Fiscal Policy — Budget**
- Union Budget — annual financial statement presented under Article 112 of the Constitution; typically presented 1 February each year **[High]**
- Nirmala Sitharaman — presented the FIRST Union Budget in a paperless/digital format (2021) **[Low]**
- Budget components: Revenue Budget (Revenue Receipts + Revenue Expenditure) and Capital Budget (Capital Receipts + Capital Expenditure) **[Medium]**
- Revenue Receipts — tax + non-tax income (no liability created); Capital Receipts — borrowings, disinvestment (creates liability or reduces assets) **[Medium]**
- Economic Survey — presented by the Finance Ministry a day BEFORE the Union Budget, reviews economic performance of the past year **[High]**

**Taxation**
- Direct Tax — paid directly by the individual/entity on whom it is levied (e.g., Income Tax, Corporate Tax); burden CANNOT be shifted **[High]**
- Indirect Tax — levied on goods/services, burden CAN be shifted to the consumer (e.g., GST) **[High]**
- GST (Goods and Services Tax) — implemented 1 July 2017, subsumed multiple indirect taxes (VAT, service tax, excise, etc.) into ONE unified tax, "One Nation One Tax" **[High]**
- GST structure: CGST (Central), SGST (State), IGST (Inter-state) **[High]**
- GST Council — constitutional body (101st Amendment) chaired by the Union Finance Minister, decides GST rates/rules, includes state finance ministers **[High]**
- Income Tax — direct tax on individual/entity income, governed by Income Tax Act 1961; administered by CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes) **[Medium]**
- CBIC (Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs) — administers indirect taxes including GST and customs duties **[Low]**

**FRBM Act & Fiscal Deficit**
- FRBM Act (Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act), 2003 — aims to ensure fiscal discipline, reduce fiscal deficit, improve fiscal management by the government **[High]**
- Fiscal Deficit — difference between total government expenditure and total revenue (excluding borrowings); indicates how much the government needs to borrow **[High]**
- Revenue Deficit — excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts (does not include capital transactions) **[Medium]**
- Primary Deficit — Fiscal Deficit MINUS interest payments on previous borrowings; shows the current year's borrowing need excluding past debt burden **[Medium]**
- Fiscal Deficit is usually expressed as a percentage of GDP; FRBM Act originally targeted 3% fiscal deficit of GDP (target has been revised/deferred multiple times) **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Repo Rate vs Reverse Repo Rate | Repo = RBI LENDS to banks (banks borrow FROM RBI); Reverse Repo = RBI BORROWS from banks (banks lend TO RBI) — opposite directions |
| CRR vs SLR | CRR = reserve kept as CASH with RBI (cannot earn interest/be used); SLR = reserve kept as LIQUID ASSETS (cash/gold/govt securities) with the BANK ITSELF (can earn some return) |
| Repo Rate vs Bank Rate | Repo Rate = SHORT-TERM lending, WITH collateral (repurchase agreement); Bank Rate = LONG-TERM lending, WITHOUT collateral |
| Direct Tax vs Indirect Tax | Direct Tax = burden CANNOT be shifted (Income Tax, Corporate Tax); Indirect Tax = burden CAN be shifted to the end consumer (GST, Customs Duty) |
| Fiscal Deficit vs Revenue Deficit vs Primary Deficit | Fiscal Deficit = broadest measure (total borrowing need); Revenue Deficit = only revenue account gap; Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit minus interest payments (shows CURRENT year's borrowing need only) |
| Gross NPA vs Net NPA | Gross NPA = before provisioning; Net NPA = AFTER subtracting provisions already made by the bank — Net NPA is always lower than Gross NPA |
| NABARD vs SIDBI | NABARD = AGRICULTURE/rural development focus; SIDBI = MSME/small industries focus — both are apex refinancing institutions but for different sectors |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Repo vs Reverse Repo direction** — "Repo = RBI Releases money (lends OUT); Reverse Repo = RBI Removes money (takes IN)" — both start with "R," use the second word (Releases vs Removes) to fix the direction.
- **CRR vs SLR location of reserve** — "CRR = Cash with RBI (both start with 'C' and 'R' pairing — Cash, RBI); SLR = Self-held Liquid reserves (Self = bank keeps it itself)" — the word "Self" cues that SLR stays with the bank, not RBI.
- **NABARD vs SIDBI focus** — "NABARD = Nature/Agriculture-Based; SIDBI = Small Industries (the 'SI' in SIDBI literally stands for Small Industries)" — the acronym itself is the memory hook.
- **Fiscal vs Revenue vs Primary Deficit** — picture three concentric circles: Fiscal Deficit (biggest, outer circle, everything) → Revenue Deficit (only revenue-side gap) → Primary Deficit (Fiscal minus interest, smallest/most refined figure).
- **GST implementation date** — "**1-7-17**" (1st July 2017) — reads almost like a countdown (1,7,17), easy to lock in as a single number sequence.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Repo vs Reverse Repo** — the most common trap in this topic; questions test the DIRECTION of fund flow (who lends to whom).
- **CRR vs SLR** — confusing WHERE the reserve is held (RBI vs bank itself) and WHAT FORM it takes (pure cash vs liquid assets including securities/gold).
- **Fiscal Deficit vs Revenue Deficit vs Primary Deficit** — swapped definitions are extremely common; always check if interest payments are being excluded (that signals Primary Deficit specifically).
- Direct vs Indirect tax — assuming GST is a direct tax (it is INDIRECT, since the burden shifts to the consumer).
- NABARD and SIDBI establishment years and sector focus often get swapped in "match the institution" questions.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- RBI: est. 1935, nationalized 1949, sets Repo/Reverse Repo/CRR/SLR/Bank Rate; MPC targets 4% (±2%) inflation.
- Repo=RBI lends short-term; Reverse Repo=RBI borrows; CRR=cash with RBI; SLR=liquid assets with bank; Bank Rate=long-term, no collateral.
- NABARD (1982, agri/rural) vs SIDBI (1990, MSME) vs EXIM Bank (1982, trade).
- NPA = overdue 90+ days; Basel III = post-2008 global capital adequacy norms.
- Budget: Art 112, presented 1 Feb, Economic Survey comes a day before.
- Tax: Direct (Income Tax, non-shiftable) vs Indirect (GST, shiftable); GST launched 1 July 2017, CGST+SGST+IGST, GST Council chaired by FM.
- FRBM Act (2003): fiscal discipline; Fiscal Deficit (total borrowing need) > Revenue Deficit (revenue-only gap) > Primary Deficit (Fiscal minus interest).

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** The rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks is called:
A) Reverse Repo Rate B) Repo Rate C) Bank Rate D) MCLR

**Q2.** CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) reserves are held by banks:
A) With themselves, in liquid assets
B) With RBI, in cash form
C) With the Finance Ministry
D) With NABARD

**Q3.** Which apex institution focuses specifically on financing India's MSME (Small Industries) sector?
A) NABARD B) EXIM Bank C) SIDBI D) NHB

**Q4.** GST (Goods and Services Tax) is classified as which type of tax?
A) Direct Tax B) Indirect Tax C) Wealth Tax D) Capital Gains Tax

**Q5.** Primary Deficit is calculated as:
A) Fiscal Deficit + Interest Payments
B) Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments
C) Revenue Deficit − Capital Expenditure
D) Total Expenditure − Total Receipts

## Answer Key
1. **B) Repo Rate** — This is the RBI's main policy tool for short-term lending to commercial banks against collateral.
2. **B) With RBI, in cash form** — CRR must be maintained as cash reserves with the RBI, unlike SLR which is held by the bank itself in liquid assets.
3. **C) SIDBI** — Small Industries Development Bank of India is the apex refinancing institution dedicated to MSME financing.
4. **B) Indirect Tax** — GST's burden can be shifted to the end consumer, classifying it as an indirect tax, unlike Income Tax.
5. **B) Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments** — Primary Deficit isolates the current year's borrowing need by excluding interest obligations from past debt.

⚠️ Repo rate, CRR, SLR values, and current fiscal deficit figures change frequently with each RBI Monetary Policy review and Union Budget — verify current rates/figures if relevant to a recent exam.
