# Miscellaneous GK — Books & Authors, Awards, Important Days, Sports

## 1. Overview
This topic covers static facts about literature, honors/awards systems, commemorative days, and sports structures. Much of this category (current award winners, latest record holders) changes yearly — this file focuses on the STABLE structural facts (award hierarchies, historical firsts, rules) that remain reliable for exams, while flagging areas where you should check the latest updates separately.

- Nobel Prizes awarded by Swedish institutions EXCEPT Peace Prize, which is awarded by a Norwegian Committee in Oslo **[High]**
- C.V. Raman — first Indian to win a Nobel Prize IN SCIENCE (Physics, 1930, for the "Raman Effect") **[High]**
- Mother Teresa — Nobel Peace Prize (1979) **[Medium]**
- Amartya Sen — Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences (1998) **[Medium]**

- Dronacharya Award — for excellence in sports COACHING; Arjuna Award — for outstanding sports PERFORMANCE **[High]**
- Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna — highest sporting honor in India (renamed from "Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna") **[High]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Arjuna Award vs Dronacharya Award | Arjuna Award = for the ATHLETE'S performance; Dronacharya Award = for the COACH's excellence — named after the mythological archer (Arjuna) and his teacher (Dronacharya) respectively |
| Padma Vibhushan vs Padma Bhushan vs Padma Shri | Ranked HIGH to LOW: Padma Vibhushan (2nd highest overall, after Bharat Ratna) > Padma Bhushan (3rd) > Padma Shri (4th) |
| Nobel Prize categories vs Economics addition | Original 5 categories (Physics, Chemistry, Medicine, Literature, Peace) were in Alfred Nobel's will; Economics was added LATER (1969) by Sweden's central bank, technically a different prize name |
| C.V. Raman vs Rabindranath Tagore (first Indian Nobel laureates) | Tagore = FIRST Indian Nobel laureate overall (Literature, 1913); C.V. Raman = FIRST Indian to win in a SCIENCE category (Physics, 1930) — don't confuse "first overall" with "first in science" |
| Teachers' Day vs Children's Day | Teachers' Day (5 Sept) = birthday of Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (a teacher/philosopher who became President); Children's Day (14 Nov) = birthday of Jawaharlal Nehru (fond of children, called "Chacha Nehru") |
| Olympics vs Commonwealth Games vs Asian Games | Olympics = GLOBAL, all nations, IOC; Commonwealth Games = only Commonwealth (former British colony) nations; Asian Games = only Asian nations, OCA — different geographic scopes, all held every 4 years |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Padma Award ranking** — "**V**ery **B**ig **S**hri" → **V**ibhushan (biggest) > **B**hushan > **Shri** (smallest) — descending size of the word roughly matches descending rank.
- **Arjuna vs Dronacharya Award** — "Arjuna the WARRIOR earned the award through his OWN skill (player); Dronacharya was his TEACHER (coach)" — using the Mahabharata relationship itself as the memory hook.
- **First Indian Nobel Laureate trio** — "**T**agore = **T**otal first (1913, Literature); **R**aman = **R**eal science first (1930, Physics)" — T before R alphabetically also matches their chronological order.
- **Important days by "who's birthday"** — link each day to a person's birthday: Teachers' Day=Radhakrishnan, Children's Day=Nehru, Gandhi Jayanti=Gandhi — a simple "birthday calendar" mental trick.
- **Sports governing bodies** — "**I**nternational **C**ricket = **ICC**; **I**nternational **O**lympic = **IOC**; **F**ootball **I**nternational = **FIFA**" — each acronym literally spells out "International + Sport," making them self-explanatory once you notice the pattern.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Arjuna vs Dronacharya Award** — swapping which one honors the player vs the coach is a very frequent trap.
- **"First Indian Nobel laureate" vs "first Indian Nobel laureate in science"** — Tagore (1913, overall first) vs C.V. Raman (1930, first in a science category) are often confused as the same "first."
- Padma Award ranking order — questions test whether students know Vibhushan > Bhushan > Shri (descending), sometimes reversing the order in wrong options.
- Confusing **which governing body oversees which sport/event** — ICC (cricket) vs FIFA (football) vs IOC (Olympics) vs OCA (Asian Games) get mixed up in "match the organization" questions.
- Assuming Bharat Ratna is given to a FIXED number of people each year — it has NO fixed quota and is sometimes not awarded at all in a given year, a fact often tested directly.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which award is given for excellence in sports COACHING, as opposed to playing performance?
A) Arjuna Award B) Khel Ratna C) Dronacharya Award D) Padma Shri

**Q2.** Who was the first Indian to win a Nobel Prize in a SCIENCE category?
A) Rabindranath Tagore B) C.V. Raman C) Amartya Sen D) Kailash Satyarthi

**Q3.** In order of rank (highest to lowest), the correct sequence of Padma Awards is:
A) Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan, Padma Vibhushan
B) Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri
C) Padma Bhushan, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Shri
D) All three are equal rank

**Q4.** Teachers' Day in India is celebrated on 5 September to mark the birthday of:
A) Jawaharlal Nehru B) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan C) Mahatma Gandhi D) B.R. Ambedkar

**Q5.** The FIFA World Cup, held every four years, is the premier international tournament for which sport?
A) Cricket B) Hockey C) Football D) Basketball

## Answer Key
1. **C) Dronacharya Award** — Named after the guru from the Mahabharata, this award honors excellence in coaching, unlike the Arjuna Award which honors playing performance.
2. **B) C.V. Raman** — He won the Nobel Prize in Physics (1930) for the Raman Effect, making him the first Indian Nobel laureate in a science category (Tagore's 1913 Literature win came earlier but wasn't a science category).
3. **B) Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri** — This is the correct descending order of India's Padma civilian awards.
4. **B) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan** — India's second President and noted philosopher-teacher, whose birthday is observed as Teachers' Day.
5. **C) Football** — FIFA (International Federation of Association Football) governs the World Cup, the top global football tournament.
