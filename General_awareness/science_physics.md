# General Science — Physics

## 1. Overview
Physics in GK exams covers fundamental mechanics, energy, waves, optics, electricity/magnetism, modern physics, and thermodynamics. Unlike pure conceptual physics exams, GK-level physics tests direct definitions, units, laws by name, and everyday applications — ideal for quick factual recall rather than calculation-heavy problems.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Units & Measurements**
- SI Unit System — 7 base units: meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount of substance), candela (luminous intensity) **[High]**
- SI unit of Force = Newton (N); Energy/Work = Joule (J); Power = Watt (W); Pressure = Pascal (Pa) **[High]**
- Instruments: Vernier Caliper & Screw Gauge (small length measurement), Barometer (atmospheric pressure), Thermometer (temperature), Ammeter (current), Voltmeter (voltage) **[Medium]**

**Laws of Motion (Newton's Laws)**
- Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia) — an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force **[High]**
- Newton's Second Law — Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma); rate of change of momentum is proportional to applied force **[High]**
- Newton's Third Law — for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction **[High]**
- Momentum = mass × velocity (p = mv); Law of Conservation of Momentum — total momentum of an isolated system remains constant **[Medium]**
- Friction — opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact; types: static, kinetic (sliding), rolling **[Medium]**

**Gravitation**
- Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation — every object attracts every other object with force proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance between them (F = Gm₁m₂/r²) **[High]**
- Universal Gravitational Constant (G) — value ≈ 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² **[Medium]**
- Acceleration due to gravity (g) — approximately 9.8 m/s² on Earth's surface; DECREASES with altitude and depth from Earth's center **[High]**
- Weight = mass × g (weight varies with location, mass remains constant) **[High]**
- Escape Velocity — minimum velocity needed to escape a planet's gravitational pull; for Earth, approximately 11.2 km/s **[Medium]**
- Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion — describe elliptical orbits of planets around the Sun (Law of Orbits, Law of Areas, Law of Periods) **[Medium]**

**Work, Energy, Power**
- Work = Force × Displacement (in the direction of force); SI unit = Joule **[High]**
- Energy — capacity to do work; two main mechanical forms: Kinetic Energy (KE = ½mv², energy of motion) and Potential Energy (PE = mgh, energy of position/state) **[High]**
- Law of Conservation of Energy — energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only transformed from one form to another **[High]**
- Power = Work done / Time taken; SI unit = Watt; commercial unit = kWh (kilowatt-hour, "unit" of electricity) **[High]**
- 1 Horsepower (HP) ≈ 746 Watts **[Low]**

**Waves & Sound**
- Wave — disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter; two types: Mechanical (needs medium, e.g., sound) and Electromagnetic (no medium needed, e.g., light) **[Medium]**
- Sound — a mechanical/longitudinal wave, requires a medium to travel (CANNOT travel in vacuum) **[High]**
- Speed of sound — fastest in solids, slower in liquids, slowest in gases (opposite of light) **[High]**
- Speed of sound in air ≈ 343 m/s (at room temperature) **[Medium]**
- Ultrasound — frequency above human hearing range (>20,000 Hz), used in medical imaging (sonography), SONAR **[Medium]**
- Doppler Effect — apparent change in frequency/pitch of a wave due to relative motion between source and observer **[Medium]**
- Echo — reflection of sound, requires minimum distance (~17m) and time gap (~0.1 sec) to be distinctly heard by human ear **[Low]**

**Light**
- Light is an electromagnetic wave, travels fastest in VACUUM (~3×10⁸ m/s), speed DECREASES in denser media **[High]**
- Reflection — bouncing back of light from a surface; angle of incidence = angle of reflection **[High]**
- Refraction — bending of light when passing from one medium to another due to change in speed **[High]**
- Dispersion — splitting of white light into its 7 constituent colors (VIBGYOR) when passed through a prism, due to different wavelengths bending at different angles **[High]**
- Total Internal Reflection — occurs when light passes from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle; basis for optical fibers **[Medium]**
- Convex lens — converges light, used to correct hypermetropia (long-sightedness/farsightedness); Concave lens — diverges light, used to correct myopia (short-sightedness/nearsightedness) **[High]**
- Human eye — uses a convex lens to focus image on the retina; the eye's lens focal length is adjusted by ciliary muscles (accommodation) **[Medium]**
- Rainbow formation — combination of refraction, dispersion, and total internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets **[Medium]**

**Electricity**
- Electric Current — flow of electric charge; SI unit = Ampere (A) **[High]**
- Ohm's Law — Voltage = Current × Resistance (V = IR), at constant temperature **[High]**
- SI unit of Resistance = Ohm (Ω); of Voltage/Potential Difference = Volt (V); of Charge = Coulomb (C) **[Medium]**
- Series Circuit — same current flows through all components, resistances ADD UP; Parallel Circuit — voltage same across all branches, resistances combine reciprocally (total resistance decreases) **[Medium]**
- Conductors (allow current flow easily, e.g., metals) vs Insulators (resist current flow, e.g., rubber, wood) vs Semiconductors (intermediate, e.g., silicon) **[Medium]**
- Fuse — safety device that melts/breaks circuit during excess current flow to prevent damage/fire **[Low]**

**Magnetism**
- Magnetic field — region around a magnet where magnetic force is experienced; field lines flow from North to South pole (outside the magnet) **[Medium]**
- Electromagnet — temporary magnet created by passing electric current through a coil (solenoid), often around an iron core **[Medium]**
- Electromagnetic Induction — generation of electric current by changing magnetic field (Faraday's Law); basis of electric generators **[Medium]**
- Earth behaves like a giant magnet — magnetic North and South poles NOT exactly aligned with geographic poles (magnetic declination) **[Low]**

**Modern Physics**
- Radioactivity — spontaneous emission of radiation (alpha, beta, gamma particles/rays) from unstable atomic nuclei; discovered by Henri Becquerel **[High]**
- Alpha particles — positively charged (helium nuclei), least penetrating; Beta particles — electrons, moderate penetration; Gamma rays — electromagnetic radiation, most penetrating, no charge **[Medium]**
- Half-life — time taken for half the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay; used in carbon dating (Carbon-14) **[Medium]**
- Nuclear Fission — splitting of a heavy atomic nucleus into smaller nuclei, releasing large energy; basis of nuclear power plants and atomic bombs **[High]**
- Nuclear Fusion — combining of light atomic nuclei into a heavier nucleus, releasing even MORE energy than fission; occurs in the Sun and stars (hydrogen bombs) **[High]**
- Fission = SPLITTING (heavy → lighter); Fusion = JOINING (light → heavier) — opposite processes **[High]**

**Heat & Thermodynamics**
- Heat — form of energy transferred due to temperature difference; SI unit = Joule (calorie also commonly used) **[Medium]**
- Modes of heat transfer: Conduction (through solids, direct contact), Convection (through fluids, via movement of particles), Radiation (through vacuum/space, via electromagnetic waves, e.g., sunlight) **[High]**
- Temperature scales: Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin; 0°C = 273.15 K = 32°F; absolute zero = 0 K = -273.15°C **[Medium]**
- First Law of Thermodynamics — energy conservation applied to heat/work systems: heat added = change in internal energy + work done **[Medium]**
- Second Law of Thermodynamics — heat naturally flows from hotter to colder body; entropy of an isolated system tends to increase **[Medium]**
- Latent Heat — heat absorbed/released during a phase change (solid↔liquid↔gas) WITHOUT change in temperature **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Mass vs Weight | Mass = amount of matter (constant everywhere, SI unit kg); Weight = force due to gravity (varies with location, = mass × g) |
| Nuclear Fission vs Fusion | Fission = SPLITTING a heavy nucleus (man-made reactors, atomic bombs); Fusion = COMBINING light nuclei (occurs in Sun, hydrogen bombs) — Fusion releases MORE energy than fission |
| Speed of sound vs speed of light in media | Sound travels FASTEST in solids, slowest in gases; Light travels FASTEST in vacuum, slows down in denser media — completely opposite behavior |
| Convex lens vs Concave lens | Convex = CONVERGES light, corrects hypermetropia (farsightedness); Concave = DIVERGES light, corrects myopia (nearsightedness) |
| Conduction vs Convection vs Radiation | Conduction = solids, direct contact; Convection = fluids (liquid/gas), via particle movement; Radiation = NO medium needed (works in vacuum, e.g., sunlight) |
| Alpha vs Beta vs Gamma radiation | Alpha = LEAST penetrating (stopped by paper), positively charged; Beta = MODERATE penetration (stopped by aluminum), electrons; Gamma = MOST penetrating (needs lead/concrete), no charge |
| Series vs Parallel circuits | Series = SAME current throughout, resistances ADD; Parallel = SAME voltage across branches, total resistance DECREASES |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Newton's 3 Laws quick tags** — "**I**nertia, **F**=ma, **A**ction-Reaction" → 1st=Inertia, 2nd=Force formula, 3rd=Action-reaction — a simple 3-word tag per law number.
- **VIBGYOR (light dispersion colors)** — "**V**iolet **I**ndigo **B**lue **G**reen **Y**ellow **O**range **R**ed" — the classic rainbow color acronym, remember it goes from shortest wavelength (Violet) to longest (Red).
- **Modes of heat transfer** — "**C**onduction **C**ontacts, **C**onvection **C**irculates, **R**adiation **R**eaches (even through empty space)" — alliteration ties each mode to its defining behavior.
- **Fission vs Fusion memory hook** — "Fission = FISSURE = splits/cracks apart; Fusion = FUSE = joins/melts together" — the words themselves hint at the opposite actions.
- **Alpha-Beta-Gamma penetration power** — "**A**lpha = **A** paper stops it (weakest); **B**eta = **B**locked by metal foil (medium); **G**amma = **G**oes through most things (strongest, needs lead)" — power increases alphabetically from Alpha to Gamma.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Mass vs Weight** confusion is a perennial favorite — questions test whether students know weight changes with gravity (e.g., on the Moon) while mass stays constant.
- **Fission vs Fusion** — swapping which one occurs in the Sun (Fusion) vs which one is used in conventional nuclear power plants (Fission).
- Sound and light speed-in-media traps — assuming light behaves like sound (faster in denser media) when it's actually the OPPOSITE.
- **Convex vs Concave lens** — mixing up which corrects myopia vs hypermetropia; a very frequently tested pairing.
- Conduction/Convection/Radiation — questions about how heat from the Sun reaches Earth (through vacuum, so it must be RADIATION) trip up students who default to "conduction" as the most familiar term.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Newton's Laws: 1st=Inertia, 2nd=F=ma, 3rd=Action-Reaction; g≈9.8 m/s² (decreases with altitude/depth).
- Work=Force×Displacement; KE=½mv², PE=mgh; Power=Work/Time (Watt); Energy conservation law.
- Sound: needs medium, fastest in solids; Light: needs NO medium, fastest in vacuum — opposite behaviors.
- Reflection/Refraction/Dispersion (VIBGYOR); Convex=converges/hypermetropia fix, Concave=diverges/myopia fix.
- Electricity: Ohm's Law (V=IR); Series=current same/resistance adds, Parallel=voltage same/resistance drops.
- Modern Physics: Fission=splits heavy nucleus (reactors), Fusion=joins light nuclei (Sun, more energy); Alpha<Beta<Gamma in penetration power.
- Heat transfer: Conduction (solids)/Convection (fluids)/Radiation (no medium needed); absolute zero=0K=-273.15°C.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which of Newton's Laws states that force equals mass times acceleration?
A) First Law B) Second Law C) Third Law D) Law of Gravitation

**Q2.** Sound travels fastest through which medium?
A) Vacuum B) Gas C) Liquid D) Solid

**Q3.** A convex lens is used to correct which vision defect?
A) Myopia (nearsightedness) B) Hypermetropia (farsightedness) C) Astigmatism D) Color blindness

**Q4.** Nuclear fusion, the process occurring in the Sun, involves:
A) Splitting of heavy nuclei into lighter ones
B) Combining of light nuclei into a heavier nucleus
C) Emission of alpha particles only
D) Conversion of matter into pure heat without nuclei involved

**Q5.** Which mode of heat transfer allows heat from the Sun to reach Earth through the vacuum of space?
A) Conduction B) Convection C) Radiation D) Insulation

## Answer Key
1. **B) Second Law** — Newton's Second Law is expressed as F = ma (Force = mass × acceleration).
2. **D) Solid** — Sound travels fastest through solids due to closely packed particles, and slowest through gases.
3. **B) Hypermetropia (farsightedness)** — A convex lens converges light rays to correct farsightedness, unlike a concave lens used for myopia.
4. **B) Combining of light nuclei into a heavier nucleus** — This is nuclear fusion, which powers the Sun and releases more energy than fission.
5. **C) Radiation** — Radiation is the only heat transfer mode that doesn't require a medium, allowing heat to cross the vacuum of space.
