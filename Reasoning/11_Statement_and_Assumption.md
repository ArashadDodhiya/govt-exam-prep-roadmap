# 11. Statement and Assumption

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is an Assumption?**
An assumption is something that is **unsaid but taken for granted** by the speaker *before* they make a statement. It is the hidden foundation upon which the statement is built. 

*Analogy:* If I say, "Bring an umbrella, it is cloudy," my hidden assumption is: "Clouds usually lead to rain, and you do not want to get wet." I didn't say that, but I absolutely assumed it before speaking.

**Why do examiners test this?**
This tests your emotional and logical intelligence. It is a dominant topic in UPSC CSAT, Bank Mains, and SSC Tier-2. Examiners want to see if you can step into the shoes of a speaker (a CEO, a government official, an advertiser) and understand their underlying intent.

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Conclusion Trap:** The biggest trap. An Assumption comes *before* the statement. A Conclusion comes *after*. Students frequently select conclusions thinking they are assumptions.
2. **The "Cynical Reality" Trap:** Statement: "Please turn off your phones in the library." Assumption: "People will read the notice and turn off their phones." Students think: *"In reality, nobody listens to notices, so this is false."* **TRAP!** You are not judging reality. You are judging the *Speaker*. The speaker obviously assumed people would listen, otherwise, they wouldn't have put up the notice!
3. **The Extreme Assumption Trap:** Words like "All," "Always," "Only," and "Never" usually make an assumption invalid because people rarely make absolute assumptions.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Advertisements and Notices (High Frequency)
*   "Buy X brand TV for the best picture."
*   *Hidden rule:* The advertiser assumes people want good pictures, and that people read/respond to ads.

### 2. Advice, Warnings, and Appeals
*   "Do not lean out of the moving train window."
*   *Hidden rule:* Warnings are given assuming people might actually do the dangerous thing, and that the warning will deter them.

### 3. Government Policies / Decisions
*   "The Govt has banned plastic bags to save the environment."
*   *Hidden rule:* The Govt assumes this action will effectively help the environment and that citizens will (mostly) comply.

### 4. General Logic / Cause & Effect
*   "Because of heavy rain, schools are closed."
*   *Hidden rule:* Rain makes travel unsafe or difficult for students.

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **The "Speaker's Mindset" Rule:** You must mentally become the person who made the statement. Ask yourself: *"Why did I just say this? What was I expecting?"*
2. **The "Positive Intent" Rule:** Assumptions made by a speaker are almost always optimistic about their statement. A government makes a policy assuming it will work, not assuming it will fail. An advertiser puts out an ad assuming it will increase sales.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional (Gut Feeling)
*   Read the statement and assumption, and guess if it feels right. 
*   *When to use:* NEVER. Gut feelings bring in your personal biases and cause negative marking.

### Method 2: The Intent Method
*   Ask: "Does this assumption give a logical reason for the speaker to make the statement?" If yes, it is implicit (valid).

### Method 3: Expert Method (The Negation Test)
*   This is the ultimate weapon. Take the assumption and **make it negative**.
*   *Example:* Statement: "Donate to our charity." Assumption: "People have money to donate."
    *   *Negate it:* "People do NOT have money to donate."
*   Now ask: If the negated assumption is true, does the original statement look completely stupid and illogical? 
*   If YES (why ask for money if nobody has it?), then the original assumption is **100% Valid (Implicit)**.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Is it an Advertisement?** -> Instantly validate assumptions that say "People will be attracted to this" or "People desire the feature mentioned."
*   **Is it a Warning?** -> Instantly validate assumptions that say "People might get hurt" or "People usually read and obey warnings."
*   **Does the assumption restate the statement in different words?** -> Invalid. An assumption must be an unsaid premise, not a repetition.
*   **Does the assumption contain "Only", "All", or "Best"?** -> Treat it with extreme suspicion. Usually invalid.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **The "Valid" Checklist (Usually Implicit):**
    *   People read notices/ads and respond to them.
    *   Appeals/requests might be accepted.
    *   Warnings are necessary because the danger is real.
    *   The problem being addressed actually exists.
2. **The "Invalid" Checklist (Usually Not Implicit):**
    *   Extreme words (All, Every, None, Only).
    *   Past or Future predictions unrelated to the statement.
    *   Comparisons (unless the statement explicitly compares).
    *   Conclusions (what happens *after* the statement).

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Applying Personal Cynicism:** "The government issued a notice to stop spitting." Assumption: "People will stop spitting." You mark it invalid because "Indians never stop spitting." *MISTAKE!* The government *assumed* people would stop; that's why they issued the notice. It is VALID.
*   **Confusing Assumption with Fact:** An assumption doesn't have to be factually true in the real world. It only has to be true in the mind of the speaker.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC MTS / GD)**
*   Statement: "Please drop your donations in the box." - A notice at an orphanage.
*   Assumption I: People who read the notice may donate.
    *   *(Implicit. The whole point of the notice is the hope that people will donate).*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   Statement: "Use 'X' brand face wash for glowing skin" - An advertisement.
*   Assumptions: I. People want glowing skin. II. People like 'X' brand.
    *   *(I is Implicit: Why advertise glowing skin if nobody wants it? II is NOT Implicit: The ad is trying to make them like it; the advertiser doesn't assume they already do).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO Prelims / CGL Mains)**
*   Statement: The State Government has decided to declare a holiday tomorrow due to the massive cyclone warning.
*   Assumptions: I. The cyclone might cause damage and disrupt travel. II. People prefer staying home during cyclones.
    *   *(I is Implicit: Use the Negation Test. If it WON'T cause damage, the holiday looks stupid. II is NOT Implicit: Personal preference isn't the reason; safety is).*

**Level 8 (SBI PO Mains / RBI Grade B)**
*   Statement: "Despite a 20% increase in the price of petrol, the number of private vehicles on the road has not decreased."
*   Assumptions: I. Petrol price is the only factor deciding vehicle usage. II. People can afford to pay higher prices for convenience.
    *   *(I is NOT Implicit: "Only" makes it extreme. II is Implicit: If they couldn't afford it, usage would have decreased).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation - UPSC CSAT)**
*   A complex paragraph regarding a new agricultural policy aiming to double farmer income, followed by subtle philosophical assumptions about market economics and farmer adaptability. You must apply the Negation Test meticulously.

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **The Negation Drill:** For the next 20 questions you practice, force yourself to write down the *negative* of the assumption and see if it destroys the statement. This is the only way to build foolproof accuracy.
2. **Remove "You" from the Equation:** Never ask "What do I think about this?" Only ask "What did the speaker think?"

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Basic Ad/Notice:** Ideal: 20s | Expert: 10s (Instant pattern match).
*   **Govt Policy / Cause-Effect:** Ideal: 35s | Expert: 20s.
*   **Applying Negation Test (when stuck):** Takes +15 seconds, but guarantees 100% accuracy.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `Notice: "Trespassers will be prosecuted." Assumption: Trespassers exist.` -> *(Answer: Implicit. If nobody ever trespassed, why put up the board?)*
2. `Statement: "Vote for Party X to eradicate poverty." Assumption: Party X will definitely eradicate poverty.` -> *(Answer: NOT Implicit. That is a future conclusion. The assumption is simply that poverty is a problem and people want it gone).*
3. `Statement: "Eat an apple a day." Assumption: Apples are the ONLY healthy fruit.` -> *(Answer: NOT Implicit. "Only" ruins it).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Assumption = Unsaid Premise.** It happens BEFORE the statement.
*   **Conclusion = Deduction.** It happens AFTER the statement.
*   **The Negation Test:** Make the assumption negative. If the statement now sounds idiotic, the assumption was Valid (Implicit).
*   **Positive Intent:** Assume Ads, Warnings, and Appeals will be somewhat successful.
*   **Extreme Words:** All, Always, Only, None $\rightarrow$ Usually Invalid.
