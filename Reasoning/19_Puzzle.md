# 19. Puzzle Test

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is a Puzzle?**
A Puzzle is a complex set of raw data that must be organized into a structured matrix (table) using logical deductions. You are given variables (e.g., 7 people, living on 7 floors, liking 7 different colors) and a series of clues. Some clues are direct ("A lives on floor 3"), but most are indirect ("The one who likes Red lives two floors above B"). 

**Why do examiners test this?**
This is the **King of Banking Exams** (SBI PO, IBPS PO, RBI) and is increasingly dominating SSC CGL Mains and UPSC CSAT. It tests your ability to process massive amounts of data, handle multiple variables simultaneously, and connect indirect clues. 

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Overwhelm Trap:** Presenting a massive paragraph to induce panic.
2. **The "Negative Clue" Trap:** "A does not like Red, Green, or Blue." Students try to place A based on this. You cannot place someone based on what they *don't* like until the very end.
3. **The "Two Floors Above" Trap:** "A lives two floors above B." This means A - (one empty floor) - B. Students often leave two empty floors between them.
4. **The False Start:** Providing a very juicy clue at the beginning that actually yields 4 different possibilities, forcing you to draw 4 tables.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Floor / Box Puzzles (Extremely High Frequency)
*   Arranging people on numbered floors (1 to 8) or stacking boxes one above the other.

### 2. Day / Month / Year Scheduling
*   People attending seminars from Monday to Sunday, or born in different Months/Years. (Age must be calculated from a base year).

### 3. Categorization / Classification
*   9 people working in 3 different departments (HR, IT, Marketing), with rules like "Not more than 4 in one dept."

### 4. Comparison Puzzles
*   Arranging people based on height, weight, or marks (usually combined with Order & Ranking).

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **Choose the Right Base:** Every table needs a fixed leftmost column. 
    *   If Days are given (Mon-Sun), **Days** are the base.
    *   If Floors are given (1-8), **Floors** (8 at top, 1 at bottom) are the base.
    *   If Months are given, **Months** are the base.
    *   *Never* make the People the base if time/floors are involved.
2. **The Rule of Two:** Always draw TWO identical tables side-by-side before reading the first clue. As soon as a clue creates a possibility, split them into Table 1 and Table 2.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: The "Read and Guess" Method
*   Reading the whole puzzle, then trying to fill the table by guessing.
*   *When to use:* NEVER.

### Method 2: The "Dot/Condition Writing" Method (Expert Method)
*   **Step 1:** Draw your two base tables.
*   **Step 2:** Read the puzzle sentence by sentence. If a clue is Direct (e.g., "A lives on Floor 5"), put it in the table.
*   **Step 3:** If a clue is Indirect (e.g., "B lives somewhere above C"), **write it down in shorthand** on the side of your page (e.g., $B \uparrow C$). 
*   **Step 4:** Never read the paragraph twice. Once you reach the end, use your shorthand notes to fill the remaining gaps.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Does the puzzle mention Months?** -> Immediately write the number of days next to each month (Jan-31, Feb-28, Mar-31) because there *will* be a clue saying "A was born in a month having 30 days."
*   **Does it mention Boxes?** -> Boxes do NOT have fixed numbers unless stated. Treat them as floating entities until a clue locks them (e.g., "Box A is at the top").
*   **Is it a Blood Relation Puzzle?** -> Draw the Family Tree FIRST, before placing anyone in the seating arrangement or table.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **Shorthand Mastery:**
    *   "Two floors between A and B": `A -- B`
    *   "A is immediately above B": `A / B` (with a box around them).
    *   "C does not like Red": `C =/= Red`
2. **The "Consecutive" Trick:** "A and B do not live on adjacent floors." This is a powerful elimination tool at the end of the puzzle.
3. **The "Either/Or" Ending:** Sometimes, the last two variables cannot be fixed. Don't panic and think you did it wrong. Options will be "Either A or B" or "Cannot be determined."

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Reading the paragraph multiple times:** You lose 3 minutes just re-reading. Write indirect clues as shorthand so you never look at the computer screen again.
*   **Wrong Floor Numbering:** Writing Floor 1 at the top and Floor 8 at the bottom. Floor 1 is always the Ground Floor (bottom) unless specified otherwise.
*   **Getting Stuck on 1 Possibility:** You think "A must be on floor 4" and proceed. Halfway through, it fails. You have to erase everything. *Always run two possibilities parallelly.*

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC MTS / GD)**
*   5 people live on 5 floors. A lives on top. B lives immediately below A. C lives on the bottom floor. Where do D and E live if D is above E?
    *   *(Easy direct placement).*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   7 days of the week. 7 people teaching 7 subjects. A teaches on Wednesday. Two days between A and the one who teaches Math. B teaches immediately before Math...
    *   *(Requires parallel tables for the "Two days between" clue, as Math could be on Saturday or Sunday).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO Prelims / SBI Clerk Mains)**
*   8 Floors. 8 people. 8 different cars. "The one who owns Audi lives on an even numbered floor above floor 4." (Means floor 6 or 8. Split into two tables immediately).

**Level 8 (SBI PO Mains / RBI Grade B)**
*   10 Boxes. Unknown total number of shelves. Some boxes are empty. "Box A is 3 boxes above Box B. The number of boxes between B and C is the same as between C and D..."
    *   *(Requires extreme patience and mastery of the shorthand method. No fixed base available initially).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation)**
*   Blood Relation + Circular Seating + Professions. (Solve Family tree first, then seating).

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **Master the Shorthand:** Your speed depends 100% on how fast and neatly you write indirect clues on the side. Create your own symbols ($\uparrow, \downarrow, \times, \rightarrow$) and stick to them.
2. **Never Quit Mid-Way during Practice:** Even if it takes you 15 minutes at home, finish the puzzle. You are building mental endurance.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Basic Categorization (1 variable):** Ideal: 2 mins | Expert: 1.5 mins.
*   **Floor/Day/Month (2 variables):** Ideal: 3 mins | Expert: 2 mins.
*   **Mains Level Box/Unknown (3+ variables):** Ideal: 8-10 mins | Expert: 6 mins.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `A lives two floors above B.` -> How many people live between them? *(Answer: ONE. A is on 3, B is on 1).*
2. `3 people attend seminars between P and Q.` -> If P is on Monday, when is Q? *(Answer: Friday. Tue, Wed, Thu are between them).*
3. `What is the base for a puzzle involving 8 people, 8 colors, and 8 months?` -> *(Answer: The 8 Months, written sequentially Jan to Dec).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Base Rule:** Fix Days, Months, or Floors on the left. Never fix People.
*   **Rule of Two:** Always draw two parallel tables.
*   **One-Time Reading:** Read the paragraph once. Direct clues go in the table. Indirect clues go in shorthand on the side.
*   **Negative Clues:** Save them for the end to eliminate wrong placements.
