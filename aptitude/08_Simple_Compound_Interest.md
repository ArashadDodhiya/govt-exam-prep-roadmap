# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Interest?

**Interest** is the **cost of using someone else's money**, or the **reward for lending your money**.

> You deposit ₹10,000 in a bank. After 1 year, the bank gives you ₹10,800.
> The extra ₹800 is **interest** — the bank's "rent" for using your money.

### The Key Players

| Term | Meaning | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| **Principal** | The original amount lent/borrowed | P |
| **Rate** | The percentage charged per year | R or r |
| **Time** | Duration of lending/borrowing | T or n |
| **Interest** | The extra amount earned/paid | I (SI or CI) |
| **Amount** | Principal + Interest | A |

## Simple vs Compound Interest — The Core Difference

| Simple Interest (SI) | Compound Interest (CI) |
|---|---|
| Interest is calculated on **original principal** only | Interest is calculated on **principal + accumulated interest** |
| Interest same every year | Interest INCREASES every year |
| Linear growth | Exponential growth |
| Bank rarely uses this | Banks, loans, investments USE this |

### The Story

> **Simple Interest**: You lend ₹1000 at 10%.
> Year 1: Interest = 10% of 1000 = ₹100. Total = ₹1100.
> Year 2: Interest = 10% of **1000** = ₹100. Total = ₹1200.
> Year 3: Interest = 10% of **1000** = ₹100. Total = ₹1300.
> Interest is ALWAYS on ₹1000 (original principal).

> **Compound Interest**: Same ₹1000 at 10%.
> Year 1: Interest = 10% of 1000 = ₹100. Total = ₹1100.
> Year 2: Interest = 10% of **1100** = ₹110. Total = ₹1210.
> Year 3: Interest = 10% of **1210** = ₹121. Total = ₹1331.
> Interest is on the GROWING amount — "interest on interest."

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Real-life relevance** | Loans, FDs, EMIs, investments — everyone uses interest |
| **2–4 direct questions per paper** | SSC, Banking, Railways |
| **CI−SI difference** | A favourite exam pattern |
| **Installments** | Practical and tricky |
| **Connects to Percentage** | Interest rate IS a percentage |
| **Banking exams** | This is the bank's core business! |

# STEP 3 — CORE CONCEPTS (Logical Order)


---

## CONCEPT 1: Simple Interest (SI)

### The Formula

> **SI = P × R × T / 100**
>
> **Amount (A) = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)**

Where:
- P = Principal (original sum)
- R = Rate of interest per annum (%)
- T = Time in years

### Rearrangements

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Principal | P = SI × 100 / (R × T) |
| Rate | R = SI × 100 / (P × T) |
| Time | T = SI × 100 / (P × R) |
| Amount | A = P + PRT/100 = P(1 + RT/100) |

### Example 1

Find SI on ₹5000 at 8% for 3 years.

SI = 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100 = **₹1200**
Amount = 5000 + 1200 = **₹6200**

### Example 2

A sum becomes ₹7500 in 5 years at 10% SI. Find the sum.

A = P(1 + RT/100)
7500 = P(1 + 10×5/100) = P(1.5)
P = 7500/1.5 = **₹5000**

### Key Properties of SI

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Interest is the SAME every year | SI per year = P × R / 100 |
| Growth is LINEAR | Amount grows by a constant each year |
| Interest does NOT compound | No "interest on interest" |
| Graph: Amount vs Time | Straight line |

### SI When Time is in Months/Days

> If time = m months: T = m/12 years
> If time = d days: T = d/365 years

**Example**: SI on ₹2000 at 12% for 9 months
= 2000 × 12 × (9/12) / 100 = 2000 × 12 × 0.75 / 100 = **₹180**

---

## CONCEPT 2: Compound Interest (CI)

### The Formula

> **Amount = P × (1 + R/100)ⁿ**
>
> **CI = Amount − P = P × [(1 + R/100)ⁿ − 1]**

Where n = number of compounding periods (usually years for annual compounding).

### Example 1

Find CI on ₹10000 at 10% for 2 years.

A = 10000 × (1 + 10/100)² = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = ₹12100
CI = 12100 − 10000 = **₹2100**

Compare with SI: 10000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = ₹2000
**CI is ₹100 MORE than SI** (that ₹100 is the "interest on interest").

### Example 2

Find CI on ₹8000 at 5% for 3 years.

A = 8000 × (1.05)³

Calculate (1.05)³:
- 1.05² = 1.1025
- 1.05³ = 1.1025 × 1.05 = 1.157625

A = 8000 × 1.157625 = ₹9261
CI = 9261 − 8000 = **₹1261**

### Key Properties of CI

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Interest INCREASES each year | Because it's calculated on a growing amount |
| Growth is EXPONENTIAL | Amount grows faster and faster |
| "Interest on interest" | This is what makes CI > SI |
| Graph: Amount vs Time | Upward curve (exponential) |

---

## CONCEPT 3: Year-by-Year CI Calculation (Exam Method)

For 2-year and 3-year questions, this method is FASTER than the formula:

### 2-Year CI at Rate R%

```
Year 1 interest = P × R/100
Year 2 interest = P × R/100 + (Year 1 interest) × R/100
                = P × R/100 × (1 + R/100)
```

Total CI for 2 years = P × R/100 + P × R/100 × (1 + R/100)

### Even Faster: Table Method for 2 Years

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Year 1 SI | PR/100 |
| Year 2 SI (same as Year 1) | PR/100 |
| Extra CI (interest on Year 1's interest) | PR/100 × R/100 = PR²/10000 |
| **Total CI** | **2PR/100 + PR²/10000** |

### For 3 Years

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| 3 years' SI | 3PR/100 |
| Extra CI from Year 2 | PR²/10000 |
| Extra CI from Year 3 (on Year 1 + Year 2 interest) | 2PR²/10000 + P×R³/1000000 |
| **Total CI** | **3PR/100 + 3PR²/10000 + PR³/1000000** |

This is actually: P[(1+R/100)³ − 1] expanded.

---

## CONCEPT 4: CI − SI Difference (THE MOST TESTED CONCEPT)

### For 2 Years

> **CI − SI = P × (R/100)²**
>
> **CI − SI = SI × R / (2 × 100)**
>
> (The difference = interest on first year's interest)

### Proof

- SI for 2 years = 2PR/100
- CI for 2 years = 2PR/100 + P(R/100)²
- Difference = **P(R/100)² = PR²/10000**

### Example

P = ₹5000, R = 10%, T = 2 years.

CI − SI = 5000 × (10/100)² = 5000 × 1/100 = **₹50**

Verify: SI = 5000 × 10 × 2/100 = ₹1000
CI = 5000 × (1.1)² − 5000 = 5000 × 1.21 − 5000 = 6050 − 5000 = ₹1050
Difference = ₹50 ✅

### For 3 Years

> **CI − SI = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)**

### Proof

CI − SI = P[(1+R/100)³ − 1] − 3PR/100

Expanding (1+R/100)³ = 1 + 3R/100 + 3(R/100)² + (R/100)³

CI − SI = P[3R/100 + 3(R/100)² + (R/100)³] − 3PR/100
= P × 3(R/100)² + P × (R/100)³
= P(R/100)²[3 + R/100]

### Example

P = ₹10000, R = 5%, T = 3 years.

CI − SI = 10000 × (5/100)² × (3 + 5/100)
= 10000 × 1/400 × (3.05)
= 25 × 3.05 = **₹76.25**

### THE SHORTCUT for 2-Year Problems

> If CI − SI is given, you can find P:
>
> **P = (CI − SI) × (100/R)²**

**Example**: CI − SI for 2 years at 8% = ₹320. Find P.

P = 320 × (100/8)² = 320 × 156.25 = **₹50000**

---

## CONCEPT 5: Half-Yearly Compounding

### When interest is compounded TWICE a year

> **Rate becomes R/2, Time becomes 2n**
>
> **A = P × (1 + R/200)²ⁿ**

### Example

₹10000 at 10% CI compounded half-yearly for 1 year.

A = 10000 × (1 + 10/200)² = 10000 × (1.05)² = 10000 × 1.1025 = **₹11025**

Compare with annual: 10000 × 1.1 = ₹11000. Half-yearly gives ₹25 MORE.

> **More frequent compounding → Higher effective interest.**

---

## CONCEPT 6: Quarterly Compounding

> **Rate becomes R/4, Time becomes 4n**
>
> **A = P × (1 + R/400)⁴ⁿ**

### Example

₹20000 at 8% CI compounded quarterly for 1 year.

A = 20000 × (1 + 8/400)⁴ = 20000 × (1.02)⁴

(1.02)⁴ = 1.08243216

A = 20000 × 1.08243 ≈ **₹21648.64**

---

## CONCEPT 7: Effective Rate of Interest

### When compounding is more frequent than annually

Nominal rate = R% per annum
If compounded m times per year:

> **Effective Rate = (1 + R/(100m))ᵐ − 1 (expressed as decimal)**

### Example

10% compounded half-yearly. Effective rate?

= (1 + 10/200)² − 1 = (1.05)² − 1 = 1.1025 − 1 = 0.1025 = **10.25%**

So 10% compounded half-yearly is equivalent to **10.25% compounded annually**.

---

## CONCEPT 8: Amount Doubling / Tripling

### Under SI

> **Doubling time = 100/R years**

If R = 10%: Time to double = 100/10 = **10 years**

> **n times → Time = (n−1) × 100/R**

Tripling: Time = 2 × 100/R = **200/R years**

### Under CI

> **Doubling: P(1+R/100)ⁿ = 2P → (1+R/100)ⁿ = 2**

No clean formula — use the given relation.

### Common Exam Pattern

"Sum doubles in 5 years under CI. In how many years does it become 8 times?"

If doubles in 5 years: (1+R/100)⁵ = 2
8 = 2³ → [(1+R/100)⁵]³ = 2³ → (1+R/100)¹⁵ = 8

**Answer: 15 years** ✅

### Rule of 72 (Quick Estimation for CI)

> **Approximate doubling time = 72 / R**

At 12%: doubling time ≈ 72/12 = **6 years**

This is an approximation, not exact, but useful for quick checks.

---

## CONCEPT 9: Installments

### Equal Annual Installments Under CI

If a person borrows P at R% CI and pays back in n equal annual installments of ₹x:

> **P = x/(1+R/100) + x/(1+R/100)² + … + x/(1+R/100)ⁿ**

This is a geometric series.

### For 2 Installments (EXAM SHORTCUT)

> **P = x/(1+R/100) + x/(1+R/100)²**

### Example

A person borrows ₹6300 at 10% CI and pays back in 2 equal annual installments. Find installment.

6300 = x/1.1 + x/1.1²
6300 = x/1.1 + x/1.21
6300 = x(1/1.1 + 1/1.21)
6300 = x(1.21 + 1.1)/(1.1 × 1.21)
6300 = x × 2.31/1.331
x = 6300 × 1.331/2.31 = 6300 × 1331/2310 = **₹3630**

### Equal Annual Installments Under SI

If each installment is ₹x, paid annually for n years at R% SI:

The last installment earns no interest.
Second-last earns 1 year's interest.
…and so on.

> **P = x + x(1 + R/100) + x(1 + 2R/100) + … + x(1 + (n−1)R/100)** (order reversed)

Actually, the standard approach:

> **P × (1 + nR/100) = x × n + x × R/100 × n(n−1)/2**

## CONCEPT 10: SI and CI on Same Sum — Finding Unknowns

### Pattern 1: Given SI and CI for same P, R, T

"SI on a sum for 2 years at 10% = ₹600. CI on same sum for same period = ₹630."

SI = 600 → P × 10 × 2/100 = 600 → P = ₹3000
CI − SI = 630 − 600 = ₹30

Verify: P(R/100)² = 3000 × (0.1)² = 3000 × 0.01 = ₹30 ✅

### Pattern 2: Ratio of CI to SI

For 2 years at R%:

> **CI/SI = [2 + R/100] / 2 = 1 + R/200**

Proof: CI = 2PR/100 + P(R/100)², SI = 2PR/100
CI/SI = 1 + (R/100)/2 × [P(R/100)] / [P(R/100)] = 1 + R/200

### Pattern 3: Interest doubles/triples the sum

"At what rate does ₹P become 3P in 8 years at SI?"

3P = P(1 + R×8/100) → 3 = 1 + 8R/100 → 8R/100 = 2 → R = **25%**

---

## CONCEPT 11: Different Rates for Different Years (CI)

### When rate is R₁% for year 1, R₂% for year 2, R₃% for year 3:

> **A = P × (1 + R₁/100) × (1 + R₂/100) × (1 + R₃/100)**

### Example

₹5000 at CI: 10% for year 1, 12% for year 2.

A = 5000 × 1.10 × 1.12 = 5000 × 1.232 = **₹6160**
CI = 6160 − 5000 = **₹1160**

---

## CONCEPT 12: CI Applied to Population & Depreciation

### Population Growth (same as CI)

> **P(after n years) = P₀ × (1 + R/100)ⁿ**

### Depreciation (same as CI with negative rate)

> **V(after n years) = V₀ × (1 − R/100)ⁿ**

These are the SAME formulas from Percentage — now you see how everything connects.

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: CI for 2 Years Using SI

> **CI for 2 years = SI for 2 years + SI on 1 year's SI**

**Example**: P=₹5000, R=10%.
- SI for 2 years = 5000 × 10 × 2/100 = ₹1000
- SI on 1 year's SI = 500 × 10/100 = ₹50
- CI for 2 years = 1000 + 50 = **₹1050**

---

## Shortcut 2: CI for 3 Years Using SI

> **CI for 3 years = SI for 3 years + 3 × (SI for 1 year × R/100) + SI for 1 year × (R/100)²**

Or more simply: use the year-by-year method:

Year 1 interest = PR/100
Year 2 interest = PR/100 + (PR/100)(R/100) = PR/100 × (1 + R/100)
Year 3 interest = PR/100 × (1 + R/100)²

---

## Shortcut 3: Finding P from CI−SI

> **For 2 years: P = (CI−SI) / (R/100)²**
>
> **For 2 years: P = (CI−SI) × (100/R)²**

**Example**: CI−SI = ₹40, R = 20%. Find P.

P = 40 × (100/20)² = 40 × 25 = **₹1000**

---

## Shortcut 4: When Sum Doubles Under SI

> Doubles in n years → Rate = 100/n %
> Triples in m years → Rate = 200/m %
> Becomes k times → Rate = (k−1)×100/n %

---

## Shortcut 5: CI When Rate is a "Nice" Fraction

| Rate | (1 + R/100) | As fraction |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 1.05 | 21/20 |
| 10% | 1.1 | 11/10 |
| 12.5% | 1.125 | 9/8 |
| 20% | 1.2 | 6/5 |
| 25% | 1.25 | 5/4 |
| 33.33% | 1.333 | 4/3 |
| 50% | 1.5 | 3/2 |

Use fractions for clean mental calculation:

**Example**: CI on ₹1600 at 25% for 2 years.
A = 1600 × (5/4)² = 1600 × 25/16 = **₹2500**
CI = 2500 − 1600 = **₹900**

---

## Shortcut 6: Common Powers to Memorize

| Value | Result |
|---|---|
| (1.1)² | 1.21 |
| (1.1)³ | 1.331 |
| (1.05)² | 1.1025 |
| (1.05)³ | 1.157625 |

| (1.2)³ | 1.728 |

## Shortcut 7: Doubling Under CI — Power Pattern

If sum doubles in n years at CI:
- 4 times in **2n** years (² = 4)
- 8 times in **3n** years (2³ = 8)
- 16 times in **4n** years
- k times: solve 2^(m/n) = k

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: SI vs CI — When to Use Which

| Clue in question | Use |
|---|---|
| "Simple interest" explicitly | SI |
| "Compound interest" explicitly | CI |
| "Compounded annually/half-yearly/quarterly" | CI |
| "Interest is calculated annually on original" | SI |
| Just "interest" without specification | Usually SI in SSC, CI in Banking |
| FD, loan, EMI | Almost always CI |

---

## Confusion 2: CI−SI ≠ 0 Even for 1 Year!

Wait — actually, for 1 year:
- SI = PR/100
- CI (annual) = PR/100

**For 1 year, CI = SI** (no compounding has occurred yet).

The difference only appears from **year 2 onwards**.

---

## Confusion 3: Half-Yearly vs Annual

| | Annual | Half-Yearly | Quarterly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate per period | R | R/2 | R/4 |
| Number of periods | n | 2n | 4n |
| CI result | Lower | Higher | Even higher |

> **More frequent compounding = Higher CI** (for same nominal rate)

---

## Confusion 4: Amount vs CI

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Amount (A)** | P + CI = total money you get back |
| **CI** | Only the interest part = A − P |

Students sometimes give A when asked for CI, or vice versa. Read carefully!

---

## Confusion 5: "Per Annum" vs "For the Period"

"10% per annum for 6 months" → SI = P × 10 × (6/12) / 100 = PR/200

"10% for 6 months" (not per annum) → SI = P × 10 × (6) / 100 = different!

Usually exams mean "per annum." But read carefully.

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


| # | Trap | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CI − SI for 1 year = 0** | Difference exists only from 2 years onwards |
| 2 | **Half-yearly: forgetting to halve rate AND double time** | Do BOTH: R→R/2, T→2T |
| 3 | **Amount given, asked for CI (or vice versa)** | CI = Amount − P. Read what's asked! |
| 4 | **Time in months but formula needs years** | Convert: 18 months = 1.5 years |
| 5 | **"Doubles in 5 years CI" → students use SI formula** | CI doubling: (1+R/100)ⁿ = 2, not 100/R |
| 6 | **CI−SI formula for 3 years: students use 2-year formula** | 3-year formula is different: P(R/100)²(3+R/100) |
| 7 | **Installment: forgetting that each installment covers interest TOO** | Each payment covers part principal + part interest |
| 8 | **Different rates for different years** | Multiply multipliers: (1+R₁/100)(1+R₂/100)… |
| 9 | **Confusing nominal rate with effective rate** | 10% half-yearly = 10.25% effective annually |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


## Speed Rule 1: Use Fraction Multipliers for CI

10% → ×11/10. For 2 years: ×121/100. For 3 years: ×1331/1000.
25% → ×5/4. For 2 years: ×25/16. For 3 years: ×125/64.

These give exact answers without decimal calculation.

## Speed Rule 2: Memorize Common Powers

(1.1)² = 1.21, (1.1)³ = 1.331
(1.05)² = 1.1025
(1.2)² = 1.44, (1.2)³ = 1.728

## Speed Rule 3: CI−SI = "Interest on First Year's Interest" (for 2 years)

For 2 years, CI−SI = (first year's SI) × R/100

This is the fastest mental calculation.

**Example**: P = ₹8000, R = 5%, T = 2 years.
First year's SI = 8000 × 5/100 = ₹400.
CI−SI = 400 × 5/100 = **₹20**

## Speed Rule 4: Doubling Under SI — Instant Answer

Sum doubles at SI → T = 100/R.
Sum triples → T = 200/R.
Sum becomes n times → T = (n−1) × 100/R.

# STEP 11 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES


## Mnemonic 1: SI Formula

> **"PRT over 100"** — say it fast: "P-R-T-hundredth"
> Or: **"Principal × Rate × Time, divided by a century"**

## Mnemonic 2: CI−SI for 2 Years

> **"Interest on First Year's Interest"**
>
> CI earns extra because Year 2's interest includes interest on Year 1's interest.
> That "extra bit" = First year's interest × R/100.

## Mnemonic 3: Half-Yearly Compounding

> **"Halve the Rate, Double the Time"** — HRDT
>
> Think: "Half Rate, Double Time — like a slow worker doing overtime"

## Mnemonic 4: Quarterly Compounding

> **"Quarter the Rate, Quadruple the Time"** — QRQT

## Mnemonic 5: Doubling Under CI

> **"Doubles in n years → 2× in n, 4× in 2n, 8× in 3n"**
>
> Each "doubling period" doubles whatever you have.

## Story Trick: Why CI > SI

> Imagine planting a tree (P = seed).
> **SI**: Every year, you plant the SAME number of new seeds. Growth is steady.
> **CI**: Every year, ALL your trees produce seeds, and those seeds ALSO become trees. Growth explodes.
> After many years, CI's "forest" is MUCH larger than SI's "garden."

**Q7.** A sum becomes ₹7200 in 2 years at 20% SI. Find P.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
7200 = P(1 + 20×2/100) = 1.4P → P = 7200/1.4 = **₹5142.86** ≈ **₹5143**. For clean answer: if A = P + PRT/100, P = 7200 × 100/140 = **₹5142.86**.

# STEP 14 — REVISION PACK


```
SI & CI — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

SI = PRT/100. Amount = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100). LINEAR growth.
CI: Amount = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ. CI = Amount − P. EXPONENTIAL growth.

CI − SI (2 YEARS) = P(R/100)² = "Interest on 1st year's interest"
CI − SI (3 YEARS) = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100)

FIND P FROM CI−SI: P = (CI−SI) × (100/R)²  [for 2 years]

HALF-YEARLY: Rate → R/2, Time → 2n. A = P(1+R/200)²ⁿ
QUARTERLY: Rate → R/4, Time → 4n. A = P(1+R/400)⁴ⁿ

DOUBLING:
  SI: T = 100/R.  Tripling: T = 200/R.
  CI: 2× in n yrs → 4× in 2n → 8× in 3n → 2ᵏ× in kn yrs.
  Rule of 72: T ≈ 72/R (CI approximation).

VARIABLE RATES: A = P(1+R₁/100)(1+R₂/100)...

KEY FRACTIONS: 10%=11/10, 20%=6/5, 25%=5/4, 50%=3/2

CI > SI always (for n ≥ 2). Difference appears from Year 2.
For 1 year (annual): CI = SI.
```

## 🚨 Trap Sheet

| Trap | Defense |
|---|---|
| CI − SI for 1 year = 0 | Difference starts from Year 2 |
| Half-yearly: only halve rate | Must ALSO double time |
| Amount vs CI confusion | CI = Amount − P |
| SI doubling formula used for CI | Different formulas! |
| Time in months | Convert to years |
| "Doubles in n years CI" → "4× in 2n, 8× in 3n" | Use power logic |
