# Physical Geography — Solar System to Plate Tectonics

## 1. Overview
Physical Geography covers the fundamentals of Earth's place in the solar system, its internal structure, rock types, and the dynamic processes (earthquakes, volcanoes, plate tectonics) that shape the planet. Exams test this as pure science-geography overlap — expect direct factual recall plus cause-effect reasoning questions.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Solar System**
- Sun is a star at the center; 8 planets orbit it (Pluto reclassified as dwarf planet in 2006 by IAU) **[High]**
- Order from Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune **[High]**
- Mercury — smallest, closest to Sun, no atmosphere **[Medium]**
- Venus — hottest planet (greenhouse effect), rotates opposite direction (retrograde), called "Earth's twin" (size) and "Morning/Evening Star" **[High]**
- Earth — only known planet with life, 71% water surface, one natural satellite (Moon) **[High]**
- Mars — "Red Planet" (iron oxide), two moons (Phobos, Deimos) **[High]**
- Jupiter — largest planet, Great Red Spot (storm), most moons **[High]**
- Saturn — prominent ring system, second largest **[High]**
- Uranus — rotates on its side (~98° tilt), ice giant **[Medium]**
- Neptune — farthest planet, windiest **[Medium]**
- Asteroid Belt — between Mars and Jupiter **[Medium]**
- Comets — icy bodies with tails pointing away from Sun (solar wind pressure); Halley's Comet visible every ~76 years **[Medium]**

**Earth's Interior**
- Three main layers: Crust, Mantle, Core **[High]**
- Crust — thinnest layer, oceanic crust (denser, basaltic, SIMA) vs continental crust (lighter, granitic, SIAL) **[High]**
- Mantle — largest layer by volume, divided into upper (asthenosphere - semi-molten, source of magma) and lower mantle **[High]**
- Core — divided into outer core (liquid, iron-nickel, generates Earth's magnetic field) and inner core (solid, due to immense pressure despite highest temperature) **[High]**
- Discontinuities (boundaries): Mohorovicic (Moho) = crust-mantle; Gutenberg = mantle-outer core; Lehmann = outer-inner core **[Medium]**
- Temperature increases with depth (geothermal gradient); pressure also increases **[Medium]**

**Rocks**
- Three types: Igneous, Sedimentary, Metamorphic — form the "Rock Cycle" **[High]**
- Igneous Rocks — formed from cooling magma/lava; Intrusive (cools inside Earth, e.g., Granite) vs Extrusive (cools on surface, e.g., Basalt) **[High]**
- Sedimentary Rocks — formed by deposition/compaction of sediments over time; e.g., Sandstone, Limestone, Shale; often contain fossils **[High]**
- Metamorphic Rocks — formed by heat/pressure transforming existing rocks; e.g., Marble (from Limestone), Slate (from Shale), Quartzite (from Sandstone) **[High]**
- Rock cycle: Igneous ⇌ Sedimentary ⇌ Metamorphic, continuous transformation via weathering, heat, pressure, melting **[Medium]**

**Earthquakes**
- Caused by sudden release of energy from tectonic plate movement along faults **[High]**
- Focus (Hypocenter) = point inside Earth where earthquake originates; Epicenter = point directly above focus on surface **[High]**
- Measured using Richter Scale (magnitude, energy released) and Mercalli Scale (intensity, observed damage) **[High]**
- Seismograph/Seismometer — instrument to detect and record earthquakes **[Medium]**
- Seismic waves: P-waves (Primary, fastest, travel through solids & liquids), S-waves (Secondary, slower, only through solids), Surface waves (slowest, most destructive) **[High]**
- "Ring of Fire" — Pacific Ocean rim, zone of highest earthquake/volcano activity **[High]**

**Volcanoes**
- Opening in Earth's crust through which magma, gas, ash escape **[High]**
- Magma (below surface) becomes Lava (once erupted on surface) **[High]**
- Types by activity: Active (currently/recently erupting), Dormant (inactive but may erupt again), Extinct (no eruption expected) **[High]**
- Types by shape: Shield volcano (broad, gentle slopes, e.g., Hawaiian), Composite/Stratovolcano (steep, alternating layers, explosive, e.g., Mt. Fuji), Cinder Cone (small, steep) **[Medium]**
- Caldera — large depression formed after a massive eruption/collapse **[Low]**
- Most volcanoes located along the Pacific "Ring of Fire" and mid-ocean ridges **[Medium]**
- Barren Island — India's only active volcano (Andaman & Nicobar Islands) **[High]**

**Plate Tectonics**
- Earth's lithosphere is broken into several rigid plates that float on the semi-molten asthenosphere **[High]**
- Major plates: Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic **[High]**
- Plate boundary types: Convergent (plates collide — creates mountains/subduction zones, e.g., Himalayas), Divergent (plates move apart — creates rift valleys/mid-ocean ridges), Transform (plates slide past each other — causes earthquakes, e.g., San Andreas Fault) **[High]**
- Continental Drift Theory — proposed by Alfred Wegener (1912); supercontinent "Pangaea" broke apart **[High]**
- Seafloor spreading — new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges, evidence supporting plate tectonics **[Medium]**
- Himalayas formed by convergent collision of Indian Plate and Eurasian Plate (ongoing, India still moving north) **[High]**

|---|---|---|

| Mantle | Semi-molten (asthenosphere) to solid | Largest by volume, source of magma |

| Inner Core | Solid | Highest temperature, solid due to pressure |

|---|---|---|
| Igneous | Cooling of magma/lava | Granite, Basalt |

|---|---|---|

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Focus vs Epicenter | Focus = point INSIDE Earth where quake starts; Epicenter = point directly ABOVE it on the surface |
| Richter Scale vs Mercalli Scale | Richter = measures MAGNITUDE (energy released, numeric); Mercalli = measures INTENSITY (observed damage/impact, descriptive) |
| P-waves vs S-waves | P-waves = fastest, travel through solids AND liquids; S-waves = slower, travel ONLY through solids |
| Magma vs Lava | Magma = molten rock BELOW the surface; Lava = same material AFTER it erupts onto the surface |
| Active vs Dormant vs Extinct volcano | Active = erupting/recently erupted; Dormant = inactive now but could erupt again; Extinct = not expected to erupt ever again |
| Oceanic vs Continental crust | Oceanic = thinner, denser, basaltic (SIMA); Continental = thicker, lighter, granitic (SIAL) |
| Convergent vs Divergent boundary | Convergent = plates COLLIDE (mountains form); Divergent = plates SEPARATE (new crust/rift valleys form) |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Planet order from Sun** — "**M**y **V**ery **E**ducated **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**achos" → **M**ercury, **V**enus, **E**arth, **M**ars, **J**upiter, **S**aturn, **U**ranus, **N**eptune.
- **Earth's layers (outside to inside)** — "**C**rust **M**akes **O**range **I**nside" → **C**rust → **M**antle → **O**uter Core → **I**nner Core.
- **Rock cycle** — picture a triangle: Igneous (top, cools from magma) → Sedimentary (deposits/compacts) → Metamorphic (heat/pressure transforms) → melts back to Igneous. Visualizing the closed triangle helps recall it's a CYCLE, not a line.
- **P-wave vs S-wave speed** — "**P** for **P**rimary/**P**owerful/fastest, **S** for **S**econdary/**S**lower" — alphabetical order (P before S) matches arrival order (P arrives first).
- **Seismic wave destructiveness** — Surface waves arrive LAST but cause the MOST damage — remember "last but worst."

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- Swapping **Focus and Epicenter** definitions in "which of the following is correct" questions.
- Confusing **Richter (magnitude) vs Mercalli (intensity)** — a very common trap in earthquake-measurement questions.
- **Magma vs Lava** — used interchangeably by mistake in wrong options; magma is always BELOW surface.
- Mixing up **which boundary type creates which landform** (convergent=mountains vs divergent=rift valleys) — a frequent pairing trap.
- Claiming India has "no active volcano" — Barren Island (Andaman & Nicobar) IS active and is a favorite exception fact examiners test.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Solar System: 8 planets, Mercury→Neptune order; Venus hottest, Jupiter largest, Mars=Red Planet.
- Earth's interior: Crust→Mantle→Outer Core (liquid)→Inner Core (solid); Moho/Gutenberg/Lehmann discontinuities.
- Rocks: Igneous (cooled magma) → Sedimentary (deposited) → Metamorphic (heat/pressure) → cycle repeats.
- Earthquakes: Focus (inside) vs Epicenter (surface); Richter=magnitude, Mercalli=intensity; P-waves fastest, Surface waves most destructive.
- Volcanoes: Magma (below) becomes Lava (above); Active/Dormant/Extinct; Barren Island = India's only active volcano.
- Plate Tectonics: Convergent (collide, mountains), Divergent (separate, rift), Transform (slide, earthquakes); Wegener's Continental Drift Theory (Pangaea); Himalayas from Indian-Eurasian plate collision.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which planet is known as the "Red Planet"?
A) Venus B) Mars C) Jupiter D) Mercury

**Q2.** The layer of Earth that generates the planet's magnetic field is the:
A) Crust B) Mantle C) Outer Core D) Inner Core

**Q3.** Marble is a metamorphic rock formed from which sedimentary rock?
A) Sandstone B) Shale C) Limestone D) Basalt

**Q4.** Which scale measures the intensity (observed damage) of an earthquake rather than its magnitude?
A) Richter Scale B) Mercalli Scale C) Beaufort Scale D) Kelvin Scale

**Q5.** India's only active volcano, Barren Island, is located in:
A) Lakshadweep B) Andaman & Nicobar Islands C) Gulf of Kutch D) Sundarbans

## Answer Key
1. **B) Mars** — Called the "Red Planet" due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface.
2. **C) Outer Core** — The liquid iron-nickel outer core's movement generates Earth's magnetic field.
3. **C) Limestone** — Marble forms from the metamorphism of Limestone under heat and pressure.
4. **B) Mercalli Scale** — Measures intensity/observed damage, unlike the Richter Scale which measures magnitude/energy.
5. **B) Andaman & Nicobar Islands** — Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located here.
