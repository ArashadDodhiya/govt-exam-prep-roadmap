# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Ratio?

A **ratio** is a way of comparing two quantities **of the same kind** by division.

When you say "the ratio of boys to girls in a class is 3:2", you mean:
> For every 3 boys, there are 2 girls.
> Boys are 3 PARTS, Girls are 2 PARTS.
> Total = 5 PARTS.

A ratio is NOT the actual count. It tells you the **relative size**.

> Think of it as a **recipe**:
> "Mix sugar and water in the ratio 1:4" means for every 1 spoon of sugar, use 4 spoons of water.
> Whether you make 1 glass or 100 glasses, the RATIO stays 1:4.

## What is Proportion?

**Proportion** says that two ratios are **equal**.

> If a/b = c/d, then a, b, c, d are in proportion.
> Written as: a : b :: c : d (read as "a is to b as c is to d")

Proportion is the mathematical way of saying: **"These two comparisons are the same."**

## Where is it used?

| Application | How |
|---|---|
| **Mixture & Alligation** | Mixing two things in a ratio → needs Ratio |
| **Partnership** | Sharing profit in ratio of investment → needs Ratio |
| **Ages** | "Father is 3 times as old as son" → ratio = 3:1 |
| **Maps & Scale** | 1 cm = 10 km → ratio = 1:1000000 |
| **Time & Work** | Efficiency ratio → faster worker does more |
| **Sharing money/items** | Dividing ₹5000 among 3 people in a ratio |
| **Speed-Time-Distance** | If speed ratio is 3:4, time ratio is 4:3 |

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Foundation for 6+ topics** | Mixture, Partnership, Ages, T&W, TSD, P&L all use ratio |
| **Direct questions** | 2–4 direct ratio questions per paper |
| **Tests logical thinking** | Not just formula — tests whether you understand relationships |
| **Versatile question types** | Combine with percentage, ages, money — infinite possibilities |
| **Appears in EVERY exam** | SSC, Banking, Railways, UPSC — always 2–5 questions |

> **Ratio is the LANGUAGE of comparison. If you can't speak it fluently, you can't solve half the syllabus.**

## CONCEPT 1: Simple Ratio — The Basics

### Definition

> A **ratio** a:b means a/b, where b ≠ 0.
>
> "a" is called the **antecedent** (first term).
> "b" is called the **consequent** (second term).

### Key Properties

| Property | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A ratio has no unit | It's a pure comparison | 5 kg : 10 kg = 1 : 2 (kg cancels) |
| Must compare same units | Convert first if different | 2 km : 500 m = 2000 m : 500 m = 4 : 1 |
| Multiply/Divide both terms equally → ratio unchanged | a:b = ka:kb | 3:5 = 6:10 = 9:15 |
| Order matters | a:b ≠ b:a (unless a=b) | 3:5 ≠ 5:3 |
| Simplest form | Divide both by HCF | 12:18 → HCF=6 → 2:3 |

### Converting Ratio to Fraction and Vice Versa

| Ratio | As Fraction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| a : b | a/b | a is a/b times of b |
| 3 : 5 | 3/5 | First is 3/5 of second |
| 2 : 3 : 5 | 2/(2+3+5), 3/(2+3+5), 5/(2+3+5) | Parts are 2/10, 3/10, 5/10 of total |

### Common Mistake

❌ "Ratio of A to B is 3:5" → Students think A = 3 and B = 5.
✅ A = 3k and B = 5k for some constant k. The ACTUAL values depend on k.

> **The "k" (common multiplier) is the most important concept in Ratio. Never forget it.**

---

## CONCEPT 2: The k-Method (Common Multiplier)

### What is it?

When a ratio is a:b, the actual quantities are **ak** and **bk**, where k is some positive number.

### Why is it the MOST POWERFUL technique?

Because it converts ratio problems into simple algebra.

### Example 1

"The ratio of A to B is 3:5 and their sum is 40. Find A and B."

- Let A = 3k, B = 5k
- 3k + 5k = 40 → 8k = 40 → k = 5
- A = 15, B = 25

### Example 2

"Ratio of two numbers is 7:3 and their difference is 36. Find the numbers."

- Let numbers = 7k and 3k
- 7k − 3k = 36 → 4k = 36 → k = 9
- Numbers = 63 and 27

### Example 3

"A's salary is 2/3 of B's salary. If A gets ₹4000 more, the new ratio is 6:7. Find original salaries."

- Original: A = 2k, B = 3k
- (2k + 4000) / 3k = 6/7
- 7(2k + 4000) = 6 × 3k
- 14k + 28000 = 18k
- 4k = 28000 → k = 7000
- A = ₹14000, B = ₹21000

> **The k-method is the SWISS ARMY KNIFE of ratio problems. Use it for 80% of questions.**

## CONCEPT 3: Dividing a Quantity in a Given Ratio

### Formula

If quantity Q is divided in ratio a : b : c, then:

> **First part = Q × a/(a+b+c)**
> **Second part = Q × b/(a+b+c)**
> **Third part = Q × c/(a+b+c)**

### Example

Divide ₹2100 among A, B, C in ratio 3 : 5 : 7.

- Total parts = 3 + 5 + 7 = 15
- A = 2100 × 3/15 = 2100 × 1/5 = **₹420**
- B = 2100 × 5/15 = 2100 × 1/3 = **₹700**
- C = 2100 × 7/15 = **₹980**
- Check: 420 + 700 + 980 = 2100 ✅

### Shortcut

> **Each person's share = (Their parts / Total parts) × Total amount**

### Common Exam Variation

"A, B, C share ₹3600 such that A gets twice as much as B, and B gets thrice as much as C."

Translation:
- B = 3C → B:C = 3:1
- A = 2B = 6C → A:B:C = 6:3:1
- Total = 10 parts
- A = 3600 × 6/10 = **₹2160**, B = **₹1080**, C = **₹360**

## CONCEPT 4: Comparison of Ratios

### Method 1: Cross Multiplication

Compare a:b and c:d → Compare a×d with b×c

If a×d > b×c → a/b > c/d → a:b > c:d

### Method 2: Make Consequents Equal

Convert both ratios so the second term is the same (LCM).

### Method 3: Convert to Decimal/Percentage

3:7 = 0.4286, 5:11 = 0.4545 → 5:11 > 3:7

### Example

Arrange in ascending order: 2:3, 4:7, 5:8

- 2/3 = 0.667
- 4/7 = 0.571
- 5/8 = 0.625

Ascending: 4:7 < 5:8 < 2:3

## CONCEPT 5: Types of Ratios

### Compound Ratio

The **compound ratio** of a:b and c:d is **ac : bd** (multiply corresponding terms).

> **Compound ratio = Product of antecedents : Product of consequents**

**Example**: Compound ratio of 2:3 and 4:5 = (2×4) : (3×5) = **8:15**

**Example**: Compound ratio of 3:4, 5:7, and 2:3 = (3×5×2) : (4×7×3) = 30 : 84 = **5:14**

### When is Compound Ratio used?

When a quantity depends on TWO or more factors.

**Example**: "A does 2/3 of the work in 3/4 of the time that B takes. What is the ratio of their efficiencies?"

Work ratio = 2:3 (not actually, this is a simplification)
Time ratio = 3:4

Efficiency = Work/Time → compound of work and inverse of time ratios.

### Duplicate Ratio

> Duplicate ratio of a:b = **a² : b²** (square both terms)

**Example**: Duplicate ratio of 3:5 = 9:25

### Triplicate Ratio

> Triplicate ratio of a:b = **a³ : b³** (cube both terms)

**Example**: Triplicate ratio of 2:3 = 8:27

### Sub-duplicate Ratio

> Sub-duplicate ratio of a:b = **√a : √b**

**Example**: Sub-duplicate ratio of 16:25 = 4:5

### Sub-triplicate Ratio

> Sub-triplicate ratio of a:b = **³√a : ³√b**

**Example**: Sub-triplicate ratio of 8:27 = 2:3

### Inverse / Reciprocal Ratio

> Inverse ratio of a:b = **b:a** (or 1/a : 1/b)

**Example**: Inverse ratio of 3:7 = 7:3

### Exam Importance

| Type | SSC Frequency | How it's asked |
|---|---|---|
| Compound Ratio | ★★★★ | "Find compound ratio of 2:3 and 5:7" |
| Duplicate | ★★★ | "Find duplicate ratio of 4:5" |
| Sub-duplicate | ★★★ | "Sub-duplicate ratio of 49:81" |
| Triplicate | ★★ | Rare — mostly SSC Tier-2 |
| Inverse | ★★★★ | Implicit in speed-time, work problems |

## CONCEPT 6: Proportion

### Definition

> **Four numbers a, b, c, d are in proportion if a/b = c/d**
>
> Written as: **a : b :: c : d** (read: "a is to b as c is to d")

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition | In a:b::c:d |
|---|---|---|
| **Extremes** | First and last terms | a and d |
| **Means** | Middle two terms | b and c |
| **Product rule** | Product of extremes = Product of means | a × d = b × c |

### This is the MOST IMPORTANT property:

> **a × d = b × c**
>
> (Product of extremes = Product of means)

### Example

Are 3, 4, 6, 8 in proportion?
- Product of extremes: 3 × 8 = 24
- Product of means: 4 × 6 = 24
- Equal → **YES, they are in proportion** ✅

---

## CONCEPT 7: Types of Proportional Relationships

### Direct Proportion

> If A increases → B increases (at the same rate), then A ∝ B (A is directly proportional to B).
>
> **A/B = constant**
>
> **A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂**

**Real-life examples**:
- More items → more cost (same price per item)
- More workers → more work done (same efficiency)
- More speed → more distance (same time)

**Example**: If 5 pens cost ₹75, how much do 12 pens cost?
- 5/75 = 12/x → x = 12 × 75/5 = **₹180**

### Inverse Proportion

> If A increases → B decreases (at the same rate), then A ∝ 1/B.
>
> **A × B = constant**
>
> **A₁ × B₁ = A₂ × B₂**

**Real-life examples**:
- More workers → less time (same work)
- More speed → less time (same distance)
- More price → less consumption (same expenditure)

**Example**: If 6 workers can finish a job in 10 days, how many days will 15 workers take?
- 6 × 10 = 15 × x → x = 60/15 = **4 days**

### How to Identify: Direct or Inverse?

Ask yourself:
> **"If one increases, does the other ALSO increase?"**
> YES → Direct Proportion
> NO (other decreases) → Inverse Proportion

| Situation | Type |
|---|---|
| More items → More cost | Direct |
| More men → Less time | Inverse |
| More speed → Less time | Inverse |
| More distance → More time | Direct |
| More price → Less consumption | Inverse |
| More hours → More work | Direct |
| More pipes → Less time to fill | Inverse |

## CONCEPT 8: Third Proportional and Fourth Proportional

### Fourth Proportional

If a : b :: c : x, find x.

> **x = (b × c) / a**

**Example**: Fourth proportional to 3, 7, 9 = ?
- 3:7 :: 9:x → x = 7×9/3 = **21**

### Third Proportional

If a : b :: b : x, find x.

> **x = b²/a**

**Example**: Third proportional to 4, 12 = ?
- 4:12 :: 12:x → x = 12²/4 = 144/4 = **36**

### Mean Proportional (Geometric Mean)

If a : x :: x : b, find x.

> **x = √(a × b)**

**Example**: Mean proportional between 4 and 9 = ?
- x = √(4 × 9) = √36 = **6**

**Verification**: 4:6 :: 6:9 → 4/6 = 2/3, 6/9 = 2/3 ✅

### Exam Frequency

| Type | SSC | Banking |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth Proportional | ★★★★ | ★★★ |
| Third Proportional | ★★★★ | ★★ |
| Mean Proportional | ★★★★★ | ★★★ |

## CONCEPT 9: Continued Proportion

### Definition

Three numbers a, b, c are in **continued proportion** if:

> **a/b = b/c**
> **i.e., b² = a × c**

b is the **mean proportional** between a and c.

### Example

Are 2, 6, 18 in continued proportion?
- 2/6 = 1/3, 6/18 = 1/3
- Equal → **YES** ✅
- Check: b² = 36, a×c = 2×18 = 36 ✅

### Extended Continued Proportion

a, b, c, d are in continued proportion if:
> a/b = b/c = c/d

This means: b² = ac, c² = bd, and b³ = a²d (can be derived).

## CONCEPT 10: Componendo and Dividendo

### This is one of the MOST POWERFUL exam shortcuts. Learn it cold.

### The Theorem

If **a/b = c/d**, then:

> **Componendo**: (a+b)/b = (c+d)/d
>
> **Dividendo**: (a−b)/b = (c−d)/d
>
> **Componendo-Dividendo**: **(a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)**

### Why It's Powerful

It converts complex expressions into simple ones in ONE step.

### Example 1 (Classic SSC pattern)

If (x+y)/(x−y) = 7/3, find x:y.

Using Componendo-Dividendo **in reverse**:
- (x+y)/(x−y) = 7/3

Apply Componendo-Dividendo:
- [(x+y)+(x−y)] / [(x+y)−(x−y)] = (7+3)/(7−3)
- 2x / 2y = 10/4
- x/y = 10/4 = **5/2**
- **x : y = 5 : 2** ✅

### Example 2

If (√x + √y)/(√x − √y) = 5/3, find x:y.

Apply Componendo-Dividendo:
- [2√x] / [2√y] = (5+3)/(5−3) = 8/2 = 4
- √x/√y = 4/1
- x/y = 16/1
- **x : y = 16 : 1** ✅

### Example 3

If a/b = 3/4, find (3a + 4b)/(3a − 4b).

Let a = 3k, b = 4k:
- 3a + 4b = 9k + 16k = 25k
- 3a − 4b = 9k − 16k = −7k
- Answer = 25k/(−7k) = **−25/7**

### The General Pattern

If **a/b = c/d = k**, then:

> **(pa + qb)/(ra + sb) = (pc + qd)/(rc + sd)**

This works because a = kb → everything factors out.

### Exam Shortcut: k-substitution

Instead of Componendo-Dividendo, you can also substitute a/b = given ratio directly.

If a/b = 3/5, find (2a+3b)/(4a−b):
- Let a = 3, b = 5 (just substitute ratio values directly!)
- (6+15)/(12−5) = 21/7 = **3**

> **This direct substitution is often FASTER than Componendo-Dividendo.**

## CONCEPT 11: Combining Ratios (Bridging Ratios)

### The Problem

You know A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Find A:B:C.

### The Method

Make the COMMON term (B) the SAME in both ratios.

- A:B = 2:3 → B = 3
- B:C = 4:5 → B = 4
- LCM of 3 and 4 = 12
- A:B = 2:3 = 8:12 (multiply by 4)
- B:C = 4:5 = 12:15 (multiply by 3)
- **A:B:C = 8:12:15**

### General Formula

If A:B = a:b and B:C = c:d, then:

> **A : B : C = ac : bc : bd**

**Example**: A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5
- A:B:C = 2×4 : 3×4 : 3×5 = 8:12:15 ✅

### Three-Ratio Combination

If A:B = 2:3, B:C = 5:4, C:D = 2:3, find A:B:C:D

**Step 1**: Combine A:B:C first
- A:B = 2:3, B:C = 5:4
- A:B:C = 2×5 : 3×5 : 3×4 = 10:15:12

**Step 2**: Combine with C:D
- Current C = 12, new C:D = 2:3
- LCM of 12 and 2 = 12
- C:D = 2:3 = 12:18 (multiply by 6)
- **A:B:C:D = 10:15:12:18**

Simplify by dividing by HCF if possible. HCF(10,15,12,18) = 1. So already simplest.

## CONCEPT 12: Income, Expenditure, Savings Ratio Problems

### The Relationship

> **Income = Expenditure + Savings**
> **Savings = Income − Expenditure**

### Standard Pattern

"Income ratio of A and B is 5:4. Expenditure ratio is 3:2. If each saves ₹1000, find incomes."

**Solution**:
- Income: A = 5k, B = 4k
- Expenditure: A = 3m, B = 2m
- Savings: A → 5k − 3m = 1000, B → 4k − 2m = 1000
- Equation 1: 5k − 3m = 1000
- Equation 2: 4k − 2m = 1000

From equation 2: 2k − m = 500 → m = 2k − 500

Substitute in equation 1:
5k − 3(2k − 500) = 1000
5k − 6k + 1500 = 1000
−k = −500 → k = 500

- A's income = 5 × 500 = **₹2500**
- B's income = 4 × 500 = **₹2000**

### Shortcut for this pattern

When savings are EQUAL:
- Income difference ratio part × k = Expenditure difference ratio part × m
- Use the equal savings to create two equations and solve

## CONCEPT 13: Ratio Change Problems

### Pattern 1: A quantity is added/subtracted

"A:B = 3:5. If 10 is added to each, the new ratio is 5:7. Find original numbers."

- Original: A = 3k, B = 5k
- (3k + 10)/(5k + 10) = 5/7
- 7(3k + 10) = 5(5k + 10)
- 21k + 70 = 25k + 50
- 4k = 20 → k = 5
- A = 15, B = 25

### Pattern 2: A quantity is subtracted

"A:B = 5:3. If 6 is subtracted from each, new ratio = 7:3. Find original values."

- (5k − 6)/(3k − 6) = 7/3
- 3(5k − 6) = 7(3k − 6)
- 15k − 18 = 21k − 42
- 6k = 24 → k = 4
- A = 20, B = 12

### Pattern 3: Different amounts added

"A:B = 2:3. If 5 is added to A and 10 to B, new ratio = 3:5. Find original."

- (2k + 5)/(3k + 10) = 3/5
- 5(2k + 5) = 3(3k + 10)
- 10k + 25 = 9k + 30
- k = 5
- A = 10, B = 15

## CONCEPT 14: Ratio in Geometry & Mensuration

### Ratios of Areas and Volumes

| If length ratio is L₁:L₂ | Then... |
|---|---|
| Perimeter ratio | L₁ : L₂ (same as length) |
| Area ratio | L₁² : L₂² |
| Volume ratio | L₁³ : L₂³ |

**Example**: If sides of two cubes are in ratio 2:3:
- Surface area ratio = 4:9
- Volume ratio = 8:27

### Ratio of Areas of Triangles

| Given | Area ratio |
|---|---|
| Same base, different heights | h₁ : h₂ |
| Same height, different bases | b₁ : b₂ |
| Sides in ratio a:b (similar triangles) | a² : b² |

## CONCEPT 15: Special Properties and Theorems

### Property 1: If a/b = c/d = e/f = k, then

> **k = (a+c+e)/(b+d+f)** = (Sum of all numerators)/(Sum of all denominators)

This is called the **Addendo** property.

### Property 2: If a/b = c/d, then

> **a/c = b/d** (Alternendo)
> **b/a = d/c** (Invertendo)
> **(a+b)/b = (c+d)/d** (Componendo)
> **(a−b)/b = (c−d)/d** (Dividendo)
> **(a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)** (Componendo-Dividendo)

### Property 3: Equal Ratios

If a:b = c:d, then each ratio also equals:

> **(pa + qc) / (pb + qd)** for any values of p, q

This is extremely useful for algebraic simplification in exams.

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Direct Substitution for Expression-Based Questions

If a:b = 3:5, find (2a + 3b)/(4a − b):

**Don't use k. Just put a = 3, b = 5 directly!**

= (6 + 15)/(12 − 5) = 21/7 = **3**

This works because all such expressions are "ratio-dependent" — the actual scale doesn't matter.

---

## Shortcut 2: Combining Ratios — Product Method

A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5 → A:B:C = ?

**Fastest**: A:B:C = 2×4 : 3×4 : 3×5 = **8:12:15**

Rule: A takes first ratio's A × second ratio's B-value.
Middle term takes first ratio's B × second ratio's B-value.
C takes first ratio's B × second ratio's C-value.

Actually simpler: just make B same:
- First ratio: B = 3, second ratio: B = 4 → LCM = 12
- Scale first: 2:3 → 8:12. Scale second: 4:5 → 12:15
- Answer: **8:12:15**

---

## Shortcut 3: Inverse Ratio for Speed/Time/Work

> If Speed ratio = a:b → Time ratio = b:a (for same distance)
> If Efficiency ratio = a:b → Time ratio = b:a (for same work)

**Example**: A is 3 times as fast as B. A takes 20 days less than B. Find B's time.

- Speed ratio A:B = 3:1 → Time ratio A:B = 1:3
- Let A = k days, B = 3k days
- 3k − k = 20 → 2k = 20 → k = 10
- B = **30 days**

---

## Shortcut 4: Splitting a Difference Using Ratio

If A:B = 3:5 and A − B = 40 (or B − A = 40):

**Difference in ratio parts** = |5 − 3| = 2 parts
2 parts = 40 → 1 part = 20
A = 3 × 20 = 60, B = 5 × 20 = 100

> **1 part = Difference / (Difference in ratio terms)**

---

## Shortcut 5: Sum and Difference Together

If A:B = 5:3:
- Sum = 8 parts
- Difference = 2 parts
- If sum = 160: each part = 20 → A = 100, B = 60
- If difference = 40: each part = 20 → A = 100, B = 60

---

## Shortcut 6: Ratio of Remainders

"A:B = 7:5. Each donates ₹1000. New ratio = 5:3. Find original."

- Original: 7k, 5k
- After donation: (7k−1000)/(5k−1000) = 5/3
- 3(7k−1000) = 5(5k−1000)
- 21k − 3000 = 25k − 5000
- 4k = 2000 → k = 500
- A = 3500, B = 2500

---

## Shortcut 7: Percentage + Ratio Combined

"A is 20% more than B. Find A:B."

- A = 1.2B → A/B = 6/5 → **A:B = 6:5**

"A is 1/3 less than B. Find A:B."

- A = (2/3)B → A/B = 2/3 → **A:B = 2:3**

> **Converting percentage to fraction to ratio is a 3-second operation. Practice it.**

---

## Shortcut 8: Componendo-Dividendo Speed Application

If (a+b)/(a−b) = 7/1, find a:b.

Apply C-D in reverse:
- a/b = (7+1)/(7−1) = 8/6 = **4:3**

If (√a + √b)/(√a − √b) = 3/1:
- √a/√b = (3+1)/(3−1) = 4/2 = 2/1
- a/b = 4/1 → **a:b = 4:1**

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Ratio vs Fraction

| Parameter | Ratio | Fraction |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Comparison of two quantities | Part of a whole |
| Example | Boys:Girls = 3:2 | Boys are 3/5 of total |
| Notation | a:b | a/b |
| Can be > 1? | Yes (7:3 is valid) | Yes (7/3 is valid, it's improper) |
| Conversion | a:b = a/(a+b) of total | a/b as ratio = a:b |

> 3:2 as a ratio → Boys are 3/5 (fraction of total), Girls are 2/5.
> 3:2 does NOT mean boys are 3/2 of total!

---

## Confusion 2: Direct vs Inverse Proportion

| Direct Proportion | Inverse Proportion |
|---|---|
| Both increase together | One increases, other decreases |
| A/B = constant | A × B = constant |
| A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂ | A₁ × B₁ = A₂ × B₂ |
| More items → more cost | More workers → less time |
| Graph: straight line through origin | Graph: hyperbola |

---

## Confusion 3: Third Proportional vs Fourth Proportional vs Mean Proportional

| Type | Given | Find | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fourth proportional | a, b, c | x in a:b::c:x | x = bc/a |
| Third proportional | a, b | x in a:b::b:x | x = b²/a |
| Mean proportional | a, b | x in a:x::x:b | x = √(ab) |

> **Third**: The third number continues the proportion (b appears twice).
> **Fourth**: The fourth number completes a 4-term proportion.
> **Mean**: The middle number that creates a proportion.

---

## Confusion 4: Compound Ratio vs Adding Ratios

| Operation | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Compound ratio** of 2:3 and 4:5 | Multiply: (2×4):(3×5) = **8:15** | ✅ Correct |
| **Adding** ratios 2:3 and 4:5 | Does NOT mean 6:8 or 2+4:3+5 | ❌ You can't "add" ratios directly |

Compound ratio is MULTIPLICATION of ratios, not addition.

---

## Confusion 5: a:b = 3:5 → Is A = 3?

| Misconception | Truth |
|---|---|
| A = 3, B = 5 | ❌ Wrong (unless specifically stated) |
| A = 3k, B = 5k for some k | ✅ Correct |
| For EXPRESSION questions (find (2a+b)/(a−b)) | You CAN put a=3, b=5 directly |
| For VALUE questions (find actual A, B) | You MUST use k and solve for k |

---

## Confusion 6: Componendo vs Componendo-Dividendo

| Operation | Result |
|---|---|
| **Componendo** only | (a+b)/b = (c+d)/d |
| **Dividendo** only | (a−b)/b = (c−d)/d |
| **Componendo-Dividendo** (combined) | (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d) |

C-D combined is the MOST useful for exams. The individual forms are rarely needed.

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


---

| # | Trap | How it works | How to detect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Different units in ratio** | Comparing km and m without converting | Always check: are both quantities in the SAME unit? | 2 km : 500 m = 4:1 (not 2:500) |
| 2 | **Forgetting k** | Students assume A:B = 3:5 means A=3, B=5 | For finding actual values, ALWAYS use k | A=3k, B=5k |
| 3 | **Ratio of PARTS vs ratio of PART to WHOLE** | A:B = 3:2 → A is 3/5 of total, NOT 3/2 of total | Denominator = sum of all parts | A/(A+B) = 3/5 |
| 4 | **Direct vs Inverse confusion** | "More workers → less time" is inverse, not direct | Ask: "If one goes up, does other go up or down?" | |
| 5 | **Wrong cross-multiplication in proportion** | a:b::c:d → students write ac = bd | Correct: ad = bc (extremes × means) | |
| 6 | **Duplicate vs compound** | "Duplicate ratio of 3:5" → students do 3×3:5×5? or something else? | Duplicate = square: 9:25. Compound = multiply TWO ratios | |
| 7 | **Combining ratios: wrong LCM** | A:B=2:3, B:C=5:7 → making A same instead of B | The COMMON element must be equalized | Make B = LCM(3,5) = 15 |
| 8 | **C-D applied when a/b ≠ c/d** | Componendo-Dividendo only works when two ratios are EQUAL | Verify that the given equation is indeed a proportion | |
| 9 | **Confusing "ratio" with "difference"** | "A:B = 3:5" does NOT mean A is 2 less than B | Difference = (5−3) × k = 2k (depends on k!) | |
| 10 | **Savings in income-expenditure** | Students set up expenditure ratio = income ratio | Income ratio ≠ Expenditure ratio ≠ Savings ratio | Use separate variables |
| 11 | **Ratio order reversal** | "Ratio of boys to girls" vs "ratio of girls to boys" | Read the ORDER in the question carefully | B:G = 3:5 ≠ G:B = 3:5 |
| 12 | **Adding a number changes ratio non-linearly** | Students think adding 5 to each changes ratio by a fixed amount | Work algebraically with k-method | (3k+5)/(5k+5) ≠ (3+5):(5+5) |

# STEP 11 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES


---

## Mnemonic 1: Direct vs Inverse

> **"Same Direction = Divide"** (Direct proportion → A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂)
> **"Opposite Direction = Multiply"** (Inverse → A₁ × B₁ = A₂ × B₂)

---

## Mnemonic 2: Componendo-Dividendo

> **"Plus over Minus stays Plus over Minus"**
>
> If a/b = c/d → (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)
> "Add on top of subtract, on BOTH sides"

---

## Mnemonic 3: Third Proportional vs Mean Proportional

> **Third**: "**T**hird = **T**op squared over bottom" → b²/a (a:b::b:x)
> **Mean**: "**M**ean = **M**ultiply and root" → √(ab)

---

## Mnemonic 4: Combining Ratios

> **"Match the Middle"**
>
> A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5
> The MIDDLE term (B) must match in both.
> Make B = LCM(3,4) = 12.
> Scale accordingly.

---

## Visualization: The k-Method

```
Question says: A:B = 3:5

DON'T think:        DO think:
A = 3               A = 3k
B = 5               B = 5k
                    k = UNKNOWN (find from given condition)
```

Imagine k as a **zoom lens** — the ratio 3:5 is the SHAPE, k is the SIZE.

---

## Story Trick: Why Cross Product Works in Proportion

> Imagine a seesaw (balance).
> If a:b :: c:d, the seesaw is balanced.
> The "weight" on each side is: extremes (a×d) vs means (b×c).
> Balance means: a × d = b × c.
> If one side is heavier → seesaw tips → not in proportion.

---

## Pattern Trick: Percentage-to-Ratio Speed

> **"Percentage MORE? Add to 100, put over 100, simplify."**
> 25% more → 125/100 = 5/4 → ratio 5:4
> 40% more → 140/100 = 7/5 → ratio 7:5
> 75% more → 175/100 = 7/4 → ratio 7:4

> **"Percentage LESS? Subtract from 100, put over 100, simplify."**
> 20% less → 80/100 = 4/5 → ratio 4:5
> 37.5% less → 62.5/100 = 5/8 → ratio 5:8

## 📝 One-Page Notes

```
RATIO & PROPORTION — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

BASICS: a:b → actual = ak, bk. Always use k-method for actual values.
        For expressions, substitute ratio values directly.
        Same units required. Simplify by dividing by HCF.

DIVISION: Part of A = [a/(a+b+c)] × Total

COMPOUND RATIO: a:b and c:d → ac:bd (multiply corresponding terms)
DUPLICATE: a²:b²     SUB-DUPLICATE: √a:√b

PROPORTION: a:b::c:d → ad = bc (extremes × means)
MEAN PROPORTIONAL: √(ab)    THIRD: b²/a    FOURTH: bc/a

COMPONENDO-DIVIDENDO: If a/b = c/d → (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)
  Reverse C-D: (a+b)/(a−b) = P/Q → a:b = (P+Q):(P−Q)

COMBINING: A:B=a:b, B:C=c:d → make B equal → A:B:C = ac:bc:bd

DIRECT: A↑ B↑ → A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂
INVERSE: A↑ B↓ → A₁B₁ = A₂B₂

% TO RATIO: 25% more → 5/4 → 5:4.  20% less → 4/5 → 4:5.
```

| # | Mistake | Correct Version |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A:B = 3:5 means A = 3 | A = 3k (use k!) |
| 2 | Ratio 3:5 → A is 3/5 of total | A is 3/(3+5) = 3/8 of total |
| 3 | Combining: making wrong term equal | Always equalize the COMMON term |
| 4 | Direct when should be Inverse | Ask: "same direction?" Yes=Direct, No=Inverse |
| 5 | Cross product in proportion: ac = bd | Correct: ad = bc (extremes = means) |
| 6 | Duplicate ratio = multiply two ratios | Duplicate = SQUARE. Compound = multiply |
| 7 | Adding ratios: 2:3 + 4:5 = 6:8 | Cannot add ratios like fractions! |
| 8 | Forgetting to simplify final ratio | Always divide by HCF |

| Trap | Detection | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| Different units | km vs m, hours vs minutes | Convert to SAME unit first |
| k=1 assumption | "Find actual values" questions | Always use k |
| Part vs Whole confusion | "3/5 of total" vs "3:5 ratio" | Denominator = sum of parts |

## 🏃 Last-Minute Revision Notes

**5 Things to Remember Walking into the Exam Hall:**

1. **A:B = a:b → actual values = ak, bk. ALWAYS use k for actual value questions.**
2. **Componendo-Dividendo reverse: (a+b)/(a−b) = P/Q → a:b = (P+Q):(P−Q)**
3. **Mean proportional = √(ab). Third proportional = b²/a.**
4. **Combining ratios: equalize the COMMON term (use LCM).**
5. **Direct: same direction, divide. Inverse: opposite direction, multiply.**

**3 Most Common Traps:**
1. A:B = 3:5 does NOT mean A=3 (use k!)
2. Part of total = a/(a+b+c), NOT a/(b+c)
3. "More workers, less time" = INVERSE (multiply), not divide
