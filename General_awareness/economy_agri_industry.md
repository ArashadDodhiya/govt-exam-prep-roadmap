# Indian Economy — Agriculture & Industry Policy

## 1. Overview
This topic covers the POLICY side of India's agriculture and industrial sectors — land reforms, food security mechanisms, market reforms (MSP/APMC/e-NAM), and industrial policy evolution (from License Raj to Make in India and Startup India). Note: this complements the Economic Geography file (crop/mineral locations) by focusing on the schemes, laws, and institutional mechanisms instead.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Land Reforms (Post-Independence)**
- Abolition of Zamindari System — ended intermediary landlordism, land rights transferred to actual cultivators; implemented state-wise starting early 1950s **[High]**
- Tenancy Reforms — aimed to regulate rent, provide security of tenure to tenant farmers, "land to the tiller" **[Medium]**
- Land Ceiling Acts — fixed maximum land a family could hold; surplus land redistributed to landless farmers **[Medium]**
- Consolidation of Holdings — combined fragmented land plots into single units for efficient farming **[Low]**
- Bhoodan Movement — Vinoba Bhave's voluntary land-gift movement (1951) for landless farmers, a social (non-legislative) reform effort **[Medium]**

**Green Revolution (Policy Angle)**
- Introduced High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, irrigation expansion, mechanization (tractors, tube wells) **[High]**
- Concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, Western UP; primarily boosted WHEAT and RICE production **[High]**
- Criticism: regional imbalance, groundwater depletion, over-dependence on chemical inputs, benefited large farmers more than small/marginal ones **[Medium]**
- Led India from a food-deficit to a food-surplus nation by the 1970s-80s **[High]**

**Food Security**
- National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013 — legal right to subsidized foodgrains for ~2/3rd of India's population (67%) **[High]**
- Public Distribution System (PDS) — distributes subsidized foodgrains via ration shops (Fair Price Shops) **[High]**
- Targeted PDS (TPDS) — categorizes households into APL (Above Poverty Line) and BPL (Below Poverty Line) for differential subsidy **[Medium]**
- Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) — targets the poorest of the poor households with highest subsidy **[Medium]**
- Buffer Stock — foodgrain reserves maintained by Food Corporation of India (FCI) for price stability & emergency supply **[Medium]**
- One Nation One Ration Card — allows portability of ration card benefits across states **[Medium]**

**MSP, APMC, e-NAM**
- MSP (Minimum Support Price) — guaranteed price set by government (on recommendation of CACP — Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices) to protect farmers from price crashes **[High]**
- MSP announced for 22+ crops each season (Kharif & Rabi), but NOT a legal entitlement (no law mandates purchase at MSP for all crops) **[High]**
- APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) — state-level regulated wholesale markets ("mandis") where farmers sell produce; aims to prevent exploitation by middlemen **[High]**
- Criticism of APMC — creates monopoly of middlemen/commission agents, cartelization, farmers often get low realized prices despite regulation **[Medium]**
- e-NAM (National Agriculture Market) — launched 2016, online trading platform integrating APMC mandis across states for better price discovery and pan-India market access **[High]**
- Farm Laws 2020 (since repealed in 2021) — attempted to allow farmers to sell outside APMC mandis; led to major farmer protests, eventually withdrawn **[Medium]**

**Industrial Policy**
- Industrial Policy Resolution 1948 — first policy after independence; mixed economy approach (public + private sector coexistence) **[Medium]**
- Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 — basis of India's industrial policy for decades; classified industries into Schedule A (public sector only), Schedule B (mixed), Schedule C (private sector) **[High]**
- License Raj — pre-1991 system requiring government licenses/permits for industrial production, expansion, investment; criticized for red tape and inefficiency **[High]**
- New Industrial Policy 1991 (LPG Reforms) — Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization; delicensing of most industries, reduced import restrictions, opened FDI **[High]**
- Manmohan Singh — Finance Minister who introduced the 1991 economic reforms (under PM P.V. Narasimha Rao) **[High]**

**Make in India, Startup India, MSME**
- Make in India — launched 2014, aims to boost domestic manufacturing, attract FDI, increase manufacturing's share of GDP **[High]**
- Startup India — launched 2016, provides tax benefits, easier compliance, funding support for startups **[High]**
- MSME (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises) — classified by investment in plant/machinery AND annual turnover (revised criteria, 2020) **[High]**
- MSME sector — second-largest employer in India after agriculture; major contributor to exports and GDP **[High]**
- MUDRA Yojana (Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana) — provides collateral-free loans to micro/small enterprises **[Medium]**
- Udyam Registration — current online registration process for MSMEs (replaced earlier Udyog Aadhaar) **[Low]**
- PLI Scheme (Production Linked Incentive) — offers incentives to boost domestic manufacturing in specific sectors (electronics, pharma, etc.) **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| MSP vs APMC | MSP = a PRICE guarantee set by the government (via CACP recommendation); APMC = a MARKET/mandi system where farmers actually sell produce — MSP is about price, APMC is about the marketplace |
| APMC vs e-NAM | APMC = physical, state-regulated mandi system (localized); e-NAM = ONLINE platform integrating APMC mandis for wider, pan-India market access — e-NAM works WITHIN the APMC framework, doesn't replace it |
| Industrial Policy Resolution 1948 vs 1956 | 1948 = FIRST policy, established mixed economy concept; 1956 = became the actual BASIS of industrial policy for decades, introduced Schedule A/B/C classification |
| Make in India vs Startup India | Make in India = focuses on MANUFACTURING sector broadly (attracting FDI, boosting production); Startup India = focuses specifically on NEW BUSINESSES/entrepreneurs (tax benefits, funding support) |
| MSP legal status | MSP is NOT a legally guaranteed right for farmers to sell at that price for all crops — a commonly misunderstood point, especially post-2020 farm law protests |
| NFSA coverage | Covers approximately 67% of India's population (about 75% rural + 50% urban, combined ~67% national) — a specific number often tested |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Land reform types** — "**Z**amindari **T**enancy **C**eiling **C**onsolidation" → **Z**amindari abolition → **T**enancy reform → land **C**eiling → **C**onsolidation of holdings — think of it as a 4-step cleanup process, from removing middlemen to organizing farmland.
- **1991 Reforms trio (LPG)** — "**L**iberalization **P**rivatization **G**lobalization" = literally "LPG," like the cooking gas — an easy real-world object hook for the 3-part reform package.
- **MSP vs APMC role** — "MSP = the PRICE tag; APMC = the SHOP" — MSP tells you what price is guaranteed, APMC is where the actual selling happens.
- **Make in India vs Startup India** — "Make = factories/manufacturing (India as a producer); Start = new businesses/entrepreneurs (India as an innovator)" — the verb itself hints at the target audience.
- **Industrial Policy 1956 Schedule mnemonic** — "**A**lways **B**oth **C**ompany" → Schedule **A** (public only) → Schedule **B** (both/mixed) → Schedule **C** (private/Company) — alphabetical order matches ownership sequence from fully public to fully private.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- Confusing **MSP as a legally enforceable right** — it is NOT a legal guarantee for all crops; this nuance is frequently tested, especially post-farm-law-protest era.
- **APMC vs e-NAM** — assuming e-NAM REPLACED the APMC system; it actually integrates and digitizes APMC mandis, not eliminates them.
- Swapping **Industrial Policy Resolution 1948 and 1956** — 1948 was the FIRST policy, but 1956 is the more famous/tested one due to its industry classification system.
- **Make in India vs Startup India vs Digital India** — three similarly-named 2014-16 initiatives frequently mixed up in "match the scheme to its focus area" questions.
- NFSA coverage percentage (67%) is a specific, frequently misquoted number — watch for close-but-wrong percentages in options (e.g., 50%, 75%).

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Land Reforms: Zamindari abolition → Tenancy reform → Land Ceiling → Consolidation; Bhoodan Movement (Vinoba Bhave, 1951).
- Green Revolution: HYV seeds, Punjab/Haryana/W.UP, wheat & rice focus, made India food-surplus.
- Food Security: NFSA 2013 (67% coverage), PDS/TPDS, AAY (poorest households), FCI buffer stock.
- MSP/APMC/e-NAM: MSP=guaranteed price (not legally binding for all crops), APMC=regulated mandi system, e-NAM=online integration of mandis (2016).
- Industrial Policy: 1948 (first) → 1956 (Schedule A/B/C, long-term basis) → License Raj (pre-1991) → 1991 LPG reforms (Manmohan Singh, under PM Rao).
- Recent initiatives: Make in India (2014, manufacturing), Startup India (2016, entrepreneurship), MSME (2nd-largest employer), PLI Scheme (manufacturing incentives).

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** The Green Revolution in India was primarily concentrated in which states?
A) Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka
B) Punjab, Haryana, Western UP
C) West Bengal, Odisha, Bihar
D) Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan

**Q2.** MSP (Minimum Support Price) is recommended by which body?
A) NITI Aayog B) Finance Commission C) CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices) D) RBI

**Q3.** e-NAM (National Agriculture Market), launched in 2016, primarily aims to:
A) Replace the APMC system entirely
B) Provide an online platform integrating APMC mandis across India
C) Set minimum support prices for crops
D) Provide crop insurance to farmers

**Q4.** The 1991 economic reforms (LPG — Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) were introduced under which Finance Minister?
A) P. Chidambaram B) Manmohan Singh C) Arun Jaitley D) Yashwant Sinha

**Q5.** The National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013, provides a legal right to subsidized foodgrains to approximately what percentage of India's population?
A) 50% B) 67% C) 80% D) 100%

## Answer Key
1. **B) Punjab, Haryana, Western UP** — These regions saw the most concentrated adoption of Green Revolution technologies (HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilizers).
2. **C) CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices)** — This body recommends MSP levels to the government for various crops each season.
3. **B) Provide an online platform integrating APMC mandis across India** — e-NAM digitizes and connects existing APMC markets rather than replacing them.
4. **B) Manmohan Singh** — As Finance Minister under PM P.V. Narasimha Rao, he introduced the landmark 1991 LPG reforms.
5. **B) 67%** — NFSA covers roughly two-thirds of India's population (about 75% rural and 50% urban households).
