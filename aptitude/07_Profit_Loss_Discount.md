# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Profit & Loss?

Every business transaction has two sides:
- **Cost Price (CP)**: What you PAY to buy/make something
- **Selling Price (SP)**: What you RECEIVE when you sell it

> **If SP > CP → Profit**
> **If SP < CP → Loss**
> **If SP = CP → No profit, no loss**

That's it. Every P&L question is just: "How much did you pay? How much did you get? What's the difference?"

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Real-life relevance** | Shopping, business, GST — everyone deals with P&L |
| **3–5 direct questions per paper** | SSC, Banking, Railways — always present |
| **Combines with percentage** | P%, L%, Discount%, MP — all in percentages |
| **Tricky question design** | Dishonest dealers, successive discounts, MP puzzles |
| **Foundation for other topics** | Partnership profit-sharing uses P&L |

# STEP 3 — CORE CONCEPTS (Logical Order)

## CONCEPT 1: Basic Definitions and Formulas

### The Core Formulas

| Formula | Expression |
|---|---|
| **Profit** | SP − CP |
| **Loss** | CP − SP |
| **Profit %** | (Profit / CP) × 100 = [(SP − CP) / CP] × 100 |
| **Loss %** | (Loss / CP) × 100 = [(CP − SP) / CP] × 100 |
| **SP when Profit%** | SP = CP × (100 + P%) / 100 |
| **SP when Loss%** | SP = CP × (100 − L%) / 100 |
| **CP when Profit%** | CP = SP × 100 / (100 + P%) |
| **CP when Loss%** | CP = SP × 100 / (100 − L%) |

### The MULTIPLIER Approach (Fastest)

| Situation | Multiplier | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 20% profit | CP × **1.2** = SP | CP=100 → SP=120 |
| 20% loss | CP × **0.8** = SP | CP=100 → SP=80 |
| 25% profit | CP × **5/4** = SP | CP=400 → SP=500 |
| 25% loss | CP × **3/4** = SP | CP=400 → SP=300 |
| 33.33% profit | CP × **4/3** = SP | CP=300 → SP=400 |
| 33.33% loss | CP × **2/3** = SP | CP=300 → SP=200 |

### Key Insight

> **Profit% and Loss% are ALWAYS calculated on CP (Cost Price).**
> CP is the BASE. Never calculate on SP.

### Example

A bought a phone for ₹15000 and sold it for ₹18000. Find Profit%.

Profit = 18000 − 15000 = ₹3000
Profit% = (3000/15000) × 100 = **20%**

## CONCEPT 2: Finding SP/CP from Given Conditions

### "CP of x articles = SP of y articles" (VERY HIGH FREQUENCY SSC)

> If CP of **x** articles = SP of **y** articles:
>
> **Profit% = [(x − y) / y] × 100** (when x > y → profit)
> **Loss% = [(y − x) / x] × 100** (when y > x → loss)

Wait, let me derive this properly:

Let CP of 1 article = c, SP of 1 article = s.

Given: x × c = y × s → s/c = x/y

Profit% = (s − c)/c × 100 = (s/c − 1) × 100 = (x/y − 1) × 100 = **(x − y)/y × 100**

### Example

CP of 20 articles = SP of 16 articles. Find Profit%.

Profit% = (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = 4/16 × 100 = **25%** ✅

### Quick Formula

> **P/L% = [(x−y)/y] × 100** (positive = profit, negative = loss)
>
> Where x = CP count, y = SP count

## CONCEPT 3: Marked Price (MP) & Discount

### The Supply Chain of Pricing

```
    CP (Cost Price)
    → Add Profit Margin → MP (Marked Price / MRP / List Price)
    → Give Discount → SP (Selling Price)
    → Customer pays SP
```

### Formulas

| Formula | Expression |
|---|---|
| **Discount** | MP − SP |
| **Discount%** | (Discount / MP) × 100 = [(MP − SP) / MP] × 100 |
| **SP after discount** | SP = MP × (100 − D%) / 100 |
| **Relation: MP, CP, Profit, Discount** | MP × (100 − D%) / 100 = CP × (100 + P%) / 100 |

### The Master Equation

> **SP = MP × (1 − D/100) = CP × (1 + P/100)**

This connects MP, CP, Discount%, and Profit% in ONE equation.

### Example

MP = ₹500. Discount = 20%. Profit = 25%. Find CP.

SP = 500 × (1 − 0.20) = 500 × 0.8 = ₹400
400 = CP × 1.25 → CP = 400/1.25 = **₹320**

### Fraction Shortcut

| Discount% | Multiplier (MP → SP) |
|---|---|
| 10% | × 9/10 |
| 20% | × 4/5 |
| 25% | × 3/4 |
| 30% | × 7/10 |
| 33.33% | × 2/3 |
| 40% | × 3/5 |
| 50% | × 1/2 |

## CONCEPT 4: Successive Discounts

### When TWO or MORE discounts are given one after another

"Successive discounts of 20% and 10%" means:
1. First give 20% off on MP
2. Then give 10% off on the ALREADY REDUCED price

> **Single equivalent discount ≠ Sum of discounts!**

### Formula (from Successive Percentage Change)

For successive discounts d₁% and d₂%:

> **Equivalent single discount = d₁ + d₂ − (d₁ × d₂)/100 %**

### Example

Successive discounts of 20% and 10%:
= 20 + 10 − (20×10)/100 = 30 − 2 = **28%** (NOT 30%)

### Multiplier Method (Faster)

SP = MP × (1 − d₁/100) × (1 − d₂/100)

= MP × 0.8 × 0.9 = MP × **0.72**

Effective discount = 1 − 0.72 = 0.28 = **28%** ✅

### For THREE successive discounts

SP = MP × (1 − d₁/100) × (1 − d₂/100) × (1 − d₃/100)

### Key Insight

> Successive discounts of 20% and 10% give LESS discount than a single 30%.
> Because the second discount is on a SMALLER base.

## CONCEPT 5: Dishonest Dealer / False Weights (SSC FAVOURITE)

### Pattern 1: Uses less weight

A dealer claims to sell at CP but uses 800g weight instead of 1000g.

> **Profit% = [(True weight − False weight) / False weight] × 100**

= (1000 − 800)/800 × 100 = 200/800 × 100 = **25%**

### Pattern 2: Sells above CP AND uses false weights

A dealer marks up 10% above CP and uses 900g instead of 1 kg.

Effective gain = gain from markup × gain from false weight:

Multiplier = (1 + markup%) × (true weight/false weight)

= 1.10 × (1000/900) = 1.10 × 10/9 = 110/90 = 11/9

Profit% = (11/9 − 1) × 100 = (2/9) × 100 = **22.22%**

### Pattern 3: Claims discount but uses false weights

Gives 10% discount but uses 800g instead of 1 kg.

Multiplier = (1 − discount%) × (true weight/false weight) ÷ 1

Wait, let me think about this differently:

He sells 800g for the price of 900g (after 10% discount on 1000g price).

SP of what he sells = 0.9 × CP of 1000g
But he only gives 800g worth of goods
So his effective SP per unit = 0.9 × 1000/800 = 0.9 × 1.25 = 1.125

Profit% = **12.5%**

### General Formula for Dishonest Dealer

> **Profit% = [(100 + markup%) × (True weight / False weight) − 100]%**
>
> If discount instead of markup: **[(100 − discount%) × (True weight / False weight) − 100]%**

## CONCEPT 6: Buy x Get y Free

"Buy 4, Get 1 Free" means customer pays for 4 but gets 5.

> **Effectively, discount = y/(x+y) × 100 = 1/5 × 100 = 20%**

### If the dealer has profit on this scheme:

The dealer bought all 5 items at CP. Sold effectively at price of 4.

Loss if sold at CP = 1 item free out of 5 = 20% loss.

But if MP has markup, then:
- Revenue = 4 × SP (per item)
- Cost = 5 × CP (per item)
- Profit/Loss = (4×SP − 5×CP)

---

## CONCEPT 7: Two Articles Sold at Same SP — One at Profit, One at Loss (CLASSIC TRAP)

### The Pattern

"Two articles are sold at ₹1200 each. One at 20% profit, other at 20% loss. Find net P/L%."

### The TRAP

Students think: "20% profit and 20% loss cancel out → No P/L"
**WRONG!**

### The Truth

> **When two articles are sold at the SAME SP, one at x% profit and other at x% loss:**
>
> **There is ALWAYS a NET LOSS.**
>
> **Net Loss% = x²/100 %**

### Proof

- Article 1 (20% profit): CP₁ = 1200/1.2 = ₹1000
- Article 2 (20% loss): CP₂ = 1200/0.8 = ₹1500
- Total CP = ₹2500
- Total SP = ₹2400
- **Loss = ₹100, Loss% = 100/2500 × 100 = 4%**

Using formula: x²/100 = 20²/100 = 400/100 = **4% loss** ✅

### When the SAME CP produces different results

If two articles have the SAME CP and one is sold at x% profit, other at x% loss:
- Then there IS no P/L → they cancel perfectly.

> **Same SP + Same %: Always a loss.**
> **Same CP + Same %: Always zero.**

---

## CONCEPT 8: MP-Discount-Profit Relationship

### Finding how much to mark up to allow for discount AND still make profit

"A shopkeeper wants to allow 20% discount and still make 25% profit. By what % should he mark up above CP?"

> **MP/CP = (100 + P%) / (100 − D%)**

= (100 + 25) / (100 − 20) = 125/80 = 25/16

Markup% = (25/16 − 1) × 100 = (9/16) × 100 = **56.25%**

### General Formula

> **Markup% = [(P% + D% + P%×D%/100) / (100 − D%)] × 100**
>
> Or simply: **MP = CP × (100 + P%) / (100 − D%)**

---

## CONCEPT 9: Selling at Loss to Find Missing Values

### "Sells at 20% loss. If he had sold for ₹100 more, he would have made 5% profit."

- SP (loss) = 0.8 × CP
- SP (profit) = 1.05 × CP
- Difference = 1.05CP − 0.80CP = 0.25CP = ₹100
- CP = 100/0.25 = **₹400**

### General Approach

Set up two SP expressions from the two given conditions. The difference in SPs = given amount. Solve for CP.

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Assume CP = 100

For ALL percentage-based P&L questions, assume CP = 100. This turns percentages into actual numbers.

**Example**: "Profit of 25% on selling at 10% discount. Find markup%."

CP = 100. SP = 125 (25% profit). MP × 0.9 = 125 → MP = 138.89.
Markup = 38.89% over CP. But with fractions: MP = 125 × 10/9 = 1250/9.
Markup% = (1250/9 − 100)/100 × 100 = (1250−900)/900 × 100 = **38.89%**

---

## Shortcut 2: Fraction Multipliers for Common Percentages

| Profit/Loss | Multiplier | As fraction |
|---|---|---|
| 10% profit | ×1.1 | ×11/10 |
| 20% profit | ×1.2 | ×6/5 |
| 25% profit | ×1.25 | ×5/4 |
| 33.33% profit | ×1.333 | ×4/3 |
| 50% profit | ×1.5 | ×3/2 |
| 10% loss | ×0.9 | ×9/10 |
| 20% loss | ×0.8 | ×4/5 |
| 25% loss | ×0.75 | ×3/4 |

---

## Shortcut 3: "Would have" Questions — Difference Method

"If sold at 20% profit instead of 20% loss, SP would be ₹200 more."

Difference = (1.2 − 0.8) × CP = 0.4 × CP = 200
CP = **₹500**

> **Difference in SP = Difference in multipliers × CP**

---

## Shortcut 4: Dishonest Dealer — One-Line

> **Effective gain% = [(What he charges for / What he gives) − 1] × 100**

Charges for 1 kg but gives 900g: (1000/900 − 1) × 100 = **11.11%**

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Profit% on CP vs on SP

| Calculation | Base |
|---|---|
| **Profit%** | Always on **CP** |
| **Discount%** | Always on **MP** |
| **Loss%** | Always on **CP** |

❌ Never calculate profit% on SP. ❌ Never calculate discount% on CP.

---

## Confusion 2: "Same SP" vs "Same CP"

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| Same SP, x% profit & x% loss | **Net LOSS = x²/100%** |
| Same CP, x% profit & x% loss | **Net = 0** (no P/L) |

---

## Confusion 3: Successive Discount ≠ Sum

20% + 10% successive discount ≠ 30%. It equals **28%**.
Second discount is on the REDUCED price, not original.

---

## Confusion 4: MP vs SP

| | MP (Marked Price) | SP (Selling Price) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Sticker price / Listed price | What customer actually pays |
| After discount | Before discount | After discount |
| Relation | SP = MP × (1 − D%) | CP relates to SP through P% |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


## Speed Rule 1: Always Use Multipliers, Not Addition/Subtraction

Don't think: "CP = 400, profit = 25%, so profit = 100, SP = 500"
Think: "CP = 400, × 5/4 = **500**"

## Speed Rule 2: CP = 100 for Percentage Questions

Eliminates division. Everything becomes simple arithmetic.

## Speed Rule 3: Know the "CP of x = SP of y" Formula Cold

This single formula saves 1–2 minutes per question. SSC asks it every year.

# STEP 12 — PRACTICE SESSION


---

**Q1.** A man buys at ₹500 and sells at ₹600. Profit%?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
(600−500)/500 × 100 = **20%**
</details>

**Q2.** CP of 15 articles = SP of 12 articles. P/L%?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
(15−12)/12 × 100 = **25% profit** (x > y → profit)
</details>

**Q3.** MP = ₹800. Discount 15%. SP = ?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
800 × 0.85 = **₹680**
</details>

**Q4.** Successive discounts 20% and 25%. Equivalent discount?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
20+25−(20×25)/100 = 45−5 = **40%**. Or: 0.8 × 0.75 = 0.60 → 40% off.
</details>

**Q5.** Dealer sells at CP but uses 800g instead of 1 kg. Profit%?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
(1000−800)/800 × 100 = **25%**
</details>

**Q6.** Two items sold at ₹960 each. One at 20% profit, other at 20% loss. Net P/L%?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
**Net loss = 20²/100 = 4% loss**. CP₁=800, CP₂=1200. Total CP=2000, Total SP=1920. Loss=80. L%=80/2000×100=4%.
</details>

**Q7.** A wants 20% profit after 10% discount. Markup% over CP?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
MP/CP = (100+20)/(100−10) = 120/90 = 4/3. Markup = 1/3 = **33.33%**
</details>

**Q8.** Sold at 10% loss. Had sold for ₹180 more, profit would be 8%. CP?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Difference = (1.08−0.90) × CP = 0.18 × CP = 180. CP = **₹1000**
</details>

**Q9.** Buy 5 get 1 free. If SP = CP, what is effective discount%?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Free/Total = 1/6 × 100 = **16.67%**
</details>

**Q10.** SP = ₹450 at 25% profit. Find CP.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
CP = 450 × 100/125 = 450 × 4/5 = **₹360**
</details>

# STEP 14 — REVISION PACK


```
PROFIT & LOSS — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

P% = (SP−CP)/CP × 100. BASE IS ALWAYS CP.
D% = (MP−SP)/MP × 100. BASE IS ALWAYS MP.

MULTIPLIER: SP = CP × (1 + P/100) or CP × (1 − L/100)
DISCOUNT: SP = MP × (1 − D/100)
MASTER: SP = CP(1+P%) = MP(1−D%)

CP of x = SP of y → P% = (x−y)/y × 100
SUCCESSIVE DISC: d₁+d₂−d₁d₂/100
FALSE WEIGHT: P% = (True−False)/False × 100
SAME SP TRAP: Same SP, x% P & x% L → Net Loss = x²/100%
MARKUP: MP = CP × (100+P%)/(100−D%)
BUY x GET y FREE: Disc = y/(x+y) × 100
```
