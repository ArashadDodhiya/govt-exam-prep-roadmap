# Indian Polity — Constitutional Bodies, Articles, Emergency, Centre-State Relations

## 1. Overview
This topic covers the independent constitutional watchdog bodies (Election Commission, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission, National Commissions), the Constitution's structural elements (Schedules, key Articles, Amendment process), Emergency Provisions, and the relationship between Centre and States. Exams love this topic for its long list of "which body does what" and "which article says what" pairings.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Election Commission of India (ECI)**
- Constitutional body under Article 324; conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President (NOT Panchayat/Municipal elections — those are State Election Commissions) **[High]**
- Originally single-member body; became multi-member (3 members: 1 CEC + 2 Election Commissioners) since 1993 **[High]**
- CEC and ECs appointed by President; removal of CEC only via process similar to a Supreme Court judge (impeachment-like); other ECs can be removed on CEC's recommendation **[Medium]**
- Sukumar Sen — first Chief Election Commissioner of India **[Medium]**

**Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)**
- Constitutional body under Article 148; audits accounts of Union and State governments **[High]**
- Called "Guardian of the Public Purse" / watchdog of financial system **[High]**
- Appointed by President; term 6 years or until age 65, whichever earlier **[Medium]**
- Reports submitted to President (for Union) or Governor (for States), then laid before Parliament/State Legislature — examined by Public Accounts Committee (PAC) **[Medium]**

**Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)**
- Constitutional body under Article 315; conducts recruitment exams for All India Services (IAS, IPS, IFS) and Central Civil Services (via Civil Services Exam) **[High]**
- Members appointed by President; Chairman & members hold office for 6 years or until age 65 (whichever earlier) **[Medium]**
- State Public Service Commissions (SPSC) — parallel bodies for state-level recruitment, Article 315 also covers these **[Medium]**

**Finance Commission**
- Constitutional body under Article 280; constituted every 5 years by the President **[High]**
- Main function: recommends distribution of tax revenue between Centre and States, and among states (vertical & horizontal devolution) **[High]**
- Also recommends principles for grants-in-aid to states **[Medium]**
- K.C. Neogy — chaired the FIRST Finance Commission (1951) **[Medium]**
- Different from NITI Aayog — Finance Commission deals with tax devolution (constitutional, mandatory); NITI Aayog is a policy think tank (statutory body, not constitutional) **[High]**

**National Commissions**
- National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) — Article 338; safeguards SC interests **[Medium]**
- National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) — Article 338A (separated from NCSC via 89th Amendment, 2003) **[Medium]**
- National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) — given constitutional status via 102nd Amendment (2018), Article 338B **[Medium]**
- National Commission for Women (NCW) — STATUTORY body (NOT constitutional), established 1992 under NCW Act 1990 **[High]**
- National Commission for Minorities (NCM) — STATUTORY body, established 1992 **[Medium]**
- National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) — STATUTORY body, established 1993 under Protection of Human Rights Act **[High]**
- Distinguishing constitutional vs statutory bodies is a key exam distinction — SC, ST, and OBC Commissions are constitutional; Women, Minorities, and Human Rights Commissions are statutory **[High]**

**Schedules of the Constitution**
- Originally 8 Schedules, now 12 (added over time via amendments) **[High]**
- 1st Schedule — States and Union Territories **[Medium]**
- 2nd Schedule — salaries/allowances of President, Governors, judges, etc. **[Low]**
- 7th Schedule — Union List, State List, Concurrent List (division of powers) **[High]**
- 8th Schedule — official languages (22 languages currently recognized) **[High]**
- 9th Schedule — protects certain laws from judicial review (added by 1st Amendment, 1951) **[Medium]**
- 10th Schedule — Anti-Defection Law (added by 52nd Amendment, 1985) **[High]**
- 11th Schedule — Panchayati Raj subjects (added by 73rd Amendment) **[Medium]**
- 12th Schedule — Municipalities subjects (added by 74th Amendment) **[Medium]**

**Important Articles (Quick Reference)**
- Article 1 — India, "a Union of States" **[Medium]**
- Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty **[High]**
- Article 356 — President's Rule (State Emergency) **[High]**
- Article 368 — Constitutional Amendment procedure **[High]**
- Article 370 — special status to Jammu & Kashmir (abrogated in 2019) **[High]**

**Amendment Process (Article 368)**
- Three ways to amend: (1) Simple majority of Parliament (like ordinary law, e.g., new state creation), (2) Special majority (2/3 of members present & voting + majority of total membership in each House), (3) Special majority + ratification by at least half of State Legislatures (for federal provisions) **[High]**
- 1st Amendment (1951) — added 9th Schedule, restricted freedom of speech grounds **[Medium]**
- 42nd Amendment (1976) — called "Mini Constitution," added Socialist/Secular/Integrity to Preamble, added Fundamental Duties **[High]**
- 44th Amendment (1978) — removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights **[Medium]**
- 73rd & 74th Amendments (1992) — Panchayati Raj & Municipalities **[High]**
- 101st Amendment (2016) — introduced GST **[High]**
- 103rd Amendment (2019) — 10% EWS (Economically Weaker Section) reservation **[Medium]**

**Emergency Provisions**
- Three types: National Emergency (Art 352), State Emergency/President's Rule (Art 356), Financial Emergency (Art 360) **[High]**
- National Emergency — grounds: war, external aggression, armed rebellion (word "internal disturbance" changed to "armed rebellion" by 44th Amendment, 1978); proclaimed by President on Cabinet's written advice; needs Parliamentary approval within 1 month, then every 6 months **[High]**
- National Emergency invoked 3 times: 1962 (China war), 1971 (Pakistan war), 1975-77 (internal disturbance, most controversial — "The Emergency") **[High]**
- State Emergency (President's Rule, Art 356) — imposed when state government "cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution"; max 3 years with periodic extensions (originally 6 months, extendable) **[High]**
- S.R. Bommai case (1994) — landmark SC judgment restricting arbitrary use of Article 356, made it subject to judicial review **[High]**
- Financial Emergency (Art 360) — proclaimed if financial stability/credit of India is threatened; NEVER invoked so far in India **[High]**

**Inter-State & Centre-State Relations**
- Seventh Schedule divides subjects into Union List (Centre only), State List (States only), Concurrent List (both; Centre's law prevails in case of conflict) **[High]**
- Inter-State Council (Art 263) — established to discuss Centre-State/inter-state disputes **[Medium]**
- Zonal Councils — advisory bodies (not constitutional, statutory) promoting cooperation among states in a region; 5 zonal councils **[Low]**
- Sarkaria Commission (1983) — reviewed Centre-State relations, made recommendations on Governor's role, Article 356 usage, etc. **[Medium]**
- Punchhi Commission (2007) — another Centre-State relations review commission **[Low]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Constitutional vs Statutory bodies | Constitutional = created by/mentioned in the Constitution itself (ECI, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission, NCSC, NCST, NCBC); Statutory = created by an ordinary law/Act of Parliament (NCW, NCM, NHRC, NITI Aayog) |
| Finance Commission vs NITI Aayog | Finance Commission = constitutional, deals with TAX REVENUE distribution, every 5 years; NITI Aayog = statutory think tank, deals with POLICY/PLANNING, replaced Planning Commission in 2015 |
| National Emergency vs State Emergency vs Financial Emergency | National (Art 352) = war/external aggression/armed rebellion, whole country; State (Art 356) = state government breakdown, President's Rule; Financial (Art 360) = economic/credit crisis, NEVER used |
| NCSC vs NCST vs NCBC | NCSC (Art 338) = Scheduled Castes; NCST (Art 338A, separated 2003) = Scheduled Tribes; NCBC (Art 338B, constitutional status 2018) = Other Backward Classes |
| 9th Schedule vs 10th Schedule | 9th Schedule = protects laws from judicial review (1st Amendment, 1951); 10th Schedule = Anti-Defection Law (52nd Amendment, 1985) — very different purposes despite similar numbering |
| Article 356 vs S.R. Bommai case | Article 356 = the power itself (President's Rule); S.R. Bommai (1994) = the SC judgment that curtailed its MISUSE by making it subject to judicial review |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Constitutional bodies list** — "**E**lections **C**ount **U**nder **F**air **N**orms" → **E**lection Commission, **C**AG, **U**PSC, **F**inance Commission, **N**ational Commissions (SC/ST/OBC only) — all under the Constitution's direct umbrella.
- **Statutory (NOT constitutional) trio** — "**W**omen **M**inorities **H**uman-rights = **W**ait, **M**ade by law, **H**ence statutory" — NCW, NCM, NHRC — remember these three as the "made later, by ordinary law" group, unlike SC/ST/OBC commissions.
- **Three Emergency types by number** — "**3-5-2** = National (war-level, biggest number feel), **3-5-6** = State (one step further = President's Rule), **3-6-0** = Financial (360 = 'full circle' of financial collapse, never happened)" — the increasing digit pattern (352→356→360) mirrors increasing specificity.
- **National Emergency invoked years** — "**6**2 **C**hina, **7**1 **P**akistan, **7**5 **I**nternal" — three wars/crises in a growing pattern of severity, ending with the most controversial (1975 Emergency).
- **Schedule numbers to remember** — "9=protects laws, 10=defects (defection), 11=rural (Panchayat), 12=urban (Municipal)" — link the schedule number directly to a one-word tag for instant recall.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Constitutional vs Statutory body traps** are extremely common — options frequently claim NCW, NCM, or NHRC are "constitutional bodies," which is FALSE.
- Confusing which **Emergency Article** applies to which scenario — 352 (national threat) vs 356 (state government failure) vs 360 (financial crisis) get swapped in scenario-based questions.
- **9th vs 10th Schedule** mix-up — a favorite pairing trap given their sequential numbering but completely different content.
- "**Which body was never invoked**" — Financial Emergency (Art 360) is the standalone correct answer; National and State Emergencies HAVE been invoked multiple times.
- NCSC/NCST/NCBC — examiners test whether students know NCBC only got constitutional status in 2018 (before that it was statutory, established 1993) — a "changed status over time" trap.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Constitutional bodies: ECI (Art 324, elections), CAG (Art 148, audit), UPSC (Art 315, recruitment), Finance Commission (Art 280, tax devolution, every 5 yrs), NCSC/NCST/NCBC (Art 338/338A/338B).
- Statutory (NOT constitutional): NCW, NCM, NHRC, NITI Aayog.
- Schedules: 12 total; 7th=Union/State/Concurrent Lists, 8th=languages, 9th=law protection, 10th=anti-defection, 11th=Panchayat, 12th=Municipal.
- Amendment (Art 368): simple majority / special majority / special majority+state ratification, depending on provision; 42nd="Mini Constitution," 101st=GST.
- Emergency: National (352, war/rebellion, invoked 1962/71/75), State (356, President's Rule, Bommai case limits misuse), Financial (360, never invoked).
- Centre-State: 7th Schedule (3 lists), Inter-State Council (Art 263), Sarkaria Commission (1983) reviewed relations.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which of the following is a STATUTORY body, NOT a constitutional body?
A) Election Commission of India
B) Union Public Service Commission
C) National Human Rights Commission
D) Comptroller and Auditor General

**Q2.** The Finance Commission of India is constituted under which Article, and for what primary purpose?
A) Article 280; distribution of tax revenue between Centre and States
B) Article 324; conducting elections
C) Article 315; civil service recruitment
D) Article 356; imposing President's Rule

**Q3.** Which type of Emergency has NEVER been invoked in India so far?
A) National Emergency B) State Emergency (President's Rule) C) Financial Emergency D) All have been invoked

**Q4.** The landmark S.R. Bommai case (1994) is associated with restricting misuse of which Article?
A) Article 352 B) Article 356 C) Article 360 D) Article 368

**Q5.** The 10th Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with:
A) Official languages B) Anti-Defection Law C) Panchayati Raj subjects D) Union and State Lists

## Answer Key
1. **C) National Human Rights Commission** — NHRC is a statutory body (established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993), unlike ECI, UPSC, and CAG which are constitutional bodies.
2. **A) Article 280; distribution of tax revenue between Centre and States** — The Finance Commission, constituted every 5 years, recommends how tax revenue is shared.
3. **C) Financial Emergency** — Article 360 has never been invoked in India, unlike National Emergency (invoked 3 times) and President's Rule (invoked many times).
4. **B) Article 356** — The S.R. Bommai judgment made the imposition of President's Rule subject to judicial review, curbing its arbitrary use.
5. **B) Anti-Defection Law** — The 10th Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment (1985), deals with disqualification of legislators on grounds of defection.
