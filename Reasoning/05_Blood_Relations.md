# 05. Blood Relations

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What are Blood Relations?**
Blood Relations test your ability to trace family lineage and connections using logical deduction. You are given a chain of relationships (e.g., A is the brother of B's father), and you must deduce the exact connection between two specific individuals.

**Why do examiners test this?**
It tests your structural thinking, working memory, and ability to handle confusing linguistic phrasing without losing track of the core data. It is heavily tested in all exams, from basic SSC/Railways to high-level IBPS/SBI PO Mains and UPSC CSAT.

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Name Trap:** Giving a person a gender-specific name (like 'Kiran', 'Sonu', or 'Alex') and expecting you to assume their gender. *Never assume gender based on a name in Government Exams unless explicitly stated with words like "he/his/brother/father"!*
2. **The "Reverse Answer" Trap:** The question asks, "How is A related to B?" The student calculates the tree perfectly, finds out A is the Uncle and B is the Nephew, and hurriedly marks "Nephew" in the options. The correct answer was Uncle. You must answer *who A is to B* (What B calls A).
3. **The "Only Son/Daughter" Trap:** "A is the son of B. C is the only son of B." Wait, how? This means A and C cannot be brothers if C is the *only* son. If C is the only son, and A is also a son, then A and C are the same person! Or, if A is a different person, A must be a daughter. Pay extreme attention to the word **"only"**.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Indicating Form / Pointing Form (High Frequency - SSC, State PSC, CSAT)
*   *Example:* "Pointing to a photograph of a boy, Suresh said, 'He is the son of the only son of my mother.'"
*   Tests mental tracing and working backwards from the speaker.

### 2. Puzzle / Paragraph Form (High Frequency - Banking Prelims, Railways, SSC CGL)
*   *Example:* "In a family of 6 members A, B, C, D, E, F, there are two married couples. A is the father of B..."
*   Tests your ability to draw a multi-generational family tree using scattered clues.

### 3. Coded Blood Relations (High Frequency - Banking Mains, SSC CGL Mains)
*   *Example:* "A + B means A is the father of B. A - B means A is the mother of B. What does P + Q - R mean?"
*   Tests your speed in decoding symbols and applying them to a family tree under time pressure.

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
Do NOT try to solve complex puzzles in your head. You must translate English sentences into a **Standard Family Tree Diagram**.

**The Standard Notation (Memorize this instantly):**
*   **Male:** Square `[ ]` or a Plus sign `(+)`
*   **Female:** Circle `( )` or a Minus sign `(-)`
*   **Unknown Gender:** Just the letter `A` without any shape/sign.
*   **Vertical Line `|`:** Represents a generation gap (Parent to Child).
*   **Horizontal Line `-`:** Represents siblings (Brother/Sister).
*   **Double Horizontal Line `=`:** Represents a married couple (Husband/Wife).

*Example Sentence:* "A is the father of B, who is the sister of C."
*Drawing:* Put A in a Square. Draw a vertical line down from A. At the bottom of the line, write B in a Circle. Draw a single horizontal line from B to C. Leave C without a shape because C's gender is unknown!

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional (The Family Tree)
*   Read sentence by sentence. Draw the standard notation. Connect the nodes. Read the final question carefully.
*   *When to use:* Puzzle Form and complex Coded Form.

### Method 2: Fast Method (Backward Tracing for Indicating Form)
*   For "Pointing to a photograph" questions, **do not draw a tree**. Start reading from the word **"MY"** (which represents the speaker) and move backwards.
*   *Example:* "He is the son of the only son of **my** mother."
    *   *Step 1:* "my mother" (Speaker's mother)
    *   *Step 2:* "only son of [Speaker's mother]" (This is the speaker himself, assuming speaker is male).
    *   *Step 3:* "son of [Speaker]" (This is the speaker's son).
*   *When to use:* Strictly for Indicating/Pointing form questions.

### Method 3: Expert Method (Gender & Generation Elimination for Coded Form)
*   When a question asks: *"Which of the following expressions shows that P is the paternal uncle of Q?"*
*   **Do not draw trees for all 4 options!**
*   *Step 1 (Gender Elimination):* P must be an Uncle, so P **MUST BE MALE**. Look at the options. If an option ends with `P` (e.g., `M + N - P`), P's gender is usually unknown (unless the symbol means husband/wife). Eliminate it. If an option says `P - R` (and `-` means mother), P is female. Eliminate it.
*   *Step 2 (Generation Gap):* Uncle to Nephew is a **+1 Generation Gap**. Assign values: Father/Mother = +1, Brother/Sister = 0, Son/Daughter = -1. Calculate the total gap in the remaining options. The correct option must total +1.
*   *When to use:* High-level Coded Blood Relations. Saves 2-3 minutes.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Question starts with "Pointing to..."?** -> Scan for the word "my". Trace backwards. Do not touch your pen.
*   **Question is a block of text with 6-8 people?** -> Grab your pen. Find the most definite connecting link (usually a married couple or someone with the most connections) and start drawing the tree.
*   **Question has +, -, \*, % symbols?** -> It's Coded. Look at the question asked. Can you eliminate options using the Gender Elimination trick?

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **The Grammar Rule ("Who" vs "And"):** 
    *   In the sentence "A is the father of B, **who** is the sister of C," the word **"who"** (or whose/whom/which) always refers to the person immediately preceding it (i.e., B). 
    *   If the sentence was "A is the father of B **and** is the brother of C," the word **"and"** (or but/while) refers to the first person (i.e., A). This grammar rule is the #1 reason students draw wrong trees.
2. **Paternal vs Maternal:** Paternal = Father's side (Chacha, Tau, Bua). Maternal = Mother's side (Mama, Masi). If the exam just says "Uncle", assume Paternal by default unless the options dictate otherwise.
3. **In-Laws Ambiguity:** "Brother-in-law" can mean Sister's Husband OR Wife's Brother OR Husband's Brother. Draw all possibilities if the puzzle gets stuck.

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **The Gender Assumption:** Assuming "Kamal" is male. If Kamal's gender is not given, and the question asks "How is Kamal related to X?", the answer is "Cannot be determined" or "Brother/Sister".
*   **The Directional Error:** Answering "Grandson" when the question asked "How is the old man related to the boy?" (Answer should be Grandfather). Always read: **"How is [First Person] related to [Second Person]?"** -> Meaning, *What does the Second Person call the First Person?*
*   **Ignoring "Only Sibling":** "A is the only sibling of B." This means A and B are the only two children of their parents.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC MTS / GD)**
*   A is B's brother. C is A's mother. D is C's father. How is D related to A?
    *   *(Tree: D(m) -> C(f) -> A(m) - B. D is A's Grandfather. Answer: Maternal Grandfather).*

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL / CHSL)**
*   Pointing to a man, a woman said, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is the woman related to the man?
    *   *(Backward trace from "my": "my mother" = Woman's mother. "only daughter of [Woman's mother]" = The Woman herself! So, "His mother is [The Woman]". Therefore, the woman is his mother).*

**Level 5 (IBPS PO Prelims - Puzzle)**
*   There are 6 members in a family. P is the mother of Q. Q is the sister of R. S is the son of R. T is the brother of S. U is the father of T. How is U related to P?
    *   *(Tree: P(-) is mother of Q(-) and R. R has a son S(+) and T(+). U is father of T. Since R and U are parents of T, and U is father(+), R must be mother(-). So R and U are husband(=)wife. P is R's mother. U is R's husband. Therefore, U is P's Son-in-law).*

**Level 8 (SBI PO Prelims / CGL Mains - Coded)**
*   $A + B$ means A is father of B. $A - B$ means A is wife of B. $A \times B$ means A is brother of B. $A \div B$ means A is daughter of B.
*   If $P \div R + S + Q$, which of the following is true?
    *   *(Tree: P is daughter of R. R is father of S. S is father of Q.
    *   P(-) and S(+) are siblings. R(+) is their father. Q is S's child.
    *   Is P the aunt of Q? Yes! P is the sister of S, who is Q's father).*

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation - SBI PO Mains / RBI Grade B)**
*   Coded Blood Relation mixed with Seating Arrangement or Age. 
    *   *Example:* "A@B means A is the father of B and sits second to the right of B." (Requires drawing a family tree AND a circular arrangement simultaneously based on gender rules. The tree solves the arrangement).

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **Draw Clean Trees:** Do not scribble. Make your squares and circles distinct. Make generation lines explicitly vertical. A messy tree leads to a wrong answer 100% of the time.
2. **Never guess the final step:** Always put your finger on the "Second Person" in the tree and ask aloud, "What does this person call the First Person?"

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Indicating Form:** Ideal: 20s | Expert: 10s (Mental tracing only, no pen).
*   **Puzzle Form (3 questions):** Ideal: 1.5 mins | Expert: 45s (Drawing tree fast and accurately).
*   **Coded Form (Elimination):** Ideal: 30s | Expert: 15s (Using the gender/generation gap mathematical trick).

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `A is the brother of B.` -> What is B's gender? *(Answer: Unknown).*
2. `Pointing to a photo of a man, Ravi said, "He is the son of my father's only son."` -> Who is the man in the photo? *(Answer: Ravi's son, because Ravi is the only son of his father).*
3. `"How is the girl related to the boy?"` -> Are you looking for the girl's title or the boy's title? *(Answer: The girl's title. What the boy calls the girl).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Squares = Male. Circles = Female. No shape = Unknown.**
*   **Vertical = Generations. Horizontal = Siblings. Double Horizontal = Spouses.**
*   **The "MY" Rule:** Always start backward tracing from the word "my" in pointing questions.
*   **The Gender/Generation Trick:** For coded options, eliminate options where the gender of the target person is wrong or unknown (usually the last person in the string).
*   **Grammar Alert:** "who" / "which" = Second person. "and" / "but" = First person.
