# Indian Polity — Preamble, Fundamental Rights, DPSP, Fundamental Duties

## 1. Overview
This topic covers the philosophical and rights-based core of the Indian Constitution — the Preamble (its ideals), Fundamental Rights (enforceable citizen rights, Articles 12-35), Directive Principles of State Policy (non-enforceable governance guidelines, Articles 36-51), and Fundamental Duties (moral obligations, Article 51A). Exams test this heavily via Article-number matching and "which right/duty is this" scenario questions.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Preamble**
- Begins: "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA..."; adopted 26 November 1949, enforced 26 January 1950 **[High]**
- Original words: Sovereign, Democratic, Republic; added by 42nd Amendment (1976): Socialist, Secular, and "Integrity" (to unity) **[High]**
- Key ideals: Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty, Equality, Fraternity **[High]**
- Preamble is NOT enforceable in court but used to interpret the Constitution (per Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973) **[High]**
- Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) — established the "Basic Structure Doctrine"; Preamble is part of the Constitution and can be amended but not its basic structure **[High]**
- Berubari Union case (1960) — SC initially held Preamble is NOT part of the Constitution; later overruled by Kesavananda Bharati **[Medium]**

**Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35, Part III)**
- Originally 7 rights; Right to Property removed as Fundamental Right by 44th Amendment (1978), made a legal right (Article 300A) — now only 6 Fundamental Rights **[High]**
- Article 12 — defines "State" for Part III purposes **[Medium]**
- Article 13 — laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void **[Medium]**
- Right to Equality (Art 14-18) — Art 14 (equality before law), Art 15 (no discrimination), Art 16 (equal opportunity in public employment), Art 17 (abolition of untouchability), Art 18 (abolition of titles) **[High]**
- Right to Freedom (Art 19-22) — Art 19 (six freedoms: speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession), Art 20 (protection in conviction), Art 21 (protection of life & personal liberty — widely expanded by SC), Art 21A (Right to Education, added by 86th Amendment 2002), Art 22 (protection against arrest & detention) **[High]**
- Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24) — Art 23 (prohibits human trafficking & forced labour), Art 24 (prohibits child labour below 14 in hazardous jobs) **[High]**
- Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28) — Art 25 (freedom of conscience & religion), Art 26 (freedom to manage religious affairs), Art 27 (no tax for promotion of religion), Art 28 (freedom from religious instruction in state-funded schools) **[High]**
- Cultural and Educational Rights (Art 29-30) — Art 29 (protection of minority interests), Art 30 (minority right to establish educational institutions) **[Medium]**
- Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32) — allows moving Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights; called "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar **[High]**
- Writs under Article 32/226: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto **[High]**
- Fundamental Rights can be suspended during a National Emergency (Article 359), EXCEPT Articles 20 and 21 **[High]**

**Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) (Articles 36–51, Part IV)**
- Borrowed from the Irish Constitution **[High]**
- Non-justiciable (NOT enforceable by courts) but "fundamental in governance" — guide the State in policy-making **[High]**
- Classified broadly into: Socialistic (e.g., Art 39 — equal pay, distribution of wealth), Gandhian (e.g., Art 40 — village panchayats, Art 43 — cottage industries, Art 47 — prohibition), Liberal-Intellectual (e.g., Art 44 — Uniform Civil Code, Art 45 — early childhood care, Art 48 — scientific agriculture) **[Medium]**
- Article 44 — Uniform Civil Code (still not implemented nationally, frequently in news/debate) **[High]**
- Article 45 — free & compulsory education (originally for children up to 14, now linked with Art 21A) **[Medium]**
- Article 48A — protection of environment & wildlife (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976) **[Medium]**
- Article 50 — separation of judiciary from executive **[Low]**
- Article 51 — promotion of international peace & security **[Low]**
- In case of conflict between Fundamental Rights and DPSP, historically FR prevailed, but post-Kesavananda Bharati/Minerva Mills case, a balance/harmony between both is maintained **[Medium]**

**Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA)**
- Added by 42nd Amendment Act (1976) on recommendation of Swaran Singh Committee; inspired by erstwhile USSR Constitution **[High]**
- Originally 10 duties; 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002) — duty of parents/guardians to provide education to children aged 6-14 **[High]**
- Duties include: abide by Constitution, cherish freedom struggle ideals, uphold sovereignty/unity/integrity, defend the country, promote harmony, preserve composite culture, protect environment/wildlife, develop scientific temper, safeguard public property, strive for excellence **[High]**
- Fundamental Duties are also NON-justiciable (not enforceable by courts directly) **[High]**
- Applicable only to CITIZENS of India (unlike some Fundamental Rights which apply to all persons, citizens and non-citizens) **[Medium]**

## 3. Key Details Table

| Category | Article Range | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | Articles 12–35 | Justiciable (enforceable in court) |
| Directive Principles (DPSP) | Articles 36–51 | Non-justiciable (guiding principles) |
| Fundamental Duties | Article 51A | Non-justiciable (moral obligation) |

| Right | Article | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| Right to Equality | 14–18 | Includes abolition of untouchability (Art 17) |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 | Art 19 = six freedoms; Art 21 = life & liberty |
| Right against Exploitation | 23–24 | Bans trafficking, forced labour, child labour |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 | Freedom of conscience & religious practice |
| Cultural & Educational Rights | 29–30 | Protects minority interests/institutions |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32 | "Heart and Soul" (Ambedkar); enables writs |

| Writ | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Habeas Corpus | Produce a detained person before court |
| Mandamus | Command to perform a public duty |
| Prohibition | Stop a lower court from exceeding jurisdiction |
| Certiorari | Transfer/quash a case from lower to higher court |
| Quo Warranto | Question authority of a person holding public office |

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights vs DPSP | FR = justiciable (Part III, Art 12-35); DPSP = non-justiciable (Part IV, Art 36-51) — FR can be enforced in court, DPSP cannot |
| DPSP vs Fundamental Duties | DPSP = directs the STATE (government) on policy; Fundamental Duties = obligations of the CITIZEN — both non-justiciable but different subjects |
| Article 32 vs Article 226 | Article 32 = right to move SUPREME COURT only, for Fundamental Rights; Article 226 = power of HIGH COURTS, wider scope (FR + other legal rights) |
| Original vs added Preamble words | Original (1949): Sovereign, Democratic, Republic; ADDED by 42nd Amendment (1976): Socialist, Secular, Integrity |
| Original vs added Fundamental Duties | Original 10 duties = 42nd Amendment (1976); 11th duty (education for children 6-14) = added later by 86th Amendment (2002) |
| Right to Property status | It is NO LONGER a Fundamental Right (removed by 44th Amendment, 1978); now only a LEGAL right under Article 300A |
| Habeas Corpus vs Mandamus | Habeas Corpus = "produce the body" (illegal detention); Mandamus = "we command" (compel duty performance) — different purposes entirely |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **6 Fundamental Rights (after Right to Property removed)** — "**E**qual **F**reedom **E**xploits **R**eligion's **C**ultural **R**emedies" → **E**quality, **F**reedom, right against **E**xploitation, freedom of **R**eligion, **C**ultural-educational rights, constitutional **R**emedies.
- **Preamble added words (42nd Amendment)** — "**S**ocialist **S**ecular **I**ntegrity" = "**SSI**" — a short 3-letter tag for what was ADDED in 1976, separate from the 3 ORIGINAL words (Sovereign, Democratic, Republic).
- **DPSP borrowed source** — "DPSP = Directly Picked from Sham-rock (Ireland)" — Ireland's shamrock symbol as a visual hook for "borrowed from Irish Constitution."
- **5 Writs** — "**H**appy **M**onkeys **P**lay **C**ricket **Q**uietly" → **H**abeas Corpus, **M**andamus, **P**rohibition, **C**ertiorari, **Q**uo Warranto.
- **Fundamental Duties count** — "10 original + 1 bonus (2002) = 11" — remember it like a "bonus round" added 26 years after the original set, tied to child education (linking it to Art 21A logically).

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- Examiners frequently ask **"which word was NOT in the original Preamble"** — Socialist/Secular/Integrity are the trap answers (all added in 1976).
- **Justiciable vs non-justiciable** confusion is the most common trap across this entire topic — always identify if the question is about Fundamental Rights (enforceable) vs DPSP/Duties (not enforceable).
- Article number swapping — e.g., mixing Art 21 (life & liberty) with Art 21A (education) or Art 19 (six freedoms) with Art 20 (protection in conviction).
- "**Which Fundamental Right can be suspended during Emergency**" — the trap is forgetting that Articles 20 and 21 CANNOT be suspended even during a National Emergency.
- Right to Property trap — many questions test whether students know it was REMOVED from Part III and is now only a legal right (Art 300A), not a Fundamental Right anymore.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Preamble: adopted 1949, enforced 1950; original = Sovereign/Democratic/Republic; added (1976, 42nd Amdt) = Socialist/Secular/Integrity; non-justiciable but interpretive tool (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973 — Basic Structure Doctrine).
- Fundamental Rights (Part III, Art 12-35): 6 rights now (Property removed via 44th Amdt); Art 32 = "Heart and Soul"; Art 20 & 21 can't be suspended even in Emergency; 5 writs available.
- DPSP (Part IV, Art 36-51): borrowed from Ireland, non-justiciable, guides governance; Art 44 = UCC, Art 48A = environment protection.
- Fundamental Duties (Art 51A): added 1976 (42nd Amdt), 10 original + 1 added (2002, 86th Amdt) = 11 total; non-justiciable; apply only to citizens.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which words were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976)?
A) Sovereign, Democratic, Republic
B) Socialist, Secular, Integrity
C) Justice, Liberty, Equality
D) Federal, Parliamentary, Republic

**Q2.** Which Article is described as the "Heart and Soul" of the Indian Constitution by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar?
A) Article 21 B) Article 19 C) Article 32 D) Article 14

**Q3.** The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) were borrowed from the Constitution of:
A) USA B) UK C) Ireland D) Canada

**Q4.** Which two Fundamental Rights CANNOT be suspended even during a National Emergency?
A) Articles 14 and 19 B) Articles 20 and 21 C) Articles 25 and 26 D) Articles 32 and 226

**Q5.** Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment, based on which committee's recommendation?
A) 44th Amendment; Swaran Singh Committee
B) 42nd Amendment; Swaran Singh Committee
C) 86th Amendment; Sarkaria Committee
D) 42nd Amendment; Sarkaria Committee

## Answer Key
1. **B) Socialist, Secular, Integrity** — These three words were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976; the original 1949 Preamble had only Sovereign, Democratic, Republic.
2. **C) Article 32** — The Right to Constitutional Remedies, enabling citizens to directly approach the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights.
3. **C) Ireland** — The DPSP concept, along with several specific provisions, was borrowed from the Irish Constitution.
4. **B) Articles 20 and 21** — Protection in respect of conviction (Art 20) and protection of life & personal liberty (Art 21) remain in force even during a National Emergency.
5. **B) 42nd Amendment; Swaran Singh Committee** — Fundamental Duties were added via the 42nd Amendment (1976) based on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendations.
