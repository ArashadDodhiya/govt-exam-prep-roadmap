# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Time & Work?

**Time & Work** deals with the relationship between the **number of workers**, the **time they take**, and the **amount of work they complete**.

> A can build a wall in 10 days. B can build the same wall in 15 days.
> How long will they take **working together**?
> This is the essence of Time & Work.

### The Core Idea

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Work** | The total task to be completed (always treated as **1 unit** unless stated otherwise) |
| **Time** | Number of days/hours taken to finish the work |
| **Efficiency** | Amount of work done per day (or per hour) |
| **Rate of Work** | Same as efficiency = 1/Time (if one person does the whole work) |

### The Golden Relationship

> **Work = Efficiency × Time**
>
> **W = E × T**
>
> If A can do a job in **n** days, A's **1 day's work** = **1/n** (fraction of total work)

### The Key Players

| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Total Work | W | Usually taken as **1** (or LCM for shortcut) |
| Time taken | T | Days or hours to complete the work |
| Efficiency (per day work) | E | Fraction of work done per day = 1/T |
| Number of workers | N | Persons or machines doing the work |

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **2–4 questions per paper** | SSC, Banking, Railways — guaranteed |
| **Real-life relevance** | Project management, resource allocation |
| **Multiple sub-types** | Pipes, alternate days, wages — diverse questions |
| **Connects to Ratio & Proportion** | Efficiency ratios, work ratios |
| **Connects to Percentage** | Efficiency as percentage |
| **Speed-dependent** | Whoever solves faster wins |

## CONCEPT 1: Basic Time & Work — Fraction Method

### The Fundamental Rule

> If A can complete a work in **n days**, then
> **A's 1 day's work = 1/n**
>
> If B can complete the same work in **m days**, then
> **B's 1 day's work = 1/m**
>
> **(A+B)'s 1 day's work = 1/n + 1/m**
>
> **Time for A+B together = nm/(n+m) days**

### Example 1

A can do a work in 10 days. B can do the same work in 15 days. In how many days can they do it together?

A's 1 day's work = 1/10
B's 1 day's work = 1/15

(A+B)'s 1 day's work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6

Together: **6 days** ✅

Or directly: 10×15/(10+15) = 150/25 = **6 days**

### Example 2

A can do a work in 12 days. A and B together can do it in 8 days. In how many days can B alone do it?

B's 1 day's work = (A+B)'s work − A's work = 1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24

B alone: **24 days** ✅

### Key Properties

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| More workers → Less time | Inversely proportional (same total work) |
| Combined rate = Sum of individual rates | 1/T_together = 1/T_A + 1/T_B |
| Total work is always constant | = 1 unit in fraction method |

## CONCEPT 2: LCM Method (THE EXAM SHORTCUT — MOST IMPORTANT)

### Why LCM Method?

The fraction method requires adding fractions → messy calculations.
The LCM method converts everything to **whole numbers** → lightning fast.

### How It Works

> **Step 1**: Take LCM of all given times → This = **Total Work (in units)**
> **Step 2**: Each person's efficiency = Total Work / Their time
> **Step 3**: Combined efficiency = Sum of individual efficiencies
> **Step 4**: Time together = Total Work / Combined efficiency

### Example 1

A: 10 days, B: 15 days. Together?

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| LCM(10, 15) = 30 | Total work = 30 units |
| A's efficiency | 30/10 = 3 units/day |
| B's efficiency | 30/15 = 2 units/day |
| Combined | 3 + 2 = 5 units/day |
| Time together | 30/5 = **6 days** ✅ |

### Example 2

A: 12 days, B: 18 days, C: 36 days. Together?

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| LCM(12, 18, 36) = 36 | Total work = 36 units |
| A's efficiency | 36/12 = 3 units/day |
| B's efficiency | 36/18 = 2 units/day |
| C's efficiency | 36/36 = 1 unit/day |
| Combined | 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 units/day |
| Time together | 36/6 = **6 days** ✅ |

### Example 3

A: 20 days, B: 30 days. A+B together, find B alone given together = 12 days.

LCM(20, 12) = 60. Total work = 60 units.
A = 60/20 = 3 units/day.
(A+B) = 60/12 = 5 units/day.
B = 5 − 3 = 2 units/day.
B alone = 60/2 = **30 days** ✅

### Why This is Better

| Fraction Method | LCM Method |
|---|---|
| 1/10 + 1/15 = 5/30 = 1/6 | 3 + 2 = 5 → 30/5 = 6 |
| Adding fractions, finding LCD | Simple addition of whole numbers |
| Prone to calculation errors | Clean and fast |

> **USE LCM METHOD IN EXAMS — ALWAYS.**

## CONCEPT 3: A Works Alone, Then B Joins / A Leaves Midway

### Pattern 1: A works for some days alone, then A+B work together

> A works alone for **d** days, then B joins.
> Work done by A in d days = d × (A's efficiency)
> Remaining work = Total − d × (A's efficiency)
> Time for remaining = Remaining work / (A+B's combined efficiency)

### Example

A: 20 days, B: 30 days. A works alone for 5 days, then B joins. When will the work be complete?

LCM(20, 30) = 60. Total work = 60 units.
A = 60/20 = 3 units/day. B = 60/30 = 2 units/day.

A works alone for 5 days: 5 × 3 = 15 units done.
Remaining: 60 − 15 = 45 units.
A+B together: 3 + 2 = 5 units/day.
Time for remaining: 45/5 = 9 days.

Total time: 5 + 9 = **14 days** ✅

### Pattern 2: A and B start together, then A leaves

> A and B work together for **d** days, then A leaves. B finishes the rest.
> Work done in d days = d × (A+B's efficiency)
> Remaining work = Total − d × (A+B's efficiency)
> Time for remaining = Remaining / B's efficiency

### Example

A: 10 days, B: 15 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. B finishes the rest.

LCM(10, 15) = 30. Total = 30 units.
A = 3, B = 2 units/day. Combined = 5 units/day.

Work in 4 days: 4 × 5 = 20 units.
Remaining: 30 − 20 = 10 units.
B alone: 10/2 = 5 days.

Total time: 4 + 5 = **9 days** ✅
(A worked for 4 days, B worked for 9 days.)

## CONCEPT 4: Work & Wages

### The Core Rule

> **Wages are distributed in the ratio of WORK DONE** (not time spent, not the number of people).
>
> **Work done = Efficiency × Time worked**
>
> If efficiency is same, wages ∝ Time worked.
> If time worked is same, wages ∝ Efficiency.

### Example 1

A can do a work in 6 days, B in 8 days. They complete it together. Total wage = ₹2800. Find each person's share.

A's efficiency : B's efficiency = 1/6 : 1/8 = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3

A's share = 4/7 × 2800 = **₹1600**
B's share = 3/7 × 2800 = **₹1200** ✅

### Example 2 (Partial Work)

A and B work together, but A leaves after 3 days and B finishes the remaining work in 5 more days. A: 12 days, B: 16 days. Total wage ₹3600.

LCM(12, 16) = 48. A = 4 units/day. B = 3 units/day.

A works 3 days: 3 × 4 = 12 units.
B works 8 days (3 together + 5 alone): 8 × 3 = 24 units.

Work ratio = A : B = 12 : 24 = 1 : 2
A's share = 1/3 × 3600 = **₹1200**
B's share = 2/3 × 3600 = **₹2400** ✅

### Key Principle

> **Whoever does more work, gets more wage.**
> Not who was present longer, but who PRODUCED more.

## CONCEPT 5: Pipes & Cisterns

### The Analogy

| Time & Work | Pipes & Cisterns |
|---|---|
| Worker | Pipe |
| Building / Constructing | Filling the tank |
| Destroying / Breaking | Emptying the tank (leak/outlet) |
| Work done | Tank filled |

### The Core Difference

> **Filling pipe** → POSITIVE efficiency (adds work)
> **Emptying pipe / Leak** → NEGATIVE efficiency (removes work)
>
> Net filling rate = (Filling rate) − (Emptying rate)

### Example 1: Two Filling Pipes

Pipe A fills a tank in 10 hours. Pipe B fills it in 15 hours. Together?

Exactly like Time & Work: LCM(10, 15) = 30.
A = 3 units/hr. B = 2 units/hr. Combined = 5 units/hr.
Time = 30/5 = **6 hours** ✅

### Example 2: Filling + Emptying

Pipe A fills in 12 hours. Pipe B empties in 18 hours. Both open together?

LCM(12, 18) = 36. Total = 36 units.
A (fills) = +3 units/hr.
B (empties) = −2 units/hr.
Net = 3 − 2 = +1 unit/hr.
Time to fill = 36/1 = **36 hours** ✅

### Example 3: Filling + Emptying (Emptying faster)

Pipe A fills in 6 hours. Pipe B empties in 4 hours. Both open, tank is full.

LCM(6, 4) = 12.
A = +2 units/hr. B = −3 units/hr.
Net = 2 − 3 = −1 unit/hr. (Tank is emptying!)
Time to empty = 12/1 = **12 hours** ✅

### Example 4: Leak Problem

A pipe fills a tank in 8 hours. Due to a leak, it takes 10 hours. Find leak's emptying time.

LCM(8, 10) = 40. Total = 40 units.
Pipe = +5 units/hr. Pipe + Leak = +4 units/hr.
Leak = 4 − 5 = −1 unit/hr. (empties 1 unit/hr)
Leak alone empties in: 40/1 = **40 hours** ✅

### Key Patterns

| Scenario | Net Rate |
|---|---|
| All pipes fill | Sum of filling rates |
| All pipes empty | Sum of emptying rates |
| Mix of fill + empty | Fill rates − Empty rates |
| Net positive | Tank fills |
| Net negative | Tank empties |
| Net = 0 | Tank never fills/empties |

## CONCEPT 6: Alternate Days Work

### The Concept

> A and B work on **alternate days** (not together).
> Day 1: A works alone.
> Day 2: B works alone.
> Day 3: A works alone.
> … and so on.

### Strategy

> **Find work done in 2-day cycle** (1 day A + 1 day B).
> Then calculate how many complete cycles fit before the work is done.
> Handle the remainder.

### Example 1

A: 10 days, B: 15 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A.

LCM(10, 15) = 30. Total = 30 units.
A = 3 units/day. B = 2 units/day.

2-day cycle: A + B = 3 + 2 = 5 units in 2 days.
Complete cycles in 30 units: 30/5 = 6 cycles = 12 days.

Work finishes in exactly **12 days** ✅

### Example 2

A: 12 days, B: 15 days. Alternate days, starting with A.

LCM(12, 15) = 60. Total = 60 units.
A = 5 units/day. B = 4 units/day.

2-day cycle: 5 + 4 = 9 units in 2 days.
After 6 cycles (12 days): 6 × 9 = 54 units done.
Remaining: 60 − 54 = 6 units.

Day 13 is A's turn: A does 5 units. Total = 59 units.
Remaining: 1 unit.
Day 14 is B's turn: B's rate = 4 units/day. Time for 1 unit = 1/4 day.

Total = 13 + 1/4 = **13¼ days** ✅

### Watch Out!

> Always check **who starts**. The answer changes if B starts instead of A.
>
> If "they work on alternate days" without specifying who starts, the question is ambiguous (but usually specifies or says "A starting first").

## CONCEPT 7: Efficiency-Based Problems

### Efficiency as Ratio

> If A is **twice as efficient** as B, then:
> A's efficiency = 2 × B's efficiency
>
> If A does a work in **n** days, B takes **2n** days (double the time).
>
> **Efficiency ∝ 1/Time** (inversely proportional)

### Efficiency as Percentage

> If A is **40% more efficient** than B:
> A's efficiency = 1.4 × B's efficiency
>
> If B takes **n** days, A takes **n/1.4 = 5n/7 days**

### Example 1

A is twice as efficient as B. B can do a work in 18 days. In how many days can they do it together?

B = 18 days. A is twice as efficient → A = 18/2 = 9 days.

LCM(9, 18) = 18. Total = 18 units.
A = 2 units/day. B = 1 unit/day.
Combined = 3 units/day.
Time = 18/3 = **6 days** ✅

### Example 2

A is 25% more efficient than B. Together they finish a work in 20 days. How long does A take alone?

Let B's efficiency = 4 units. A's efficiency = 5 units (25% more).
Combined = 9 units/day.

Total work = 9 × 20 = 180 units.
A alone = 180/5 = **36 days**
B alone = 180/4 = **45 days** ✅

### Example 3

A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. They work together for 4 days. Then A's efficiency becomes half. How many more days to finish?

LCM(15, 20) = 60. Total = 60 units.
A = 4, B = 3 units/day. Combined = 7 units/day.

First 4 days: 4 × 7 = 28 units. Remaining = 32 units.
A's new efficiency = 4/2 = 2 units/day. New combined = 2 + 3 = 5 units/day.
Time = 32/5 = **6.4 days = 6⅖ days** ✅

## CONCEPT 8: Group Work (MDH / Man-Days Formula)

### The Core Formula

> **M₁ × D₁ × H₁ = M₂ × D₂ × H₂** (when total work is same)
>
> With work included:
> **M₁ × D₁ × H₁ / W₁ = M₂ × D₂ × H₂ / W₂**

Where:
- M = Number of men/workers
- D = Number of days
- H = Hours per day
- W = Amount of work

### Example 1

10 men can do a work in 20 days. In how many days can 25 men do it?

M₁D₁ = M₂D₂
10 × 20 = 25 × D₂
D₂ = 200/25 = **8 days** ✅

### Example 2

12 men working 8 hours/day finish a work in 10 days. How many men are needed to finish the same work in 6 days working 10 hours/day?

M₁ × D₁ × H₁ = M₂ × D₂ × H₂
12 × 10 × 8 = M₂ × 6 × 10
960 = 60 × M₂
M₂ = **16 men** ✅

### Example 3 (Different Work Quantities)

15 men can build 3 walls in 12 days working 8 hours/day. How many days will 20 men take to build 5 walls working 6 hours/day?

M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂
15 × 12 × 8 / 3 = 20 × D₂ × 6 / 5
1440/3 = 120D₂/5
480 = 24D₂
D₂ = **20 days** ✅

### With Efficiency Included

> **M₁ × D₁ × H₁ × E₁ / W₁ = M₂ × D₂ × H₂ × E₂ / W₂**

If a man is twice as efficient as a boy:
E_man = 2, E_boy = 1 (or treat 1 man = 2 boys)

### Example 4

3 men and 4 boys can do a work in 8 days. 4 men and 4 boys can do it in 6 days. How long will 2 men and 4 boys take?

Let 1 man = m units/day, 1 boy = b units/day.

Total work = (3m + 4b) × 8 = 24m + 32b … (i)
Total work = (4m + 4b) × 6 = 24m + 24b … (ii)

(i) = (ii): 24m + 32b = 24m + 24b → 8b = 0? 

Wait — let me redo:
From (i) and (ii): 24m + 32b = 24m + 24b gives 8b = 0, which is wrong.

Correction — these give total work equal:
8(3m + 4b) = 6(4m + 4b)
24m + 32b = 24m + 24b
32b − 24b = 24m − 24m
8b = 0 → This means the problem setup has an issue.

Let me use a correct problem: 2 men and 3 boys can do a work in 10 days. 3 men and 2 boys can do it in 8 days. Ratio of man to boy efficiency?

10(2m + 3b) = 8(3m + 2b)
20m + 30b = 24m + 16b
14b = 4m
m/b = 14/4 = **7/2**

So 1 man = 3.5 boys.

## CONCEPT 9: Three or More Workers — Combinations

### Pattern: A+B, B+C, A+C given → Find A, B, C individually

> (A+B) + (B+C) + (A+C) = 2(A+B+C)
>
> **A+B+C = [(A+B) + (B+C) + (A+C)] / 2**
>
> Then: A = (A+B+C) − (B+C), etc.

### Example

A+B: 10 days, B+C: 12 days, A+C: 15 days.

LCM(10, 12, 15) = 60. Total = 60 units.

A+B = 6 units/day. B+C = 5 units/day. A+C = 4 units/day.

2(A+B+C) = 6 + 5 + 4 = 15
A+B+C = 7.5 units/day.

A = 7.5 − 5 = 2.5 units/day → A alone = 60/2.5 = **24 days**
B = 7.5 − 4 = 3.5 units/day → B alone = 60/3.5 = **120/7 ≈ 17.14 days**
C = 7.5 − 6 = 1.5 units/day → C alone = 60/1.5 = **40 days** ✅

---

## CONCEPT 10: Work Done as a Fraction / Remaining Work

### Pattern 1: A works for d days, what fraction is done?

> Fraction done = d × (1/n) = d/n (if A takes n days for full work)

### Pattern 2: Fraction remaining

> Fraction remaining = 1 − d/n

### Example

A can do a work in 20 days. After working for 8 days, B finishes the remaining work in 9 days. How long would B take alone?

A does in 8 days: 8/20 = 2/5 of the work.
Remaining: 1 − 2/5 = 3/5.
B does 3/5 in 9 days.
B's full work time = 9 × (5/3) = **15 days** ✅

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Product-over-Sum for Two Workers

> **Time together = (a × b) / (a + b)**

A: 12 days, B: 18 days. Together = 12×18/(12+18) = 216/30 = **7.2 days** = **36/5 days**

---

## Shortcut 2: LCM Method (Already covered, but emphasizing)

This is THE shortcut. For every problem:
1. Take LCM → Total work.
2. Divide by individual times → Efficiencies.
3. Add/subtract efficiencies → Combined rate.
4. Divide total work by combined → Answer.

---

## Shortcut 3: If A is x times as efficient as B

> A takes **n** days.
> B takes **xn** days.
> Together: **xn/(x+1)** days.

If A is 3× as efficient as B, and A takes 12 days:
B takes 36 days. Together = 36/4 = **9 days**.

---

## Shortcut 4: Efficiency Percentage Shortcut

> If A is **p%** more efficient than B, and B takes **T** days:
> A takes **T × 100/(100+p)** days

B: 21 days, A is 40% more: A = 21 × 100/140 = 21 × 5/7 = **15 days**

---

## Shortcut 5: Pipe + Leak

> If a pipe fills in **a** hours, but with a leak it fills in **b** hours (b > a):
> **Leak empties in ab/(b−a) hours**

Pipe: 6 hrs, with leak: 8 hrs. Leak = 6×8/(8−6) = 48/2 = **24 hours** ✅

---

## Shortcut 6: Three Workers — Quick Combination

If A+B = p, B+C = q, A+C = r (efficiencies):
- A = (p + r − q) / 2
- B = (p + q − r) / 2
- C = (q + r − p) / 2

---

## Shortcut 7: Remaining Work Scaling

> If A does **f** fraction of work in **d** days,
> Time for remaining **(1−f)** fraction at same rate = **d × (1−f)/f** days

A does 2/5 in 8 days. Time for remaining 3/5 = 8 × (3/5)/(2/5) = 8 × 3/2 = **12 days**

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Work ≠ Wages

| Work | Wages |
|---|---|
| Total output produced | Money distributed for the work |
| All workers do SAME total work | Wages are split by INDIVIDUAL contribution |
| Worker present longer ≠ more wages necessarily | Efficient worker who produces more gets more |

---

## Confusion 2: Pipe Fills vs Empties — Sign Convention

| Pipe Type | Efficiency Sign |
|---|---|
| Inlet / Filling pipe | **POSITIVE** |
| Outlet / Emptying pipe / Leak | **NEGATIVE** |

If net efficiency is negative → Tank empties (not fills).
If net = 0 → Tank level doesn't change.

---

## Confusion 3: Alternate Days ≠ Working Together

| Together | Alternate Days |
|---|---|
| Both work every day | Only one works each day |
| Combined efficiency per day | Take 2-day cycle |
| Faster than both | Slower than together, faster than either alone |

---

## Confusion 4: \"A and B can do in 10 days\" vs \"A can do in 10 days\"

Read carefully:
- \"A and B together can do…\" → Combined time
- \"A can do…\" → A's individual time

Students often confuse who is doing the work.

---

## Confusion 5: Man-Days vs Efficiency

In M₁D₁ = M₂D₂, we assume all workers are **equally efficient**.

If they have different efficiencies, use: M₁D₁E₁ = M₂D₂E₂

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


| # | Trap | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **\"A and B together\" confused with \"A or B alone\"** | Read carefully: together vs alone |
| 2 | **Leak/emptying pipe: forgetting the negative sign** | Empty pipe = negative efficiency |
| 3 | **Alternate days: wrong person starts** | Check who starts; answer changes |
| 4 | **Wages based on days present vs work done** | Wages ∝ Work Done, NOT days present |
| 5 | **Man-days: assuming different efficiencies are equal** | Only use MDH when workers are homogeneous |
| 6 | **Fraction done vs fraction remaining** | If 2/5 done, remaining = 3/5 (not 2/5) |
| 7 | **Hours per day ignored in MDH formula** | Include H: M₁D₁H₁ = M₂D₂H₂ |
| 8 | **\"A is 3× as efficient\" → students make A's time 3×** | A is FASTER → A's time = B's time / 3 |
| 9 | **Pipe problem: net rate = 0 means tank never fills** | Don't assume it fills eventually |

# STEP 9 — SOLVING STRATEGY


| Question Type | Fastest Method |
|---|---|
| Two workers together | LCM method (always) |
| Three+ workers together | LCM method |
| Find one from combined | LCM → Subtract efficiencies |
| Wages | Find work-done ratio → split proportionally |
| Pipes (fill/empty) | LCM → Sign convention (+/−) |
| Alternate days | 2-day cycle → count → handle remainder |
| Efficiency ratio/percentage | Convert efficiency ratio → find times |
| Man-Days | Direct formula M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂ |
| Midway join/leave | Split into phases, track work done |
| Fraction remaining | Subtract done fraction from 1 |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


## Speed Rule 1: Always Use LCM Method

Never use fraction method in exams. LCM converts fractions to whole numbers = faster mental math.

## Speed Rule 2: Product-over-Sum for 2 Workers

For just 2 workers: T = ab/(a+b). Memorize this — it's one step.

## Speed Rule 3: Know Common LCMs Instantly

| Numbers | LCM |
|---|---|
| 10, 15 | 30 |
| 12, 18 | 36 |
| 12, 15, 20 | 60 |
| 10, 12, 15 | 60 |
| 6, 8, 12 | 24 |
| 15, 20, 30 | 60 |
| 8, 12, 16 | 48 |

## Speed Rule 4: Pipes — Same as Workers with Signs

Don't learn a separate method. Just use LCM with +/− signs.

## Speed Rule 5: Alternate Days — Count Full Cycles First

Don't go day-by-day. Calculate the 2-day (or 3-day) cycle output, divide total work by it, handle remainder.

## Speed Rule 6: Efficiency Ratio → Time Ratio (Inverse)

If efficiency ratio = 3:2, time ratio = 2:3.
This saves conversion steps.

# STEP 11 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES


## Mnemonic 1: LCM Method Steps

> **\"LEDA\"** — **L**CM → **E**fficiency → **D**ivide → **A**nswer
>
> Take LCM (total work), find Efficiency (divide), combine and Divide, get Answer.

## Mnemonic 2: Pipes Sign Convention

> **\"Fill is Positive, Empty is Negative\"** → **FP-EN**
>
> Think: \"Filling adds, Emptying subtracts\"

## Mnemonic 3: Wages Rule

> **\"Work करो, Wage लो\"** (Do Work, Get Wage)
> 
> Wages go to the one who DOES the work, proportional to how MUCH work.

## Mnemonic 4: MDH Formula

> **\"M-D-H over W\"** — \"Men, Days, Hours over Work = constant\"
>
> M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂

## Mnemonic 5: Efficiency and Time

> **\"Fast worker, Less time\"**
> **\"Efficient worker, Quick finisher\"**
>
> Efficiency ↑ means Time ↓ (inversely proportional)

## Story Trick: Why Combined Workers are Faster

> Imagine filling a bucket with 2 taps instead of 1.
> Each tap adds water → bucket fills faster.
> 2 taps ≠ twice as fast (unless both have same flow).
> Combined flow = sum of individual flows.

**Q1.** A can do a work in 15 days, B in 20 days. Together?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
LCM(15,20) = 60. A=4, B=3. Together=7. Time = 60/7 = **8⁴⁄₇ days**
</details>

**Q2.** A and B together can do a work in 12 days. A alone in 20 days. B alone?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
LCM(12,20) = 60. A+B=5, A=3. B=2. B alone = 60/2 = **30 days**
</details>

**Q3.** Pipe A fills a tank in 10 hrs, Pipe B empties in 15 hrs. Both open, time to fill?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
LCM(10,15) = 30. A=+3, B=−2. Net=1. Time = 30/1 = **30 hours**
</details>

**Q4.** A: 10 days, B: 15 days. Alternate days, A starts. Total time?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
LCM=30. A=3, B=2. 2-day cycle = 5 units. 30/5 = 6 cycles = **12 days**
</details>

**Q5.** A can do in 6 days, B in 8 days. Total wage ₹2100. A's share?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Efficiency ratio A:B = 1/6 : 1/8 = 4:3. A's share = 4/7 × 2100 = **₹1200**
</details>

**Q6.** 10 men finish work in 12 days. How many men needed for 8 days?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
10 × 12 = M × 8 → M = 120/8 = **15 men**
</details>

**Q7.** A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish in 14 days. A alone?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Let B's efficiency = 1, A's = 2. Combined = 3. Total work = 3 × 14 = 42. A alone = 42/2 = **21 days**
</details>

**Q8.** A: 12 days, B: 18 days. A works 4 days alone, then B joins. When is work finished?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
LCM=36. A=3, B=2. A works 4 days: 12 done. Remaining = 24. Combined = 5. Time = 24/5 = 4.8 days. Total = 4 + 4.8 = **8.8 days = 8⅘ days**
</details>

**Q9.** Pipe A fills in 6 hrs. With a leak, it takes 9 hrs. Leak empties the full tank in?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Leak = 6×9/(9−6) = 54/3 = **18 hours**
</details>

**Q10.** A does 2/5 of a work in 8 days. B finishes the rest in 12 days. B alone for the full work?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
B does 3/5 in 12 days. Full work = 12 × 5/3 = **20 days**
</details>

# STEP 14 — REVISION PACK


```
TIME & WORK — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

BASIC: A does work in n days → 1 day's work = 1/n.
TOGETHER: 1/T = 1/a + 1/b. Or T = ab/(a+b).

LCM METHOD (USE THIS!):
  LCM of times = Total Work.
  Efficiency = Total Work / Time.
  Combined efficiency = Sum of individual.
  Answer = Total Work / Combined efficiency.

PIPES & CISTERNS:
  Fill pipe = + efficiency.
  Empty pipe / Leak = − efficiency.
  Net = Fill − Empty.
  Leak time = ab/(b−a) where b > a (with leak vs without).

ALTERNATE DAYS:
  2-day cycle work = A + B (1 day each).
  Count full cycles. Handle remainder.
  WHO STARTS matters!

WORK & WAGES:
  Wages ∝ Work Done (not days present).
  Work Done = Efficiency × Days Worked.

EFFICIENCY:
  A is x times efficient as B → A's time = B's time / x.
  Efficiency ∝ 1/Time.

MAN-DAYS: M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂

THREE WORKERS:
  A+B+C = [(A+B) + (B+C) + (A+C)] / 2
  Individual = Total − opposite pair.
```

## 🚨 Trap Sheet

| Trap | Defense |
|---|---|
| Fill vs Empty sign | Fill = +, Empty = − |
| Together vs Alone confusion | Read carefully |
| Alternate days: wrong starter | Check who starts |
| Wages ∝ days present | NO! Wages ∝ work done |
| A 3× efficient → time 3× | NO! Time = 1/3 |
| MDH with different efficiencies | Add E factor |
| Net rate = 0 in pipes | Tank never fills/empties |
