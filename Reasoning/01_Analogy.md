# 01. Analogy (Word, Letter, Number)

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Analogy?**
Analogy literally means "Similarity" or "Corresponding Relationship." The standard format is **A : B :: C : ?**. It requires you to identify the logical relationship between the first pair (A and B) and apply that exact same relationship to the second pair (C and the missing term) to find the answer.

**Why do examiners test this?**
Examiners use Analogy to evaluate three main attributes under time pressure:
1. **General Intelligence & Knowledge** (Word Analogy)
2. **Mathematical fluency and pattern recognition** (Number Analogy)
3. **Observation and coding speed** (Letter Analogy)

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Directional Trap:** If A is the "Capital" of B, they will purposefully include an option where the 2nd word is the capital of the 1st word. Direction strictly matters (A → B is NOT the same as B → A).
2. **The Secondary Logic Trap (Numbers):** Example: `4 : 16 :: 5 : ?`. Options given: `20, 25`. If the logic is $x : x^2$, the answer is 25. If the logic is $x : x \times 4$, the answer is 20. Examiners test if you know the **Priority Rule** of patterns.
3. **The "Similar but Wrong" Trap:** Giving words that are closely associated in real life but do not share the exact structural or grammatical relationship as the first pair.

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations
There are three main pillars of Analogy in Indian Government Exams. We must cover every variation.

### 1. Word Analogy (High Frequency - SSC, State PSC, Railways)
*   **Country / State & Capital:** India : New Delhi :: Japan : Tokyo
*   **Country & Currency:** USA : Dollar :: UK : Pound
*   **Worker & Tool:** Blacksmith : Anvil :: Carpenter : Saw
*   **Worker & Workplace:** Doctor : Hospital :: Chef : Kitchen
*   **Instrument & Measurement:** Barometer : Pressure :: Seismograph : Earthquakes
*   **Quantity & Unit:** Force : Newton :: Current : Ampere
*   **Animal & Young One:** Dog : Puppy :: Kangaroo : Joey
*   **Animal & Sound:** Lion : Roar :: Snake : Hiss
*   **Synonyms / Antonyms:** Huge : Gigantic :: Tiny : Miniature
*   **Product & Raw Material:** Paper : Pulp :: Furniture : Wood
*   **Study & Topic:** Botany : Plants :: Seismology : Earthquakes

### 2. Number Analogy (Very High Frequency - SSC CGL, CHSL, Railways)
*   **Squares/Cubes:** $x : x^2$ or $x : x^3$
*   **Square/Cube + Constant:** $x : x^2 + y$ or $x : x^3 - y$
*   **Multiplication/Division:** $x : x \times y$
*   **Prime Numbers:** Sequence of primes (e.g., `2 : 3 :: 5 : 7`)
*   **Digital Sum/Product:** `453 : 12` (4+5+3) `:: 721 : 10` (7+2+1)
*   **N and $N^2+N$ or $N^3+N$:** Exceptionally common in TCS-pattern SSC exams.

### 3. Letter/Alphabet Analogy (High Frequency - Banking, SSC, CSAT)
*   **Positional Addition/Subtraction:** A : C (+2) :: M : O (+2)
*   **Opposite Letters:** A : Z :: B : Y (Sum of positional values = 27)
*   **Vowel/Consonant Logic:** Number of vowels replaced by digits, or vowels shifted differently than consonants.
*   **Reverse Order / Cross Coding:** ABCD : DCBA

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **Identify the Type:** Are you dealing with Word, Number, or Letter?
2. **For Words:** Create a rigid **"Sentence Frame"**. Do not just "feel" the relationship. Explicitly say in your mind: "A is a tool primarily used by B." Apply this exact frame to C and the options. If it doesn't fit perfectly, reject the option.
3. **For Numbers:** You must follow the **BODMAS of Analogy (The Priority Rule)**. If multiple logics work, you MUST prioritize in this order:
    1. Prime Numbers
    2. Cubes (and Cube roots)
    3. Squares (and Square roots)
    4. Multiplication / Division
    5. Addition / Subtraction
4. **For Letters:** Instantly convert letters to their numerical positional values (A=1, B=2... Z=26) mentally. Do not count on your fingers. Look for shifts (+2, -1) or opposite pairs.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional
*   Look closely at A and B. Find the relation. Apply it to C. Scan options to find the match.
*   *When to use:* Best for Word Analogies and simple letter shifts.

### Method 2: Fast Method (Option Elimination)
*   Look at C. Look at the 4 options. Often, the category of C dictates the answer. If C is a Disease, and only one option is a Body Part affected by a disease, pick it instantly without hyper-analyzing A and B.
*   *When to use:* When running out of time, or when A and B have a very obscure relationship.

### Method 3: Expert Method (Pattern Prediction)
*   **Numbers:** Experts do not check addition first. They see a number like `68` and immediately recognize it as $4^3 + 4$. They see `120` and see $11^2 - 1$ or $5^3 - 5$. 
*   **Letters:** Experts see opposite pairs (M-N, G-T) instantly without needing to calculate $27 - x$.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Are numbers very large (3 or 4 digits)?** -> Immediately think Digital Sum or Add/Subtract a large constant (e.g., +1111).
*   **Are numbers growing exponentially from A to B?** -> Think Cubes or Multiplication.
*   **Are letters completely different but the string is the same length?** -> Think Opposite Letters or Reverse Cross-Coding.
*   **Do the words belong to Science/Geography?** -> Think Units, Instruments, Capitals.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
To solve within 10 seconds, you MUST memorize:
1. **Alphabet Positions:** Memorize the word **EJOTY** (5, 10, 15, 20, 25) to quickly find nearby letters.
2. **Opposite Letters (Sum = 27):**
    *   A-Z (Azad)
    *   B-Y (Boy)
    *   C-X (Crux)
    *   D-W (Dew)
    *   E-V (Evening)
    *   F-U (Fun)
    *   G-T (GT Road)
    *   H-S (High School)
    *   I-R (Indian Railway)
    *   J-Q (Jungle Queen)
    *   K-P (Kanpur / PK)
    *   L-O (Love)
    *   M-N (Man)
3. **Number Mastery:** Memorize Squares up to 30, Cubes up to 15. Prime numbers up to 100.

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Ignoring Priority Rule (Numbers):** Marking a multiplication logic answer when a Square/Cube logic is also present in the options. (Cube/Square logic always wins over multiplication).
*   **Reversing Direction (Words):** If A is the worker and B is the tool, marking an option where C is the tool and D is the worker. This is the #1 reason students lose marks in easy questions.
*   **Not checking all options:** Sometimes Option A looks correct via addition, but Option C is correct via Prime number logic. 

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression
**Level 1 (Very Easy)**
*   Word: Moon : Satellite :: Earth : ? (Answer: Planet)
*   Number: 5 : 25 :: 6 : ? (Answer: 36)

**Level 3 (Moderate)**
*   Number: 8 : 28 :: 27 : ?
    *   Logic: $2^3$ : $3^3 + 1$. So, $3^3$ : $4^3 + 1 = 65$.

**Level 5 (IBPS PO / SSC CGL Tier-1)**
*   Letter: BUCKET : ACTVBDJLDFSU :: BONUS : ?
    *   Logic: Each letter in the first word is replaced by the letter immediately before and after it in the alphabet (B -> A, C).

**Level 8 (Mixed High-Level - TCS Pattern)**
*   Number: 13 : 109 :: 24 : ?
    *   Logic: $13 \times 8 + 5 = 109$. High-level number analogy requires fast checking of multiples and remainders.

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation)**
*   Number: 42 : 56 :: 72 : ?
    *   Logic 1 (Factors): $6 \times 7$ : $7 \times 8$ :: $8 \times 9$ : $9 \times 10 = 90$.
    *   Logic 2 ($N^2 + N$): $6^2 + 6$ : $7^2 + 7$ :: $8^2 + 8$ : $9^2 + 9 = 90$.

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
When practicing Analogy independently:
1. Look at the question and start your mental timer.
2. Find the logic. If you can't find it in 30-40 seconds (especially for numbers), **SKIP IT**.
3. After solving, if you got it wrong or missed the logic, write the pattern down in a separate "Mistake Notebook".

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Word Analogy:**
    *   Ideal Time: 10 seconds.
    *   Expert Time: 5 seconds. (It relies on GK; you either know it instantly or you don't).
*   **Letter Analogy:**
    *   Ideal Time: 20 seconds.
    *   Expert Time: 10 seconds. (Requires instant positional value recall).
*   **Number Analogy:**
    *   Ideal Time: 30-40 seconds.
    *   Expert Time: 15-20 seconds. (If you don't see it in 30s in the exam, skip to avoid a time trap).

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Train your brain to identify these instantly:
1. `11 : 1331` -> Instant Cube recognition.
2. `7 : 56` -> Instant $N : N^2 + N$ recognition.
3. `CE : GI` -> Instant Positional jump (+2,+2).
4. `Ornithologist : Birds` -> Instant "Study of" recognition.
5. `343 : 10` -> Instant Digital Sum recognition (3+4+3).

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **Priority for Numbers:** Prime > Cube > Square > Multiply > Add.
*   **Letters:** Memorize Opposite words (A-Z, B-Y, GT, HS, KP). Memorize EJOTY.
*   **Words:** Constantly update your Static GK (Capitals, Currencies, Instruments, Animal sounds/homes).
*   **Golden Rule:** The A : B direction MUST perfectly match the C : D direction.
