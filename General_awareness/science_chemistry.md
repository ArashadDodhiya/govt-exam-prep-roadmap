# General Science — Chemistry

## 1. Overview
Chemistry in GK exams covers atomic structure, the periodic table, bonding, acid-base chemistry, metals/non-metals, carbon compounds, electrochemistry, and everyday applications like soaps and alloys. Exams favor direct factual recall — element symbols, reaction names, and practical applications — over calculation-heavy problems.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Atomic Structure**
- Atom — smallest unit of matter; consists of Protons (positive, in nucleus), Neutrons (neutral, in nucleus), Electrons (negative, orbiting nucleus) **[High]**
- Atomic Number = number of protons (defines the element); Mass Number = protons + neutrons **[High]**
- Isotopes — atoms of the same element with different number of neutrons (same atomic number, different mass number) **[High]**
- J.J. Thomson — discovered the electron (1897); Ernest Rutherford — discovered the nucleus/proton (gold foil experiment); James Chadwick — discovered the neutron (1932) **[High]**
- Niels Bohr — proposed the model of electrons orbiting the nucleus in fixed energy levels/shells **[Medium]**
- Electron shells — K, L, M, N (from innermost); max electrons per shell = 2n² (n = shell number) **[Medium]**
- Valence electrons — electrons in the outermost shell, determine chemical reactivity/bonding behavior **[High]**

**Periodic Table**
- Modern Periodic Table — arranged by increasing ATOMIC NUMBER (Mendeleev's original table was by atomic mass) **[High]**
- Dmitri Mendeleev — developed the first widely-used Periodic Table (1869), based on atomic mass **[High]**
- Henry Moseley — established that atomic number (not mass) is the basis for periodic arrangement (modern periodic law) **[Medium]**
- Groups (18, vertical columns) — elements with similar chemical properties/same valence electrons; Periods (7, horizontal rows) — elements with same number of electron shells **[High]**
- Noble Gases (Group 18) — inert, complete outer shell, unreactive (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn) **[Medium]**
- Alkali Metals (Group 1) — highly reactive metals (Li, Na, K); Halogens (Group 17) — highly reactive non-metals (F, Cl, Br, I) **[Medium]**
- Metals (left side) — good conductors, malleable, ductile, lose electrons (form cations); Non-metals (right side) — poor conductors, brittle, gain electrons (form anions) **[High]**
- Metalloids — elements with properties between metals and non-metals (e.g., Silicon, Boron, Germanium) **[Medium]**

**Chemical Bonding**
- Ionic Bond — formed by transfer of electrons between a metal and non-metal (e.g., NaCl); results in charged ions **[High]**
- Covalent Bond — formed by SHARING of electrons between non-metal atoms (e.g., H₂O, CO₂) **[High]**
- Metallic Bond — bonding in metals via a "sea of electrons" shared among metal atoms, explains conductivity/malleability **[Medium]**
- Octet Rule — atoms tend to gain/lose/share electrons to achieve 8 electrons in the outermost shell (stability, like noble gases) **[Medium]**

**Acids, Bases, Salts**
- Acid — releases H⁺ ions in water, turns blue litmus red, sour taste (e.g., HCl, citric acid) **[High]**
- Base — releases OH⁻ ions in water, turns red litmus blue, bitter taste, soapy feel (e.g., NaOH); soluble bases called Alkalis **[High]**
- pH Scale — measures acidity/alkalinity, ranges 0-14; pH 7 = neutral, pH < 7 = acidic, pH > 7 = basic **[High]**
- Salt — formed by reaction of acid + base (neutralization reaction), along with water **[High]**
- Common salt (NaCl) — obtained from sea water, used to produce washing soda, baking soda, bleaching powder **[Medium]**
- Antacid — a base (e.g., Milk of Magnesia) used to neutralize excess stomach acid **[Medium]**
- Litmus — natural indicator (from lichen); Universal Indicator — shows a range of colors corresponding to different pH values **[Medium]**

**Metals & Non-Metals**
- Physical properties of Metals — lustrous, malleable (can be hammered into sheets), ductile (can be drawn into wires), good conductors of heat/electricity, sonorous **[High]**
- Reactivity Series — arranges metals by reactivity (most to least): Potassium > Sodium > Calcium > Magnesium > Aluminium > Zinc > Iron > ... > Copper > Silver > Gold (most to least reactive) **[Medium]**
- Gold and Platinum — least reactive metals, found in free/native state in nature **[Medium]**
- Mercury — the only metal that is LIQUID at room temperature **[High]**
- Non-metals — generally poor conductors (EXCEPT Graphite, a form of carbon, which conducts electricity) **[High]**
- Rusting — corrosion of iron due to reaction with oxygen & moisture, forms iron oxide (Fe₂O₃); prevented via galvanization (zinc coating), painting, oiling **[High]**

**Carbon Chemistry**
- Carbon — forms the basis of organic chemistry due to CATENATION (ability to form long chains/bonds with itself) **[High]**
- Allotropes of Carbon — Diamond (hardest natural substance, poor conductor), Graphite (soft, slippery, good conductor of electricity), Fullerene (spherical/cage-like carbon molecules, e.g., Buckminsterfullerene C60) **[High]**
- Hydrocarbons — compounds of only carbon and hydrogen; Saturated (single bonds, alkanes) vs Unsaturated (double/triple bonds, alkenes/alkynes) **[Medium]**
- Functional groups — determine chemical behavior of organic compounds (e.g., -OH = alcohol, -COOH = carboxylic acid) **[Low]**

**Electrochemistry**
- Electrolysis — decomposition of a compound using electric current (e.g., water into hydrogen and oxygen) **[Medium]**
- Electrolyte — substance that conducts electricity when dissolved in water/molten state, due to free ions **[Medium]**
- Galvanic/Voltaic Cell — converts chemical energy into electrical energy (basis of batteries) **[Medium]**
- Electroplating — using electrolysis to coat one metal with a thin layer of another metal (e.g., chrome plating) **[Medium]**

**Polymers**
- Polymer — large molecule made of repeating smaller units (monomers) **[Medium]**
- Natural polymers — e.g., cellulose, natural rubber, proteins, DNA **[Medium]**
- Synthetic polymers — e.g., plastic (polythene, PVC), nylon, polyester, synthetic rubber **[Medium]**
- Thermoplastics (soften on heating, can be remolded, e.g., PVC) vs Thermosetting plastics (harden permanently after molding, e.g., Bakelite) **[Low]**

**Everyday Chemistry — Soaps & Detergents**
- Soap — sodium/potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid; made via SAPONIFICATION (reaction of fat/oil with NaOH/KOH) **[High]**
- Soap molecule structure — hydrophilic (water-loving) head + hydrophobic (water-repelling) tail; forms micelles to trap dirt/oil **[Medium]**
- Soaps DON'T work well in hard water (form scum/precipitate with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions); Detergents (synthetic) work well even in hard water **[High]**
- Baking Soda (Sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃) — used in cooking, antacids, fire extinguishers **[Medium]**
- Washing Soda (Sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃) — used in glass, soap, paper industries, water softening **[Medium]**
- Bleaching Powder (Calcium oxychloride) — used for disinfecting water, bleaching textiles **[Low]**

**Alloys**
- Alloy — homogeneous mixture of two or more metals (or metal + non-metal), created to enhance properties (strength, corrosion resistance) **[High]**
- Steel — Iron + Carbon (+ other elements for special steels); Stainless Steel — Iron + Chromium + Nickel (corrosion-resistant) **[High]**
- Brass — Copper + Zinc; Bronze — Copper + Tin **[High]**
- Duralumin — Aluminium + Copper + Magnesium + Manganese, used in aircraft (lightweight, strong) **[Medium]**
- Amalgam — an alloy in which one component is Mercury (e.g., dental amalgam) **[Low]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Atomic Number vs Mass Number | Atomic Number = PROTONS only (defines the element); Mass Number = PROTONS + NEUTRONS |
| Mendeleev vs Moseley's Periodic Table basis | Mendeleev = arranged by ATOMIC MASS (1869 original table); Moseley = established ATOMIC NUMBER as the true basis (modern periodic law) |
| Ionic Bond vs Covalent Bond | Ionic = electron TRANSFER (metal + non-metal, forms ions); Covalent = electron SHARING (non-metal + non-metal) |
| Brass vs Bronze | Brass = Copper + ZINC; Bronze = Copper + TIN — both copper alloys but different second metal |
| Diamond vs Graphite | Both are pure carbon (allotropes) but Diamond = hardest, poor conductor (all 4 bonds used in 3D structure); Graphite = soft, GOOD conductor (layered structure, free electrons) |
| Soap vs Detergent (hard water) | Soap = does NOT work well in hard water (forms scum with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺); Detergent = works well even in hard water (synthetic, doesn't form scum) |
| Baking Soda vs Washing Soda | Baking Soda = Sodium BICARBONATE (NaHCO₃), used in cooking/antacids; Washing Soda = Sodium CARBONATE (Na₂CO₃), used in cleaning/industry |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Subatomic particle discoverers** — "**T**iny **R**utherford **C**hadwick" → **T**homson (electron) → **R**utherford (proton/nucleus) → **C**hadwick (neutron) — discovered roughly in this chronological order (1897→1911→1932).
- **pH scale direction** — "pH LOW = SOUR/acidic (like lemon, pH~2); pH HIGH = bitter/basic (like soap, pH~12); pH 7 = plain water, perfectly neutral" — anchor with real examples (lemon=acid, soap=base) for quick recall.
- **Brass vs Bronze** — "**Br**ass = **Br**ight yellowish (Zinc gives a golden look, used in musical instruments); Bronze = old/statue-colored (Tin gives a duller, ancient look, used in statues/medals)" — visual color association.
- **Diamond vs Graphite structure** — "Diamond = Damn hard (3D rigid structure, all bonds locked); Graphite = Glides/slides (layered structure, layers slip over each other — used as a lubricant AND pencil lead)."
- **Reactivity series memory line** — "**P**lease **S**end **C**ats **M**onkeys **A**nd **Z**ebras **I**n **C**old (**H**ot) **S**ummer" → K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, (H), Cu, Ag — a classic long-form mnemonic for the reactivity series order.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Atomic Number vs Mass Number** — the most repeated numeric-definition trap in atomic structure questions.
- **Mendeleev vs Modern Periodic Table basis** — questions test whether students know the table SHIFTED from atomic mass (Mendeleev) to atomic number (Moseley/modern).
- Confusing **which carbon allotrope conducts electricity** — Graphite conducts (layered, free electrons), Diamond does NOT (all electrons locked in bonds) — a very common trap since both are "pure carbon."
- **Soap vs Detergent in hard water** — a frequently tested practical-chemistry question about which one forms scum.
- Alloy composition swaps — Brass (Cu+Zn) and Bronze (Cu+Sn) are constantly mixed up in "which metals make up X alloy" questions.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Atom: protons(+)/neutrons(neutral) in nucleus, electrons(-) in shells; Atomic No.=protons, Mass No.=protons+neutrons.
- Periodic Table: Mendeleev (atomic mass, 1869) → Modern (atomic number, Moseley); 18 groups, 7 periods.
- Bonding: Ionic=transfer (metal+non-metal), Covalent=sharing (non-metal+non-metal).
- Acids/Bases: pH<7=acid, pH>7=base, pH=7=neutral; salt=acid+base neutralization product.
- Metals/Non-metals: metals malleable/ductile/conductive; Mercury=only liquid metal; Gold/Platinum=least reactive; Graphite=non-metal that conducts.
- Carbon: catenation basis of organic chemistry; Diamond (hardest, insulator) vs Graphite (soft, conductor) vs Fullerene.
- Everyday chemistry: Soap=saponification, fails in hard water; Detergent=works in hard water; Baking Soda=NaHCO₃, Washing Soda=Na₂CO₃.
- Alloys: Steel (Fe+C), Brass (Cu+Zn), Bronze (Cu+Sn), Duralumin (Al+Cu+Mg+Mn) — all designed to enhance metal properties.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** The Atomic Number of an element is determined by the number of:
A) Neutrons B) Protons C) Electrons + Neutrons D) Protons + Neutrons

**Q2.** Which scientist established that atomic number, not atomic mass, is the correct basis for the modern periodic table?
A) Dmitri Mendeleev B) Henry Moseley C) Niels Bohr D) J.J. Thomson

**Q3.** Which allotrope of carbon is a good conductor of electricity, unlike diamond?
A) Diamond B) Graphite C) Fullerene D) Charcoal

**Q4.** Brass is an alloy of copper and which other metal?
A) Tin B) Zinc C) Nickel D) Lead

**Q5.** Why do soaps NOT work effectively in hard water, unlike detergents?
A) Soaps evaporate quickly in hard water
B) Soaps form scum/precipitate with calcium and magnesium ions
C) Soaps dissolve too fast in hard water
D) Hard water neutralizes the soap's pH

## Answer Key
1. **B) Protons** — Atomic Number is defined strictly by the number of protons in an atom's nucleus, which also determines the element's identity.
2. **B) Henry Moseley** — He established that atomic number (not atomic mass, as Mendeleev originally used) is the true basis of periodic arrangement.
3. **B) Graphite** — Its layered structure with delocalized electrons allows it to conduct electricity, unlike diamond's rigid, fully-bonded 3D structure.
4. **B) Zinc** — Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, distinct from bronze (copper and tin).
5. **B) Soaps form scum/precipitate with calcium and magnesium ions** — This scum formation makes soap ineffective in hard water, while synthetic detergents avoid this reaction.
