# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Geometry?

**Geometry** is the branch of mathematics that deals with **shapes, sizes, angles, positions, and properties** of figures. In competitive exams, it focuses on 2D figures — lines, triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, and their properties.

> In a triangle, if two sides are equal, what can you say about the angles?
> The angles opposite to equal sides are also equal.
> That's geometry — understanding shapes and their rules.

### Why Geometry Dominates SSC?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **4–6 questions per SSC paper** | The HIGHEST-weighted single topic in Tier-II |
| **Theorem-based questions** | Once you know the theorem, questions solve in seconds |
| **Limited set of theorems** | ~30 key theorems cover 95% of questions |
| **Property recall** | Most answers come from remembering a property, not calculating |
| **Centers of triangles** | Circumcenter, Incenter, Centroid, Orthocenter — exam favourites |
| **Circle theorems** | Tangent, Chord, Cyclic Quadrilateral — guaranteed questions |

### The Core Figures

| Figure | Key Properties to Know |
|---|---|
| **Lines & Angles** | Parallel lines, transversal, angle pairs |
| **Triangles** | Types, centers, congruence, similarity, theorems |
| **Quadrilaterals** | Parallelogram, Rectangle, Square, Rhombus, Trapezium |
| **Circles** | Chord, Tangent, Secant, Arc, Sector, Cyclic Quadrilateral |
| **Polygons** | Interior/Exterior angles, diagonals |

## CONCEPT 1: Lines & Angles

### Basic Angle Types

| Angle Type | Measure |
|---|---|
| Acute | 0° < θ < 90° |
| Right | θ = 90° |
| Obtuse | 90° < θ < 180° |
| Straight | θ = 180° |
| Reflex | 180° < θ < 360° |
| Complete | θ = 360° |

### Angle Pairs

| Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Complementary** | Sum = 90° |
| **Supplementary** | Sum = 180° |
| **Vertically Opposite** | Equal |
| **Linear Pair** | Sum = 180° (on a straight line) |

### Parallel Lines + Transversal

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines:

| Angle Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Corresponding angles** | Equal (F-shape) |
| **Alternate interior angles** | Equal (Z-shape) |
| **Alternate exterior angles** | Equal |
| **Co-interior (Same-side interior)** | Supplementary (sum = 180°) (U-shape) |

### Example

Two parallel lines cut by a transversal. One angle = 65°.
- Corresponding angle = 65°
- Alternate interior = 65°
- Co-interior = 180° − 65° = 115°

## CONCEPT 2: Triangles — Basic Properties

### Sum of Angles

> **Sum of interior angles = 180°**
>
> **Exterior angle = Sum of two non-adjacent interior angles**

### Types of Triangles

**By Sides:**

| Type | Property |
|---|---|
| **Equilateral** | All sides equal, all angles = 60° |
| **Isosceles** | Two sides equal, base angles equal |
| **Scalene** | All sides different, all angles different |

**By Angles:**

| Type | Property |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | All angles < 90° |
| **Right** | One angle = 90° |
| **Obtuse** | One angle > 90° |

### Key Properties

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sum of any two sides > third side | Triangle Inequality |
| Difference of two sides < third side | Reverse Inequality |
| Largest angle is opposite the largest side | Side-Angle relationship |
| Smallest angle is opposite the smallest side | Side-Angle relationship |

### Important Angle Results

> In a **right triangle**: Other two angles sum to 90°
> In an **equilateral triangle**: Each angle = 60°
> In an **isosceles right triangle**: Angles = 90°, 45°, 45°

## CONCEPT 3: Triangle Congruence & Similarity

### Congruence (Same shape AND size)

Two triangles are congruent if:

| Rule | Condition |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | All three sides equal |
| **SAS** | Two sides and included angle equal |
| **ASA** | Two angles and included side equal |
| **AAS** | Two angles and any side equal |
| **RHS** | Right angle + Hypotenuse + One side equal |

> ⚠️ **SSA is NOT a valid congruence rule** (ambiguous case)
> ⚠️ **AAA is NOT congruence** — it gives SIMILARITY

### Similarity (Same shape, different size)

Two triangles are similar if:

| Rule | Condition |
|---|---|
| **AA** | Two angles equal (third is automatically equal) |
| **SAS** | Two sides in proportion and included angle equal |
| **SSS** | All three sides in proportion |

### Properties of Similar Triangles

If △ABC ~ △DEF with ratio k:

| Property | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Sides | k : 1 |
| Perimeters | k : 1 |
| Altitudes, Medians, Angle Bisectors | k : 1 |
| **Areas** | **k² : 1** |

> **CRITICAL**: Area ratio = **Square** of the side ratio

### Example

If two similar triangles have sides in ratio 3:5, their areas are in ratio:

3² : 5² = **9 : 25** ✅

## CONCEPT 4: Pythagoras Theorem & Triplets

### The Theorem

> In a right triangle, **Hypotenuse² = Base² + Perpendicular²**
>
> **c² = a² + b²** (where c is the hypotenuse)

### Common Pythagorean Triplets (MEMORIZE!)

| Triplet | Multiples |
|---|---|
| **3, 4, 5** | 6-8-10, 9-12-15, 12-16-20, 15-20-25 |
| **5, 12, 13** | 10-24-26 |
| **8, 15, 17** | 16-30-34 |
| **7, 24, 25** | 14-48-50 |
| **9, 40, 41** | — |
| **11, 60, 61** | — |
| **20, 21, 29** | — |

### Converse of Pythagoras

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| c² = a² + b² | Right angle opposite to c |
| c² > a² + b² | Obtuse angle opposite to c |
| c² < a² + b² | Acute angle opposite to c |

### Example

Sides 6, 8, 10. Check: 10² = 100. 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. **Right triangle** ✅

Sides 7, 8, 12. Check: 12² = 144. 7² + 8² = 49 + 64 = 113. 144 > 113. **Obtuse triangle** ✅

## CONCEPT 5: Triangle Area Formulas

| Method | Formula | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Base × Height** | A = ½ × b × h | When base and height are known |
| **Heron's Formula** | A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)] where s = (a+b+c)/2 | When all three sides are known |
| **Equilateral** | A = (√3/4) × a² | Equilateral triangle |
| **Right Triangle** | A = ½ × leg₁ × leg₂ | Right triangle (legs are base and height) |
| **Two sides + included angle** | A = ½ × a × b × sin C | When two sides and included angle are known |
| **With inradius** | A = r × s (where s = semi-perimeter) | When inradius is given |
| **With circumradius** | A = abc / 4R | When circumradius is given |

### Special Results for Equilateral Triangle (side = a)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Area | (√3/4)a² |
| Height | (√3/2)a |
| Perimeter | 3a |
| Inradius (r) | a/(2√3) = a√3/6 |
| Circumradius (R) | a/√3 = a√3/3 |
| **R = 2r** | Always! |

### Example

Equilateral triangle with side 6 cm.
Area = (√3/4) × 36 = **9√3 cm²** ✅
Height = (√3/2) × 6 = **3√3 cm** ✅

## CONCEPT 6: Medians, Altitudes, Angle Bisectors

### Median

> A line from a **vertex** to the **midpoint of the opposite side**.
>
> All 3 medians meet at the **CENTROID (G)**.

**Properties of Centroid:**
- Divides each median in ratio **2:1** from vertex
- Centroid = (x₁+x₂+x₃)/3, (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3 (coordinate geometry)
- **Area of 6 small triangles formed by 3 medians are ALL EQUAL**

**Apollonius' Theorem (Median Length):**

> If m_a is the median to side a:
>
> **m_a² = (2b² + 2c² − a²) / 4**

Or: **4m_a² = 2b² + 2c² − a²**

**Relation between medians and sides:**

> 3(a² + b² + c²) = 4(m_a² + m_b² + m_c²)

### Altitude (Height)

> A perpendicular from a **vertex** to the **opposite side** (or its extension).
>
> All 3 altitudes meet at the **ORTHOCENTER (H)**.

**Properties of Orthocenter:**
- In an **acute triangle**: Orthocenter is **inside**
- In a **right triangle**: Orthocenter is at the **vertex of the right angle**
- In an **obtuse triangle**: Orthocenter is **outside**

### Angle Bisector

> A line that divides an **angle** into two equal parts.
>
> All 3 internal angle bisectors meet at the **INCENTER (I)**.

**Angle Bisector Theorem (VERY IMPORTANT):**

> The angle bisector of angle A divides the opposite side BC in the ratio of the adjacent sides:
>
> **BD/DC = AB/AC**

**Properties of Incenter:**
- Equidistant from all three sides
- Distance from incenter to each side = **inradius (r)**
- **r = Area / s** (s = semi-perimeter)
- Incenter is ALWAYS inside the triangle

**Angle at Incenter:**

> **∠BIC = 90° + A/2** (where A = ∠BAC)

## CONCEPT 7: Triangle Centers (THE EXAM GOLDMINE)

### The Four Centers

| Center | Created By | Position | Key Property |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Circumcenter (O)** | Perpendicular bisectors of sides | Inside (acute), On hypotenuse (right), Outside (obtuse) | Equidistant from all 3 **vertices** |
| **Incenter (I)** | Angle bisectors | Always inside | Equidistant from all 3 **sides** |
| **Centroid (G)** | Medians | Always inside | Divides medians 2:1 from vertex |
| **Orthocenter (H)** | Altitudes | Inside (acute), At right-angle vertex (right), Outside (obtuse) | — |

### Circumcenter — Key Results

- Distance from circumcenter to each vertex = **Circumradius (R)**
- **R = abc / 4×Area**
- **R = a / (2sinA)** (sine rule)
- **∠BOC = 2∠BAC** (Central angle = 2 × Inscribed angle)

### For RIGHT TRIANGLE

| Center | Position |
|---|---|
| Circumcenter | **Midpoint of hypotenuse** |
| Circumradius | R = Hypotenuse/2 |
| Orthocenter | **At the right-angle vertex** |

### For EQUILATERAL TRIANGLE

> All four centers **coincide** (same point!)
>
> **R = 2r** (Circumradius = 2 × Inradius)

### The Euler Line

> In any triangle, O (Circumcenter), G (Centroid), and H (Orthocenter) are **collinear**.
>
> G divides OH in ratio **1:2** (OG:GH = 1:2)

### Angle at Centers (EXAM FAVOURITE)

| At Circumcenter | At Incenter |
|---|---|
| **∠BOC = 2A** | **∠BIC = 90° + A/2** |
| (Central angle = 2 × inscribed angle) | |

### Example

In △ABC, ∠A = 50°. Find ∠BOC (O = circumcenter) and ∠BIC (I = incenter).

∠BOC = 2 × 50° = **100°**
∠BIC = 90° + 50°/2 = 90° + 25° = **115°** ✅

## CONCEPT 8: Quadrilaterals

### Sum of Interior Angles = 360°

### Properties Table

| Quadrilateral | Sides | Angles | Diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parallelogram** | Opposite sides equal & parallel | Opposite angles equal; Adjacent angles supplementary | Bisect each other |
| **Rectangle** | Opposite sides equal; All right angles | All 90° | Equal and bisect each other |
| **Square** | All sides equal; All right angles | All 90° | Equal, bisect at 90° |
| **Rhombus** | All sides equal | Opposite angles equal | Bisect at 90° (NOT equal) |
| **Trapezium** | One pair of parallel sides | — | — |
| **Isosceles Trapezium** | Non-parallel sides equal | Base angles equal | Equal diagonals |
| **Kite** | Two pairs of adjacent sides equal | One pair of opposite angles equal | One diagonal bisects the other at 90° |

### Area Formulas

| Quadrilateral | Area Formula |
|---|---|
| **Parallelogram** | base × height |
| **Rectangle** | length × breadth |
| **Square** | side² or d²/2 |
| **Rhombus** | (d₁ × d₂) / 2 |
| **Trapezium** | ½ × (a + b) × h (a, b = parallel sides, h = height) |
| **General Quadrilateral** | ½ × d × (h₁ + h₂) (d = diagonal, h₁ h₂ = perpendicular distances) |

### Key Properties to Memorize

**Parallelogram:**
- Diagonals bisect each other (NOT necessarily at 90°)
- Each diagonal divides it into 2 triangles of equal area
- Sum of squares of diagonals = Sum of squares of all 4 sides
  - **d₁² + d₂² = 2(a² + b²)**

**Rectangle:**
- Diagonal = √(l² + b²)
- Diagonals are equal

**Rhombus:**
- Side = √[(d₁/2)² + (d₂/2)²] = ½√(d₁² + d₂²)
- Diagonals bisect each other at 90°

**Square:**
- Diagonal = a√2
- Area = a² = d²/2

### Example

Rhombus with diagonals 10 cm and 24 cm.
Area = (10 × 24)/2 = **120 cm²**
Side = ½√(100 + 576) = ½√676 = ½ × 26 = **13 cm** ✅

## CONCEPT 10: Circle — Chord Properties

### Key Theorems

| # | Theorem |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Perpendicular from center to a chord bisects the chord** |
| 2 | **Equal chords are equidistant from center** |
| 3 | **The longer chord is closer to the center** |
| 4 | **Diameter is the longest chord** |
| 5 | **Chords equidistant from center are equal** |

### Chord-Distance Formula

> If a chord of length **l** is at distance **d** from the center of a circle of radius **r**:
>
> **r² = d² + (l/2)²**
>
> Or: **l = 2√(r² − d²)**

### Example

Circle with radius 13 cm. A chord is at distance 5 cm from center. Chord length?

l = 2√(169 − 25) = 2√144 = 2 × 12 = **24 cm** ✅

### Intersecting Chords Theorem

> When two chords intersect inside a circle:
>
> **PA × PB = PC × PD**
> (Products of segments are equal)

### Example

Two chords intersect. One chord is divided into segments 4 cm and 6 cm. The other has one segment = 3 cm. Find the other segment.

4 × 6 = 3 × x → x = **8 cm** ✅

## CONCEPT 11: Circle — Tangent Properties

### Key Theorems

| # | Theorem |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Tangent ⊥ Radius at point of contact** (angle = 90°) |
| 2 | **Two tangents from an external point are EQUAL** |
| 3 | **The line joining external point to center bisects the angle between tangents** |
| 4 | **Tangent-Radius: OT ⊥ TP (where T = point of tangency)** |

### Tangent Length from External Point

> If a tangent from external point P touches the circle at T, and OP = d, radius = r:
>
> **PT = √(d² − r²)** (using Pythagoras in △OTP)

### Example

Circle with radius 5 cm. External point is 13 cm from center. Tangent length?

PT = √(169 − 25) = √144 = **12 cm** ✅

### Two Tangents from Same Point

> PA = PB (tangent lengths are equal)
> ∠OPA = ∠OPB (OP bisects the angle)
> ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius)

### Angle Between Tangents

> If ∠AOB = θ (at center), then ∠APB = **180° − θ**
>
> Alternatively: **∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°**
>
> Also: **∠APB = 2 × ∠OAP**

---

## CONCEPT 12: Circle — Secant & Tangent-Secant Theorems

### Secant-Secant (External Point)

> From external point P, two secants PAB and PCD:
>
> **PA × PB = PC × PD**

### Tangent-Secant (External Point)

> From external point P, tangent PT and secant PAB:
>
> **PT² = PA × PB**

### Example

Tangent from P = 6 cm. Secant from P passes through circle with PA = 4 cm. Find PB.

36 = 4 × PB → PB = **9 cm**
(So AB = PB − PA = 9 − 4 = 5 cm) ✅

---

## CONCEPT 13: Circle — Angles in a Circle

### Central Angle vs Inscribed Angle

> **Central angle = 2 × Inscribed angle** (for the same arc)
>
> ∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB (where O = center, C = point on circle)

### Angle in a Semicircle

> **Angle in a semicircle = 90°** (angle subtended by diameter)

### Angles in the Same Segment

> All angles in the same segment of a circle are **EQUAL**

### Alternate Segment Theorem (Tangent-Chord Angle)

> The angle between a tangent and a chord = the inscribed angle in the alternate segment

### Example

A chord subtends 40° at the center. Inscribed angle on the major arc?

Inscribed angle = Central angle / 2 = 40°/2 = **20°**

On the minor arc: 360° − 40° = 320° → Inscribed = 320°/2 = **160°** ✅

## CONCEPT 14: Cyclic Quadrilateral

### Definition

> A quadrilateral whose all 4 vertices lie on a circle.

### Key Properties

| # | Property |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Opposite angles are supplementary**: ∠A + ∠C = 180°, ∠B + ∠D = 180° |
| 2 | **Exterior angle = Opposite interior angle** |
| 3 | **Ptolemy's Theorem**: AC × BD = AB × CD + AD × BC (product of diagonals = sum of products of opposite sides) |

### How to check if a quadrilateral is cyclic

> Opposite angles must sum to 180°.

### Example

In a cyclic quadrilateral, ∠A = 70° and ∠B = 110°. Find ∠C and ∠D.

∠C = 180° − 70° = **110°**
∠D = 180° − 110° = **70°** ✅

---

## CONCEPT 15: Polygons

### Interior Angle Sum

> Sum of interior angles of n-sided polygon = **(n − 2) × 180°**

### Each Interior Angle (Regular Polygon)

> Each interior angle = **(n − 2) × 180° / n**

### Each Exterior Angle (Regular Polygon)

> Each exterior angle = **360° / n**

### Sum of Exterior Angles

> Sum of exterior angles = **360°** (always, for any convex polygon)

### Number of Diagonals

> Diagonals = **n(n − 3) / 2**

### Common Polygons

| Polygon | n | Interior Angle Sum | Each Interior Angle | Each Exterior Angle | Diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° | 60° | 120° | 0 |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° | 90° | 90° | 2 |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 108° | 72° | 5 |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 120° | 60° | 9 |
| Heptagon | 7 | 900° | 128.57° | 51.43° | 14 |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° | 135° | 45° | 20 |
| Nonagon | 9 | 1260° | 140° | 40° | 27 |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° | 144° | 36° | 35 |

### Example

Each exterior angle = 30°. How many sides?

n = 360°/30° = **12 sides** (Dodecagon) ✅

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Pythagoras — Recognize Triplets Instantly

If you see sides 5, 12, 13 or 8, 15, 17 — IMMEDIATELY know it's a right triangle. Don't waste time squaring.

---

## Shortcut 2: Equilateral Triangle — Everything in Terms of Side

Height = (√3/2)a. Area = (√3/4)a². R = a/√3. r = a/(2√3). R = 2r.

---

## Shortcut 3: Centers Quick Recall

> **\"COIG\"** — **C**ircumcenter (Perpendicular Bisectors), **O**rthocenter (Altitudes), **I**ncenter (Angle Bisectors), **G** (Centroid — Medians)

Memory: **\"Come Over, I'm Great\"**

---

## Shortcut 4: Right Triangle Centers

- Circumcenter = Midpoint of hypotenuse
- R = Hypotenuse/2
- Orthocenter = Right-angle vertex
- r = (a + b − c)/2 (where c = hypotenuse)

---

## Shortcut 5: Inradius of Right Triangle

> **r = (a + b − c) / 2** (a, b = legs, c = hypotenuse)

Example: Right △ with legs 6, 8, hypotenuse 10.
r = (6 + 8 − 10)/2 = 4/2 = **2** ✅

---

## Shortcut 6: Circle — Quick Tangent Length

> PT = √(OP² − r²). Just Pythagoras!

---

## Shortcut 7: Central Angle = 2 × Inscribed Angle

If you see a circle with an inscribed angle, double it for the central angle (or halve the central for inscribed).

---

## Shortcut 8: Polygon — Sides from Exterior Angle

> **n = 360° / Exterior Angle**

Much easier than working with interior angles.

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Congruence vs Similarity

| Congruence | Similarity |
|---|---|
| Same shape AND size | Same shape, DIFFERENT size |
| ≅ | ~ |
| Side ratios = 1:1 | Side ratios = k:1 |
| Area ratio = 1:1 | **Area ratio = k²:1** |
| SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS | AA, SAS, SSS |

---

## Confusion 2: Four Centers — Which is Which?

| Center | Formed by | Mnemonic |
|---|---|---|
| Circumcenter | **P**erpendicular **B**isectors | \"**P**erfect **B**oys\" → Circumcenter |
| Incenter | **A**ngle **B**isectors | \"**A**ngles **B**isected\" → Incenter |
| Centroid | **M**edians | \"**M**edians\" → centroid (Middle point) |
| Orthocenter | **A**ltitudes | \"**A**ltitudes\" → Orthocenter (Height) |

## Confusion 3: ∠BIC vs ∠BOC

| At Incenter | At Circumcenter |
|---|---|
| ∠BIC = 90° + A/2 | ∠BOC = 2A |
| Always **90 + half** | Always **double** |

Students mix these up constantly!

---

## Confusion 4: Chord vs Secant vs Tangent

| | Chord | Secant | Tangent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Both ends ON circle | Line THROUGH circle (2 points) | Touches circle at 1 point |
| **Inside** | Entirely inside or on | Passes through | Only touches |
| **Length** | Finite segment | Extends beyond | Point of contact |

---

## Confusion 5: Tangent Length from Point vs Secant Length

- **Tangent**: PT² = PA × PB (tangent-secant)
- **Chords**: PA × PB = PC × PD (intersecting chords)

The tangent uses a **square** because it's a single point of contact (PT = PT, so PT × PT = PT²).

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


## Speed Rule 1: Recognize Pythagorean Triplets at Sight

Don't calculate 5² + 12². Just know 5-12-13 is a triplet. This saves 15–20 seconds per question.

## Speed Rule 2: For Triangle Centers — Know the Formulas by Heart

∠BIC = 90° + A/2 and ∠BOC = 2A are asked in almost every SSC paper. Just plug in and answer.

## Speed Rule 3: Similar Triangle Area — Square the Ratio

Side ratio 2:3 → Area ratio 4:9. Instant. Don't find actual areas.

## Speed Rule 4: Right Triangle Inradius — Direct Formula

r = (a+b−c)/2. No need for Area/s calculation.

## Speed Rule 5: Tangent Length — Instant Pythagoras

PT = √(OP²−r²). Recognize this immediately.

## Speed Rule 6: Polygon Questions — Use Exterior Angle

n = 360°/Exterior is much faster than solving (n−2)×180°/n = Interior.

**Q1.** In △ABC, ∠A = 80°, ∠B = 50°. Find ∠C.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
∠C = 180° − 80° − 50° = **50°**
</details>

**Q2.** In △ABC, ∠A = 40°. Find ∠BIC (I = incenter).
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
∠BIC = 90° + 40°/2 = 90° + 20° = **110°**
</details>

**Q3.** In △ABC, ∠A = 50°. Find ∠BOC (O = circumcenter).
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
∠BOC = 2 × 50° = **100°**
</details>

**Q4.** Two similar triangles have sides in ratio 4:7. Area ratio?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
4² : 7² = **16 : 49**
</details>

**Q5.** Right triangle with legs 5 and 12. Find hypotenuse and inradius.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Hypotenuse = 13 (triplet). r = (5+12−13)/2 = 4/2 = **2**
</details>

**Q6.** Circle radius 10. Chord at distance 6 from center. Chord length?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
l = 2√(100−36) = 2√64 = 2×8 = **16 cm**
</details>

**Q7.** Tangent from a point 25 cm from center of circle with radius 7 cm. Tangent length?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
PT = √(625−49) = √576 = **24 cm**
</details>

**Q8.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 40°. Number of sides?
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
n = 360°/40° = **9 sides**
</details>

**Q9.** Cyclic quadrilateral: ∠A = 75°. Find ∠C.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
∠C = 180° − 75° = **105°**
</details>

**Q10.** Rhombus with diagonals 16 cm and 12 cm. Find area and side.
<details><summary>🔑 Answer</summary>
Area = 16×12/2 = **96 cm²**. Side = ½√(256+144) = ½√400 = ½×20 = **10 cm**
</details>

TRIANGLE:
  Angles sum = 180°. Exterior = Sum of remote interior.

  Circumcenter (O) → Perpendicular Bisectors. ∠BOC = 2A.

  Circumcenter = Midpoint of hypotenuse. R = c/2.
  Orthocenter = Right-angle vertex. r = (a+b−c)/2.

SIMILARITY:
  AA, SAS, SSS. Area ratio = (side ratio)².

ANGLE BISECTOR THEOREM: BD/DC = AB/AC.

CIRCLE:
  Chord: r² = d² + (l/2)².
