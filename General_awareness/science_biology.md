# General Science — Biology

## 1. Overview
Biology in GK exams spans cell structure, human body systems, diseases, genetics, ecology, plant biology, and nutrition. This is one of the highest-weightage science topics because it's directly relatable to everyday life (health, food, environment) and has many clean fact-pairs (organ-function, disease-cause, vitamin-deficiency) ideal for MCQs.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Cell Biology**
- Cell — basic structural and functional unit of life; discovered by Robert Hooke (1665, observed cork cells) **[High]**
- Cell Theory — proposed by Schleiden & Schwann (all living things made of cells); Rudolf Virchow added "all cells arise from pre-existing cells" **[Medium]**
- Prokaryotic cells (no true nucleus, e.g., bacteria) vs Eukaryotic cells (true nucleus, membrane-bound organelles, e.g., plant/animal cells) **[High]**
- Mitochondria — "powerhouse of the cell," site of ATP/energy production via cellular respiration **[High]**
- Nucleus — control center, contains DNA/chromosomes, discovered by Robert Brown **[High]**
- Ribosomes — site of protein synthesis **[Medium]**
- Chloroplast — site of photosynthesis, found ONLY in plant cells (contains chlorophyll) **[High]**
- Cell Wall — present in PLANT cells (made of cellulose), ABSENT in animal cells; gives rigidity **[High]**
- Golgi Apparatus — packaging and dispatch of proteins/materials; Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) — transport network, Rough ER (has ribosomes) vs Smooth ER (no ribosomes) **[Medium]**
- Lysosomes — "suicide bags," contain digestive enzymes for breaking down waste/damaged components **[Medium]**

**Cell Division**
- Mitosis — cell division producing 2 IDENTICAL daughter cells (same chromosome number as parent); used for growth/repair **[High]**
- Meiosis — cell division producing 4 daughter cells with HALF the chromosome number (haploid); occurs in gamete/sex cell formation **[High]**
- Human somatic cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); gametes (sperm/egg) have 23 chromosomes (haploid) **[High]**

**Tissues**
- Plant Tissues — Meristematic (dividing/growing regions) and Permanent (Simple: parenchyma/collenchyma/sclerenchyma; Complex: xylem/phloem) **[Medium]**
- Xylem — transports WATER & minerals (upward, from roots); Phloem — transports FOOD/nutrients (both directions, made by photosynthesis) **[High]**
- Animal Tissues — 4 main types: Epithelial (covering/lining, e.g., skin), Connective (support/binding, e.g., bone, blood), Muscular (movement, e.g., cardiac/skeletal/smooth), Nervous (signal transmission, e.g., neurons) **[High]**

**Human Body Systems**
- Digestive System — mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine (main absorption) → large intestine → rectum; liver produces bile, pancreas produces digestive enzymes & insulin **[High]**
- Respiratory System — nose → trachea → bronchi → lungs → alveoli (site of gas exchange, O₂/CO₂) **[High]**
- Circulatory System — heart (4 chambers: 2 atria, 2 ventricles) pumps blood; Red Blood Cells (carry oxygen via hemoglobin), White Blood Cells (immunity), Platelets (clotting) **[High]**
- Blood groups — ABO system (A, B, AB, O) + Rh factor; discovered by Karl Landsteiner **[High]**
- O-negative — universal donor; AB-positive — universal recipient **[High]**
- Nervous System — Central (brain + spinal cord) and Peripheral (nerves); Neuron = basic functional unit; Cerebrum (thinking/voluntary actions), Cerebellum (balance/coordination), Medulla oblongata (involuntary actions like heartbeat/breathing) **[High]**
- Excretory System — kidneys (filter blood, form urine, basic unit = nephron), ureters, urinary bladder, urethra **[High]**
- Endocrine System — ductless glands secreting hormones directly into blood; Pituitary gland = "master gland"; Thyroid (thyroxine, metabolism), Pancreas (insulin, blood sugar), Adrenal (adrenaline, stress response) **[High]**
- Skeletal System — 206 bones in adult human body; functions: support, protection, movement (with muscles), blood cell production (in bone marrow) **[High]**
- Muscular System — three types: Skeletal (voluntary, striated), Smooth (involuntary, in organs), Cardiac (involuntary, only in heart, striated) **[Medium]**
- Reproductive System — Male (testes produce sperm), Female (ovaries produce eggs, uterus for pregnancy); menstrual cycle ~28 days **[Medium]**

**Diseases**
- Bacterial diseases — Tuberculosis (TB, lungs), Cholera, Typhoid, Tetanus, Leprosy, Pneumonia (bacterial or viral) **[High]**
- Viral diseases — Common Cold, Influenza (Flu), Chickenpox, Measles, Mumps, Polio, Hepatitis, AIDS (HIV), COVID-19 **[High]**
- Fungal diseases — Ringworm, Athlete's Foot **[Medium]**
- Protozoan diseases — Malaria (caused by Plasmodium, spread by female Anopheles mosquito), Amoebiasis (Entamoeba histolytica) **[High]**
- Deficiency diseases — caused by lack of specific nutrients (see Nutrition section below): Scurvy (Vitamin C), Rickets (Vitamin D), Beriberi (Vitamin B1), Night blindness (Vitamin A), Anemia (Iron) **[High]**
- Vaccination — introduces weakened/inactive pathogen to stimulate immune response; Edward Jenner — developed the first vaccine (smallpox) **[High]**

**Genetics**
- Gregor Mendel — "Father of Genetics," discovered laws of inheritance through pea plant experiments **[High]**
- Genes — units of heredity, made of DNA, located on chromosomes; DNA = Deoxyribonucleic Acid, double helix structure (discovered by Watson & Crick, 1953) **[High]**
- Dominant vs Recessive traits — dominant trait expresses even with one copy of the gene; recessive trait needs both copies to express **[Medium]**
- Human chromosomes — 23 pairs (46 total); sex determined by X/Y chromosomes (XX=female, XY=male) — MALE determines the child's sex (via X or Y sperm) **[High]**

**Evolution**
- Charles Darwin — proposed the Theory of Evolution by Natural Selection ("On the Origin of Species," 1859) **[High]**
- Natural Selection — "survival of the fittest," organisms best adapted to environment survive and reproduce **[High]**
- Fossils — preserved remains providing evidence for evolution **[Low]**

**Ecology & Environment**
- Ecosystem — community of living organisms interacting with their physical environment **[Medium]**
- Food Chain — sequence showing energy transfer: Producers (plants) → Primary Consumers (herbivores) → Secondary Consumers (carnivores) → Tertiary Consumers → Decomposers **[High]**
- Biodiversity Hotspots in India — 4 recognized: Himalayas, Western Ghats, Indo-Burma region, Sundaland (Nicobar Islands) **[Medium]**
- Ozone Layer — protects Earth from harmful UV radiation, located in the stratosphere; depletion linked to CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) **[Medium]**
- Greenhouse Effect — trapping of heat by gases (CO₂, methane, water vapor) in the atmosphere, causes global warming **[Medium]**
- Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) — indicator of water pollution level (higher BOD = more pollution) **[Low]**

**Plant Biology**
- Photosynthesis — process by which plants make food (glucose) using sunlight, water, and CO₂, releasing oxygen; occurs in chloroplasts (chlorophyll pigment) **[High]**
- Photosynthesis equation (conceptual): Carbon dioxide + Water + Sunlight → Glucose + Oxygen **[High]**
- Respiration (in plants) — breakdown of glucose to release energy, occurs continuously (unlike photosynthesis, which needs light) **[High]**
- Transpiration — loss of water vapor from plant surfaces (mainly leaves, via stomata); helps in water/mineral transport and cooling **[High]**
- Stomata — small pores on leaf surface, regulate gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ out) and water loss **[Medium]**

**Nutrition**
- Carbohydrates — primary energy source; Proteins — growth & repair (body-building foods); Fats — energy storage, insulation **[High]**
- Vitamins & their deficiency diseases: Vitamin A (night blindness), Vitamin B1/Thiamine (Beriberi), Vitamin B3 (Pellagra), Vitamin C (Scurvy), Vitamin D (Rickets in children, Osteomalacia in adults), Vitamin K (blood clotting issues) **[High]**
- Minerals: Calcium (bones/teeth), Iron (hemoglobin formation, deficiency = Anemia), Iodine (thyroid function, deficiency = Goiter) **[High]**
- Vitamin C — only vitamin NOT stored in the body (water-soluble, needs regular intake); Vitamin A, D, E, K = fat-soluble (can be stored) **[Medium]**
- Balanced Diet — includes appropriate proportions of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Mitosis vs Meiosis | Mitosis = 2 IDENTICAL cells, same chromosome number (growth/repair); Meiosis = 4 cells, HALF chromosome number (gamete formation) |
| Xylem vs Phloem | Xylem = transports WATER/minerals UPWARD (one direction, from roots); Phloem = transports FOOD (both directions, made by leaves) |
| Photosynthesis vs Respiration (in plants) | Photosynthesis = makes food, needs SUNLIGHT, releases OXYGEN, only in green parts; Respiration = releases ENERGY, happens CONTINUOUSLY (day & night), in all living cells |
| Vitamin A vs Vitamin D deficiency | Vitamin A deficiency = Night blindness (affects EYES); Vitamin D deficiency = Rickets (affects BONES) — both fat-soluble but affect different systems |
| Bacterial vs Viral vs Protozoan diseases | Bacterial = TB, Cholera, Typhoid (treatable by antibiotics); Viral = Flu, Measles, AIDS (antibiotics DON'T work); Protozoan = Malaria, Amoebiasis (caused by single-celled parasites) |
| O-negative vs AB-positive blood | O-negative = UNIVERSAL DONOR (can give to anyone); AB-positive = UNIVERSAL RECIPIENT (can receive from anyone) — opposite roles |
| Cell Wall presence | ONLY in plant cells (made of cellulose) — animal cells have NO cell wall, only a cell membrane |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Mitochondria = "Powerhouse"** — this nickname itself is the mnemonic; just remember "Mito = Motor/energy" to link the organelle name to its ATP-producing function.
- **Xylem vs Phloem direction** — "**X**ylem = e**X**press elevator UP (water from roots to leaves); Phloem = "Flow-em" both ways (food distributed everywhere)" — the "X" visually suggests a single upward path.
- **Universal Donor/Recipient** — "O = Overall giver (0 antigens to react badly, so anyone can accept it); AB = Accepts Both (already has A and B antigens, so nothing new bothers it)" — link the letters to their literal blood-type logic.
- **Vitamin-deficiency pairs** — build one memorable image per vitamin: A=eyes (night blindness), B1=nerves/Beriberi (leg weakness), C=gums/Scurvy (bleeding gums), D=bones/Rickets (bowed legs) — one body part per vitamin letter.
- **Food Chain order** — "**P**lants **F**eed **H**erbivores **W**hich **F**eed **C**arnivores" → Producer → Primary Consumer (herbivore) → Secondary Consumer (carnivore) → Tertiary Consumer — a simple feeding-order sentence.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **Mitosis vs Meiosis** — the most common cell-division trap; check whether the question mentions "identical cells" (mitosis) or "half chromosome number/gametes" (meiosis).
- **Xylem vs Phloem function swap** — a frequently tested pairing trap in plant biology.
- Vitamin-deficiency disease MISMATCHES — e.g., wrongly pairing Vitamin C with Rickets instead of Scurvy — always double-check the vitamin letter against its specific deficiency disease.
- **Bacterial vs Viral disease classification** — questions test whether a disease (e.g., Typhoid=bacterial vs Measles=viral) is correctly categorized, since treatment approaches differ.
- Blood group universal donor/recipient — swapping O-negative (donor) and AB-positive (recipient) roles is a very common trap.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- Cell: Prokaryotic (no nucleus) vs Eukaryotic; Mitochondria=powerhouse, Nucleus=control, Chloroplast=photosynthesis (plants only), Cell wall=plants only.
- Cell Division: Mitosis=2 identical cells (growth); Meiosis=4 half-chromosome cells (gametes); humans=46 chromosomes (23 pairs).
- Body Systems: Digestive (mouth→intestine), Respiratory (lungs/alveoli), Circulatory (heart, RBC/WBC/platelets, blood groups), Nervous (brain/spinal cord), Excretory (kidneys/nephron), Endocrine (pituitary=master gland), Skeletal (206 bones), Muscular (3 types).
- Diseases: Bacterial (TB/Cholera/Typhoid), Viral (Flu/Measles/AIDS), Protozoan (Malaria via Anopheles), deficiency diseases tied to specific vitamins/minerals.
- Genetics: Mendel=father of genetics; DNA=Watson&Crick; XY=male-determines-sex.
- Evolution: Darwin=natural selection/survival of the fittest.
- Plant Biology: Photosynthesis (light-dependent, releases O₂) vs Respiration (continuous, releases energy); Xylem=water up, Phloem=food both ways; Transpiration=water loss via stomata.
- Nutrition: Carbs=energy, Proteins=growth, Fats=storage; Vitamin C=only non-storable vitamin; Iron=anemia, Iodine=goiter, Calcium=bones.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** Which cell organelle is known as the "powerhouse of the cell"?
A) Nucleus B) Mitochondria C) Golgi Apparatus D) Ribosome

**Q2.** Which type of cell division results in gametes (sperm/egg cells) with half the chromosome number?
A) Mitosis B) Meiosis C) Binary fission D) Budding

**Q3.** Deficiency of Vitamin C leads to which disease?
A) Rickets B) Beriberi C) Scurvy D) Night blindness

**Q4.** Malaria is caused by a protozoan parasite and is transmitted by which vector?
A) Aedes mosquito B) Female Anopheles mosquito C) Housefly D) Tsetse fly

**Q5.** Which blood group is considered the "universal donor"?
A) AB-positive B) A-positive C) O-negative D) B-negative

## Answer Key
1. **B) Mitochondria** — It generates ATP through cellular respiration, earning its nickname as the cell's powerhouse.
2. **B) Meiosis** — This division produces four haploid daughter cells (gametes) with half the parent's chromosome number, unlike mitosis.
3. **C) Scurvy** — Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, characterized by bleeding gums and weakness; Rickets is linked to Vitamin D instead.
4. **B) Female Anopheles mosquito** — This is the specific vector for Plasmodium, the parasite causing malaria.
5. **C) O-negative** — Lacking A, B, and Rh antigens, O-negative blood can be safely given to recipients of any blood type.
