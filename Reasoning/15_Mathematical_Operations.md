# 15. Mathematical Operations

## STEP 1: Explaining the Topic from Zero
**What is Mathematical Operations?**
In this topic, you are given a standard mathematical equation ($10 + 5 \times 2 = ?$ ), but there is a catch. The standard signs ($+ , -, \times, \div$) are either replaced by codes (e.g., "$A$ means $+$"), or interchanged (e.g., "$+$ is interchanged with $\times$"). Your job is to decode the equation and then solve it using the strict rules of mathematics.

**Why do examiners test this?**
This is not a test of high-level mathematics; it is a test of **Concentration, Speed, and Rule Adherence (BODMAS)** under stress. It appears heavily in SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, State PSCs, and Railways. 

**How Examiners Create Traps:**
1. **The Left-to-Right Trap:** You decode the equation as $10 + 5 \times 2$. Because you are rushing, you solve it left-to-right: $10 + 5 = 15$, then $15 \times 2 = 30$. *Trap!* BODMAS dictates multiplication happens first ($5 \times 2 = 10$, then $10 + 10 = 20$).
2. **The "Cannot Be Formed Whole Number" Trap:** Examiners give you options to interchange signs. One option makes the equation $17 \div 3$. You waste 30 seconds trying to calculate decimals, but the final answer is a whole number like 45. 
3. **The Reverse Decoding Trap:** The question states: " $+$ means $\times$ " and " $\times$ means $-$ ". Students hastily read this and swap them, assuming it means they interchange. It is a one-way street unless stated otherwise!

## STEP 2: Subtopics and Variations

### 1. Symbol / Letter Substitution (Very Common)
*   If $P$ denotes $\div$, $Q$ denotes $\times$, $R$ denotes $+$, find the value of $18 P 3 Q 5 R 4$.

### 2. Sign Interchange (High Time-Consumer)
*   "Which two signs should be interchanged to make the given equation correct: $12 + 6 \div 3 \times 2 - 8 = 14$ ?"

### 3. Number Interchange
*   "Which two numbers should be interchanged to make the equation correct?"

### 4. Coded Inequalities / Equations
*   $4 * 5 * 6 = 26$. $5 * 6 * 7 = 37$. What is $6 * 7 * 8$? (Finding the hidden mathematical logic).

## STEP 3: Solving Framework
**How should you think?**
1. **BODMAS is God:** **B**rackets, **O**f, **D**ivision, **M**ultiplication, **A**ddition, **S**ubtraction. You cannot violate this order, ever.
2. **The "Decode First, Solve Second" Rule:** Never try to solve the equation *while* you are decoding the symbols. It strains your working memory. Write down the decoded equation cleanly on your rough sheet, *then* apply BODMAS.

## STEP 4: Solving Methods
### Method 1: Traditional (Brute Force)
*   For "interchange" questions, you check Option A, write the whole equation, solve it. If wrong, check Option B, write, solve.
*   *When to use:* NEVER. It takes 2-3 minutes.

### Method 2: Fast Method (The Division Filter)
*   **The Division sign ($\div$) is your greatest weapon.** In 95% of government exam questions, the final answer is a whole integer. Therefore, the division operation *must* result in a whole number.
*   If an option asks you to place the $\div$ sign between 17 and 4 ($17 \div 4$), you instantly eliminate that option without calculating the rest of the equation.

### Method 3: Expert Method (Block Solving)
*   Once decoded, experts don't write multiple steps. They mentally group the Division and Multiplication parts into "Blocks" and solve them instantly. 
*   Equation: $20 + 15 \div 3 \times 2 - 5$.
*   Block: $(15 \div 3 \times 2) = 5 \times 2 = 10$.
*   Mental solve: $20 + 10 - 5 = 25$.

## STEP 5: Pattern Recognition Guide (5-10 Second Rule)
*   **Is it an "Interchange two signs" question?** -> Immediately locate the numbers currently around the $\div$ sign. If they don't divide perfectly (e.g., $14 \div 5$), the $\div$ sign **MUST** be one of the signs you interchange. Look at options containing $\div$.
*   **Is it a letter coding ($A, B, C, D$)?** -> Don't read the whole paragraph. Look at the equation, see the letter, glance at the paragraph for that specific letter, write the sign.

## STEP 6: Shortcuts & Memory Tricks
1. **The Division Filter Trick:** (Explained in Method 2). Always hunt for $\div$ first.
2. **The Addition/Subtraction Merging:** Once Multiplication and Division are done, the order of Addition and Subtraction doesn't actually matter mathematically as long as you keep the signs attached to the numbers. ($-5 + 10$ is the same as $10 - 5$). 
3. **Digit Sum (Advanced):** If the calculation is massive, calculate the digital root of the LHS and RHS. They must match. (Used rarely, only for massive calculations).

## STEP 7: Common Mistakes
*   **Misreading $+$, $\div$, and $\times$:** On computer screens, $+$ and $\div$ often look identical if the resolution is poor. Be extremely careful.
*   **Left-to-Right Execution:** $20 - 5 \times 2$. Student writes $15 \times 2 = 30$. Correct is $20 - 10 = 10$.
*   **Forgetting the sign of a number:** $10 - 5 + 2$. Student does $5+2 = 7$, then $10 - 7 = 3$. *Disastrous Mistake!* It is $(10 + 2) - 5 = 7$.

## STEP 8: Difficulty Progression

**Level 1 (Very Easy - SSC MTS / GD)**
*   If $+$ means $-$, $-$ means $\times$, $\times$ means $\div$, and $\div$ means $+$, then $15 \times 3 \div 4 - 6 + 7 = ?$
    *   *Decode:* $15 \div 3 + 4 \times 6 - 7$.
    *   *Solve:* $5 + 24 - 7 = 22$.

**Level 3 (Moderate - SSC CGL)**
*   Which two signs should be interchanged to make the equation correct?
    $18 + 6 - 6 \div 3 \times 3 = 6$
    Options: a) $+$ and $-$ b) $+$ and $\div$ c) $-$ and $\div$ d) $+$ and $\times$
    *   *Fast Method:* Current division is $6 \div 3 = 2$. It's a whole number, so $\div$ *might* not need changing. Let's check Option (a): swap $+$ and $-$. 
    Equation becomes: $18 - 6 + 6 \div 3 \times 3 \rightarrow 18 - 6 + 2 \times 3 \rightarrow 18 - 6 + 6 = 18$. Incorrect.
    Let's check Option (b): swap $+$ and $\div$.
    Equation: $18 \div 6 - 6 + 3 \times 3 \rightarrow 3 - 6 + 9 = 6$. Correct!

**Level 5 (State PSC / SSC CGL Mains)**
*   Which two numbers AND two signs should be interchanged to correct the equation?
    *   *(Requires high mental processing speed. Still rely heavily on the Division Filter).*

**Level 8 (Logical Equations - Railways/SSC)**
*   If $3 \% 8 \# 4 = 6$ and $9 \% 4 \# 3 = 12$, what is $12 \% 6 \# 24$?
    *   *Logic:* $\%$ means multiply, $\#$ means divide. $(3 \times 8) \div 4 = 6$. So, $(12 \times 6) \div 24 = 3$.

**Level 10 (Exam Simulation)**
*   A paragraph of 5 complex coded equations. You must find which of the 4 options represents a mathematically correct statement. (Pure speed test).

## STEP 9: Practice Session Guidelines
1. **Never skip writing the decoded equation:** Toppers can do many things mentally, but they always write down the newly decoded equation. The visual mistake of looking at a $+$ and trying to mentally treat it as a $-$ will cost you 2 marks.
2. **Master the Block Method:** Practice solving `(Div x Mult)` blocks in one single breath.

## STEP 10: Speed Training
*   **Simple Substitution:** Ideal: 30s | Expert: 15s.
*   **Sign Interchange (using Division Filter):** Ideal: 45s | Expert: 25s.
*   **Logical / Hidden signs:** Ideal: 40s | Expert: 20s.

## STEP 11: Pattern Mastery Test (Self-Assessment Checklist)
Can you avoid the traps?
1. `Solve: 16 - 4 + 2` -> *(Answer: 14. If you answered 10, you added 4+2 first, which is completely wrong. It is 16 + 2 = 18, 18 - 4 = 14).*
2. `Interchange to fix: 14 + 7 x 5 / 3 = ?` -> What should you look at first? *(Answer: The division block 5 / 3. It doesn't yield a whole number, so the / sign MUST be interchanged).*

## STEP 12: Revision (Cheat Sheet)
*   **BODMAS:** Bracket, Of, Divide, Multiply, Add, Subtract.
*   **The Division Filter:** In interchange questions, if the current $\div$ leaves a fraction, the $\div$ sign MUST be moved. Options without $\div$ can be eliminated.
*   **Write it down:** Always write the decoded equation cleanly before solving.
*   **Sign attachment:** The sign belongs to the number on its RIGHT. (In $10 - 5$, the minus belongs to the 5).
