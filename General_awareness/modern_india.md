# Modern India — British East India Company to INA

## 1. Overview
Modern India covers the rise and consolidation of British rule, the constitutional acts that shaped governance, the 1857 Revolt, and the entire freedom struggle from the formation of the INC to Independence. This is one of the highest-weightage GK topics because it's rich in dates, names, and cause-effect chains that examiners love to test.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**British East India Company (EIC) — Rise to Power**
- Established 1600 via Royal Charter from Elizabeth I; started as a trading company **[High]**
- Battle of Plassey (1757) — Robert Clive defeats Siraj-ud-Daulah, real start of British political power in Bengal **[High]**
- Battle of Buxar (1764) — decisive British victory over combined forces of Bengal, Awadh, Mughal Emperor; led to Diwani rights (1765) **[High]**
- Diwani Rights (1765) — EIC gets right to collect revenue in Bengal, Bihar, Odisha **[High]**
- Dual Government system in Bengal (1765–1772) by Robert Clive — administration split, later abolished by Warren Hastings **[Medium]**

**Governor-Generals of Bengal / India (Key Names)**
- Warren Hastings — first Governor-General of Bengal (1773), Regulating Act **[High]**
- Lord Cornwallis — Permanent Settlement (1793), Cornwallis Code **[High]**
- Lord Wellesley — Subsidiary Alliance system **[High]**
- Lord William Bentinck — abolition of Sati (1829), first Governor-General of India (post-1833 Act) **[High]**
- Lord Dalhousie — Doctrine of Lapse, railways/telegraph introduced, last Governor-General before 1857 **[High]**
- Lord Canning — last Governor-General of India & first Viceroy (1858 transition point) **[High]**

**Viceroys of India (post-1858)**
- Lord Canning — first Viceroy; 1857 Revolt suppressed during his tenure **[High]**
- Lord Curzon — Partition of Bengal (1905), Ancient Monuments Act **[High]**
- Lord Hardinge II — capital shifted Calcutta→Delhi (1911), Delhi Durbar **[Medium]**
- Lord Chelmsford — Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919), Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919), Rowlatt Act **[High]**
- Lord Irwin — Dandi March/Salt Satyagraha (1930), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) **[High]**
- Lord Willingdon — Communal Award, Poona Pact (1932) **[Medium]**
- Lord Linlithgow — Quit India Movement (1942), Cripps Mission (1942) **[High]**
- Lord Wavell — Wavell Plan, Cabinet Mission (1946) **[Medium]**
- Lord Mountbatten — last Viceroy, Mountbatten Plan, Independence & Partition (1947) **[High]**
- C. Rajagopalachari — only Indian & LAST Governor-General of independent India (1948–50) **[High]**

**1857 Revolt**
- Started at Meerut (10 May 1857); immediate cause = greased cartridges (Enfield rifle) **[High]**
- Leaders: Mangal Pandey (Barrackpore trigger), Bahadur Shah II (nominal leader, Delhi), Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Tantia Tope (Kanpur), Nana Saheb (Kanpur), Begum Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow) **[High]**
- Called by different names: Sepoy Mutiny (British term), First War of Independence (V.D. Savarkar's term) **[High]**
- Failed due to lack of unity, no common leadership/ideology, better British resources **[Medium]**
- Result: EIC rule ended, Crown took direct control via Government of India Act 1858 **[High]**

**Indian National Congress (INC) & Freedom Movement Phases**
- INC founded 1885 by A.O. Hume (retired British civil servant); first session in Bombay, President W.C. Bonnerjee **[High]**
- Moderate Phase (1885–1905) — Dadabhai Naoroji (Drain of Wealth theory), Gopal Krishna Gokhale; believed in petitions/dialogue **[High]**
- Extremist Phase (1905–1917) — Lal-Bal-Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal); Swadeshi Movement (1905, anti-Bengal Partition) **[High]**
- Surat Split (1907) — Congress split into Moderates and Extremists **[Medium]**
- Lucknow Pact (1916) — Congress-Muslim League unity, both factions of Congress reunite **[Medium]**
- Revolutionary Phase — Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Khudiram Bose, Ghadar Party, Ram Prasad Bismil (HRA/HSRA) **[High]**
- Gandhian Era (1919–1947): Champaran Satyagraha (1917, first), Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22, ended due to Chauri Chaura), Civil Disobedience Movement (1930, Dandi March), Quit India Movement (1942, "Do or Die") **[High]**
- Indian National Army (INA/Azad Hind Fauj) — founded by Rash Behari Bose, led by Subhas Chandra Bose; slogan "Jai Hind," "Delhi Chalo" **[High]**
- Simon Commission (1927) — all-British members, boycotted ("Simon Go Back"), Lala Lajpat Rai died from lathi charge protest **[Medium]**
- Round Table Conferences (1930–32, London) — three conferences; Gandhi attended only the 2nd **[Medium]**
- Direct Action Day (1946) — Muslim League call, communal violence, path toward Partition **[Medium]**

**Social Reformers**
- Raja Ram Mohan Roy — Brahmo Samaj (1828), abolition of Sati; "Father of Indian Renaissance" **[High]**
- Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar — widow remarriage, women's education **[High]**
- Swami Dayanand Saraswati — Arya Samaj (1875), "Back to Vedas" **[High]**
- Swami Vivekananda — Ramakrishna Mission (1897), Chicago speech (1893) **[High]**
- Jyotiba Phule — Satyashodhak Samaj, caste/women's rights in Maharashtra **[Medium]**
- Sir Syed Ahmed Khan — Aligarh Movement, Muslim modernization, MAO College **[Medium]**
- B.R. Ambedkar — Dalit rights, later chief architect of the Constitution **[High]**

**Important Constitutional Acts**
- Regulating Act 1773 — first step of British Parliament control over EIC; created Governor-General of Bengal post **[High]**
- Pitt's India Act 1784 — dual control (Court of Directors + Board of Control) **[High]**
- Charter Act 1813 — EIC trade monopoly ended (except tea, China trade) **[Medium]**
- Charter Act 1833 — EIC trade monopoly fully ended, Governor-General of Bengal became Governor-General of INDIA (Lord William Bentinck first) **[High]**
- Charter Act 1853 — separate legislative and executive functions, open competition for civil services introduced **[Medium]**
- Government of India Act 1858 — EIC rule ended, Crown rule (British Raj) began, Secretary of State for India created **[High]**
- Indian Councils Act 1861, 1892, 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms — separate electorates for Muslims introduced) **[High]**
- Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) — Dyarchy in provinces **[High]**
- Government of India Act 1935 — provincial autonomy, blueprint for Indian Constitution, federal structure proposed **[High]**
- Indian Independence Act 1947 — British Parliament act granting independence & partition **[High]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| Battle of Plassey vs Battle of Buxar | Plassey (1757) = first political foothold, vs Siraj-ud-Daulah; Buxar (1764) = decisive victory, led to Diwani Rights (1765) |
| Warren Hastings vs Lord Canning as "first" | Warren Hastings = first Governor-General of BENGAL (1773); Lord Canning = first VICEROY of India (1858) — different titles, different years |
| Charter Act 1813 vs 1833 | 1813 = trade monopoly ended EXCEPT tea & China; 1833 = trade monopoly ended COMPLETELY + title changed to Governor-General of India |
| Moderates vs Extremists | Moderates (Naoroji, Gokhale) = petitions, dialogue, gradual reform; Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal) = mass agitation, Swadeshi, self-reliance |
| Non-Cooperation vs Civil Disobedience vs Quit India | Non-Cooperation (1920, boycott) → Civil Disobedience (1930, break law directly, Dandi March) → Quit India (1942, "leave India now," most intense) |
| Simon Commission vs Round Table Conference | Simon Commission = all-British, no Indians, boycotted; Round Table Conferences = Indians included, held in London |
| Rash Behari Bose vs Subhas Chandra Bose (INA) | Rash Behari Bose = founded INA first (Japan support); Subhas Chandra Bose = later led and revitalized INA, more famous association |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **Governor-Generals of Bengal order** — "**W**arren **H**as **C**old **W**ine **B**efore **D**inner" → **W**arren **H**astings → **C**ornwallis → **W**ellesley → **B**entinck → **D**alhousie (simplified key sequence).
- **1857 Revolt leaders by city** — picture a map: Delhi=Bahadur Shah II, Jhansi=Lakshmibai, Kanpur=Nana Saheb/Tantia Tope, Lucknow=Begum Hazrat Mahal — one leader per city pin.
- **Freedom movement phase order** — "**M**oderate **E**xtremist **R**evolutionary **G**andhian" → M-E-R-G(e): the movement "merged" into one force by Gandhi's era.
- **Charter Acts progression** — remember it as trade rights shrinking each time: 1793(monopoly full)→1813(partial loss)→1833(monopoly gone)→1853(open competition) — trade power decreases as years increase.
- **Round Table Conferences** — Gandhi attended only the **2nd** one — remember "2nd RTC = 2 way talk with Gandhi," 1st and 3rd had no Congress/Gandhi presence.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- Swapping **"first Governor-General of Bengal"** (Warren Hastings) with **"first Governor-General of India"** (Bentinck) or **"first Viceroy"** (Canning) — three different "firsts," commonly confused.
- **Act-year mismatches**: examiners swap provisions between Charter Act 1813/1833/1853 or GoI Act 1919/1935.
- "Sepoy Mutiny" vs "First War of Independence" naming trap — both refer to 1857 Revolt, just different perspectives (British vs Savarkar's nationalist framing).
- Confusing **which movement ended due to violence** — Non-Cooperation (Chauri Chaura, 1922) is the one Gandhi called off after violence, NOT Civil Disobedience or Quit India.
- Mixing up **social reformer–organization pairs** (Brahmo Samaj=Ram Mohan Roy, Arya Samaj=Dayanand Saraswati, Ramakrishna Mission=Vivekananda) — a very frequent MCQ pairing trap.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- EIC rise: Plassey (1757) → Buxar (1764) → Diwani Rights (1765).
- GGs of Bengal: Hastings→Cornwallis→Wellesley→Bentinck→Dalhousie; Canning = last GG & first Viceroy.
- 1857 Revolt: Meerut start, greased cartridges, key leaders per city, ended EIC rule → Crown rule (1858).
- INC: founded 1885 (A.O. Hume) → Moderate (Naoroji/Gokhale) → Extremist (Lal-Bal-Pal) → Gandhian (Champaran→NCM→CDM→QIM) → INA (Bose) → Independence 1947.
- Reformers: Ram Mohan Roy (Brahmo Samaj/Sati abolition), Dayanand Saraswati (Arya Samaj), Vivekananda (Ramakrishna Mission), Ambedkar (Dalit rights/Constitution).
- Acts: Regulating (1773) → Pitt's (1784) → Charter Acts (1813/1833/1853) → GoI 1858 (Crown rule) → Councils Acts (1861/1892/1909) → GoI 1919 (Dyarchy) → GoI 1935 (federal blueprint) → Independence Act 1947.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** The Battle of Buxar (1764) was fought between the British and a combined force of:
A) Siraj-ud-Daulah alone
B) Bengal, Awadh, and the Mughal Emperor
C) Tipu Sultan and Hyderabad
D) Marathas and Sikhs

**Q2.** Who was the first Governor-General of India (post Charter Act 1833)?
A) Warren Hastings B) Lord Cornwallis C) Lord William Bentinck D) Lord Canning

**Q3.** The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 by:
A) Dadabhai Naoroji B) A.O. Hume C) W.C. Bonnerjee D) Gopal Krishna Gokhale

**Q4.** The Non-Cooperation Movement was called off by Gandhi after which incident?
A) Jallianwala Bagh B) Chauri Chaura C) Dandi March D) Quit India arrests

**Q5.** Which Act introduced Dyarchy (dual government) in the provinces?
A) Government of India Act 1919
B) Government of India Act 1935
C) Indian Councils Act 1909
D) Charter Act 1853

## Answer Key
1. **B) Bengal, Awadh, and the Mughal Emperor** — Buxar (1764) was won by the British against this combined force, a more decisive win than Plassey.
2. **C) Lord William Bentinck** — After the Charter Act of 1833 changed the title from Governor-General of Bengal to Governor-General of India.
3. **B) A.O. Hume** — A retired British civil servant who founded the INC in 1885; W.C. Bonnerjee was its first President, not founder.
4. **B) Chauri Chaura** — Violence at Chauri Chaura (1922) led Gandhi to call off the Non-Cooperation Movement.
5. **A) Government of India Act 1919** — Introduced Dyarchy in provinces as part of the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms.
