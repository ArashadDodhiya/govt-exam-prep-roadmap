# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Percentage?

**Percentage** means **"per hundred"** or **"out of 100"**.

When you say "I scored 80%", you mean: out of every 100 marks, you got 80.

> **Percentage is a way of comparing things on a COMMON SCALE of 100.**

This is its biggest power. Without percentages, you cannot compare:
- A score of 45/60 with 72/90 — which is better?
- A profit of ₹500 on ₹2000 vs ₹800 on ₹5000 — which business is more profitable?

Percentage makes everything comparable.

## The Logical Idea

Imagine you have 3/5 of a pizza and your friend has 7/10 of a pizza. Who has more?

Without percentage: You need to find LCM (10) and compare 6/10 vs 7/10.

With percentage: 3/5 = 60% and 7/10 = 70%. Your friend has more. **Done in 2 seconds.**

> **Percentage = The universal translator of mathematics.**

| **Profit & Loss** | Profit% = (Profit/CP) × 100 → NEEDS percentage |

>

## CONCEPT 2: "x% of y" — The Basic Calculation

### Formula

> **x% of y = (x/100) × y**

### Shortcut

> **x% of y = y% of x** (commutative property)
>
> Always pick the EASIER calculation!

**Example**: 35% of 40
- Method 1 (hard): 35/100 × 40 = complicated
- Method 2 (easy): 40% of 35 = 0.4 × 35 = **14** ✅

**Example**: 12% of 250
- Method 1: 12/100 × 250 = 30
- Method 2: 250% of 12 = 2.5 × 12 = **30** ✅ (same answer, but pick whichever feels faster)

### Breaking Down Percentages

| To find | Break it as |
|---|---|
| 15% | 10% + 5% (half of 10%) |
| 17.5% | 10% + 5% + 2.5% |
| 35% | 30% + 5% = 3×10% + 5% |
| 45% | 50% − 5% |
| 7% | 10% − 3% |
| 2.5% | Half of 5% |
| 37.5% | 3/8 (use fraction, way faster) |

**Example**: 35% of 600
- 10% = 60
- 30% = 180
- 5% = 30
- 35% = 180 + 30 = **210**

## CONCEPT 3: "What percentage is A of B?"

### Formula

> **A is what % of B = (A/B) × 100**

### Example

36 is what % of 80?
= (36/80) × 100 = (9/20) × 100 = **45%**

### Common Mistake

❌ Students confuse "A is what % of B" with "B is what % of A"

**Always identify**: What is the PART? What is the WHOLE?
- "A is what % of B" → A is part, B is whole
- "What % of B is A" → Same thing: A is part, B is whole

## CONCEPT 4: Percentage Increase and Decrease

### Percentage Increase

> **Percentage Increase = [(New − Original) / Original] × 100**
>
> **New Value = Original × (1 + Increase%/100)**

### Percentage Decrease

> **Percentage Decrease = [(Original − New) / Original] × 100**
>
> **New Value = Original × (1 − Decrease%/100)**

### Key Insight: The BASE Matters!

The percentage is ALWAYS calculated on the **ORIGINAL** value (not the new value).

**Example**: If price increases from ₹200 to ₹250:
- Increase = 250 − 200 = ₹50
- Increase % = (50/200) × 100 = **25%**
- NOT (50/250) × 100 = 20% ← WRONG BASE!

### Multiplier Method (FASTEST APPROACH)

Instead of calculating increase/decrease separately, use multipliers:

| Change | Multiplier | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 10% increase | × 1.1 | 500 × 1.1 = 550 |
| 10% decrease | × 0.9 | 500 × 0.9 = 450 |
| 20% increase | × 1.2 | 500 × 1.2 = 600 |
| 25% decrease | × 0.75 | 800 × 0.75 = 600 |

| 33.33% decrease | × 2/3 | 300 × 2/3 = 200 |
| 50% increase | × 1.5 = 3/2 | 400 × 3/2 = 600 |
| 12.5% increase | × 9/8 | 800 × 9/8 = 900 |

## CONCEPT 5: "A is what % MORE/LESS than B?"

### This is the MOST CONFUSING concept in Percentage. Pay full attention.

### Formulas

> **A is X% more than B → A = B × (1 + X/100)**
> → **X = [(A − B) / B] × 100** (base is B)
>
> **A is Y% less than B → A = B × (1 − Y/100)**
> → **Y = [(B − A) / B] × 100** (base is B)

### The CRITICAL RULE

> **"A is __% more than B" → Base is B (denominator is B)**
> **"B is __% less than A" → Base is A (denominator is A)**

### Example

If A = 60 and B = 50:
- "A is what % more than B?" = [(60−50)/50] × 100 = (10/50) × 100 = **20%**
- "B is what % less than A?" = [(60−50)/60] × 100 = (10/60) × 100 = **16.67%**

**Same two numbers, but different percentages because the BASE changed!**

### Conversion Formula Between "More" and "Less"

If A is x% more than B, then B is y% less than A:

> **y = [x/(100+x)] × 100**

If A is x% less than B, then B is y% more than A:

> **y = [x/(100−x)] × 100**

### Quick Conversion Table (MEMORIZE)

| If A is ___% more than B | Then B is ___% less than A |
|---|---|
| 10% more | 9.09% less (1/11) |
| 20% more | 16.67% less (1/6) |
| 25% more | 20% less (1/5) |
| 33.33% more | 25% less (1/4) |
| 50% more | 33.33% less (1/3) |
| 60% more | 37.5% less (3/8) |
| 100% more | 50% less (1/2) |

### Why This Works — Fraction Logic

If A is 25% more than B → A = 5/4 × B → B = 4/5 × A → B is 1/5 less = 20% less

If A is 50% more than B → A = 3/2 × B → B = 2/3 × A → B is 1/3 less = 33.33% less

**This fraction approach is FAR faster than the formula!**

## CONCEPT 6: Successive Percentage Change

### Definition

When **two or more percentage changes** happen one after another on the SAME quantity.

### Formula for Two Successive Changes

If a value changes by a% and then by b%:

> **Net effect = a + b + (ab/100) %**

### Why the formula works

Original value = 100
After a% increase = 100 + a = 100(1 + a/100)
After b% increase on new value = (100 + a)(1 + b/100) = 100 + a + b + ab/100
Net change from original 100 = **a + b + ab/100**

### Important Cases

| Case | Formula gives |
|---|---|
| Both increase (a%, b%) | a + b + ab/100 (positive usually) |
| Both decrease (−a%, −b%) | −a − b + ab/100 (note: ab/100 is positive) |
| First increase a%, then decrease b% | a − b − ab/100 |
| Increase then same decrease | −a²/100 (always a net DECREASE!) |

### Example 1: Both increase

Price increases by 10% then by 20%. Net effect?
= 10 + 20 + (10×20)/100 = 10 + 20 + 2 = **32%** increase

### Example 2: Increase then decrease

Salary increases by 20% then decreases by 10%. Net effect?
= 20 − 10 − (20×10)/100 = 20 − 10 − 2 = **8%** increase

### Example 3: Same increase and decrease

Price increases by 25% then decreases by 25%. Net effect?
= 25 − 25 − (25×25)/100 = 0 − 625/100 = **−6.25%** decrease

> **TRAP**: Increase by x% then decrease by x% does NOT give 0%. It always gives a NET DECREASE of x²/100 %!

### Three Successive Changes

For three changes a%, b%, c% — apply the formula twice:
1. First find net of a% and b% → call it m%
2. Then find net of m% and c%

Or use the multiplier method (cleaner):
Net multiplier = (1 + a/100) × (1 + b/100) × (1 + c/100)

## CONCEPT 7: Price, Expenditure, Consumption Relationship

### The Golden Relationship

> **Expenditure = Price × Consumption**
>
> If Expenditure is CONSTANT:
> **Price ↑ → Consumption ↓ (inversely proportional)**

### Standard Question Type

"If the price of sugar increases by 25%, by what percentage must a family reduce consumption so that expenditure remains the same?"

### Formula

> **If price increases by x%, reduction in consumption = [x/(100+x)] × 100%**
>
> **If price decreases by x%, increase in consumption = [x/(100−x)] × 100%**

### Example

Price increases by 25%:
Reduction = [25/(100+25)] × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = **20%**

**Fraction approach** (faster):
- 25% increase = price becomes 5/4 of original
- To keep expenditure same, consumption becomes 4/5 of original
- Reduction = 1/5 = **20%**

### Quick Reference Table

| Price Increase | Consumption Decrease |
|---|---|
| 10% | 9.09% (1/11) |
| 20% | 16.67% (1/6) |
| 25% | 20% (1/5) |
| 33.33% | 25% (1/4) |
| 50% | 33.33% (1/3) |
| 100% | 50% (1/2) |

**Notice**: This is the SAME table as "A is x% more than B, B is y% less than A"!

> **This is NOT a coincidence. The mathematical relationship is identical.** Learn ONE table, use it in 5 different question types.

## CONCEPT 8: Population / Growth / Depreciation

### Growth Formula

> **P(after n years) = P(initial) × (1 + r/100)ⁿ**

Where:
- P(initial) = initial population/value
- r = growth rate per year (in %)
- n = number of years

### Depreciation Formula

> **V(after n years) = V(initial) × (1 − r/100)ⁿ**

### Example 1: Population Growth

Population of a town is 50,000. It increases at 10% per year. What will it be after 3 years?

P = 50000 × (1 + 10/100)³ = 50000 × (1.1)³ = 50000 × 1.331 = **66,550**

### Example 2: Depreciation

A car worth ₹5,00,000 depreciates at 20% per year. Value after 2 years?

V = 500000 × (1 − 20/100)² = 500000 × (0.8)² = 500000 × 0.64 = **₹3,20,000**

### Example 3: Different Growth Rates

Population grows by 10% in 1st year, 20% in 2nd year, 30% in 3rd year. If initial = 10,000, find final.

P = 10000 × 1.1 × 1.2 × 1.3 = 10000 × 1.716 = **17,160**

### Shortcut: Working Backwards

"The population of a town is 66,550 after 3 years of 10% annual growth. What was it initially?"

P(initial) = 66550 / (1.1)³ = 66550 / 1.331 = **50,000**

## CONCEPT 9: Election Problems

### Standard Pattern

In an election between two candidates:
- Total votes = Valid votes + Invalid votes
- Winner's votes = Valid votes × (winner's %)
- Loser's votes = Valid votes × (loser's %)
- Margin = Winner's votes − Loser's votes

### Example

In an election, candidate A got 65% of valid votes and won by 2100 votes. 10% of total votes were invalid. Find total votes.

**Solution**:
- A got 65%, so B got 35% of valid votes
- Margin = 65% − 35% = 30% of valid votes = 2100
- Valid votes = 2100 / 0.30 = 7000
- Valid = 90% of Total (since 10% invalid)
- Total = 7000 / 0.90 = **7778** (approximately 7777.78)

### Key Formula

> **Margin = (Difference in %) × Valid votes / 100**

## CONCEPT 10: Percentage Restoration

### The Concept

"If X is decreased by 20%, by how much should the new value be increased to get back to original?"

### Formula

> **If decreased by x%, increase required = [x/(100−x)] × 100%**
>
> **If increased by x%, decrease required = [x/(100+x)] × 100%**

### Example

Value decreased by 20%. Increase required to restore?
= [20/(100−20)] × 100 = 20/80 × 100 = **25%**

**Fraction approach**:
- 20% decrease → value becomes 4/5 of original
- To go from 4/5 back to 1 → multiply by 5/4 → increase by 1/4 = **25%**

### Another Example

Value increased by 50%. Decrease required to restore?
= [50/(100+50)] × 100 = 50/150 × 100 = **33.33%**

**Fraction approach**:
- 50% increase → value becomes 3/2 of original
- To go from 3/2 back to 1 → multiply by 2/3 → decrease by 1/3 = **33.33%**

## CONCEPT 11: Percentage Error

### Formula

> **% Error = [(Measured value − True value) / True value] × 100**

### Application in Area/Volume

If length is measured with x% error, and breadth with y% error:

| Measurement | % Error in result |
|---|---|
| Area = L × B | ≈ x + y + xy/100 (successive percentage formula!) |
| Volume = L × B × H | Use multiplier method for 3 changes |
| Area of square (side error x%) | ≈ 2x + x²/100 |
| Volume of cube (side error x%) | ≈ 3x (approximately, for small x) |

**Example**: If side of a square is measured 10% too much, what is the % error in area?

Error in area = 10 + 10 + (10×10)/100 = **21%** too much

| 16.67% of 540 | 1/6 | 540/6 = **90** |
| 14.28% of 490 | 1/7 | 490/7 = **70** |
| 37.5% of 240 | 3/8 | 240×3/8 = **90** |
| 66.67% of 150 | 2/3 | 150×2/3 = **100** |

Time per calculation: **3–5 seconds** (vs 30+ by long method)

## Shortcut 2: "More/Less Than" — Quick Conversion

Remember: **If A is (1/n) more than B, then B is 1/(n+1) less than A**

| If A is this much more than B | Then B is this much less than A |
|---|---|
| 1/5 = 20% more | 1/6 = 16.67% less |
| 1/4 = 25% more | 1/5 = 20% less |
| 1/3 = 33.33% more | 1/4 = 25% less |
| 1/2 = 50% more | 1/3 = 33.33% less |
| 2/5 = 40% more | 2/7 = 28.57% less |

**How to remember**: If the fraction is 1/n → it becomes 1/(n+1). Just add 1 to denominator!

## Shortcut 3: "Building Block" Method

**Build ANY percentage from 10%, 5%, and 1%:**

**Example**: Find 23% of 600
- 10% of 600 = 60
- 20% = 120 (double the 10%)
- 3% = 18 (triple the 1% = 6)
- 23% = 120 + 18 = **138**

**Example**: Find 47% of 800
- 50% of 800 = 400
- 3% of 800 = 24
- 47% = 400 − 24 = **376**

## Shortcut 4: Successive Percentage — Multiplier Chain

Instead of using the formula a + b + ab/100 (which can be error-prone), use:

**Multiplier chain**: Just multiply all multipliers together.

**Example**: Price goes up 20%, then 10%, then down 5%.

Multiplier = 1.2 × 1.1 × 0.95 = 1.254

Net change = +25.4% increase

For **fraction multipliers** (even faster):
= 6/5 × 11/10 × 19/20 = 6 × 11 × 19 / (5 × 10 × 20) = 1254/1000 = **25.4% increase**

## Shortcut 5: Percentage of Percentage

**"What is 20% of 30%?"** = 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.06 = **6%**

**"25% of a number is 40% of another. What is the ratio?"**
- 25% of A = 40% of B
- A/4 = 2B/5
- A/B = 8/5

|---|---|---|
| **Percentage increase** | Relative change | From 20% to 25% → (5/20)×100 = **25% increase** |
| **Percentage point increase** | Absolute change | From 20% to 25% → **5 percentage points** increase |

Exams LOVE this distinction, especially in DI.

---

## Confusion 2: "More than" Base Confusion

| Statement | Base | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| "A is 20% more than B" | B | A = 1.2B, Increase = (A−B)/B × 100 |
| "B is how much % less than A?" | A | Decrease = (A−B)/A × 100 |
| "A is 20% of B" | B | A = 0.2B |
| "A is 20% more than B" | B | A = 1.2B (NOT 0.2B!) |

> **Rule**: The name that appears AFTER "more/less than" is the BASE.
> "A is ___% more than **B**" → B is the base.
> "**B** is ___% less than A" → A is the base.

---

## Confusion 3: Successive vs Simple Change

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| 20% increase followed by 20% decrease | NOT 0%. Net = −4% (a loss!) |
| Two 10% increases | NOT 20%. Net = 21% |
| 50% increase + 50% increase | NOT 100%. Net = 125% |
| 100% increase + 100% increase | NOT 200%. Net = 300% |

---

## Confusion 4: "Of" vs "More Than" vs "Less Than"

| Statement | Mathematical Meaning |
|---|---|
| A is 30% **of** B | A = 0.3B |
| A is 30% **more than** B | A = 1.3B |
| A is 30% **less than** B | A = 0.7B |

"Of" and "more than" give COMPLETELY different answers!

---

## Confusion 5: Expenditure, Price, Consumption

| What's constant? | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Expenditure constant** | Price × Consumption = constant → inversely proportional |
| **Consumption constant** | Expenditure changes directly with price |
| **Price constant** | Expenditure changes directly with consumption |

Read the question carefully to know WHAT is constant.

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


---

| # | Trap | How it works | How to detect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Base confusion in "more/less"** | "A is 25% more than B" → students use A as base | "More than X" → X is the base | 50 is 25% more than 40, but 40 is 20% less than 50 |
| 2 | **Successive = Simple trap** | "Increase 10% then 10%" → students write 20% | Two successive changes ≠ simple addition! Use formula | Net = 10+10+1 = 21% |
| 3 | **Same increase-decrease trap** | "Up 25%, then down 25%" → students say "no change" | Always a NET LOSS of x²/100% | 25²/100 = 6.25% loss |
| 4 | **"Of" vs "More than"** | "A is 30% of B" vs "A is 30% more than B" → very different | "of" = multiply. "more than" = add to base | 30% of 200 = 60 vs 30% more than 200 = 260 |
| 5 | **Wrong base in restoration** | "Decreased by 20%, now increase by 20% to restore" | Restoration needs x/(100−x)%, not x% | Need 25%, not 20% |
| 6 | **% points vs % change** | "Increased from 40% to 50%" → "by what %?" | Are they asking percentage points (10) or % change (25%)? | Read question VERY carefully |
| 7 | **Population: confusing years** | "After 2 years" → is current year included or not? | n in formula = number of growth periods | Usually n = stated years |
| 8 | **Invalid votes in election** | Students calculate % on total votes instead of valid votes | Always find VALID votes first, then apply % | |
| 9 | **Expenditure constant assumption** | Students assume expenditure is constant when it's not stated | Check: does the question say "expenditure remains same"? | If not stated, don't assume! |
| 10 | **x% of y vs y% of x** | Students don't realize these are equal | x% of y = y% of x → use whichever is easier! | 8% of 50 = 50% of 8 = 4 |
| 11 | **Percentage of a percentage** | "30% of 40% of 500" → confusion about order | Multiply: 0.3 × 0.4 × 500 = 60 | |
| 12 | **"Increased BY" vs "Increased TO"** | "Increased by 20%" means +20%. "Increased to 120%" means final is 120%. | "By" = change amount. "To" = final value. | Both give 120% final, but reading matters |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


---

## Speed Rule 1: Always Convert to Fractions FIRST

This single habit will save you **5–10 minutes per exam**.

| Don't do this (slow) | Do this (fast) |
|---|---|
| 33.33% of 780 = (33.33/100) × 780 | 1/3 × 780 = **260** |
| 62.5% of 480 = (62.5/100) × 480 | 5/8 × 480 = **300** |
| 14.28% of 630 = (14.28/100) × 630 | 1/7 × 630 = **90** |

## Speed Rule 2: For "More/Less Than," Use the Fraction Table

Instead of calculating [(A−B)/B] × 100 every time:

- If A/B = 6/5 → A is 1/5 more = 20% more. B is 1/6 less = 16.67% less.
- If A/B = 7/4 → A is 3/4 more = 75% more. B is 3/7 less = 42.86% less.

## Speed Rule 3: For Successive %, Think in Multipliers

20% up then 10% down = × 6/5 × 9/10 = 54/50 = 27/25 = 1.08 → **8% up**

Fraction multiplication is faster than a + b + ab/100.

## Speed Rule 4: Percentage of Large Numbers

To find 7.5% of 4800:
- 7.5% = 5% + 2.5%
- 5% of 4800 = 240
- 2.5% of 4800 = 120
- Total = **360**

OR: 7.5% = 3/40 → 4800 × 3/40 = 120 × 3 = **360**

## Speed Rule 5: DI Speed — Avoid Full Calculation

In DI, you rarely need exact values. If options are:
(A) 15% (B) 25% (C) 35% (D) 45% (E) 55%

A rough estimate is enough. Don't calculate to 2 decimal places!

> a + b + ab/100

## Mnemonic 3: Same Increase-Decrease Net Effect

> **"Going up and coming down the same stairs → you end up BELOW where you started"**
>
> 20% up then 20% down ≠ zero. You LOSE x²/100%.
> "You never come back to the same floor."

---

## Mnemonic 4: Price-Consumption Relationship

> **"If sugar gets costlier by 1/4, you eat 1/5 less to spend the same"**
>
> Price up by 1/n → Consumption down by 1/(n+1)
> Just add 1 to the denominator!

## Visualization: The Percentage Triangle

```
         x% of y = ?
              △
             / \
            /   \
           /  ?  \
          /       \
         x% ——————— y
         
   To find any one, use the other two:
   ? = x% × y
   x% = ? / y × 100
   y = ? / (x/100)
```

## Story Trick: Why Successive ≠ Simple

> Imagine you have ₹100.
> Your boss says "I'll give you 10% raise." You now have ₹110.
> Next month: "10% raise again!" → 10% of ₹110 = ₹11. You now have ₹121.
> After 2 months, you gained ₹21 — that's 21%, NOT 20%.
> The extra 1% came because the SECOND raise was calculated on the ALREADY RAISED amount.
> That "interest on the raise" = ab/100 = 10×10/100 = 1%.
