## 🏛️ Overview of GPSC (Gujarat Public Service Commission)

### What is GPSC?

The **Gujarat Public Service Commission (GPSC)** is a **constitutional body** established under **Article 315 of the Indian Constitution** for the state of Gujarat. It was constituted on **May 1, 1960**, coinciding with the formation of Gujarat as a separate state. GPSC functions as the primary recruitment body for **Class 1 (Gazetted)** and **Class 2 (Non-Gazetted)** posts in the Gujarat state government.

GPSC is headquartered at **Sector 10-A, Near Old Sachivalay, Gandhinagar, Gujarat — 382010**.

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Established** | May 1, 1960 |
| **Constitutional Provision** | Article 315–323 of the Indian Constitution |
| **Headquarters** | Gandhinagar, Gujarat |
| **Official Website** | https://gpsc.gujarat.gov.in |
| **Application Portal** | OJAS — https://gpsc-ojas.gujarat.gov.in |
| **Chairman** | Appointed by the Governor of Gujarat |
| **Reports to** | Governor of Gujarat |

### Role and Mandate of GPSC

GPSC is responsible for:

- Conducting **competitive examinations** for recruitment to Civil Services and other state government posts
- Advising the state government on matters relating to methods of **recruitment** and **promotions**
- Advising on **disciplinary matters** affecting government servants
- Conducting **departmental examinations** for existing government employees
- Recommending candidates for **Class 1 and Class 2** posts across all Gujarat government departments

## 1. GPSC Class 1 & 2 — Combined Competitive Examination (CCE) — Complete Blueprint

### 1.1 Purpose of the Exam

The **GPSC Combined Competitive Examination (CCE)** is the **most prestigious state-level civil services exam in Gujarat**. It is the gateway to the **Gujarat Administrative Service (GAS)** and the **Gujarat Civil Service (GCS)**, recruiting officers for key administrative, police, revenue, and developmental positions across the state.

This exam is the **Gujarat equivalent of the UPSC Civil Services Examination** and offers:

- **Class 1 posts** such as **Deputy Collector** and **Deputy Superintendent of Police (DySP)** — posts of immense power, prestige, and authority at the district level
- **Class 2 posts** such as **Mamlatdar, Taluka Development Officer (TDO), Section Officer** — the backbone of taluka/district-level administration
- A pathway to eventually being inducted into the **IAS/IPS** through the promotee/conferred quota (approximately 33% of cadre strength)
- Direct involvement in governance, policy implementation, disaster management, law & order, and development at the grassroots level

The exam is conducted **once every year** (though delays occasionally occur), and is the most sought-after state government examination in Gujarat.

### 1.8 Selection Process

The selection process for GPSC Class 1 & 2 consists of the following stages:

1. **Preliminary Examination** — Screening test (marks NOT counted in final merit)
2. **Mains Examination** — Descriptive/written test (marks counted in final merit — 1250 marks from merit papers)
3. **Personality Test / Interview** — 150 marks
4. **Document Verification** — Verification of original documents
5. **Medical Examination** — For certain posts (DySP, Police Inspector)
6. **Physical Test** — For police service posts (DySP, Police Inspector)

**Final Merit Calculation:**

> **Final Merit = Mains Merit Papers (Papers III to VII) — 1250 marks + Interview — 150 marks = Total 1400 marks**
>
> Prelims marks are **NOT** included in the final merit.
> Gujarati and English Language papers (Papers I & II) are **qualifying** only (minimum 25% = 75/300 required).

### 1.9 Exam Pattern — Preliminary Examination

**Mode**: Offline (OMR-based) — though some recent years have shifted to CBT (Computer Based Test)
**Nature**: Objective (Multiple Choice Questions)
**Purpose**: Screening test to shortlist candidates for Mains

| Paper | Subject | Questions | Marks | Duration | Negative Marking |
|-------|---------|-----------|-------|----------|-----------------|
| Paper 1 | General Studies I | 200 | 200 | 3 hours | −0.33 per wrong answer (1/3rd deduction) |
| Paper 2 | General Studies II | 200 | 200 | 3 hours | −0.33 per wrong answer (1/3rd deduction) |
| **Total** | | **400** | **400** | **6 hours (2 sessions)** | **−0.33 per wrong** |

- Each correct answer: +1 mark
- Each wrong answer: −0.33 marks (1/3 penalty)
- Unanswered: 0 marks
- Language: Question papers in both English and Gujarati (English version is authoritative in case of ambiguity)
- Prelims is purely a screening test — marks are NOT added to final merit

**Mode**: Offline (Descriptive/Written)
**Nature**: Subjective (Descriptive answers — short answer + long answer + essay)
**Total Papers**: 7

**Qualifying Papers (Papers I & II):**
- Minimum 25% marks required (75 out of 300) in each language paper
- Marks NOT counted in final merit
- Purpose: Test basic language proficiency in Gujarati and English

**Merit Papers (Papers III to VII):**
- These marks determine your ranking
- Total Merit from Mains: 1250 marks
- Added to Interview marks (150) for final ranking

### 1.17 PYQ Strategy

**Step-by-Step PYQ Strategy for GPSC CCE:**

1. **Collection**: Gather GPSC CCE PYQs from 2015 onwards (both Prelims and Mains). Use Liberty, World Inbox, or Rijadeja compilations
2. **Gujarat-First Approach**: Separate Gujarat-specific questions first. These are unique to GPSC and NOT covered by UPSC material
3. **Topic-wise Sorting**: Sort remaining PYQs by topic (Polity, History, Economy, Geography, Science, Current Affairs)
4. **Solve Topic-wise**: After completing theory, solve all PYQs for that topic immediately
5. **Pattern Identification**: Note topics that repeat with slight variations — these are your "guaranteed" marks
6. **Gujarat PYQ Pattern**: Note which Gujarat topics repeat (Solanki dynasty, rivers, wildlife, handicrafts, freedom fighters) — these are golden topics
7. **Mains Answer Practice**: For Mains PYQs, write full-length answers and get them evaluated (self/peer/coaching)
8. **Error Diary**: Maintain records of wrong answers — why you erred, correct approach, and related facts
9. **Revision Cycle**: Revisit PYQs at 7-day, 21-day, and 45-day intervals (spaced repetition)
10. **Mock Integration**: After completing PYQs, attempt full-length mocks under timed conditions

### 1.21 Common Mistakes

1. **Ignoring Gujarat-specific preparation**: This is the BIGGEST mistake. UPSC material alone won't help — you MUST study Gujarat history, culture, geography separately
2. **Not starting Gujarati/English language paper preparation**: These are qualifying but many candidates fail here — practice translation, précis, and essay writing
3. **Treating Prelims like UPSC Prelims**: GPSC Prelims has 200 questions per paper with 1/3rd negative marking — requires different strategy (higher accuracy needed)
4. **Ignoring Mains answer writing practice**: Many clear Prelims but fail Mains due to poor answer writing skills
5. **Not covering Gujarat's economy and industrial development**: Questions on GIDC, Vibrant Gujarat, GIFT City, Amul, diamond industry appear frequently
6. **Neglecting Ethics paper**: GS-IV (Ethics) carries 250 marks and is highly scoring if prepared well
7. **Poor essay writing**: The Essay paper (250 marks) requires structured thinking and practice — many neglect this
8. **Not reading Gujarati newspapers**: Gujarat-specific current affairs come from Gujarati papers, not English ones
9. **Over-focusing on Prelims without Mains preparation**: Start Mains prep alongside Prelims prep from Month 6
10. **Not practicing with GPSC-specific mocks**: Generic State PSC mocks don't cover Gujarat content — use GPSC-specific platforms
11. **Ignoring Interview preparation**: 150 marks for personality test — prepare systematically after Mains
12. **Not knowing Gujarat's districts, talukas, and administrative geography**: Basic administrative geography is tested frequently
13. **Neglecting physical fitness**: DySP and Police Inspector posts require physical tests — stay fit throughout preparation

### 1.23 GPSC vs UPSC — Key Differences

| Parameter | GPSC CCE | UPSC CSE |

| **Syllabus overlap with UPSC** | ~60–70% | — |
| **Unique preparation needed** | Gujarat History, Culture, Geography, Economy | Optional subject |

## 6. Why GPSC is Especially Relevant for Gujarat Residents

---

### Advantages of GPSC over Central Services (SSC/UPSC) for Gujarat Residents

| Factor | GPSC Advantage |
|--------|---------------|
| **Posting Location** | Guaranteed posting in Gujarat — your home state |
| **Language Comfort** | Exam in Gujarati + English — home language advantage |
| **Gujarat Knowledge** | Your existing knowledge of Gujarat's culture, geography, and society is a natural asset |
| **Family Proximity** | Never posted far from family |
| **Cultural Fit** | Work in familiar cultural environment |
| **Career Stability** | State government job with defined promotion path |
| **IAS/IPS Path** | Can eventually be inducted into IAS/IPS through promotee quota |
| **Local Impact** | Directly serve your own state and community |
| **Simultaneous Preparation** | 60–70% syllabus overlap with UPSC — can attempt both |

### Recommended Combined Strategy

| Priority | Exam | Status |
|----------|------|--------|
| 1 | **GPSC Class 1 & 2 (CCE)** | Primary target — highest state-level post |
| 2 | **UPSC CSE** | Simultaneous preparation — shared syllabus |
| 3 | **SSC CGL** | Backup — can prepare alongside |
| 4 | **GPSC STI / Nayab Mamlatdar** | Safety net — same Gujarat GK helps |
| 5 | **RBI Grade B / NABARD** | If interested in banking/finance sector |

---

> **🎯 FINAL NOTE**: GPSC is the **single most important exam for any Gujarat resident aspiring for government service**. It offers Class 1 posts (Deputy Collector, DySP) that carry immense power, prestige, and a pathway to IAS/IPS. The key differentiator from UPSC/SSC is **Gujarat-specific preparation** — invest heavily in Gujarat's history (especially Solanki dynasty), culture (Garba, Patola, Bhavai), geography (rivers, ports, wildlife), economy (diamond, dairy, petrochemicals), and administration. Start with "Gujarat ni Asmita" and never stop reading a Gujarati newspaper. With 12–18 months of focused preparation, this exam is very achievable.
