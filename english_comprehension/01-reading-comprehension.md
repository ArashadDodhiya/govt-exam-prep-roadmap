## STEP 1 — FOUNDATION

**What it is:** A passage (150–450 words) followed by 4–6 questions testing whether you understood what was written, what it implied, and what specific words meant *in that context*.

**Why it matters:** RC is usually the single highest-weightage block in the English section — often 10–20 marks out of a 25–30 mark paper. You cannot skip it and still clear cutoff comfortably.

**Where it appears:**
- SSC CGL/CHSL — 1 passage, 5 Qs (sometimes 2 passages)
- IBPS/SBI PO & Clerk — 1–2 passages, 5–10 Qs, often with vocabulary-based sub-questions
- RBI Grade B/Assistant — denser, editorial-style passages
- UPSC CSAT — philosophical/analytical passages, answer-choice reasoning heavy
- GPSC, Railways, EPFO, Insurance — similar to SSC/Banking pattern

**How examiners test it:** Not memory — they test whether you can (a) locate information fast, (b) not confuse "stated" with "implied," and (c) not get fooled by options that reuse passage words but change the meaning.

## STEP 3 — CORE CONCEPTS

### 3.1 Direct/Factual Questions
- **Definition:** The answer is explicitly stated in the passage, often in the exact words or a very close paraphrase.
- **Rule:** The correct option restates a sentence from the passage without changing its meaning.
- **Why it works:** Examiners test scanning speed and precision, not interpretation.
- **Common mistake:** Picking an option that sounds right from general knowledge but isn't actually *in the passage*.
- **Memory trick:** "If it's not written, it's not the answer" — RC only cares about the passage's world, not the real world.
- **Exam example pattern:** *"According to the passage, why did the author move to the city?"* → answer is a near-paraphrase of one sentence.

### 3.2 Inference-Based Questions
- **Definition:** The answer is not stated directly but can be logically concluded from what *is* stated.
- **Rule:** A correct inference is a small, safe step beyond the text — not a leap.
- **Why it works:** Tests reasoning, not just reading.
- **Exception:** If two options both seem "inferable," the safer/narrower one (closer to the text) is usually correct.
- **Common mistake:** Treating "inference" as "my own opinion" or "general logic" instead of "logic anchored to this specific passage."
- **Memory trick:** Inference = passage + one small logical step. Not passage + your worldview.

### 3.3 Theme / Title of Passage
- **Definition:** The idea that covers the *entire* passage, not just one paragraph.
- **Rule:** Correct title is broad enough to include every paragraph, but not so broad it could fit any random passage.
- **Common mistake:** Picking a title that matches only the first or last paragraph (a common trap — see Step 7).
- **Memory trick:** Ask "does this title survive if I delete any one paragraph?" If yes, it's too narrow.

### 3.4 Vocabulary in Context
- **Definition:** A word's meaning *as used in this specific passage*, which may differ from its most common dictionary meaning.
- **Rule:** Re-read the sentence with each option substituted in; the one that keeps the sentence's logic intact wins — not the "textbook" synonym.
- **Common mistake:** Choosing the most familiar dictionary synonym instead of checking passage context (e.g., "grave" can mean "serious," not just "burial site").
- **Memory trick:** Cover the original word, read the sentence with your chosen option — does it still make sense?

### 3.5 Tone / Author's Attitude
- **Definition:** How the author feels about the subject — neutral, critical, appreciative, sarcastic, concerned, hopeful, etc.
- **Rule:** Tone is carried by adjectives/adverbs and by what the author chooses to emphasize or leave out, not by the topic itself.
- **Common mistake:** Assuming a serious *topic* automatically means a negative *tone*. A passage about pollution can still be written in a hopeful/solution-focused tone.

## STEP 4 — GRAMMAR CLUES USED IN RC

Grammar isn't the main test here, but these clues help you read faster and infer correctly:
- **Pronoun reference:** "It," "this," "they" — always confirm what they point back to before answering.
- **Tense shifts:** A shift from past to present often signals the author moving from narration to a general claim/opinion — useful for tone and inference questions.
- **Connectors as signals:** "However," "although," "despite" flag a contrast — the sentence after usually holds the key opinion or twist the question will test.

## STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES

| Confused Pair | Difference | How Examiners Exploit It |
|---|---|---|
| Fact vs Inference | Fact = stated; Inference = logically concluded | Option restates a fact but is offered as answer to an "infer" question — sounds right but is the wrong *type* of answer |
| Theme vs Title | Theme = the underlying idea; Title = a short label for that idea | Options give a technically-true title that's too narrow (covers 1 paragraph only) |
| Summary vs Paraphrase | Summary = shortened whole; Paraphrase = same length, different words | "Best summary" options sometimes paraphrase just one line, not summarize the whole passage |
| "Author states" vs "Author suggests" | States = explicit; Suggests = implied | Students treat both as needing direct textual proof — "suggests" allows a reasoned step |
| Cannot Be Determined vs False | CBD = passage gives no info either way; False = passage contradicts it | Students mark "False" when the passage simply never mentions it — should be CBD |

## STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE

1. **Word-matching trap:** Option reuses exact words from the passage but twists their meaning or scope.
2. **Half-true trap:** Option is 90% correct but has one wrong detail (a number, a name, a direction) — always check the *whole* option, not just the gist.
3. **Extreme-language trap:** Options with "always," "never," "only," "completely" are usually wrong — passages rarely make absolute claims.
4. **Out-of-scope trap:** Option is true in the real world / general knowledge but was never discussed in *this* passage.
5. **First/last paragraph bias trap:** For title questions, options that summarize only the opening or closing paragraph feel "familiar" because you read them most recently — resist this.
6. **Opposite-meaning trap:** In vocabulary questions, one option is the antonym of the correct word — included specifically to catch careless skimmers.
7. **Overgeneralization trap:** Passage discusses one specific case; option expands it into a universal rule.

## STEP 8 — PATTERN RECOGNITION (Question-Type Radar)

| If the question says... | It's testing... | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| "According to the passage..." | Direct/Factual | Scan for the matching sentence, don't reason beyond it |
| "It can be inferred that..." / "It is implied that..." | Inference | Find the closest supporting line, take one small logical step |
| "The most suitable title is..." | Theme/Title | Check the option against *every* paragraph, not just one |
| "As used in the passage, the word ___ means..." | Vocabulary-in-context | Re-read the exact sentence, substitute each option |
| "The author's tone/attitude is..." | Tone | Scan for adjectives/adverbs, not just the topic |
| "Which of the following is true/false/cannot be determined?" | Fact-check | Match option to text word-for-word; if absent entirely → CBD |

## STEP 9 — READING STRATEGY (Core Method)

**Recommended order:** Skim the passage once (60–90 seconds) for topic + structure → Read all questions → Go back and hunt for each answer using keywords from the question.

1. **How to read fast:** First read is for structure only — what's paragraph 1 about, paragraph 2, etc. Don't try to absorb every detail on pass one.
2. **Finding keywords:** Pick 1–2 unique nouns/proper nouns from the question (names, numbers, specific terms) — scan the passage visually for those exact words first.
3. **Identifying tone:** Note adjectives describing the subject and any contrast connectors ("but," "yet," "however").
4. **Identifying theme:** After skimming, mentally summarize each paragraph in 4–5 words. The theme is the thread connecting all of them.
5. **Locating the answer:** Match question keywords to passage location — RC answers are almost always sequential (Q1 near the top, Q5 near the bottom), so use this to narrow your search.
6. **Avoiding assumptions:** If you find yourself thinking "well, generally speaking..." — stop. You've left the passage. Get back to the text.
7. **Solving inference questions:** Find the nearest literal statement, then ask "what is the smallest next logical step from this?"
8. **Solving vocabulary-in-context:** Never answer from memory of the word's usual meaning — always re-check the sentence.
9. **Solving title/theme questions:** Eliminate any option that only fits one paragraph.
10. **Eliminating options:** Cross out extreme-language and out-of-scope options first — this alone often halves your choices.

---

## STEP 11 — SPEED TECHNIQUES

- **Time budget:** ~6–8 minutes per passage (including reading) in most bank/SSC-level exams; UPSC CSAT allows more but has denser passages.
- **Elimination order:** Kill extreme-language options → kill out-of-scope options → compare the remaining 2 against the exact passage line.
- **Keyword scanning:** Train your eye to jump to capitalized words, numbers, and dates first — they're the fastest anchors back into the passage.
- **Context guessing (for unknown words):** Use the sentence around an unfamiliar word — positive/negative framing of the sentence usually reveals whether the word is positive or negative in meaning, even if you don't know it exactly.

---

## STEP 12 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES

- **"FITT" checklist before answering any RC question:** **F**act stated? **I**nference (small step)? **T**heme (whole passage)? **T**one (adjectives)? — quickly tag which type you're solving before you scan options.
- **Paragraph-summary mnemonic:** Turn each paragraph into a 3–4 word "tag" while skimming (e.g., "problem introduced," "expert opinion," "counter-view," "conclusion"). Recalling these tags is faster than re-reading.

> Urban planners have long debated whether cities should prioritize expanding road networks or investing in public transit. ## STEP 14 — ERROR DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

When you get a question wrong, don't just note "wrong answer" — identify *why*, using this checklist:

| Diagnosis | Signs | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading mistake** | You misread a name, number, or negative word (e.g., missed a "not") | Re-read the exact sentence slowly before finalizing |
| **Inference overreach** | You picked an option that "felt true" but needed outside knowledge | Ask: is this the *smallest* logical step, or did I add my own assumption? |
| **Vocabulary gap** | You didn't know the word's contextual meaning | Add the word to your Step 5 table immediately |
| **Trap fell for** | You picked an option with extreme language or word-matching | Re-run Step 7's trap checklist before finalizing next time |
| **Time pressure** | You rushed the last question of a passage | Practice the Step 11 time budget with a timer |
| **Careless elimination** | You didn't actually compare all 4 options against the text | Force yourself to check each option, not just the "best-sounding" one |

## STEP 16 — PREVIOUS-YEAR PATTERN INSIGHTS

- Banking exams (IBPS/SBI) have generally trended toward **opinion/editorial-style** passages over narrative ones, increasing the share of inference and tone questions.
- SSC tends to stay closer to **factual/general-awareness-adjacent** passages (science, history, society) with heavier vocabulary-in-context testing.
- UPSC CSAT passages are typically **abstract/argument-based**, testing careful reading of qualifiers ("some," "most," "only") more than vocabulary.
- Across most exams, **inference and vocabulary-in-context** questions have grown relative to pure fact-lookup — pure memory-scanning is becoming less sufficient on its own.

## STEP 17 — ONE-PAGE REVISION SHEET

**Before every RC passage, run this mental checklist:**
1. Skim for structure (60–90 sec) — don't absorb detail yet.
2. Tag each paragraph with a 3–4 word summary.
3. Read all questions — note keywords.
4. Tag each question: Fact / Inference / Theme / Vocab / Tone (FITT).
5. Hunt sequentially — Q1 answer is usually near the top, Q5 near the bottom.
6. Eliminate extreme-language and out-of-scope options first.
7. For vocabulary questions — always re-substitute into the sentence, never answer from memory.
8. For title questions — check against every paragraph, not just one.
9. Double-check the one option that "sounds right" against the *exact* text before locking it in.

**Trap reminders:** word-matching • half-true • extreme language • out-of-scope • first/last paragraph bias • opposite-meaning • overgeneralization.
