# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Mixture?

A **mixture** is formed when two or more ingredients are mixed together. The mixed result has properties that lie BETWEEN the properties of the individual ingredients.

> Mix ₹40/kg tea with ₹60/kg tea → the mixture price will be between ₹40 and ₹60.
> Mix 20% acid with 50% acid → the mixture concentration will be between 20% and 50%.

## What is Alligation?

**Alligation** is a shortcut rule to find the **ratio in which two ingredients must be mixed** to get a desired value (price, concentration, average, etc.).

> It answers: "How much of each should I mix?"

Think of it as a **see-saw**: the mixture value is the balancing point, and the quantities adjust so heavier (farther from centre) needs less weight.

## Where is it used?

| Application | Example |
|---|---|
| **Mixing tea/rice at different prices** | Find ratio to get target price |
| **Chemical solutions** | Mix two acid concentrations to get desired % |
| **Average problems** | Two groups with different averages → combined average |
| **Replacement problems** | Repeatedly removing and replacing liquid |
| **Profit & Loss** | Mixing goods to achieve target profit |
| **Speed problems** | Average speed as a "mixture" of two speeds |
| **Age problems** | Combined average age = weighted average |

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Cross-topic application** | Works in Average, Ratio, Percentage, P&L, Speed |
| **Fast shortcut** | Alligation solves in 10 seconds what algebra solves in 2 minutes |
| **2–3 direct questions per paper** | SSC, Banking both test this |
| **Replacement formula is unique** | Only way to solve repeated dilution quickly |
| **Tests conceptual understanding** | Can you THINK in ratios and mixtures? |

> **Alligation is the FASTEST shortcut in Quantitative Aptitude. Once you learn the cross-diagram, you'll use it in 10+ different topics.**

## CONCEPT 1: Rule of Alligation — The Master Shortcut

### The Idea

When two ingredients at values (price, concentration, average, etc.) **d** (dearer/higher) and **c** (cheaper/lower) are mixed to get a mixture at value **m** (mean):

> **Quantity of Cheaper : Quantity of Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)**

### The Alligation Cross-Diagram

```
    Cheaper (c)                    Dearer (d)
         \                           /
          \                         /
           \                       /
            ────── Mean (m) ──────
           /                       \
          /                         \
         /                           \
    (d − m)                       (m − c)
    
    Ratio of Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)
```

### Why It Works

If you mix Q₁ of cheaper (at c) and Q₂ of dearer (at d) to get mean m:

Q₁ × c + Q₂ × d = (Q₁ + Q₂) × m

Rearranging: Q₁(m − c) = Q₂(d − m)

Therefore: **Q₁/Q₂ = (d − m)/(m − c)**

### Example 1: Price Mixing

Mix ₹30/kg rice with ₹50/kg rice to get ₹38/kg mixture. Find the ratio.

```
    ₹30                    ₹50
        \                 /
         \               /
          ── ₹38 ──
         /               \
        /                 \
   (50−38)=12         (38−30)=8
```

Ratio = Cheaper : Dearer = 12 : 8 = **3 : 2** ✅

**Meaning**: For every 3 kg of ₹30 rice, mix 2 kg of ₹50 rice.

### Example 2: Solution Mixing

Mix 20% acid with 60% acid to get 35% acid. Find the ratio.

```
    20%                    60%
        \                 /
         ── 35% ──
   (60−35)=25         (35−20)=15
```

Ratio = 25 : 15 = **5 : 3** ✅

### Example 3: Average (Alligation applied to averages)

Class A: 40 students, average 55. Class B: ? students, average 70. Combined average: 60.

Using alligation:
```
    55                    70
        \                /
         ── 60 ──
   (70−60)=10        (60−55)=5
```

n₁ : n₂ = 10 : 5 = **2 : 1**

So n₂ = 40/2 = **20 students** ✅

## CONCEPT 2: Mean Price of a Mixture

### When quantities are GIVEN, find the mean price

> **Mean Price = (Q₁×P₁ + Q₂×P₂) / (Q₁ + Q₂)**

This is just the weighted average formula.

### Example

5 kg of ₹40/kg sugar mixed with 3 kg of ₹60/kg sugar. Mean price?

= (5×40 + 3×60) / (5+3) = (200+180)/8 = 380/8 = **₹47.50/kg**

### For Three Ingredients

Mean Price = (Q₁P₁ + Q₂P₂ + Q₃P₃) / (Q₁ + Q₂ + Q₃)

## CONCEPT 3: Concentration-Based Mixture Problems

### Key Variables

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Concentration** | Percentage of the "active" ingredient (acid, milk, alcohol, etc.) |
| **Pure ingredient** | 100% concentration |
| **Water** | 0% concentration (dilutant) |
| **Solution** | Active ingredient + solvent |

### Pattern 1: Mixing two solutions

Mix 30 litres of 40% acid with 20 litres of 60% acid. Find concentration of mixture.

- Acid from sol.1 = 30 × 0.4 = 12 litres
- Acid from sol.2 = 20 × 0.6 = 12 litres
- Total acid = 24 litres
- Total mixture = 50 litres
- Concentration = 24/50 × 100 = **48%**

### Pattern 2: Adding water (diluting)

A 40-litre mixture has milk and water in ratio 3:1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3:2?

- Milk = 40 × 3/4 = 30 litres
- Water = 40 × 1/4 = 10 litres
- New ratio required: Milk : Water = 3:2
- Milk stays 30 litres = 3 parts → 1 part = 10
- Water needed = 2 parts = 20 litres
- Water to add = 20 − 10 = **10 litres**

### Pattern 3: Adding pure ingredient

20 litres of 30% acid. How much pure acid to add to make it 50%?

Let x litres of pure acid be added.
- Total acid = 20 × 0.3 + x = 6 + x
- Total solution = 20 + x
- (6 + x)/(20 + x) = 0.5
- 6 + x = 10 + 0.5x
- 0.5x = 4 → x = **8 litres**

**Using alligation**:
```
    30% (solution)         100% (pure acid)
           \                  /
            ── 50% ──
       (100−50)=50       (50−30)=20
```
Ratio = 50:20 = 5:2
Solution : Pure acid = 5:2
20 litres corresponds to 5 parts → 1 part = 4 → pure acid = 2 × 4 = **8 litres** ✅

## CONCEPT 4: Replacement of Mixture (Single Replacement)

### Pattern

From a container of A litres of pure liquid, B litres are removed and replaced with water (or another liquid).

### After ONE replacement

> **Remaining original liquid = A × (1 − B/A) = A − B**

This is trivial for one replacement. The real power comes with REPEATED replacement.

---

## CONCEPT 5: Repeated Replacement / Dilution (HIGH FREQUENCY)

### The Formula

From a container of **A** litres of pure liquid, **B** litres are removed and replaced with water. This is done **n** times.

> **Remaining original liquid after n operations = A × (1 − B/A)ⁿ**
>
> **Concentration after n operations = (1 − B/A)ⁿ × 100%**

### Example 1

A container has 80 litres of milk. 8 litres are removed and replaced with water. This is done 3 times. How much milk remains?

= 80 × (1 − 8/80)³ = 80 × (1 − 1/10)³ = 80 × (9/10)³ = 80 × 729/1000 = **58.32 litres**

### Example 2

A container has 50 litres of alcohol. 10 litres are removed and replaced with water each time. After how many operations will the alcohol concentration become 32%?

- After n operations: 50 × (1 − 10/50)ⁿ = 50 × (4/5)ⁿ
- Concentration = (4/5)ⁿ
- 0.32 = (4/5)ⁿ = (0.8)ⁿ
- (4/5)¹ = 0.8, (4/5)² = 0.64, (4/5)³ = 0.512, wait let me recalculate
- 32% of 50 litres = 16 litres
- 50 × (4/5)ⁿ = 16
- (4/5)ⁿ = 16/50 = 8/25
- (4/5)¹ = 4/5, (4/5)² = 16/25, (4/5)³ = 64/125
- 16/25 ≠ 8/25. Let me check: 8/25 = 0.32
- (4/5)² = 16/25 = 0.64, (4/5)³ = 64/125 = 0.512

Hmm, 8/25 doesn't match any clean power of 4/5. This means the question needs adjustment. The formula works perfectly when the answer IS a clean power.

**Better Example**: Container of 125 litres milk. 25 litres replaced each time. After 3 operations, milk remaining?

= 125 × (1 − 25/125)³ = 125 × (4/5)³ = 125 × 64/125 = **64 litres** ✅

### When the Removed Quantity Varies

If different quantities are removed each time (B₁, B₂, B₃…):

> **Remaining = A × (1 − B₁/A) × (1 − B₂/A₂) × …**

Where A₂ is the total volume after first replacement (usually = A if replaced with same volume).

### Key Insight

> After each replacement, the **total volume stays the same** (because you remove and add the same amount). Only the **composition** changes.

## CONCEPT 6: Mixing More Than Two Ingredients

### When Three Items are Mixed

**Method**: Use alligation on TWO items at a time, or set up equations.

**Example**: Mix rice at ₹20/kg, ₹30/kg, and ₹50/kg in ratio 2:3:1. Find mean price.

= (2×20 + 3×30 + 1×50) / (2+3+1) = (40+90+50)/6 = 180/6 = **₹30/kg**

### Finding Ratio for Three Ingredients

This is complex. The approach:
1. Fix ratio of any two ingredients using alligation with mean price
2. Use the third ingredient to adjust

OR: Set up equations and solve.

## CONCEPT 7: Container / Vessel Mixing

### Pattern

"Three vessels contain milk-water mixtures in ratios 3:1, 5:3, and 9:7. They are mixed in ratio 2:3:1. Find the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture."

### Method

Calculate the milk fraction in each vessel:
- Vessel 1: Milk = 3/(3+1) = 3/4
- Vessel 2: Milk = 5/(5+3) = 5/8
- Vessel 3: Milk = 9/(9+7) = 9/16

Weighted milk fraction:
= (2 × 3/4 + 3 × 5/8 + 1 × 9/16) / (2+3+1)

= (3/2 + 15/8 + 9/16) / 6

LCM of 2, 8, 16 = 16:
= (24/16 + 30/16 + 9/16) / 6 = (63/16) / 6 = 63/96 = **21/32**

Milk : Water = 21 : (32−21) = **21 : 11**

---

## CONCEPT 8: Ratio Change After Addition/Removal

### Pattern 1: Adding one ingredient

"Milk and water are in ratio 5:3. If 4 litres of water is added, ratio becomes 5:4. Find total mixture."

- Milk = 5k, Water = 3k
- After adding 4 litres water: 5k/(3k+4) = 5/4
- 20k = 15k + 20
- 5k = 20 → k = 4
- Total original = 8k = **32 litres**

### Pattern 2: Removing mixture and adding ingredient

"40 litres of mixture, milk:water = 7:1. How much mixture to remove and replace with water to get 3:1?"

- Milk = 35 litres, Water = 5 litres
- Remove x litres of mixture: remove 7x/8 milk and x/8 water
- Add x litres water
- New milk = 35 − 7x/8
- New water = 5 − x/8 + x = 5 + 7x/8
- Ratio: (35 − 7x/8)/(5 + 7x/8) = 3/1
- 35 − 7x/8 = 15 + 21x/8
- 20 = 28x/8 = 7x/2
- x = 40/7 ≈ **5.71 litres**

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Alligation Cross-Diagram (10-Second Solutions)

Draw the cross:
```
    c               d
      \           /
       \         /
        ── m ──
       /         \
      /           \
   (d−m)       (m−c)
```

Ratio of Cheaper : Dearer = (d−m) : (m−c)

This works for: Prices, Concentrations, Averages, Speeds, Interest rates — ANYTHING.

---

## Shortcut 2: Alligation for Average Speed

Equal distances at speed a and b:
```
    a               b
      \           /
       ── Avg ──
   (b−Avg)    (Avg−a)
```

But time spent at speed a : time at speed b = D/a : D/b = b : a (inverse of speeds)

So: b : a = (b − Avg) : (Avg − a)

This gives: Avg = 2ab/(a+b) ✅

---

## Shortcut 3: Replacement — Fraction Remaining

After n operations of removing B from A and replacing with water:

> **Fraction of original remaining = (1 − B/A)ⁿ**

Think of it as: each operation, a fraction (1 − B/A) SURVIVES.

After 1 op: (1 − B/A)
After 2 ops: (1 − B/A)²
After n ops: (1 − B/A)ⁿ

---

## Shortcut 4: When Water is Added (Dilution)

If you add water to a solution:
- Solute amount stays SAME
- Total volume increases
- Concentration decreases

> **Original concentration × Original volume = New concentration × New volume**

---

## Shortcut 5: When Ratio Changes from a:b to c:d

If milk:water = a:b originally and you want c:d by adding water:

> **Water to add = Total × [(a×d − b×c) / (c × (a+b))]**
>
> (Only works when milk stays constant, i.e., only water is added)

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Alligation Ratio — Which is Which?

| Common confusion | Clarity |
|---|---|
| "Ratio of cheaper to dearer" | (d−m) : (m−c) → DEARER's difference with mean goes with CHEAPER |
| Students reverse the ratio | **Cross-wise**: cheaper gets the dearer's gap, dearer gets the cheaper's gap |

> **Memory**: "The cheaper one gets the bigger number if the mean is closer to the dearer."

---

## Confusion 2: Replacement — Volume vs Amount

| | Volume | Amount of original |
|---|---|---|
| After replacement | STAYS SAME (add back what you removed) | DECREASES each time |
| After n replacements | Still A litres total | A × (1−B/A)ⁿ litres of original |

---

## Confusion 3: Adding Water vs Removing Mixture

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| Adding water to mixture | Total volume increases. Original stays same. |
| Removing mixture (and replacing with water) | Total volume stays same. Original decreases. |
| Removing mixture (without replacing) | Total volume decreases. Original decreases. |

---

## Confusion 4: Mean Value Position

> **The mean/mixture value MUST lie between the two ingredient values.**

If c = 30 and d = 50, then m MUST be between 30 and 50.
If someone tells you m = 60 → **impossible** (no real mixing ratio exists).

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


---

| # | Trap | How it works | How to detect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Alligation ratio reversed** | Students assign (d−m) to dearer instead of cheaper | Remember: cross-wise assignment | Always verify: mean should be between c and d |
| 2 | **Replacement: using amount instead of fraction** | Using B instead of B/A in the formula | Formula needs the FRACTION removed | 10 from 50 → use 10/50 = 1/5 |
| 3 | **Adding water changes total volume** | Students forget total volume increases when water is added | If water added → new total = old + water added | |
| 4 | **"Mixture removed" means BOTH ingredients removed** | Students think only one ingredient is removed | When mixture is removed, it's proportional to current composition | |
| 5 | **Replacement done on CURRENT mixture, not original** | After 1st replacement, composition changed — 2nd replacement acts on NEW mixture | Use the formula (1−B/A)ⁿ which handles this automatically | |
| 6 | **Confusing concentration with amount** | "50% acid" doesn't mean 50 litres of acid | 50% of whatever the TOTAL volume is | |
| 7 | **Ratio vs Fraction** | Milk:Water = 3:2 → milk is 3/5 (not 3/2) of total | Denominator = sum of ratio parts | |

# STEP 10 — SPEED OPTIMIZATION


---

## Speed Rule 1: Master the Cross-Diagram

With practice, you can draw and solve the alligation cross in 10 seconds flat. This replaces 2+ minutes of equation setup.

## Speed Rule 2: Replacement — Use Fractions, Not Decimals

80 litres, 8 removed → fraction remaining = (1 − 1/10) = 9/10
After 3 ops: (9/10)³ = 729/1000 → amount = 80 × 729/1000

Fraction arithmetic is faster and less error-prone than decimal.

## Speed Rule 3: When Only Water is Added

> **Solute stays constant. Just track the solute.**

"20 litres of 30% acid. Add water to make 20%."
- Acid = 6 litres (stays constant)
- 6 litres = 20% of new total → new total = 30 litres
- Water to add = 30 − 20 = **10 litres**

No formula needed — just logic!

## Speed Rule 4: Alligation Works for ANY Weighted Average

Don't just use it for mixtures. Use it for:
- Average marks of two classes
- Average speed for two distances
- Average price of mixed goods
- Average interest rate on two investments

# STEP 11 — MEMORY TECHNIQUES


---

## Mnemonic 1: Alligation Cross

> **"Cheaper gets Dearer's gap, Dearer gets Cheaper's gap"**
>
> Think: **"Cross your arms"** — the lines cross!

---

## Mnemonic 2: Replacement Formula

> **"What SURVIVES each round"**
>
> Survival fraction = (1 − B/A)
> After n rounds = (1 − B/A)ⁿ
>
> Think of it as "population survival" — each round, a fraction dies (gets removed), the rest survive.

---

## Visualization: Alligation as a See-Saw

```
   ₹30 ────────────── ₹38 ────────── ₹50
   (cheaper)          (balance)       (dearer)
   
   Distance from mean: 8              12
   
   Quantities:        12         :     8    (INVERSE of distances)
                    = 3          :     2
```

The one FARTHER from the mean gets LESS quantity. Like a see-saw — the heavier side (farther from fulcrum) needs less weight to balance.

### Level 4: SSC CGL Level

**Q4.** Milk and water in a vessel are in ratio 3:1. 8 litres of mixture are removed and replaced with water. The ratio becomes 1:1. Find the original quantity.

(A) 24 (B) 32 (C) 16 (D) 20

<details>
<summary>🔑 Click to reveal answer</summary>

**Answer: (A) 24**

Let total = 4k. Milk = 3k, Water = k.
Remove 8 litres: removes 6 litres milk and 2 litres water (in 3:1 ratio).
Add 8 litres water.
New milk = 3k − 6. New water = k − 2 + 8 = k + 6.
Ratio 1:1 → 3k − 6 = k + 6 → 2k = 12 → k = 6.
Original = 4 × 6 = **24 litres** ✅
</details>

## 📝 One-Page Notes

```
MIXTURE & ALLIGATION — ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

ALLIGATION: Q_cheap : Q_dear = (d−m) : (m−c)
  Cross-diagram → 10-second solutions
  Works for: Prices, Concentrations, Averages, Speeds

MEAN PRICE: (Q₁P₁ + Q₂P₂) / (Q₁ + Q₂)

REPLACEMENT: After n ops of removing B from A total:
  Remaining original = A × (1 − B/A)ⁿ
  Volume stays same. Composition changes.

DILUTION (add water):
  Solute stays constant. Track solute only.
  New total = Solute / New concentration

RATIO CHANGE: Use k-method. Set up equation from new ratio.

KEY RULES:
  • Mean is ALWAYS between the two ingredient values
  • Water = 0% concentration, Pure = 100%
  • Removing MIXTURE removes both ingredients proportionally
```

## 🚨 Trap Sheet

| Trap | Defense |
|---|---|
| Alligation ratio reversed | Cross-wise: cheaper gets dearer's gap |
| Replacement uses amount not fraction | Always convert to fraction: B/A |
| Water added changes total volume | Track total volume carefully |
| "Mixture removed" removes BOTH ingredients | Proportional to current composition |
