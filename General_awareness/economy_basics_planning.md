# Indian Economy — Basic Concepts & Planning

## 1. Overview
This topic covers the foundational economic indicators used to measure a country's economic performance (GDP, GNP, NNP, Per Capita Income) and India's planning history (Five Year Plans, NITI Aayog). Exams test this via definitions, formula relationships, and historical plan-specific facts.

## 2. Core Facts (High-Yield)

**Basic Concepts — National Income Accounting**
- GDP (Gross Domestic Product) — total value of all final goods & services produced WITHIN a country's borders in a year, regardless of who produces it (citizens or foreigners) **[High]**
- GNP (Gross National Product) — GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA); measures output produced BY a country's citizens/residents, whether inside or outside the country **[High]**
- NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad) = income earned by residents abroad − income earned by foreigners within the country **[Medium]**
- NNP (Net National Product) = GNP − Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital) **[High]**
- NNP at Factor Cost = National Income (a key aggregate measure) **[Medium]**
- Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Total Population — average income per person, used to measure standard of living **[High]**
- GDP at Market Price vs GDP at Factor Cost — Market Price includes indirect taxes and excludes subsidies; Factor Cost excludes indirect taxes and includes subsidies **[Medium]**
- Real GDP vs Nominal GDP — Real GDP is adjusted for inflation (uses constant/base year prices); Nominal GDP uses current year prices (not inflation-adjusted) **[High]**
- GDP Deflator — ratio of Nominal GDP to Real GDP, used to measure inflation across the whole economy **[Medium]**
- Base Year for India's GDP calculation — currently 2011-12 (as per Central Statistics Office/MoSPI revisions) **[Medium]**
- Fiscal Year in India — 1 April to 31 March **[High]**
- Three sectors of economy: Primary (agriculture/extraction), Secondary (manufacturing/industry), Tertiary (services) — Tertiary sector currently contributes the LARGEST share to India's GDP **[High]**
- CSO (Central Statistics Office) / NSO (National Statistical Office) — responsible for compiling GDP and other national income data in India **[Medium]**

**Planning — Five Year Plans (1951–2017)**
- India adopted Soviet-style Five Year Plans starting 1951, discontinued after the 12th Plan (2017), replaced by NITI Aayog's policy approach **[High]**
- First Five Year Plan (1951-56) — based on Harrod-Domar model; focused on AGRICULTURE (post-partition food crisis) **[High]**
- Second Five Year Plan (1956-61) — based on Mahalanobis Model (P.C. Mahalanobis); focused on rapid INDUSTRIALIZATION, heavy industries **[High]**
- Third Five Year Plan (1961-66) — aimed for self-reliant economy; disrupted by Indo-China War (1962) and Indo-Pak War (1965), and severe drought **[Medium]**
- Plan Holiday (1966-69) — three Annual Plans instead of a Five Year Plan, due to war/drought/devaluation of rupee **[Medium]**
- Fourth Plan (1969-74) — "Growth with Stability"; bank nationalization (1969) occurred during this period **[Medium]**
- Fifth Plan (1974-79) — "Removal of Poverty" (Garibi Hatao) slogan; Emergency period overlapped **[Medium]**
- Rolling Plan (1978-80) — introduced by Janata Party government, replaced Fifth Plan's continuation **[Low]**
- Sixth Plan (1980-85) — focus on poverty alleviation, technology modernization **[Low]**
- Seventh Plan (1985-90) — focus on food, employment, productivity **[Low]**
- Eighth Plan (1992-97) — coincided with LPG reforms (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization, 1991) **[High]**
- Ninth Plan (1997-2002) — "Growth with Social Justice" **[Low]**
- Tenth Plan (2002-07) — aimed to double per capita income in 10 years **[Low]**
- Eleventh Plan (2007-12) — "Faster and More Inclusive Growth" **[Low]**
- Twelfth Plan (2012-17) — LAST Five Year Plan; "Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth" **[Medium]**
- Planning Commission — set up 1950 (non-constitutional, non-statutory body); Prime Minister was ex-officio Chairman **[High]**

**NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)**
- Replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015 **[High]**
- Non-constitutional, non-statutory body (like Planning Commission) — a policy think tank / advisory body, NOT an executing/fund-allocating body **[High]**
- Key difference from Planning Commission: NITI Aayog has NO power to allocate funds to ministries/states (that role shifted to the Finance Ministry); acts as "think tank," advisory in nature, promotes cooperative/competitive federalism (bottom-up approach involving states) **[High]**
- Prime Minister is ex-officio Chairperson of NITI Aayog (same as Planning Commission structure) **[Medium]**
- Has a CEO (fixed tenure, appointed by PM) and a Vice-Chairperson **[Medium]**
- Governing Council includes all State Chief Ministers and UT Lt. Governors — designed for greater state participation compared to the top-down Planning Commission approach **[Medium]**
- Key initiatives associated with NITI Aayog: SDG India Index, Aspirational Districts Programme, Atal Innovation Mission **[Medium]**

## 4. Commonly Confused Facts

| Confusion | Distinguishing Rule |
|---|---|
| GDP vs GNP | GDP = production WITHIN borders (territory-based); GNP = production BY citizens/residents (nationality-based), includes income from abroad |
| GNP vs NNP | GNP = gross figure (before depreciation); NNP = GNP MINUS depreciation (net figure, more accurate measure of actual new output) |
| Real GDP vs Nominal GDP | Real GDP = inflation-ADJUSTED (constant/base year prices); Nominal GDP = CURRENT year prices, NOT inflation-adjusted — Real GDP is the better indicator of actual growth |
| Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog | Planning Commission = ALLOCATED funds to states/ministries (top-down); NITI Aayog = ADVISORY only, NO fund allocation power (bottom-up, cooperative federalism) — this is the single most tested distinction |
| First Plan vs Second Plan focus | First Plan = AGRICULTURE (post-partition crisis, Harrod-Domar model); Second Plan = INDUSTRY (heavy industries, Mahalanobis model) — opposite focus areas |
| GDP at Market Price vs Factor Cost | Market Price = INCLUDES indirect taxes, EXCLUDES subsidies; Factor Cost = EXCLUDES indirect taxes, INCLUDES subsidies |

## 5. Mnemonics / Memory Tricks

- **GDP→GNP→NNP building blocks** — "**G**DP **+** **A**broad **i**ncome **=** **G**NP; **G**NP **−** **D**epreciation **=** **N**NP" — think of it as a staircase: GDP is the base, add foreign income to climb to GNP, then subtract wear-and-tear to reach NNP.
- **First vs Second Plan focus** — "First Plan Feeds (agriculture, food crisis), Second Plan Steels (industry, heavy industries)" — simple F-for-Food, S-for-Steel rhyme.
- **Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog** — "Planning Commission = Purse-holder (allocated money); NITI Aayog = Just an Advisor (no purse, only advice)" — the word "purse" (money) is the key differentiator to remember.
- **Fiscal Year** — "April Fool to March Madness" (1 April to 31 March) — a light phrase linking calendar months to the fiscal year boundaries.
- **8th Plan and LPG reforms** — "**8**th Plan = **'92** start = right after **'91** reforms" — the number 8 and year 1992 anchor this plan to the liberalization era immediately following it.

## 6. Exam Trap Patterns

- **GDP vs GNP** — a classic trap testing whether students know GDP is TERRITORY-based while GNP is NATIONALITY-based (includes income earned abroad by residents).
- **Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog powers** — the most repeated trap in this section: assuming NITI Aayog can allocate funds like the Planning Commission did — it CANNOT.
- Confusing which Five Year Plan is associated with which economic model (Harrod-Domar = 1st, Mahalanobis = 2nd) — a common swap.
- **Real vs Nominal GDP** — questions test whether students understand which one accounts for inflation (Real GDP does; Nominal does not).
- Assuming the Planning Commission still exists — it was formally replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015, a fact often tested as a straightforward "when was X replaced" question.

## 7. Quick Revision Summary
- GDP (within borders) → GNP (+ income from abroad) → NNP (− depreciation) → Per Capita Income (National Income ÷ Population).
- Real GDP = inflation-adjusted; Nominal GDP = current prices; Fiscal Year = 1 April-31 March.
- Five Year Plans (1951-2017): 1st=Agriculture (Harrod-Domar), 2nd=Industry (Mahalanobis), 8th=coincides with 1991 LPG reforms, 12th=last plan.
- Planning Commission (1950) → replaced by NITI Aayog (1 Jan 2015); key difference = NITI Aayog has NO fund allocation power, is purely advisory, promotes cooperative federalism with states.

## 8. Self-Test (5 MCQs)

**Q1.** GNP differs from GDP by including:
A) Government spending only
B) Net Factor Income from Abroad
C) Only exports
D) Depreciation

**Q2.** Which economic model formed the basis of India's Second Five Year Plan (1956-61)?
A) Harrod-Domar Model B) Mahalanobis Model C) Solow Model D) Keynesian Model

**Q3.** What is the key functional difference between NITI Aayog and the erstwhile Planning Commission?
A) NITI Aayog has more members
B) NITI Aayog cannot allocate funds to states/ministries, unlike the Planning Commission
C) NITI Aayog is a constitutional body while Planning Commission was not
D) There is no functional difference

**Q4.** Real GDP differs from Nominal GDP because Real GDP is:
A) Calculated only for exports
B) Adjusted for inflation using constant prices
C) Always higher than Nominal GDP
D) Calculated by state governments only

**Q5.** India's Fiscal Year runs from:
A) January to December
B) April to March
C) July to June
D) October to September

## Answer Key
1. **B) Net Factor Income from Abroad** — GNP = GDP + NFIA, accounting for income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by foreigners domestically.
2. **B) Mahalanobis Model** — Developed by P.C. Mahalanobis, it guided the Second Plan's focus on heavy industrialization.
3. **B) NITI Aayog cannot allocate funds to states/ministries, unlike the Planning Commission** — This is the core structural difference; NITI Aayog is purely advisory.
4. **B) Adjusted for inflation using constant prices** — Real GDP uses base-year prices to strip out the effect of inflation, unlike Nominal GDP.
5. **B) April to March** — India's fiscal year runs from 1 April to 31 March of the following year.
