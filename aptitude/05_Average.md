# STEP 1 — FOUNDATION (Zero-Level Introduction)


## What is Average?

**Average** is the **single value that can replace every value in a group** and still give the same total.

Imagine 5 friends have ₹10, ₹20, ₹30, ₹40, ₹50.
Total = ₹150. If they pool all money and divide equally, each gets ₹150 ÷ 5 = **₹30**.

That ₹30 is the **average**. It's the "fair share" — the **centre of balance** of all values.

> **Average = The "equal distribution" value.**

## The Logical Idea

Think of a see-saw (balance beam). Place all your values on it. The **average** is the point where the see-saw perfectly balances.

- Values above the average "pull up"
- Values below the average "pull down"
- The total pull-up = total pull-down (they balance)

This balance idea is the KEY to solving 90% of average problems without any formula.

## Where is it used?

| Application | How |
|---|---|
| **Data Interpretation** | "Average sales over 5 years" — DI's bread and butter |
| **Speed-Time-Distance** | Average speed (NOT the simple average of speeds!) |
| **Cricket/Sports** | Batting average, bowling average |
| **Ages** | "Average age of family members" |
| **Marks/Scores** | "Average marks in 5 subjects" |
| **Temperature/Rainfall** | "Average temperature in June" |
| **Salary/Income** | "Average salary of employees" |
| **Weighted decisions** | CGPA, weighted scores in recruitment |

## Why do exams LOVE this topic?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Conceptually deep** | Simple formula, but tricky applications |
| **Connected to many topics** | Ages, DI, Speed, Mixtures — all use average |
| **Tests logical thinking** | Can you figure out the missing number from averages? |
| **Quick to ask, slow to solve (if unprepared)** | Exam-maker's favourite combination |
| **2–4 direct questions per paper** | Plus 5–10 DI questions that USE averages |
| **Weighted average = Alligation** | This one concept opens an entire topic |

> **Average is deceptively simple. Everyone knows the formula. Very few can APPLY it under pressure.**

## CONCEPT 1: Simple Average — The Foundation

### The Formula

> **Average = Sum of all values / Number of values**
>
> **Sum = Average × Number of values**
>
> **Number of values = Sum / Average**

These three are the SAME formula rearranged. Master all three forms.

### Example

Average of 12, 15, 18, 21, 24 = ?
- Sum = 12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24 = 90
- Average = 90/5 = **18**

### The Deviation Method (EXAM SPEED HACK)

Instead of adding all numbers and dividing, **assume an average** and work with deviations:

**Step 1**: Pick a number close to the middle (assumed average). Let's pick 18.
**Step 2**: Find deviations from 18:
- 12 → −6
- 15 → −3
- 18 → 0
- 21 → +3
- 24 → +6

**Step 3**: Sum of deviations = −6 −3 + 0 + 3 + 6 = **0**
**Step 4**: Average = Assumed average + (Sum of deviations / n) = 18 + 0/5 = **18** ✅

> **If the sum of deviations from a number is 0, that number IS the average.**

### When is the Deviation Method Useful?

When numbers are large and close together:

Average of 478, 482, 491, 485, 494:
- Assumed average = 485
- Deviations: −7, −3, +6, 0, +9
- Sum = +5
- Average = 485 + 5/5 = 485 + 1 = **486**

Much faster than adding 478+482+491+485+494 = 2430 and dividing!

## CONCEPT 2: Average of Consecutive Numbers (MUST MEMORIZE)

### Natural Numbers

| Series | Average | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| First n natural numbers (1, 2, …, n) | **(n+1)/2** | Middle value |
| First n even numbers (2, 4, …, 2n) | **(n+1)** | |
| First n odd numbers (1, 3, …, 2n−1) | **n** | |
| Consecutive numbers from a to b | **(a+b)/2** | Average of first and last |

### Sum Formulas (connected to Average)

| Series | Sum |
|---|---|
| 1 + 2 + 3 + … + n | n(n+1)/2 |
| 1² + 2² + 3² + … + n² | n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 |
| 1³ + 2³ + 3³ + … + n³ | [n(n+1)/2]² |
| First n even numbers sum | n(n+1) |
| First n odd numbers sum | n² |

### Key Insight for Consecutive Numbers

> **Average of ANY arithmetic progression = Average of FIRST and LAST terms = (a+l)/2**
>
> For odd count of terms: Average = the MIDDLE term.
> For even count: Average = average of two middle terms.

### Example

Average of all integers from 1 to 99:
= (1 + 99)/2 = **50**

Average of 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23:
= (3 + 23)/2 = **13** (middle of first and last, since it's an AP)

Average of first 50 natural numbers:
= (50+1)/2 = **25.5**

### Multiples

Average of first n multiples of k = **k(n+1)/2**

Average of all multiples of 7 from 7 to 77:
- These are: 7, 14, 21, …, 77 (11 terms)
- Average = (7+77)/2 = **42**

OR: Average = 7 × (1+11)/2 = 7 × 6 = **42** ✅

## CONCEPT 3: Finding a Missing Value

### Pattern

"The average of 5 numbers is 40. Four of them are 35, 42, 38, 45. Find the fifth."

### Method

- Total sum = Average × n = 40 × 5 = 200
- Sum of known values = 35 + 42 + 38 + 45 = 160
- Missing value = 200 − 160 = **40**

### General Formula

> **Missing value = (Average × n) − Sum of known values**

### Exam Variation

"Average of 10 numbers is 15. If one number is excluded, average becomes 16. Find excluded number."

- Original sum = 15 × 10 = 150
- New sum (9 numbers) = 16 × 9 = 144
- Excluded number = 150 − 144 = **6**

## CONCEPT 4: New Average When a Value is Added

### When a new person/value JOINS the group

"Average age of 8 people is 25. A new person joins and average becomes 26. What is the age of the new person?"

**Method**:
- Original sum = 25 × 8 = 200
- New sum = 26 × 9 = 234
- New person's age = 234 − 200 = **34**

### Shortcut Formula

> **New person's value = New average + n × (New average − Old average)**
>
> Where n = original number of values

= 26 + 8 × (26 − 25) = 26 + 8 = **34** ✅

### Understanding the Shortcut

The new person must "bring enough" to:
1. Match the new average for themselves (= new average)
2. Compensate for the increase in everyone else's average (= n × increase)

---

## CONCEPT 5: New Average When a Value is Removed

### When a person/value LEAVES the group

"Average of 12 numbers is 35. If one number (42) is removed, find new average."

**Method**:
- Original sum = 35 × 12 = 420
- New sum = 420 − 42 = 378
- New average = 378 / 11 = **34.36** (≈ 34.4)

### Shortcut

> **Change in average = (Removed value − Old average) / (n − 1)**

= (42 − 35) / 11 = 7/11 ≈ 0.636

New average = 35 − 0.636 = **34.36** (average decreases since removed value was above average)

### Rule

- If removed value > old average → new average DECREASES
- If removed value < old average → new average INCREASES
- If removed value = old average → new average STAYS SAME

## CONCEPT 6: Value Replacement (VERY HIGH FREQUENCY)

### Pattern

"Average of 10 numbers is 50. If one number 45 is replaced by 63, what is the new average?"

### Method

- Change in sum = 63 − 45 = +18
- Change in average = 18/10 = +1.8
- New average = 50 + 1.8 = **51.8**

### Shortcut Formula

> **New average = Old average + (New value − Old value) / n**

### When the replaced value is UNKNOWN

"Average of 8 numbers is 20. If a number is replaced by 36, average becomes 22. Find the replaced number."

- Change in average = 22 − 20 = +2
- Total change in sum = 2 × 8 = 16
- 36 − old number = 16
- Old number = 36 − 16 = **20**

### General Formula for Unknown Replacement

> **Replaced number = New number − n × (Change in average)**

## CONCEPT 7: Weighted Average

### When Simple Average DOESN'T Work

**Simple average** gives equal weight to all values. But what if different groups have different sizes?

**Example**: Class A has 30 students with average marks 60. Class B has 20 students with average marks 80.

❌ **Wrong**: Combined average = (60+80)/2 = 70
✅ **Correct**: Combined average = (30×60 + 20×80)/(30+20) = (1800+1600)/50 = 3400/50 = **68**

The answer is NOT 70 because Class A has MORE students — it "pulls" the average towards 60.

### Formula

> **Weighted Average = (w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + … + wₙxₙ) / (w₁ + w₂ + … + wₙ)**
>
> Where w = weight (size/count/importance) and x = value

### Key Insight

> **Weighted average is ALWAYS between the individual averages.**
>
> It lies CLOSER to the average of the LARGER group.

In the example above: Average = 68, which is between 60 and 80, and closer to 60 (the larger group's average). ✅

### When Weights are in Ratio

If the weights are in ratio m:n, the formula simplifies:

> **Weighted average = (m × x₁ + n × x₂) / (m + n)**

## CONCEPT 8: Combined Average of Groups

### The Big Formula

If Group 1 has n₁ items with average a₁, and Group 2 has n₂ items with average a₂:

> **Combined average = (n₁ × a₁ + n₂ × a₂) / (n₁ + n₂)**

### This is just Weighted Average with groups!

### Shortcut: Alligation/Deviation Method

> **Combined average divides the distance between a₁ and a₂ in the INVERSE ratio of group sizes.**

If combined average = A:
- Distance from A to a₁ = |A − a₁|
- Distance from A to a₂ = |A − a₂|
- **n₁ : n₂ = |A − a₂| : |A − a₁|** (INVERSE of distances!)

### Visual: The See-Saw Method

```
    a₁ ──────── Combined Average (A) ──────── a₂
    n₁ students                              n₂ students
    
    Distance from A to a₁ × n₁ = Distance from A to a₂ × n₂
    
    n₁ : n₂ = (a₂ − A) : (A − a₁)
```

### Example

Group of 40 students: average = 55. Group of 60 students: average = 65. Combined average?

**Method 1 (Formula)**:
= (40×55 + 60×65) / (40+60) = (2200 + 3900)/100 = 6100/100 = **61**

**Method 2 (Alligation)**:
n₁:n₂ = 40:60 = 2:3
Combined average should divide 55 to 65 (distance = 10) in ratio 3:2 (inverse of 2:3)
From 55: move 3/(3+2) × 10 = 6 → 55 + 6 = **61** ✅

### Why Alligation is Faster

Once you understand the see-saw, you can find the combined average without any multiplication of large numbers.

## CONCEPT 9: Average Speed (THE MOST MISUNDERSTOOD CONCEPT)

### The Golden Rule

> **Average Speed ≠ Simple Average of Speeds**
>
> **Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time**

### Case 1: Equal Distances (MOST COMMON in exams)

If a person travels distance D at speed a, and the SAME distance D at speed b:

> **Average Speed = 2ab / (a + b)** (Harmonic Mean)

### Proof

- Time₁ = D/a, Time₂ = D/b
- Total distance = 2D
- Total time = D/a + D/b = D(a+b)/ab
- Average speed = 2D / [D(a+b)/ab] = **2ab/(a+b)**

### Example

A car goes from A to B at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Average speed?

= 2 × 60 × 40 / (60 + 40) = 4800/100 = **48 km/h**

❌ **NOT** (60+40)/2 = 50 km/h

### Why is it NOT 50?

Because the car spends MORE TIME at the slower speed (40 km/h). Time at 40 km/h is more than time at 60 km/h. So the average is "pulled" towards the slower speed.

> **Average speed is ALWAYS less than the simple average of two speeds (when distances are equal).**

### Case 2: Equal Times

If a person travels for time T at speed a, and the SAME time T at speed b:

> **Average Speed = (a + b) / 2** (Simple Arithmetic Mean)

This is the ONLY case where simple average works for speed!

### Case 3: Three Equal Distances

If distances are equal and speeds are a, b, c:

> **Average Speed = 3abc / (ab + bc + ca)**

### Case 4: Different Distances and Speeds

Use the fundamental formula:

> **Average Speed = (d₁ + d₂ + d₃) / (d₁/s₁ + d₂/s₂ + d₃/s₃)**

### Example (Different distances)

A person travels 120 km at 40 km/h and 80 km at 20 km/h. Average speed?

- Time₁ = 120/40 = 3 hours
- Time₂ = 80/20 = 4 hours
- Average speed = (120+80)/(3+4) = 200/7 = **28.57 km/h**

## CONCEPT 10: Change in Average — The Deviation Approach

### The MOST POWERFUL technique for Average problems

### Concept

If the average of n numbers changes by Δ (delta), it means the **total sum** changed by **n × Δ**.

> **Change in sum = n × Change in average**

### Application 1: A new person joins

"Average weight of 19 people is 60 kg. A new person joins, and average becomes 61 kg."

New person's weight = ?
- Old sum = 19 × 60 = 1140
- New sum = 20 × 61 = 1220
- New person = 1220 − 1140 = **80 kg**

**Shortcut thinking**: The average went up by 1 for ALL 20 people. That's 20 extra kg. The new person must "bring" 60 (the old average) + 20 (the extra) = **80 kg**.

### Application 2: Replacement

"Average of 30 players is 28 years. If a player aged 18 is replaced, average increases by 1."

Age of new player = ?
- Sum changed by: 30 × 1 = 30
- New player − 18 = 30
- New player = **48 years**

### Application 3: Wrong Value Corrected

"Average of 20 values was calculated as 46. Later it was found that one value was read as 57 instead of 75. Find correct average."

- Difference = 75 − 57 = +18
- Correction in average = 18/20 = +0.9
- Correct average = 46 + 0.9 = **46.9**

## CONCEPT 11: Cricket / Runs / Marks Type Questions

### Pattern 1: Including a new innings

"A batsman has an average of 42 runs in 30 innings. How many runs must he score in the 31st innings to raise his average to 44?"

- Required sum for 44 average = 44 × 31 = 1364
- Current sum = 42 × 30 = 1260
- Runs needed in 31st innings = 1364 − 1260 = **104**

**Shortcut**: Runs needed = New average + n × (New average − Old average)
= 44 + 30 × (44 − 42) = 44 + 60 = **104** ✅

### Pattern 2: Excluding an innings

"A batsman has average 35 in 40 innings. His average excluding the highest score is 34. Find highest score."

- Total = 35 × 40 = 1400
- Total without highest = 34 × 39 = 1326
- Highest = 1400 − 1326 = **74**

### Pattern 3: Average increases/decreases by fixed amount each innings

"After 20 innings, average is 32. After 21st innings, average increases by 2."

- New average = 34
- Runs in 21st = 34 + 20 × 2 = **74**

## CONCEPT 12: Average of Ages

### Key Principle

> **Average age changes by 1 every year** (for the same group of people).

If the average age of a family of 5 was 30 in 2020, then in 2023 it would be **33** (if no one joins or leaves).

### When a Baby is Born

"Average age of family of 4 is 25. A baby is born. New average = ?"

- Original sum = 25 × 4 = 100
- Baby's age = 0
- New sum = 100 + 0 = 100
- New average = 100/5 = **20**

### After k years from some reference

"5 years ago, average age of 6 members = 30. A child was born 3 years ago. Find present average."

- Sum 5 years ago = 30 × 6 = 180
- Present sum of original 6 = 180 + 6 × 5 = 210 (each aged 5 more)
- Child's present age = 3
- Total present sum = 210 + 3 = 213
- Present average = 213/7 = **30.43**

# STEP 5 — SHORTCUT METHODS


---

## Shortcut 1: Deviation Method for Any Average Calculation

**Don't add all numbers. Pick an assumed mean.**

Average of 83, 79, 91, 88, 74:
- Assume mean = 85
- Deviations: −2, −6, +6, +3, −11
- Sum of deviations = −10
- Correction = −10/5 = −2
- Average = 85 − 2 = **83** ✅

---

## Shortcut 2: "Change × n" for Replacement/Addition/Removal

> **If average changes by Δ and there are n values, the sum changed by n × Δ.**

This ONE principle solves 70% of all average questions.

**Example**: Average of 40 students is 55. One student scoring 80 is replaced. New average = 54.5. Find replacement score.

- Change in sum = 40 × (54.5 − 55) = 40 × (−0.5) = −20
- New score − 80 = −20
- New score = **60**

---

## Shortcut 3: Joining Person Shortcut

"n people have average A. A new person joins, new average = A'."

> **New person's value = A' + n(A' − A) = A' × (n+1) − A × n**

**Example**: 9 people, avg = 40. New person joins, avg = 42.
New person = 42 + 9(42−40) = 42 + 18 = **60**

**Intuition**: The new person must bring enough to:
- Cover their own average (42)
- Plus raise everyone else's average by 2 each (9 × 2 = 18)

---

## Shortcut 4: Average Speed — The "Product over Sum" Trick

For two equal distances:

> **Average speed = 2 × S₁ × S₂ / (S₁ + S₂)**

**Mnemonics**: "**Two** equal distances → **2** × product / sum"

**Example**: 30 km/h and 70 km/h (equal distances):
= 2 × 30 × 70 / (30+70) = 4200/100 = **42 km/h**

Quick check: Is 42 less than simple average (50)? YES ✅ (it should always be less)
Is 42 between 30 and 70? YES ✅

---

## Shortcut 5: Average of AP Without Listing Terms

**Average of any AP = (First term + Last term) / 2**

No need to list all terms!

**Example**: Average of all multiples of 3 between 1 and 100:
- First multiple = 3, Last = 99
- Average = (3+99)/2 = **51**

**Example**: Average of all odd numbers from 1 to 200:
- First = 1, Last = 199
- Average = (1+199)/2 = **100**

---

## Shortcut 6: Combined Average Using Alligation

When combining two groups, use the alligation diagram:

```
        Group 1 avg (a₁)          Group 2 avg (a₂)
                    \              /
                     \            /
                   Combined avg (A)
                    /             \
                   /               \
        (a₂ − A)                 (A − a₁)
        = n₁'s weight            = n₂'s weight
```

> **n₁ : n₂ = (a₂ − A) : (A − a₁)**

**Example**: Combined avg of 2 groups is 45. Group 1 avg = 40, Group 2 avg = 55. Find ratio of sizes.

n₁:n₂ = (55−45):(45−40) = 10:5 = **2:1**

---

## Shortcut 7: Average Age Shortcut

> **For the same group of k people, after t years, new average = old average + t.**

No calculation needed!

"Average age of 5 members 4 years ago was 28. Present average = 28 + 4 = **32**."

---

## Shortcut 8: Wrong Value Correction

> **Correct average = Wrong average + (Correct value − Wrong value) / n**

**Example**: Average of 50 numbers was found to be 38.5. One number 63 was misread as 36.

Correction = (63 − 36)/50 = 27/50 = 0.54
Correct average = 38.5 + 0.54 = **39.04**

# STEP 6 — CONFUSION ZONES


---

## Confusion 1: Average Speed vs Simple Average of Speeds

| | Simple Average | Average Speed |
|---|---|---|
| **Formula** | (a+b)/2 | Total distance / Total time |
| **When correct** | When TIMES are equal | ALWAYS correct |
| **For equal distances** | ❌ WRONG to use | Use 2ab/(a+b) |
| **For equal times** | ✅ Works: (a+b)/2 | Same result |
| **Which is larger?** | Always ≥ Average speed | Always ≤ Simple average |

> **The ONLY time simple average = average speed is when both speeds are EQUAL.**

---

## Confusion 2: Weighted Average vs Simple Average

| | Simple Average | Weighted Average |
|---|---|---|
| **When to use** | All groups have EQUAL size | Groups have DIFFERENT sizes |
| **Formula** | Sum/n | Σ(wᵢxᵢ)/Σwᵢ |
| **Example** | 5 students scored 60, 70, 80 → avg=70 | 30 students avg 60 + 20 students avg 80 → avg ≠ 70 |

> **Whenever group sizes differ, you MUST use weighted average.**

---

## Confusion 3: Median vs Average

| | Average (Mean) | Median |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Sum/count | Middle value when sorted |
| **Affected by extremes** | YES (greatly) | NO (robust) |
| **Example** | 2, 3, 4, 5, 86 → avg = 20 | median = 4 |
| **When equal** | When data is symmetric | Same |

Exams usually ask for mean. Median appears in UPSC CSAT and occasional SSC questions.

---

## Confusion 4: "n people" vs "n+1 people" After Addition

When a new person joins a group of n:
- Old group = n people
- New group = **n+1** people (NOT n)
- New average = New sum / **(n+1)**

Students sometimes divide by n instead of n+1. Always count correctly!

---

## Confusion 5: Average of "First n" vs "From 1 to n"

| Statement | Meaning | Average |
|---|---|---|
| "First n natural numbers" | 1, 2, 3, …, n | (n+1)/2 |
| "Natural numbers from 1 to n" | Same thing | (n+1)/2 |
| "First n EVEN numbers" | 2, 4, 6, …, 2n | n+1 |
| "Even numbers from 1 to n" | 2, 4, 6, …, n (if n is even) | (n+2)/2 = (n/2 + 1) |

"First n even" and "even numbers up to n" are DIFFERENT when n is the count vs the upper limit!

---

## Confusion 6: Average Increase "by" vs "to"

| Statement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Average increased **by** 2" | New average = Old + 2 |
| "Average increased **to** 42" | New average = 42 |

# STEP 7 — EXAMINER TRAP GUIDE


---

| # | Trap | How it works | How to detect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Average speed = (a+b)/2** | Students use simple average for equal distances | Check: are distances equal or times equal? | 60 and 40 km/h → avg = 48, NOT 50 |
| 2 | **Forgetting group size** | Combined average with unequal groups → students take simple average | Always check: are group sizes given? | 30 students + 20 students ≠ just average the averages |
| 3 | **Adding new person: dividing by n instead of n+1** | Group grows from n to n+1 | Count the NEW total carefully | 9 people + 1 = 10 people total |
| 4 | **Wrong "change in average" direction** | If removed value > old average → avg decreases (students may think increase) | Think: removing a TALL person lowers the group's avg height | |
| 5 | **Confusing "first n" with "up to n"** | First 10 even numbers (2 to 20) vs even numbers up to 10 (2 to 10) | Count the terms carefully | First 10 even = {2,4,...,20}, not {2,4,...,10} |
| 6 | **Two different wrong readings** | Average was computed with TWO errors → need BOTH corrections | Check: how many values were misread? | Apply both corrections to the sum |
| 7 | **Average age after years** | "3 years ago average was X" → students forget to add 3 to each person | Each person ages equally: add 3 per person = add 3 to average | |
| 8 | **Baby born: age ≠ 0** | "Baby born 2 years ago" → present age = 2, not 0 | Check: when was the baby born? | |
| 9 | **Avg speed with 3 different distances** | Students use 2ab/(a+b) with 3 speeds | For 3 equal distances: 3abc/(ab+bc+ca) | |
| 10 | **Avg of 0 to n vs 1 to n** | Including 0 adds an extra term and changes average | "0 to n" has n+1 terms, "1 to n" has n terms | Avg of 0 to 10 = 5 (11 terms), avg of 1 to 10 = 5.5 (10 terms) |
| 11 | **Partial replacement** | "Some of the numbers are replaced" → need to track which ones | Set up equations for each replacement | |

## Speed Rule 1: Memorize Number Series Averages

Don't calculate — just recall:

| Series | Average |
|---|---|
| 1 to 10 | 5.5 |
| 1 to 20 | 10.5 |
| 1 to 50 | 25.5 |
| 1 to 100 | 50.5 |
| 1 to 1000 | 500.5 |

| First 10 odd (1 to 19) | 10 |
| All 2-digit numbers | 54.5 |
| All 3-digit numbers | 549.5 |

## Speed Rule 2: The "n × Δ" Rule Eliminates All Long Calculations

> **Whenever average changes → total changed by n × Δ.**

Don't compute individual sums unless absolutely necessary.

## Speed Rule 3: Average Speed Quick Check

After calculating average speed, verify:
1. Is it LESS than the simple average? (It should be for equal distances)
2. Is it BETWEEN the two speeds? (It must be)
3. Is it CLOSER to the slower speed? (It should be for equal distances)

If any check fails, you made an error.

## Speed Rule 4: For Combined Average, Use Alligation When Possible

If you know the combined average and want to find the ratio of groups (or vice versa), alligation is instant:

> **n₁:n₂ = (a₂ − Combined) : (Combined − a₁)**

## Speed Rule 5: Uniform Change Shortcuts

| Change applied to each value | Effect on average |
|---|---|
| Add k to each | Average + k |
| Subtract k from each | Average − k |
| Multiply each by k | Average × k |
| Divide each by k | Average / k |

No calculation needed! These are direct.

## Mnemonic 1: Average Speed for Equal Distances

> **"HM not AM"** — Harmonic Mean, not Arithmetic Mean
>
> **"2-Product-Over-Sum"**: 2ab/(a+b)
>
> Remember: "Two equal trips → Two times product over sum"

## Mnemonic 2: Which Formula When

> **Equal Distances → Harmonic Mean**: 2ab/(a+b)
> **Equal Times → Arithmetic Mean**: (a+b)/2
>
> Memory: **"D-H, T-A"** (Distance → Harmonic, Time → Arithmetic)
> Think: "**D**HA**T**" — like a hat! 🎩

---

## Mnemonic 3: Average of First n Natural Numbers

> **"n Plus One, Divide by Two"**
> (n+1)/2

---

## Mnemonic 4: The n × Δ Principle

> **"If average changes by a LITTLE, sum changes by a LOT"**
>
> Δ in average × n = Δ in sum
>
> Think: "Average is the CONCENTRATED form. Multiply by n to DILUTE back to sum."

---

## Visualization: The See-Saw

```
     a₁=40          Combined=?          a₂=60
     ┌───┐               ▲               ┌───┐
     │ 30│               │               │ 20│
     │ppl│               │               │ppl│
     └───┘               │               └───┘
═════════════════════════════════════════════════
     
     See-saw balances at: 40 + 30/(30+20) × (60−40) = 40 + 12 = 52
     
     OR: 30 × 40 + 20 × 60 = 1200 + 1200 = 2400 / 50 = 48
     
     Wait: 30×(52−40) = 360 = 20×(60−52) = 160... no.
     Let me recalculate: (30×40 + 20×60)/50 = (1200+1200)/50 = 2400/50 = 48.
     
     Check alligation: n₁:n₂ = (60−48):(48−40) = 12:8 = 3:2 = 30:20 ✅
```

The combined average is CLOSER to the larger group's average.

## Story Trick: Why Average Speed ≠ Simple Average

> You drive to your friend's house at 30 km/h (took a long time — traffic).
> You drive back at 90 km/h (empty roads — very fast).
>
> Simple average: (30+90)/2 = 60 km/h.
>
> But think: you spent WAY more time driving at 30 km/h (3× more time!).
> So the average should be "pulled" towards 30, not balanced at 60.
>
> Average speed = 2×30×90/(30+90) = 5400/120 = **45 km/h**.
> Much closer to 30 than to 90! Makes sense — you spent more time being slow.

## Pattern Trick: "Added Value" Quick Mental Calculation

When someone joins a group:

> **New person's value = New average + (Number of OLD members) × (Increase in average)**

Think of it as: "The new person pays for their own average seat PLUS tips everyone else."

### Level 8: Mixed Concept

**Q8.** The average of 20 numbers was calculated as 35. Later it was found that two numbers were wrongly read as 42 and 68 instead of 24 and 86 respectively. Find the correct average.

(C) 34
(D) 35.5

<summary>🔑 Click to reveal answer after attempting</summary>

**Answer: (A) 35**

BASIC: Average = Sum/n. Sum = Average × n. These 3 forms solve everything.

AGE: Same group, t years later → avg + t.
